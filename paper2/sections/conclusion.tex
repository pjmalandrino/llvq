\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A Leech-lattice code can be read by the GPU as it is stored. Addressing the
lattice through a 64-state trellis of the Golay code and one 16~KiB table
brings the bits read per weight from 4.804 to 2.148, for a codebook that
bounds each third of a block separately rather than the whole block. Around
that codebook, three changes that leave the codes untouched give served files
of Qwen3-4B, 8B and 14B at about 2.7 bits per parameter, scoring 63.37, 69.58
and 75.66 on MMLU and decoding 113.8, 95.0 and 57.2 tokens per second in 1.38,
2.76 and 5.04~GB on one L40S. They stay below 4-bit AWQ, by 4.76, 4.21 and 2.46
points, with about half its memory, and the gap shrinks with size.

The next measurements we would make, in order: \texttt{down\_proj} instead of
\texttt{o\_proj} at 4B and 14B, a second calibration draw per size, a model
larger than 14B, and a second GPU.
