\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The GPU can read a Leech-lattice code without expanding it, if the codebook
bounds each third of a block separately. Three more changes, which leave the
lattice codes untouched, make complete Qwen3 files at about 2.7 bits per
parameter. They stay below 4-bit AWQ on MMLU and GSM8K, and perplexity ranks
them differently. At the same bits they score above llama.cpp's IQ2\_S on
MMLU and perplexity at every size, by a margin that shrinks from 9.08 points
at 4B to 2.89 at 14B.

Running a token in one launch makes the 4B 1.72 times faster on the L40S and
leaves its tokens unchanged. llama.cpp still decodes faster and holds less
memory after a long prompt, and the kernel's gain depends on the card: on an
A100, cuBLAS reads f16 weights faster than our kernel reads \Tetra{}.

Next, in this order, we would carry the chain prefill to the 8B and the 14B,
score MMLU and GSM8K in full through the served engine, add a second
calibration draw, and measure a consumer GPU. The ablation of
\S\ref{sec:ablation} places the remaining loss at 4B in the MLP. Qwen3-32B
has not been encoded.
