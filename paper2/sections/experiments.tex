\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Models and encoding.} Qwen3-4B, 8B and 14B~\citep{qwen3_2025},
encoded with 131{,}072 tokens of DCLM-edu~\citep{dclmedu} as calibration, then
trained and assembled as described in \S\ref{sec:model}. One calibration draw
per size.

\paragraph{Quality.} MMLU~\citep{mmlu2021}, 5-shot, on the full test set of
14{,}042 questions, micro-averaged, with the answer read from the logits of
the four answer letters. Each of our files is scored as its dense
reconstruction: the stored weights decoded to f16 and run through an ordinary
forward pass. The kernel computes with the same decoded weights; the per-row
checks of \S\ref{sec:kernel} and the 256-token comparison below tie the two.
Comparisons are paired on the same questions: a 95\,\% interval from a
bootstrap stratified by subject (10{,}000 draws) and an exact McNemar test. On
this test set, differences under about 0.4 points are not resolved.

\paragraph{Speed and memory.} Decode speed is measured at batch 1 with greedy
sampling on one NVIDIA L40S: tokens generated divided by the wall time of the
generation, prompt included, median of five timed rounds after warm-up. Our
files run in our engine at the served settings, 256 tokens. FP16 and AWQ run
in vLLM 0.26.0 with the Marlin kernel, 128 tokens~\citep{vllm2023}. IQ2\_XXS
runs in llama.cpp's \texttt{llama-bench}: 128 tokens from an empty prompt, mean
of five repetitions. Each engine is only compared with itself, and we never
divide one engine's speed by another's. Memory is the weight bytes: for our
files, the device buffers the engine allocates (projections and tables); for
the other formats, the size of their weight files. KV cache and activations
are not counted.

\paragraph{Baselines.} FP16 is the published checkpoint. AWQ is Qwen's official
w4g128 checkpoint at each size; its MMLU is scored in our harness after
dequantization to f16, its speed in vLLM on the packed checkpoint. IQ2\_XXS is
built with \texttt{llama-quantize} and an importance matrix computed on the
same calibration shard as our GPTQ runs, and scored with the same prompts
through \texttt{llama-server}. QTIP, QuIP\# and the original LLVQ are not run
here; their MMLU is cited from \citet{llvq2026}.

\paragraph{Quality.} The sealed files score 63.37, 69.58 and 75.66 at 4B, 8B
and 14B (Table~\ref{tab:main}). Paired on the same questions, they sit below
FP16 by 6.77 points $[6.05, 7.50]$, 5.48 $[4.83, 6.10]$ and 3.22
$[2.69, 3.75]$, and below AWQ by 4.76 $[4.02, 5.49]$, 4.21 $[3.54, 4.86]$ and
2.46 $[1.92, 3.00]$. Both gaps shrink as the model grows
(Figure~\ref{fig:scale}, left). AWQ itself is 2.00, 1.27 and 0.75 points below
FP16.

\paragraph{Memory.} The sealed files hold 2.73, 2.70 and 2.73 bits per
parameter, against 5.30, 5.96 and 5.40 for AWQ: 45 to 52\,\% of its memory
(Figure~\ref{fig:scale}, middle). AWQ's rate is highest at 8B because its
untied embedding and head stay in f16, which is 15.2\,\% of that model's
parameters.

\paragraph{Against other 2-bit formats at 4B.} IQ2\_XXS reads 39.78 at 2.48
bits per parameter, 23.6 points below our 4B file for 0.25 fewer bits per
parameter. The cited rows come from another harness and count only the
projections, so they place our 63.37 rather than rank it: it is next to the
62.8 of the original LLVQ with fine-tuning and above QTIP's 59.5.

Table~\ref{tab:chain} scores each step against the previous one. Training the
row scales gains 3.15, 3.29 and 1.67 points without adding a byte. Adding
int4 projections and 4-bit tables gains 2.26, 1.42 and 1.46 points more, and
the files get smaller at the same time. All six gains are resolved, with $p$
below $10^{-7}$.

\paragraph{Which projections get the int4 bytes.} At 8B the first sealed file
stored all of \texttt{o\_proj} and \texttt{down\_proj} of layers 2 to 33 in
int4. It scored 70.08 but needed 3.06 bits per parameter, because the 8B model
has two embedding tables to pay for. To bring it to 2.7 we spent the same
bytes in two ways. With all of \texttt{o\_proj} and \texttt{down\_proj} of
layers 15 to 20, the file reads 68.81 at 2.7047 bits per parameter. With
\texttt{down\_proj} of layers 10 to 26 and no \texttt{o\_proj}, it reads 69.58
at 2.6953. The second is better by 0.77 points $[0.26, 1.28]$, $p=0.004$, for
fewer bytes, and is the 8B file of Table~\ref{tab:main}. It sits 0.50 points
below the 3.06-bit file, a difference this test set does not resolve
($[-0.01, 1.01]$, $p=0.063$). We had predicted the opposite ranking, from an
exploration on the 4B sample. The 4B and 14B files still use \texttt{o\_proj};
whether \texttt{down\_proj} alone would do better there is not tested.

\paragraph{A second training does not help.} On the sealed 4B file, a second
training that also learns the 73 RMSNorm weight vectors lowers the KL loss from
0.262 to about 0.20 and moves MMLU by $+0.02$ points $[-0.31, +0.36]$
($p=0.93$). We keep the file without it.
