\section{The Tetra construction}
\label{sec:tetra}

\subsection{The lattice and the quantizer}

We use the integer form of the Leech lattice. A point $y\in\mathbb{Z}^{24}$
lies in $\Lambda_{24}$, up to the global scale the quantizer fixes, when there
are a bit $p$, a codeword $c$ of the binary Golay code $\mathcal{G}_{24}$ and
integers $k_j$ with
\begin{equation}
y_j = p + 2c_j + 4k_j,\qquad \textstyle\sum_j k_j \equiv p \pmod 2 .
\label{eq:leech}
\end{equation}
Two constraints cut $\mathbb{Z}^{24}$ down to the lattice
(Fig.~\ref{fig:geom}a): $c$ must be one of the $2^{12}$ Golay codewords among
$2^{24}$ bit patterns, and one parity couples all 24 coordinates at once. They
are what makes the code 24-dimensional, and a decoder has to respect both.

The encoder is the shape--gain scheme of \citet{llvq2026} inside a GPTQ-style
error-compensating loop~\citep{gptq2023} with an incoherence rotation on the
input~\citep{quip2023,quarot2024}. For each block of 24 rotated weights it
finds the nearest point of a norm-capped region of $\Lambda_{24}$ and
reconstructs
\begin{equation}
\hat{w}_j = \frac{y_j}{\lVert y\rVert}\,\mathrm{gain}[g]\,\sigma_{\mathrm{row}},
\qquad \lVert y\rVert=\sqrt{16m},
\label{eq:recon}
\end{equation}
with $m$ the shell index, $g$ a gain bit and $\sigma_{\mathrm{row}}$ one f32
scale per output row. We change two things in that pipeline: the region the
encoder searches (\S\ref{sec:kept}) and the label it writes. The loop, the
rotation, the gain centroids and the row scales are unchanged, and the encoder
still runs once, offline, on CPU.

\subsection{The trio and the trellis}

An octad is a Golay codeword of weight 8, and three pairwise disjoint octads
cover the 24 coordinates. We fix one such \emph{trio} and read the coordinates
in that order, octad by octad. In this order the code sectionalises: cutting
after coordinates 8 and 16 leaves a trellis with 64 states at each cut
(Fig.~\ref{fig:geom}b), the fewest a three-way cut of $\mathcal{G}_{24}$
reaches. The trellis has $64\times2\times16\times2 = 4096 = |\mathcal{G}_{24}|$
paths, so every codeword is one path and every path a codeword; the count is
asserted when the tables are built.

\subsection{State, rank vectors, and one table for three sections}
\label{sec:state}

The word carries eight bits of state: the trellis state $s_8$ (6 bits), the
shared parity $p$, and $r$, the parity of $\sum k_j$ over section 1. What $s_8$
holds is exactly what section 2 needs to know about section 1, since the code
forbids most byte triples. So $\Lambda_{24}$ adds two bits to the code's own
state and no more: $64\times4 = 256$ lattice states at a cut.

Each section's $k$-parity class is implicit. Section 1's is $r$. Section 2's is
$\delta$, read off which half of the shared table its index lands in. Section
3's is
\begin{equation}
r_3 = p \oplus r \oplus \delta ,
\label{eq:xor}
\end{equation}
and is stored nowhere: the parity constraint of Eq.~\eqref{eq:leech} costs one
XOR and no bits.

Inside a section the residues mod 4 are fixed once $p$ and the pattern byte are
known, $o_j = p + 2c_j$, so coordinate $j$ can only take values in the
progression $o_j + 4\mathbb{Z}$. We store its \emph{rank} in that progression
rather than its value, counting outward from zero: for $o=0$ the progression is
$0, +4, -4, +8, \dots$, for $o=2$ it is $+2, -2, +6, \dots$, for $o=1$
$+1, -3, +5, \dots$, and for $o=3$ $-1, +3, -5, \dots$. A section is then a
rank vector $\rho \in \{0\dots7\}^8$: four bits per coordinate, one
\texttt{u32}.

Two properties let a single table serve every section of every block. First,
with $\mathrm{cost}(\rho)=\sum_j(2\rho_j+1)^2$, the order of rank vectors by
cost does not depend on $p$ or on the pattern byte, so ``keep the 2{,}048
cheapest'' is one list rather than $4^8$ of them. Second,
$\mathrm{cls}(\rho)=\sum_j[\rho_j\in\{1,2\}] \bmod 2$ equals the parity of
$\sum_j k_j$ for every pattern, so a section's class is an address rather than
a field. The table holds 4{,}096 \texttt{u32}: the 2{,}048 cheapest of class 0,
then the 2{,}048 cheapest of class 1, with eleven bits addressing a half. The
middle section reads the same rows in a third, mixed order, the 2{,}048
cheapest overall, which is $N_0 = 1240$ rows of class 0 followed by 808 of
class 1; its index therefore reveals the $\delta$ that Eq.~\eqref{eq:xor}
consumes.

\subsection{The word and the decode}
\label{sec:decode}

Figure~\ref{fig:word} puts the resulting 48-bit word beside the unfolded record
it replaces. Every field range is a power of two, so any 48-bit value is a
legal word and decodes to a point of $\Lambda_{24}$; a benchmark may stream
pseudo-random words and check every decode against a reference.
Figure~\ref{fig:decode} is the decode: six loads a block, three of them on a
dependent chain.

The tables total 18{,}816~B: the rank table $4096\times$\texttt{u32}
$=16{,}384$~B, \texttt{branches} $1024\times$\texttt{u16} $=2{,}048$~B, two
byte tables of 128~B, and \texttt{invnorm}, $32\times$\texttt{f32}, holding
$1/\sqrt{16m}$ with entry $0$ set to $0$ so that the origin, a legal word,
reconstructs to zeros without a branch. The bound $m\le27$ comes from sweeping
all 4{,}096 rows against both parities and both residues, which gives
$\max|y_j|=10$.

\subsection{What the construction changes, and what it costs}
\label{sec:kept}

\Tetra{} is not a repacking of the earlier codebook but a different codebook on
the same lattice. Keeping the 2{,}048 cheapest rank vectors bounds each section
separately, so the encoder searches a product of three 8-dimensional regions
where \Planes{} searched a 24-dimensional ball. That is what buys the six-load
decode, and that is what it costs.

How much it costs, we estimate rather than measure. The best shaping gain of a
product of three 8-dimensional balls is 0.7292~dB, against 1.0958~dB for the
24-dimensional ball: a difference of 0.3666~dB, or 8.81\,\% of MSE at our
operating point, and 89.10\,\% retention against 92.14\,\% at two bits per
dimension. Our regions are neither balls nor continuous, since they are
rank-truncated and trellis-constrained, so those figures are a geometric
reference and not the cost of this codebook. One related effect is measured:
ranking rank vectors by $\mathrm{cost}(\rho)$ rather than by squared distance
loses 0.6~pp of retention against an exact per-section enumeration.

What the construction keeps is the lattice. The decoded point satisfies both
constraints of Fig.~\ref{fig:geom}: Golay membership, because the pattern bytes
come from a trellis path; and the $\sum k$ parity, by Eq.~\eqref{eq:xor}. Three
independent $E_8$ quantizers would keep neither, and the lattice alone is worth
$G(E_8)/G(\Lambda_{24}) = 0.071682/0.065771 = 1.0899$, that is 9.0\,\% of
MSE~\citep{conway1999sphere}.

Against the codebook it replaces, on the identical harness and the same model,
bare \Tetra{} reads $2.10$~pp lower MMLU and $4.64\,\%$ lower perplexity than
\Planes{}. Re-encoding the same model with a different calibration draw moves
MMLU by $2.92$~pp over three seeds, which is larger than the difference between
the two codebooks. This experiment therefore does not separate the effect of
the codebook change from that of the calibration draw.
