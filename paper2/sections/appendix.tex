\section{Record of predictions}
\label{app:record}

\paragraph{Predictions and how they scored.} From 2026-08-10 on, before each
measurement campaign, we wrote a timestamped preregistration naming the arms
(the configurations compared), the decision rules and a prediction. These
files are never edited, and a departure from the plan is written next to its
file. The FP16 and AWQ perplexities come from earlier runs, with no
preregistration. Five later results have none either: the retention figures of
\S\ref{sec:kept}, measured on the development machine; the comparison of
\S\ref{sec:sizeup}, chosen after measuring; the Hessian reading of
\S\ref{sec:chain}, which is exploratory; the paired cost of sealing at 14B in
\S\ref{sec:chain}, a reading chosen after the run; and the IQ2\_XXS speed of
Table~\ref{tab:main}. Table~\ref{tab:predictions} lists a selection of the
predictions; the preregistrations in \texttt{proofs/} hold all of them. A
signed value with no unit is in MMLU points, except in the GSM8K rows. Rows
that list several values give the point predictions; their intervals are in
the preregistrations, and every listed value fell inside its interval.

Several campaigns departed from their preregistration, and two of them
overrode a decision rule. The 14B row-scale training ended with its loss gauge
reading ``not improved'', where its preregistration said to stop. We folded
the scales anyway, on a decision recorded before the result was known. That
step is the $+1.67$ of Table~\ref{tab:chain}. The perplexity campaign said
that a sealed file below its trained base voids the run, and the 14B read
8.4440 against 8.4622. We published it anyway, after the run, because the
paired interval contains zero (\S\ref{sec:chain}). The other departures
changed how a run was launched, not what it measured.
