THE DOMAIN OF VALIDITY, WHICH IS THE POINT
-------------------------------------------
Prediction error as a fraction of the predicted move, split B (held out):

  matrices moved     alpha = 0.5     alpha = 1.0
  1                    +0.103          +0.102
  2                    +0.124          +0.150

  16                   +0.084          +0.152
  32                   +0.129          +0.226
  64                   +0.166          +0.297
  140                  +0.188          +0.355

The structure is exactly what a diagonal model predicts of itself: the error
grows with the number of matrices moved and with the size of the move, because
both grow the cross terms the surrogate does not carry. It is also always the
same sign: the surrogate OVERSTATES the gain, which makes it optimistic. What
saves the ranking is not the sign of the error but its regularity — the same
overstatement applies across configurations, so order survives (Spearman
+0.9973) even though magnitude does not.

Read as a rule: **the model predicts within 15 % up to 16 matrices at full
amplitude, and within 20 % up to 140 matrices at half amplitude. It degrades to
35 % at 140 matrices at full amplitude.** That is the domain, measured rather
than assumed.

Random selections behave like ranked ones at the same size, so the accuracy is a
property of the size of the move and not of the map's own ordering.

IS IT JUST "MANY MATRICES WERE UNDER-SCALED"?
----------------------------------------------
The external review's sharpest objection: with 147 of 196 optima at the region's
edge, the map may have found one systematic direction rather than a per-matrix
structure. That is testable on the map itself, and it is refuted.

Of the 140 matrices the loss can see, **72 want dilating and 68 want shrinking**
— 51.4 % against 48.6 %. The corrections contradict each other almost exactly,
which is why a single global scalar, swept over four re-encoded arms, bought
0.836 % where the per-matrix map measures 17.9 %. There is no global direction
to find; the content of the map is the disagreement between matrices.

WHAT IS STILL UNTESTED
-----------------------
Baselines this run does not carry, all named by the same review:
  - corrections of random SIGN and size, rather than a random subset of the
    map's own corrections, which is what `random k` is here and is therefore a
    much weaker control than its name suggests;
  - a heuristic built from the local error, which is what the map is supposed
    to beat;
  - validation on a different corpus (C4 or dclm-edu), which is the difference
    between generalizing across splits and generalizing at all.

LIMITS
-------
One model, one seed. Probes and validation are all wikitext-2: the map
generalizes across SPLITS of one corpus, which is not the same as generalizing
across domains. A C4 validation is the next thing owed, and it is cheap.

The claimed gain over all 196 matrices at their own optimum is -0.3385 of NLL.
The measured one at 140 matrices is -0.1974. The gap is the cross terms, and the
table above is what quantifies it.

147 of 196 optima sit at the edge of the trust region, so the map is bounded by
its own guard rail. A wider region would claim more and predict worse; where
that trade sits is unmeasured.

Post-hoc scaling is not re-encoding. Serving this correction means writing the
scaled centroids into the artifact after the loop, which is exactly what was
measured here — not re-running the loop with scaled centroids, which was
measured at 1.02 and is a different thing.
