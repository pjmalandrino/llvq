LOSS, as the prereg defines it. The candidate scores 57.11 % on the full MMLU split and the baseline 58.62 %: the paired
difference, candidate minus baseline, is -1.51 points, 95 % interval [-2.30, -0.70], SE 0.41 (*measured*, 14,042

same way: 16.456 for the baseline, 18.079 for the candidate, +9.86 % (*measured*, 12 windows of 4,096).

The two files differ by one setting. In the candidate, the input Hessian of every gate_proj and up_proj matrix weights
each calibration token by the squared gradient of the FP16 model's next-token loss on that matrix's output, summed over
a group of output rows, G = 4 groups (GuidedQuant's objective). Everything else is the recipe of the 2026-09-18 base.

What the result says: on this calibration draw, this form of the final-output objective, with weights taken from 64
windows, makes a worse 4B than the layer objective. What it does not say is in the last section.

A POST HOC DIAGNOSTIC, NOT PREREGISTERED: THE WEIGHTS ARE CONCENTRATED ON FEW TOKENS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Read after the result, from the 72 weight files, at no cost (weights-diagnostic/ess.py, pos.py and their outputs).
All *computed*.

- Effective sample size of a row group's weights, (sum w)^2 / sum w^2, as a share of the 131,072 tokens: 1.58 % on
  average over the 72 matrices at G = 4, about 2,070 tokens. The lowest matrix average is 0.16 % (layer 1), the highest
  15.6 % (layer 35 up_proj). The worst single group is at 0.01 %, about 13 tokens.
- The top 1 % of tokens carry 46 % of the weight mass on average, and up to 77 %.
- It is not the first position: position 0 carries 0.4 % of the mass, positions 0 to 15 carry 2.7 %.
- Clipping each weight at 10 times the mean would raise the effective share from 2.0 % to 18.8 % (G = 1).

gate_proj and up_proj have 2,560 inputs. The baseline estimates each Hessian from 131,072 tokens. The candidate
estimates each of its four from the equivalent of about 2,000, fewer than the matrix has inputs. A Hessian that thin is
dominated by its damping and by a handful of tokens. This is a candidate cause of the loss, a *conjecture*: no encode
has tested it. It predicts that tamed weights (clipped, or mixed with the uniform ones) or more windows would do
better than this candidate. It does not predict that they would beat the baseline.

WHAT THE RUN DOES NOT SAY
-------------------------
- It does not say that a final-output objective cannot help. One form ran: per-token gradient weights on the input
  Hessian, G = 4, from 64 windows, on two matrix types. The two-sided form (YAQA) did not run.
- It does not attribute the -1.51 to the objective alone. One calibration draw; changing gate/up moves the input of
  every later layer, so part of the difference behaves like another draw (prereg section 6).
- It says nothing on the sealed 4B. Neither arm carries row scales or the q4 embedding; row scales are worth +3.15 at
  4B and are trained on a final-output KL, so they may absorb part of what this objective targets.
- Perplexity agreed with MMLU here. That is one pair and does not restore perplexity as a ranking proxy (Q5).
- Secondary scores of prereg section 5 (KL to FP16 of the two exports, the answer-logit fit) were not run.
