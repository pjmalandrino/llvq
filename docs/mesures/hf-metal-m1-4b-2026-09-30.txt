THE MUTANT THAT MATTERS: THE TOKEN GATE IS THIN
-----------------------------------------------
  the row scales dropped     caught, all 4 prompts moved within 8 ids
  the rotation skipped       caught, all 4 prompts moved within 8 ids
  the tail dropped           SURVIVED 8 ids on all 4 prompts.
                             At 64 ids: caught on 2 prompts of 4, diverging at
                             token 13 and at token 21; the other two are identical
                             over all 64.

That mutant is not void, and it was checked rather than argued: with the tail
zeroed the per-row error against the dense reconstruction goes from 2.61e-5 to
8.79e-2 on k_proj and from 8.79e-6 to 3.53e-2 on down_proj. The kernel reads the
tail, and dropping it costs 8.79 % and 3.53 % of the output.

So a 3 to 9 % per-row error crosses four easy prompts for 13 tokens, and crosses
two of them entirely. The per-row check finds the same defect in 20 s with a
3,400-fold margin. Stages 1, 2 and M1 all took token identity as their gate; this
is the first measurement of what that gate cannot see, and it is a fact about the
method, not about this lot.
