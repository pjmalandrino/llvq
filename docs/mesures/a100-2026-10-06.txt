Tetra is the first lattice arm faster than our FP16 kernel on this card.
Every earlier layout stays under it, as F4 (2026-08-19) and A4 (2026-08-31)
found: Planes14 0.79x again. cuBLAS reads the same f16 weights faster still,
6.050 ms, 1.14x, and our dense path runs its projections through cuBLAS
(candle's matmul). Corrected 2026-10-06: the first version of this journal
said "faster than FP16" without naming which FP16. AWQ stays 1.65 times faster than Tetra in time, from the medians,
and the floor alone, 4.070 ms, is above AWQ's 3.777 with its weights.

The four arms F4 timed reproduce within 1 %: FP16 6.899 against 6.915, AWQ
3.777 against 3.793, Planes14 8.700 against 8.742, nullk 4.070 against 4.107.

THE TILE SWEEP, ONE PROCESS PER TILE
------------------------------------
  tile    nullk    FP16    Planes14   Tetra48   Tetra vs FP16
  128     4.032    6.908    8.673     6.183     1.12x
   64     3.960    7.144    8.676     6.361     1.12x
   32     4.098    6.939    8.645     6.517     1.07x

Tetra is fastest at 128 here, where the L40S put it at 64. Its range over the
three tiles is 5.4 %, against 19.1 % on the L40S over its 216-matrix arm.
Planes14 moves 0.4 %. An Ampere SM has 192 KiB of L1 and shared memory where
an Ada SM has 128, so a 128-block tile leaves the decoder table more cache
here. That reading is consistent with the paper's mechanism and proves it no
more than the L40S did: no counters were read.

  The x columns are fusedrun's own quotients of medians, rounds not interleaved.
  Gates: oracle MATCH (max |dhidden| 0); the three published sha256; the
  prefill argmax equal to the dense one at every size. 256 tokens identical to
  the dense arm at 4B and 8B; first divergence at token 78 at 14B, with q4 AND
  with f16 tables (on the L40S: 78 and 137).

With equal tables, the served files run 5 to 9 % SLOWER than our own dense f16
path on this card. On the L40S the same arm ran 1.27 to 1.61 times FASTER. The
whole speed advantage on the A100 comes from the 4-bit tables. The kernel bench
says the projections alone gain 1.11x over FP16; the served path adds the int4
records (84 at 4B), the online rotation and the per-token launches, and on a
card whose floor is 1.69x FP16's time those costs outweigh the gain.

The one miss is the one that matters for the paper: the Tetra kernel was
predicted to lose to FP16 here, extrapolated from F4's floor and Planes14 by a
single card ratio, and it wins. The extrapolation assumed Tetra scales like
Planes14 from the L40S to the A100; Planes14 slows 1.71x, Tetra 1.42x (from the
tile-128 medians, 4.355 on the L40S in F1d to 6.183 here, two processes,
computed and not a published ratio).

WHAT THIS ESTABLISHES
---------------------
On a second architecture, the kernel claim shrinks: Tetra reads 2.150
b/weight and beats our FP16 kernel 1.11x but not cuBLAS (1.14x), against 3.20x on the
L40S at the served tile (216 matrices; the 252-matrix L40S rerun is pending).
It does not beat AWQ on either card. The end-to-end claim does not transfer:
the served files beat our own dense path only through the 4-bit tables, and
stay under vLLM's FP16. Memory and quality do not depend on the card.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH
----------------------------
No tuned tile for sm_80: 128 is the fallback, and it happened to be the best
of three here.
No counters: the L1 reading stays a reading.
Nothing on a GDDR consumer card of another architecture.
