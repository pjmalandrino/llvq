WHY IT LOSES, MEASURED
-----------------------
The Euclidean rule moves every disagreed block DOWN a level — 204 of 204 on the
real blocks of 2026-09-14, 4,618 of 4,618 on the synthetic ones. That is not a
detail of the rule, it is the rule: <x,u> = ||x|| cos(theta) <= ||x||, so its
target is always below the norm.

So it shrinks, systematically. On the 2,016 real compensated blocks, mean
amplitude placed over block norm (*measured*, same dumps):

  served rule       0.99336   almost unbiased against the block norm
  euclidean rule    0.97073   a systematic 2.9 % shrink

  blocks placed under the euclidean target: 38.64 % served, 48.76 % euclidean

That is the mechanism. The Euclidean rule buys 1.13 % of squared error by
trading an unbiased error for a smaller but BIASED one. Squared error does not
see the difference; 196 matrices and 28 layers do. A centred error partly
cancels down the stack, a one-sided 2.9 % shrink of every reconstructed block
accumulates.

WHAT THIS CORRECTS IN THE PLAN
-------------------------------
The plan's §9 argued this lead was unlike the repository's three precedents,
because rule C "changes which of two already-admissible codes is written, and
leaves the compensation loop, the row scale, the direction and the format
untouched". That argument is wrong, and this is where.

Changing which code is written changes the DISTRIBUTION of the amplitude
written, and this rule changes it in one direction on every block of the model.
That is the same class of defect as `group_scales`, which rescales blocks after
their codes are chosen. §9 checked that the procedure around the decision was
untouched and did not check what the decision does in aggregate.

The plan's §12 said a win at stage 2 would establish nothing about perplexity.
It was right, and the converse is now measured: a win on the local objective of
1.13 % produced a loss of 4.026 % at the endpoint.

LIMITS
-------
One model, 0.6B. One calibration seed, the published prefix. One evaluation set,
12 windows of wikitext-2 test. The cross-seed range of this protocol is large —
the M1 journal read 38.4507, 39.6042 and 43.0721 at rho = 1 — so the ABSOLUTE
perplexity of either arm is not a stable quantity. The COMPARISON is paired: the
two arms share the seed, the calibration, the rotation, the windows and the
process generation, and differ by one word.

What is not measured: whether the 4 % holds at the 4B, and whether a rule that
selects the Euclidean optimum WITHOUT the one-sided bias — there is no such rule
over two fixed levels — would behave differently. The bias is not an
implementation choice here, it is what the Euclidean optimum over these two
levels IS.

No MMLU arm was run and none is licensed: the sampling error is 1.339 pp and
nothing here is of that size.
