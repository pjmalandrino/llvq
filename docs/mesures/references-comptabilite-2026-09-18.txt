WHY THIS WAS RUN
-----------------
Point 2 of the diagnostic asks for two fixed references and, for each, the bits
of the codes, the side data, the b/weight of projections, the b/param of the
whole model and the resident memory on the native path. Four of those five had
never been read off the served object. The fifth needs a card.

1. THE SERVED OBJECT'S RECORDED SHA256 IS THE PREREG'S
-------------------------------------------------------
`configs/README.md` carried `c084a47c...27d2` as the sha256 of
`qwen3-4b-tetra-q5.bin`. That digest is the sha256 of
`proofs/preregistration-tetra-q5-encodage-2026-09-09.md`, verified by `shasum`
on the prereg file itself.

The chain is traceable. The encoding journal of 2026-09-09 writes the digest on
its `Prereg` line, at lines 6 to 8, and gives the object at lines 11 and 12
with a byte count and NO digest. The f1e census prereg of 2026-09-11 then put
that digest under the heading "the object", citing the journal. `configs/README`
inherited it.

The object's own digest, measured today:

  qwen3-4b-tetra-q5.bin   1,794,564,765 B
  sha256 ae31087a4b72494d52394f2cf2070da37a845c0a7d4aded65d5bdb0bd18b2263

Two independent downloads of the bucket copy give that digest, so it is not a
transfer artifact. The byte count matches the record exactly, which is why the
mismatch survived: every check that compared sizes passed.

The f1e census prereg is timestamped and is not edited. The deviation is
written beside it, in its -ECARTS file.

2. THE TWO REFERENCES
----------------------
Reference 1, for diagnosing the codebook and the encoder: bare `Tetra`, no
mixed precision, no post-hoc correction.

  sealed   qwen3-4b-tetra.bin        1,770,529,149 B  sha256 0adb7cfd02ed7402
  artifact ~/llvq-4b-tetra.llvq        980,791,242 B  sha256 eadc9ef3c3f6478c

Both carry the same projections: 252 lattice records, 1.9907 b/weight of
stream, 16,957,440 tail weights, 1,105,920 row scales, identical to the fourth
decimal in both containers.

Reference 2, for measuring an improvement useful to deployment: the served Q5
object, 216 `Tetra` records and 36 `v_proj` int4 g128.

  sealed   qwen3-4b-tetra-q5.bin     1,794,564,765 B  sha256 ae31087a4b72494d

No `.llvq` of it exists on this Mac and none is in the bucket. The sealed file
is the only copy, and the bucket is the only place it lives.

Whole model is 4,022,468,096 parameters, of which 388,956,160 of embedding and
196,096 of norms, read in the file.

The instrument is validated by reference 1: 2.7645 b/param reproduces the
published figure exactly, and 2.1498 kernel b/weight reproduces the `ETAT`
table.

Two figures in the record move.

  * `configs/README` gives the served object 2.8138 b/param, *computed*. Read
    off the file it is **2.8126**, 0.0012 lower, 0.04 %.
  * The o_proj journal of 2026-09-17 gives the served base 2.2044 kernel
    b/weight, *computed* from a uniform 2.1498 over every type. Read off the
    file it is **2.2030**, 0.0014 lower.

Neither gap moves a verdict. Both are recorded because a *computed* figure and
a *measured* one should not share a name.

And the figure the project ought to quote for the served object is **2.7475
b/param**, not 2.8126. The card has held the tail in f16 since 2026-08-09
(`TAIL_BYTES = 2`, `llvq-llm/src/fused.rs`), so the f32 column prices a width
nothing serves. Row 7 of `ROADMAP-QUALITY` already says the margin is 0.0747
kernel b/weight larger than published. This measures it on the served object.

4. THE HYPOTHETICAL ARMS, RECOMPUTED
-------------------------------------
Section 3 of `etat-reconcilie-2026-09-17.md` prices the int4 allocation arms
with the uniform 2.1498. Those figures are *computed* and they stand, with the
measured base 0.0014 below the computed one. No arm sits within 0.0014 of
b_max: the closest is v + o + down at 2.9408 against 3.00.

5. WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH
--------------------------------
- Resident memory on the native path. That needs a card. The record cites 0.97
  GB of projections and 1.39 GB served at `ROT_SHARE=1`, and this run measures
  neither.
- Anything about quality. No token was evaluated.
- The int4 side-data rate of 4.2500 is the tool's constant for g128, not a
  count of the file's int4 bytes.
