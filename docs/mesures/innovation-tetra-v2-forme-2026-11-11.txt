1. THE GAP, RECOMPUTED ON THE REAL CODEBOOK
-------------------------------------------
The encoder of v2shape.py is exact for a given scale. Per section and parity it runs a dynamic programme over
(rank-cost level, class, lexicographic flag against the pinned boundary row), then takes the best of 4,096 Golay
words x 4 class combinations. It rebuilds the pinned bounds itself: class 0 level 10 at [1,1,0,3,0,0,1,1], class 1
level 11 at [0,0,2,2,2,0,1,1], mixed level 8 at [3,0,0,0,1,0,1,0], the three rows of CLASS_BOUNDS and MIXED_BOUND.
Checks (*measured*, v2check.py, 300 blocks at three radii): every output is a Tetra member by k1ref's `in_tetra`;
where the unconstrained Leech nearest point is a member, both agree to the last bit; no member move among the
196,560 minimal vectors improves any of 120 outputs.
The gain is the served one: 1 bit, two Lloyd centroids fitted on 4,000 training blocks, the centroid nearest the
block norm. Evaluation on 16,000 other blocks. No row scale (it moves an i.i.d. Gaussian row by about 1.4 %).

The 0.222 b/dim is right. Tonight's exact encoder and the Rust encoder agree to 0.05 %.
The brief's 0.8 dB target is 0.0710, 1.91 bits of SQNR, close to the ECVQ point.

Where the 1.34 dB goes, as a chain (*computed* from the table):
  0.505 dB  Λ24's space filling, G = 0.06577 against 1/(2πe). No lattice up to 48 dimensions is better.
  0.234 dB  fixed rate in 24 dimensions, ECVQ to the 24-dim sphere-covering bound. A variable-length code recovers it;
            the round-1 structure note closed variable rate in VRAM (decode-bound rows, 0.025 b of lossless slack).
  0.224 dB  the lattice ball against an ideal spherical code of 2^47 directions.
  0.375 dB  Tetra against the ball: the price of the trellis-readable index, about 0.30 dB of it in the per-section
            row caps (candidate i below).
The four sum to 1.338 dB.

Inside Tetra's 0.375 dB, split by shape and gain (*measured*, tetra.gauss.json):
  shape alone (ideal per-block gain): 0.07652 against the bound's 0.06713, 0.57 dB. Tetra's 2^47 directions cover
    the sphere like a perfect code of 44.8 bits (sin^2 scales as 2^(-2b/23)).
  gain: the norm rule adds 0.00854/dim, the projection rule 0.00692, the bound's ideal 1-bit gain 0.00697. The norm rule
    therefore costs 0.083 dB; with the projection rule the gain is already at its 1-bit optimum.
The three suspects named in the brief:
  - The cap on shells and ranks: no. Ranks above 4 cost more than any section budget (t(5) = 15 > 11), so allowing
    them changes nothing. The cost order Σ(2ρ+1)² is the residue-averaged squared magnitude to within +0.5 for every
    rank, so the table is already sorted by expected energy.
  - The 55.5 % of minimal moves that leave the code: a consequence, not a cause. Any 2^47-point subset of Λ24 used
    around shell 10 drops most neighbours; the ball does too. What matters is how the directions cover the sphere,
    and that is the 0.57 dB above.
  - The fixed 48-bit budget: 0.234 dB of the 0.84, and only a variable-length code gets it.
  - A fourth suspect, not in the brief, carries the measurable part: each section has its own row cap. A Gaussian
    block's energy splits unevenly over three octads, and three 8-dim caps waste directions where one section holds
    most of it. Section 2 below measures it at about 0.30 dB.

Reading the table.
- On the source that applies, no table-only member (c to h) gains 0.01 dB. Tetra's table is a local optimum of its
  own family: one step flatter, steeper, wider or narrower in gain loses or ties.
- The heavy-tailed source moves the optimum toward more gain bits and flatter costs, by 0.13 to 0.21 dB. A per-matrix
  choice would harvest that only on matrices whose block norms spread like Student-t blocks. A chi_24 norm has a
  coefficient of variation of 0.145, the Student-t5 blocks 0.231 (*measured*, v2rule.py). The decisive experiment
  below measures the real one before anything is built.
- h is inside its own noise for any model use (0.009 dB).
- i is the one real shape gain inside the Tetra family. Replace the three per-section row caps by one cap on the sum
  of the three section levels, with the same Golay path and parity thread. At 46.35 bits it reads -0.208 dB, at 47.04
  bits -0.303 dB, against Tetra on the same 4,000 blocks (*measured*, joint.gauss4k.json, tetra.gauss4k.json); at 47
  bits, -0.30 dB. Its shape-only error falls from 0.0768 to 0.0716. So the per-section caps cost about 0.30 of Tetra's
  0.375 dB against the ball. The loss is the product structure, three 8-dim balls where the source wants one 24-dim one.
- k is the decoder-light half of i, and the best candidate that passes the instruction bar. Two selector bits pick
  the index widths of the three sections, (13, 9, 9), (9, 13, 9), (9, 9, 13) or (10, 11, 10), so 2 + 31 + 14 = 47 bits
  as today. Each profile alone loses 0.46 to 1.00 dB; their union reads -0.183 dB, paired SE 0.013, with every profile
  used by 19 to 33 % of blocks (*measured*, v2prof.py). The rank rows stay sorted, so every width reads a prefix of one
  table, but 2^13 rows per class make it 64 KiB. Round 1 measured that L1 room in the activation tile is worth up to
  17.6 % of Tetra row speed (banc-252), so the table size, not the instruction count, is this candidate's speed risk.
  Four profiles were tried, chosen by hand. A tuned set would land between -0.18 and the joint member's -0.30.
- Pair coding in 96 bits has the only ceiling above 0.8 dB. A realizable 48-dim spherical code built on Λ24 x Λ24
  is not trellis-readable in 64 states, and its decoder is a new design, not a Tetra member.

Decoder cost. Candidates a to f change table contents or field widths: the 237-instruction word decode is unchanged
(*estimated*, the word layout and llvq_tetra48.cuh's lookup path). g and h make the decoded point non-integer, so
`invnorm[m]` (llvq_tetra48.cuh:130, indexed by the integer shell) must become an rsqrt of a computed sum: +2 to 4
instructions, 1 to 2 % (*estimated*). i must unrank a joint index: two searches over about 46 cumulative counts and
two 34-bit divisions, +60 to 100 instructions, +25 to 40 % (*estimated*). It fails the "few percent" bar on cost alone.
k reads the selector, fetches three widths and two table offsets from one packed constant, and masks with variable shifts: +8 to 12
instructions, +3 to 5 % (*estimated*), plus the 64 KiB table.
Encoder cost: a to h keep the encoder's shape (sections, trellis, both parities); the DP here is exact and costs about
3 ms a block in numpy against the Rust encoder's 300 us. i couples the sections and multiplies the trellis step by the
number of level splits, about x20 (*computed* from T = 22). k reuses one section DP per parity and only changes the
allowed levels per profile: about x2 to x4 on the encoder (*estimated*).
Format: every candidate except b changes the word meaning, so it needs a v2 kind with its own fingerprint beside
`Tetra48`. v1 and Tetra48 files stay readable: the reader dispatches on `kind_tag` (format-noyau §5 bis). b needs no
format change at all.

3. FROM DISTORTION TO QUALITY
-----------------------------
The law: acc = 54.60 + 14.60 ln SNR over 28 arms, in-sample rms residual 1.31 pp, per-arm SE about 1.41 pp (*measured*,
logit-snr-4b-2026-09-12). SNR there is a logit amplitude ratio. If logit noise power is proportional to weight
distortion, a cut of d dB is worth 7.30 x 0.2303 d = 1.68 MMLU per dB. Round 1's refuter used 14.60 per natural-log
unit of power, which is 3.36 per dB; that reading doubles the slope and is kept here as the generous end.

GSM8K has no law in the repository. The column scales MMLU by the ratio of the two FP16 deficits, 9.6 / 6.73 = 1.43
(*estimated*, 82.5 against 92.1 and 63.37 against 70.1). The law is too weak to promise the bar for any candidate, and
too weak to promise a sign below about 0.4 dB.

The counter-evidence says the Gaussian dB is a weak predictor for codebook changes.
- leech0c13 (the paper's 0-gain-bit ball, shell <= 13) did not test a distortion gain. On Gaussian blocks it sits at
  0.085 (paper Table 8, *reported* in llvq-bench/src/main.rs), Tetra at 0.0851: the same MSE. It tested shape at
  equal rate and equal Gaussian distortion, under Euclidean GPTQ and before row scales. It moved perplexity +21.5 % and
  MMLU +1.19, CI95 [-1.54, +3.98] (*measured*, leech0c13-2026-09-07). Equal Gaussian MSE, different model loss: what
  the model feels is not the Gaussian MSE.
- Planes14, the ball with 1 gain bit, is 0.38 dB better than Tetra on Gaussian blocks. On the 4B it read 4.64 %
  WORSE perplexity than Tetra (*measured*, tetra-4b-2026-09-06). Its MMLU moved +2.10 in the ball's favour (55.59
  against 53.49), above the law's +0.6 for 0.38 dB but about one combined SE (2.0 pp) from zero.
  Its logit SNR was 1.071 against 1.029 (logit-snr), a noise power ratio of 0.35 dB. The weight-domain dB showed up in
  logit noise, and perplexity still went the other way.
- The Euclidean gain rule, candidate b, was run end to end at 0.6B: +4 % perplexity (*measured*,
  tetrapost-ppl-0.6b-2026-09-15), although it lowers both Gaussian and real-residue block error. The GPTQ trajectory
  and the Hessian metric reverse a 0.08 dB block gain.

4. THREE OBJECTIONS
-------------------
1. "Your encoder is not Tetra's encoder; Tetra may be better than you say, or the candidates worse."
   The exact DP and the Rust encoder read 0.08506 and 0.0851 on Gaussian blocks, 0.05 % apart. Every candidate runs
   through the same DP, so the comparison is paired and exact per scale. The 7-scale search loses 3 ppm against an
   11-scale search on 1,000 blocks (0.084097 against 0.084099).
2. "Gaussian MSE is the wrong metric; a codebook could win in the Hessian metric."
   Possible, and the repository's three codebook arms say the Gaussian number does not even fix the sign below
   0.4 dB (leech0c13 0 dB and +21.5 % ppl; ball +0.38 dB and +4.64 % ppl; gain rule +0.08 dB and +4 % ppl). An H-metric
   win needs a codebook shaped to H, which varies by matrix; after the Hadamard rotation two block errors of the same
   norm still cost up to 2.84x one another, p5 to p95 (*measured*, tetraalt header).
   That is a per-matrix encoder target, round 1's YAQA lead, not a codebook member.
3. "QTIP gets 0.069 at a fixed 2 bits, so fixed rate is not the wall; you looked at the wrong decoders."
   QTIP sits 0.91 dB under Tetra and needs a trellis decoder (a 2^16-state bitshift trellis with computed codes),
   another decoder class. Even its full 0.91 dB maps to +1.5 to +3.1 MMLU under the law, under the bar at both ends,
   and on the three codebook changes measured here the Gaussian dB did not fix perplexity's sign.

5. THE CHEAPEST DECISIVE EXPERIMENT
-----------------------------------
Nothing here justifies building a v2 kind. If the operator wants the question closed on real weights, two stages.
Stage 1, $0, Mac, about 1 h of CPU for 20,000 blocks through Tetra and k (i adds 1 h 30 on 4,000), needs a go (local
  compute, one job at a time). Read Qwen3-0.6B's f16 safetensors (a file read, no forward), apply the served rotation
  per matrix, take 20,000 blocks spread over the seven projection kinds and 28 layers, and run v2shape.py and v2prof.py
  on them. Report the paired dB of k, i, c and e, and the block-norm coefficient of variation against chi_24's 0.145.
  Gate: k at -0.3 dB or better on real blocks, or a norm CV over 0.20 (the Student-t5 blocks read 0.231), opens
  stage 2. The Gaussian result (-0.18 dB) predicts the gate fails, and then the lead closes.
Stage 2, $0 card, Mac only, about 3 h of encodes, needs a go and 3 to 5 days of Rust (the profile selector in the
  encoder's trellis, a 64 KiB rank table, a kind_tag): encode the 0.6B in Tetra and in k under three calibration draws
  each (6 encodes at about 30 min, gain-ab-gate-0.6b timing), score KL to FP16 on held-out windows, paired by draw.
  Gate: the KL ratio's interval below 1 on all three draws. Unpaired, one arm's draws spread 13.9 % in perplexity at
  0.6B (gain-ab-gate-0.6b), so the pairing by draw is what makes a 0.2 dB member readable at all.
Neither stage can reach the bar. Stage 2 answers only whether the shape gain carries its sign to the model.

6. WHAT THIS NOTE DID NOT CHECK
-------------------------------
- Real weight blocks, GPTQ residues and row scales: every measured row here is synthetic.
- The joint member's exact boundary: it is bracketed by two whole budget levels, 46.35 and 47.04 bits, and
  interpolated to 47. Its 4,000-block paired SE (~0.02 dB) is estimated from the 20,000-block rows, not computed.
- The four profiles of k are hand-picked, not optimized.
- Instruction counts of the candidates are estimated from the decode path; no SASS was compiled.
- The cap bound assumes ideal spherical caps; it bounds, it is not a code.
