=======================================================================================================================

1. HOW THE SASS WAS MADE
------------------------
The module texts are pers.rs's at the card's tile define ("#define TILE_BLOCKS 64u\n"), written by the ignored test
pers::tests::dump_tree_modules to target/tree-modules/. Their sha256 equal the card's loader lines of D1:
4E tree ce1c354d (306,060 B), a12ip 63155df9 (422,018 B), a12ipg 246e99b1 (450,134 B), 4E v9 1b422b62 (252,757 B).
pers::tests::the_d1_tree_modules_keep_their_bytes pins the first three.
Each text compiled with nvcc 12.4 -arch=sm_89 -cubin -O3 -Xptxas -v in nvidia/cuda:12.4.1-devel-ubuntu22.04
(ops/tetra-sass-probe.sh's image), then cuobjdump -sass -fun <symbol>, loops counted by ops/sass_loops.py. The card's
driver ptxas compiles NVRTC's PTX: these counts are indicative (pers-sass-2026-11-05: 243 against 237 on a block).

2. PART 1: THE ATTENTION TILE (*measured* on the Mac SASS)
-----------------------------------------------------------
a12ipg (pers_4et_a12ipg_f1_t384), the tile loop at 0x30470-0x33210, one pers_attn_tile call a pass of the loop:
  731 instructions: FFMA 182, HADD2.F32 128, BRA 64, FADD 59, IMAD 51, BSSY 34, BSYNC 34, SHFL.BFLY 32, FSETP 19,
  LDS.128 18, FMUL 17, ISETP 16, FRND 9, F2I 9, FMNMX 8, CALL 3 (the __syncwarp fallback, skipped when converged).
- Widening: HADD2.F32 R, -RZ, R.H0_H0 or .H1_H1, 128 a call a lane (8 K and 8 V vectors of 8). No F2F.F32.F16
  anywhere in the kernel (4 F2F.F16.F32 narrowings, 20 F2F.F64.F32 outside the tile). The F2F hypothesis of the study
  (about 7 ms a pass at 16 a clock an SM) does not hold.
- FFMA 182 = 64 for QK, 64 for PV, 54 for the 9 software ex2. FADD 59 holds the 32 shuffle reductions.
- The cost the SASS does show: every position j of the tile sits in its own BSSY/BSYNC region behind `if (j < nv)`.
  Position j's chain (LDS.128, 8 dependent FFMA, 4 dependent SHFL.BFLY and FADD) ends before j + 1's loads start, and
  the 8 ex2 and the 8 PV steps are guarded the same way. A call is a latency chain of 8 serial positions.
  Latency read (*estimated*): about 180 cycles a position for QK, about 2,200 cycles a call, 0.9 us at 2.52 GHz,
  against the study's V of 1.22 us a row-step (*computed* there). The issue count (731 a call) would give the
  study's 3.8 ms; the latency chain is the order of the 10.1 ms.
Group sharing of the widening does not fit:
- Widened tiles in shared memory double a stage, 16 KB to 32 KB. Two stages add 32 KB to 93,568 B: 126,336 B against
  101,376 (*computed*).
- Widened tiles in registers are 64 floats of K a lane for one tile, on a kernel at 168 of its 170 registers, and the
  group's rows are on different warps (task = row x tpr + tt).
- The ceiling of the gain is small anyway: 128 of 731 issued instructions, a quarter of them kept, at most about
  1.4 ms of the 10.1 ms at 8k if the tile were issue bound (*estimated*), less since it is latency bound.
Per the brief, no widening arm. The lead the SASS points at instead, not built and not costed on a card: a full-tile
path (nv = 8, the only case on a shared split) with no guard between positions, so ptxas can interleave the 8 chains.
Each score, each ex2 and each PV sum keeps its operations and their order, so the bits stay. It is not a
group-sharing change; it would apply to a12ip, a12ipg and 4E alike.
4E v9 (pers_4e_tokens_f1_t512, module 1b422b62): the same tile, loop 0x16c80-0x19a80, 737 instructions, HADD2 128,
FFMA 182, BSSY 34, SHFL 32. The same widening and the same serialisation, once a query head (4 times a K value at 4
query heads a KV head). What the widening weighs at 8,192 (*estimated*): 4E's attention grows 1.98 ms a token from 5
to 8,192 positions (236.6 and 161 tok/s, measurement form). 589,824 tile calls a token at 8k (36 layers, 8 KV heads,
1,024 tiles, 2 head pairs) at 737 instructions is 0.30 ms at full issue on 142 SMs: the attention is latency bound
there too. The 128 widenings are 17 % of that issue, about 0.05 ms a token, under 1 % of the 6.2 ms token. 4E is not
changed here.

5. WHAT MAKES THE STUDY'S ESTIMATE WRONG
----------------------------------------
- No spill at RC = 4: the study's main risk does not show on the Mac ptxas.
- The 8k attention is latency bound by the serialised tile (section 2). A row past 16 adds the same per-row tile
  chain; the study's attention x[0.95, 1.00] at R = 32 assumes the chains of more rows overlap no worse. With 8 tasks
  a row over 12 warps, 32 rows give 256 tasks a step against 128 at 16: more warps busy, possibly better latency hiding,
  so attention could come in under the estimate, not over.
- The non-attention estimate (11.2 ms [10.7, 12.0]) rests on a10t384's RC = 4 penalty, measured at 168 registers and
  736 B: a12ipg32 compiles to the same, so that term stands.
