Research phase on decode speed (CERVEAU O11), steps R1 and R2: the framing and the route.
Main session, 2026-11-12. No measurement was run. Every figure is copied from the two R0 notes of the same day,
recherche-debit-r0-depot (the repository) and recherche-debit-r0-litterature (published systems), which cite their
journals and sources line by line. Labels are theirs.

R1. THE PROBLEM

A token of the sealed 4B reads 1,378.2 MB of packed weights (computed): 750.0 MB of Tetra words, 409.4 MB of int4
records, 218.8 MB of int4 head. The served kernel, 4E v9, takes 4.227 ms for it at a 5-token context on the L40S
(measured), 297 GB/s over the whole token (computed).

The number: the stream rate of the Tetra rows, in GB/s of packed words, on the L40S.
  Tetra rows, loop alone, served decoder      379 [368-383]   measured, pers-rowloop; all 252 matrices as Tetra,
                                                              the bare encode 0adb7cfd, not the sealed file
  Tetra rows inside 4E v9, gate/up            359 to 385      computed from stamps, mean CTA work; gate/up is all Tetra
  int4 rows inside 4E v9, o                   589             computed from stamps; o is all int4
  mixed rows inside 4E v9                     414 (q/k/v), 648 (down)   computed; Tetra and int4 together
  int4 rows alone, best arm                   548 (q4t); a bare read of the same bytes 671   measured, step1-micro

The reference, in place of a bound: the int4 rows of the same kernel, on the same card, in the same token. The card's
own read bandwidth has never been measured here; 864 GB/s is a datasheet figure, and no published measurement of it
was found. Published systems agree with the split: 4-bit kernels reach 67 to 70 % of datasheet bandwidth on this
memory system (llama.cpp on an L40 and an RTX 6000 Ada, computed from a forum scoreboard), 2-bit kernels 36 to 40 %
(QTIP, QuIP#, llama.cpp IQ2, computed). No published 2 to 3 bit kernel was found above about half.

The gap: the all-Tetra rows (gate/up, 359 to 385) stream at 0.61 to 0.65 of the rate the all-int4 rows (o, 589)
reach beside them, and at 0.54 to 0.57 of the best bare read (671). They are 54 % of the
token's bytes. The repository attributes 0.66 to 0.92 ms of their 1.81 ms to instruction time (computed,
sass-tetra-lean): the rows are bound by the decode, 243 SASS instructions a block of 24 weights, not by the memory.

The closed problem: compute y = W x for a matrix W stored as Tetra words, at the rate the card streams int4, with the
sealed words as the only input, no more VRAM, and y inside the served tolerance.

What the problem is worth at most, and what it leaves. An earlier note computed the door with the Tetra rows at
671 GB/s and everything else as served: 260 tok/s at 4B, 174 at 8B, 105 at 14B (computed, recherche-debit-maths,
cited by the R0 note), against 224, 145 and 87 served. That is the ceiling of this problem, not a forecast: about
x1.16 at 4B. The larger share of the token is elsewhere. The whole token streams 297 GB/s, 0.44 of the best bare
read; at that read rate 1,378 MB would take 2.05 ms (computed), 487 tok/s, if the memory never waited. That second
problem, the whole token against its bytes, has no verifier today: a candidate is a whole kernel, and the
instruments that would say where the memory waits do not exist (no bandwidth probe, no hardware counter, stamps
that cost 210 to 290 us).

Why this one first. The anomaly N5 as CERVEAU stated it (rows a third slower inside 4E
than alone) dates from the v2 kernel; at v9 the gap is 0 to 6 % on mean work. The loop alone is therefore a fair
stand-in for the rows in the token, which is what makes a search possible. The time outside the rows (1.44 ms of
stamped phases, the stamped-against-timed gap of 210 to 290 us, the attention at short context) stays open as
anomalies; it has no verifier and no instrument, and is not this phase's problem.

R2. THE ROUTE AND THE VERIFIER

Route: search. The verifier has two stages.
  Stage 1, Mac, $0, seconds: a candidate computes the same y as the reference decoder on recorded words covering
  every class, exactly or inside a stated error bound; a mutant must be caught.
  Stage 2, card, costly: the loop alone, on the sealed 4B's own Tetra records, one shape at a time, medians over
  rounds with their ranges, in GB/s. One job scores every candidate that passed stage 1, with the served decoder and
  the int4 arms as controls in the same job.

What stage 2 must show before any candidate counts (the R2 condition of MODES.md):
  it reproduces the served decoder at 379 [368-383] and the journals' order of the known arms;
  it reports the three shapes apart, which no journal has (d_in 2,560 is the slowest a step);
  it measures the card's read rate with a bare probe, so the phase has a bound of its own;
  it times the Tetra rows of the sealed file with no other traffic, which no journal has.
The last two are also the first instruments of the second problem, bought by the same job.

What the verifier scores is the rows' rate, not tok/s at the door. The stand-in is held faithful by the v9 stamps
above. A candidate that wins stage 2 is an idea only after a token-level measurement, which belongs to R6.

R3. THE SPACE (to be fixed when the harness exists)

Programs that produce y from the sealed words and x. It is wider than every space with a recorded ceiling, because
those were searched at the bit and inside the decode-then-dot form:
  decoders with fewer instructions (the relabeled decoder, pword, lane rotation, an int16 dot: never run on the card);
  schedules the bit forbade (several warps a row, reassociated sums);
  layouts built at load from the sealed words, at the same VRAM;
  the dot product in the code domain: partial sums of x against the patterns of a section, gathered by the word.

Cost so far: $0. Spend under the cap: $19.22 of $25. Phase budget: $5 of card, to 2026-11-15.
