ALTERNATIVE DIRECTIONS: A LOCAL GAIN THAT DOES NOT SURVIVE CHAINING (2026-09-16)
=================================================================================
Code: llvq-llm/src/bin/tetraalt.rs. Cost: $0, 40 s of Mac total over two runs.
Data: the Schur pilot's 2,016 compensated blocks and their Hessians.
Raw log in the -brut directory.

THE QUESTION
-------------
Tetra picks its direction by Euclidean distance — a Viterbi through the
three-section trellis, over both parities, already a joint choice over the 24
dimensions. That choice ignores H. Measured on the 4B's rotated Hessians, two
errors of equal norm cost between 0.60 and 1.69 times the isotropic value,
p5 to p95, a spread of 2.84x. So a slightly worse direction landing in a quiet
subspace could beat the nearest one.

Note on the rotation: an orthogonal rotation preserves the spectrum of the full
Hessian. It redistributes sensitivity across coordinates; it does not remove it.
An earlier draft of this programme claimed the rotation made the criterion
Euclidean-correct, and that was wrong.

WHAT IS SCORED
---------------
The conditional cost after the continuous suffix is re-optimized,
`||e U_BB^-1||^2`, not a raw block of H: GPTQ compensates a block's error onto
the columns that follow, so the raw block is not what the loop pays.

Candidates are the encoder's own answer at 15 scales, deduplicated — 3.60
distinct points a block. These are reachable through the production rule, not
the K best paths of the trellis. The served choice is always among them, so the
selection score cannot get worse.

RESULT 1: AT A FIXED STATE, THE GAIN IS REAL
----------------------------------------------
  K    blocks changed    conditional gain    on reserved activations
  2        13.00 %            1.139 %              0.474 %
  4        28.52 %            2.223 %              1.765 %
  6        29.96 %            2.393 %              1.918 %
  8        29.96 %            2.393 %              1.918 %

Thirty percent of blocks prefer another direction, for 2.393 % of conditional
cost. The curve stops at K = 6 because the generator runs out of distinct
candidates at 3.60, not because the seam is exhausted.

⚠️ The two columns do NOT treat the suffix alike — the first re-optimizes it
continuously, the second leaves it compensated — so their ratio is a comparison
of two different gains and NOT a transfer rate. An earlier reading of this table
called it "80 % transfer"; that was wrong.

RESULT 2: CHAINED, IT COLLAPSES
---------------------------------
The table above is measured at states the SERVED policy reached. Letting the
selector drive — choose, commit what was chosen, advance from there — changes
every later state. The same 48 rows, walked twice:

  cell                  rows   rollout gain   reserved gain
  mlp.gate_proj    L0      8       +3.796 %       -1.005 %
  mlp.gate_proj    L13     8       +1.329 %       -0.749 %
  mlp.gate_proj    L27     8       +2.876 %       +5.372 %
  self_attn.q_proj L0      8       +8.573 %       +5.552 %
  self_attn.q_proj L13     8       -0.086 %       -7.008 %
  self_attn.q_proj L27     8       +0.752 %       -9.881 %
  all                     48       +0.911 %       -8.763 %

On the objective the loop itself minimizes, the gain falls from 2.393 % per
block to **0.911 % chained** — two thirds evaporate. On reserved activations it
turns **negative, -8.763 %**.

Choosing better at every step makes the whole worse. The compensation absorbs
whatever it is given; handing it a locally better error changes what the later
columns must catch up, and the balance is unfavourable.

LIMITS
-------
48 rows, eight per cell, and the per-cell spread runs from +5.55 % to -9.88 %.
The global sign is clear; the magnitude is not. Two cells gain substantially.

The generator is poor — 3.60 candidates a block. A real K-best would explore
more, but nothing here suggests it would flip the sign: the failure is not that
the candidate is bad, it is that choosing better locally hurts what follows.

Only two projection families, one model, one seed. And this measures a proxy:
nothing here is perplexity and nothing is MMLU.

WHAT IT SETTLES, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
--------------------------------------
Row 12 of ROADMAP-QUALITY, the K-best beam, was rated "indeterminate, possibly
negative" at 2 to 3 weeks of work and ~10 h of Mac at K = 4.

What is measured: a scale-sweep selector, on this pilot, gains 2.4 % locally and
loses 8.8 % chained. Funding a better generator to feed THAT selector has no
case today.

What is NOT measured, and an earlier draft of this file overstated: K-best is
not refuted. A beam ranging over (path, gain level) explores a different
candidate set, and the failure here is of one selector on 48 rows of one pilot,
not of the idea. The row moves to PAUSED, not closed — reopening it needs a
reason to think a richer beam behaves differently when chained, which this file
does not supply and does not exclude.
