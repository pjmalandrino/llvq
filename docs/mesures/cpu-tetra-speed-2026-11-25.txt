THE CPU MATVECS ON RANDOM CODES AT THE 4B'S SHAPES: TETRA 1.56x AND INT4 G128 1.38x ON ONE NEON CORE, SAME SUMS (2026-11-25)
=============================================================================================================================
Why:    the CPU backend (docs/mesures/cpu-tetra-2026-11-07.txt) served the 4B at 8.65 tok/s on 12 threads. Reading
        where a token's time goes came first; this journal holds the microbenchmark that answered it and the two
        kernel changes it pointed to.
Cost:   $0. Apple M3 Max (12 P + 4 E cores), macOS, rustc 1.95.0, profile `ci` (release optimisation, no LTO),
        under ~/.llvq-guard/heavy.sh (nice 15), the machine shared with other sessions (load 3.5 to 6.6).
Object: no model. Random 47-bit Tetra labels and gain bits through the served transcode (tetra48), random int4
        records, at the served 4B's sites. No file was read.
Code:   tests/cpu_tetra.rs `bench_cpu_matvecs_at_the_4b_shapes` (ignored), branch cpu/compat-and-speed-2026-11-25:
          LLVQ_THREADS=<n> cargo test --profile ci -p llvq-llm --test cpu_tetra -- --ignored --nocapture bench_
Nature: every time *measured*: the median of 7 rounds of about 0.15 s, the range of the rounds in brackets; GB/s and
        the per-token sum *computed* from them. NEON only: AVX2 and AVX-512 are not measured anywhere.

WHERE A TOKEN'S TIME GOES (before, 8 threads)
---------------------------------------------
The served 4B per token: Tetra q, k, gate, up on 36 layers and down on 24; int4 g128 v, o on 36 and down on 12; the
tied int4 g64 head once. The sum of the rows below is 105.4 ms (computed), against 115 ms a token end to end at 12
threads in the 2026-11-07 journal: the matvecs are the token, and the Tetra decode is 83 % of them. The candle
adapter (rotation, trio order, tensor copies) costs 17.6 us a prepare and no time measurable on a matvec: the
slices arm and the tensor arm agree at one thread (3.18 against 3.17 ms) and swap places at eight, where rounds are
bimodal on a shared machine.

Tetra at one thread went from 5.06 to 7.86 Gweight/s on gate/up. At eight threads the machine was shared and the
rounds of one arm spread by up to 7 %; the head at 8 threads read 4.10 ms in an intermediate run of the same code,
so its 0.94 is noise around no change. An intermediate run of the Tetra change alone, at eight threads, read 0.495
ms on gate/up. Raw lines: the runs at 23:05 (before) and 23:36 (after) on 2026-11-24, reproduced here.

WHAT CHANGED
------------
  1. NEON: the two candidates of a table row load as one 128-bit vector, the code byte's mask as one 64-bit vector,
     and a bit select merges them; before, three scalar merges and three general-to-vector moves per block. Most of
     the gain.
  2. Fixed-size tables with masked indices and the row's words read two at a time from the three u32 they fill: no
     bounds check in the block loop. gscale[g] / |y| from a 64-entry table formed per matrix call.
  3. int4: one SIMD call per row, four accumulators inside a group, the scaled group sums kept in lanes.
The block order of every sum is unchanged, so a batch and a single row round alike; a two-accumulator variant broke
the 1e-5 batch test and was dropped.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SHOW
-----------------------
  - No token was generated: the end-to-end rate after the change is not measured.
  - AVX2 takes changes 2 and 3; it is type-checked for x86_64 Linux and runs in CI, never timed. Change 1 has no AVX2
    counterpart yet. No AVX-512 path.
  - One machine, shared, one run per arm.
