RULE C OF THE SHAKER, ON THE HOST, AND THE DOWN INPUT AS A COSTED LEVER, 2026-11-05
==================================================================================

Result: the down input's 800 us a token are about 32 serial round trips a layer to memory, each about 0.7 us. Each
round trip is a load that every one of the 142 CTAs makes at the same time, on the same lines. A bit-exact rewrite would
do the same work in one or two round trips:
- vector loads, all in flight at once;
- the mix matrix staged in shared memory during the wait before.
Applied to ln1 and ln2 too, which read the same way, the rewrite takes 4E v4 from 189.8 to about 243 tok/s at a
5-token prompt, range [192, 247], and from 140.1 to about 167 at 8,192, range [141, 169]. These are *estimated* from
SASS counts and the shaker's measured parts. The low end is the case where round trips are not the cost.

Rule C, the backward check of the rows:
- q/k/v rows hold for both forms.
- gate/up holds for v3 (0.854) and falls outside for v4 (0.800).
- down rows fall outside for both, in the other direction (1.25, 1.29).
The down miss is the int4 down rows: they run from L2, not from DRAM.

Prereg: none ($0, host analysis of a measured run).

2. THE THREE REDUNDANT PARTS ARE SERIAL ROUND TRIPS (v4, mean CTA work)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Every CTA computes ln1 with its rotation of 2,560, ln2 with its rotation of 2,560, and the down input (the rotation
  of 9,728 for the 24 Tetra downs, the widening for the 12 int4 ones) on the whole vector. In the SASS, every loop of
  these parts loads one element an iteration and uses it before the next load issues. The round trips a thread makes,
  in series, are these (*computed* from the code):
    exact_rms_scale at 2,560     5   (two candle virtual warps a warp: 3 + 2 columns)
    exact_rms_apply at 2,560     5   (x, alpha, signs)
    pers_mix_column at k = 5     5   (one row of `small` an iteration, 32 predicated generic loads)
    pers_rot_load at 9,728      19
    pers_mix_column at k = 19   19
    int4 widening at 9,728      19
  A ln part is 15 a layer, a Tetra down input 38, an int4 one 19.
  Measured time over round trips, an upper bound on each:
    ln1     406.6 us /   540 = 0.75 us (1,897 cycles at 2,520 MHz); this part also issues the q/k/v L2 prefetch
    ln2     261.6 us /   540 = 0.48 us (1,221 cycles)
    input   799.0 us / 1,140 = 0.70 us (1,766 cycles)
  - An unloaded L2 hit on Ada is about 270 cycles (*estimated*, public microbenchmarks, never measured on the L40S).
  - Each round trip here waits 4.5 to 7 times that. In each one, 142 CTAs x 16 warps load the same 1 KB (or the same
    128-byte row of `small`) at once.
  - The down input is the same on every CTA: slowest 815.4 against a mean of 799.0. That fits a shared resource, not
    one CTA's work.
  - What the round trips leave: the mix at k = 19 issues 105 instructions an iteration of g (KMAX = 32, predicated),
    19 iterations, 4 warps a scheduler, about 3.2 us a Tetra layer. That is 58 us a token above the same mix read from
    shared memory.

3. THE LEVER: EACH REDUNDANT PART IN ONE OR TWO ROUND TRIPS
----------------------------------------------------------
  The same operations on the same values, so bit for bit by construction:
  a. Down input:
     - act read as 16-byte vectors, at most 3 a thread at 512 threads, all issued before the first use;
     - the 304 sign words loaded with them;
     - the halves widened and signed into shared memory as today.
     - The int4 widening reads the same way.
  b. `small` (k x KMAX floats, 2.4 KB at k = 19) staged in shared memory before the wait that precedes it. That is
     the inner wait for the down input and the previous grid wait for ln1 and ln2: time a CTA spends idle today. The
     mix then reads it from shared memory, in the same fma chain a column (t ascending, the +0 add when k < KMAX).
  c. ln1 and ln2:
     - x (2,560 halves, 320 vectors), alpha and the signs read in one round trip into shared memory;
     - candle's RMS order replayed from there: the virtual thread t sums columns t, t + 1,024, t + 2,048 in order;
     - the apply from shared memory.
  Estimated after the change, at each part's present price a round trip: 2 round trips a part, plus about 1.0 us a
  layer for ln (WHT round, k = 5 mix, reductions, barriers), and 1.9 for a Tetra down input (two WHT rounds over 16
  warps, the k = 19 mix from shared memory, barriers).
    part     now      after    saved (us a token)
    ln1      406.6     90       316
    ln2      261.6     71       191
    input    799.0     91       708
    token (v4)                         5-token prompt              8,192
    now                                5,269 us, 189.8 tok/s       7,137 us, 140.1 tok/s
    point (less 60 us of int4 DRAM)    243 tok/s                   167 tok/s
    range                              [192, 247]                  [141, 169]
  - Low end: round trips are not the cost, and only the mix's issue count goes (58 us).
  - High end: every round trip saved at its present price (1,215 us).
  - The point takes off 60 us: the int4 down's DRAM fill, hidden today behind the down input, can surface in the
    int4 down rows, at most 5 us a layer.
  The 300 tok/s target is not reached by this lever alone: 243 at 5 tokens leaves about 4.1 ms a token.

  Rejected: one rotation shared across the grid.
  - It adds a grid barrier a layer (two for the WHT then the mix). The inner wait already costs 5.3 us a layer on the
    mean CTA and 9.7 on the slowest (measured).
  - Every CTA would still read the whole rotated vector, in f32, twice the bytes.
  - The lever above leaves about 3 us a Tetra layer, below one barrier's wait.
