WHAT THIS ANSWERS
------------------
Stage 0 ran on Gaussian blocks and named its own limit: the angular part is a
codebook property and should carry, the norm distribution is not and is what
sets the width of the disagreement band. Stage 0 bis settles both on the blocks
the quantizer actually sees.

It needed no new capture. The Schur pilot already walked the served policy down
48 rows of Qwen3-0.6B, shadowing every full block, and dumped for each of them
the two statistics the two rules read. Its `working` array is compensated by
`commit` after every block, its row scale is frozen before the loop, and its
centroids come from `fit_gain_centroids` on the rotated uncompensated weights,
which is what llvq-llm/src/calib.rs does in production. So the population is the
production one on all three counts.

THE SETUP
----------
2,016 shadow blocks: two calibration seeds, self_attn.q_proj and mlp.gate_proj,
layers 0, 13 and 27, four rows per cell, 42 full blocks per row. Qwen3-0.6B, one
gain bit, 47-bit label, 48-bit word. Rate, format and decoder are identical in
every arm below, as in stage 0.

Rule A, served: the centroid nearest ||x|| / row_scale (quantizer.rs:777).
Rule B, Euclidean optimum: the centroid nearest <x,u> / row_scale.
Rule C, Schur-conditioned: absent from stage 0, present here because the pilot
  held a real Hessian factor. It is reported for its distance to A and B only.

THE FOUR CONTROLS
------------------
1 passes. The algebraic cost model agrees with the costs the pilot's decoder
     wrote on all 4,032 candidate costs, worst gap 4.770e-17 against a largest
     cost of 2.167e-02.
2 passes. Both rules recompute from their own raw statistic with 0 mismatches
     on 2,016 blocks. Neither rule is read from the dump and believed.
3 passes. Two mutants fail their designated control and only theirs.
     --mutate cost scales the block norm by 1.0001: control 1 moves to 2.105e-05
     and trips. --mutate rule swaps what the two rules picked without touching
     either statistic: control 1 stays green, control 2 reads 204 and 204
     mismatches and trips.
4 passes. A dump whose bytes moved is refused by digest, and a dump absent from
     outputs.sha256 is refused by name. Both were exercised on a clone. Two
     consecutive runs are byte identical.

The centroid scalar reverses. It cost -0.8818 % on Gaussian blocks and buys
+0.4705 % here, and it takes two thirds of the rule's gain with it when the two
are applied together. The mechanism is the one the row above names. The served
centroids are fitted on uncompensated weights, compensation then raises the
relative norms the decision reads, so the served pair already sits low for the
distribution it meets; multiplying it by 0.961088 over-corrects.

Per cell, 12 cells of 168 blocks each (docs/data/gain-desaccord-reel-2026-09-14.csv):
the rule wins in 12 cells of 12, from -0.6475 % to -1.5287 %. The centroid
scalar loses in 8 of 12. The rule survives restatement by family and by depth;
the scalar does not, and one aggregate favourable number was hiding eight
unfavourable cells.

Depth is where the disagreement concentrates: 14.881 % on gate_proj at layer 27
against 7.738 % on q_proj at layer 13.

AUDIT OF THE PARAGRAPH ABOVE, ASKED FOR AND RUN
------------------------------------------------
The 2x2 moves one scalar in one direction and sums over blocks of very different
weight scales. A negative result on a lever is worth what the search behind it is
worth, so four ways it could be an artefact were tested. It survives all four,
and two of them make the verdict stronger than the 2x2 stated it.

A. The mean cosine is not the best scalar, and no scalar is worth anything.
   Swept over [0.90, 1.15] at 0.0005, the best rescaling of the served pair is
   1.0145 and it buys -0.0258 % of squared error. Two readings follow. The
   correction goes UP, which is what the norm distribution above predicts and the
   opposite of what stage 0 proposed. And its whole size is 0.026 %, against
   1.13 % for the rule: rescaling the centroids is not a small lever, it is not a
   lever.

B. A full refit does no better, fitted and evaluated on disjoint seeds.
   Lloyd-Max re-run on the compensated statistics of one seed, per family and
   depth, then scored on the other: -0.1063 % and -0.0445 % when fitted on
   <x,u>, +1.5501 % and +0.2265 % when fitted on ||x||. The rule on the same
   split reads -1.0290 % and -1.2323 %, so it is the only arm that is both
   negative and stable. The refit arms are fitted on 168 blocks where production
   fits on 86,016, so their spread is partly estimation noise; that is why the
   single-parameter sweep of A, not this, carries the verdict.

C. The headline does not come from the aggregation.
   Scored per cell and averaged with every cell counting once: the rule -1.1009 %
   mean, -1.2357 % median, 12 of 12 cells; the mean-cosine scalar +0.4350 % mean,
   4 of 12; the best scalar +0.0333 % mean, 6 of 12, which is the coin toss a
   null lever should read.

D. The rule is not compensating for badly placed centroids.
   Give each rule its own best scalar and compare the two optima: the served rule
   reaches -0.0258 %, the Euclidean rule -1.1316 %, a gap of 1.1058 points. The
   Euclidean rule's own optimum sits at 0.9990, so the served centroids are
   already the right pair for it and every point of the gain comes from the
   choice, not the placement. Per cell, with each rule at its own best scalar in
   that cell, the Euclidean rule still wins 12 of 12, by 0.55 to 0.87 points.

What this does not remove: 2,016 blocks, two projection families, one 0.6B model,
and squared error rather than perplexity. The limits below stand unchanged.

WHAT THE PLAN'S WRITTEN RULE SAYS, AND WHAT IT COLLIDES WITH
-------------------------------------------------------------
The stage 0 bis rule reads: under 1 % stop, 1 to 10 % go to stage 1, over 10 %
continue and keep stage 3 first. The rate is 10.119 %, so the third line fires
by 0.119 points, on 2,016 blocks. The family is not closed.

But stage 3 is the centroid stage, and the same measurement empties it. The
audit above closes it for good: the best possible placement of the two centroids
is worth 0.0258 %, against 1.13 % for the choice between them. So the trigger
points at a stage with nothing in it, and the next open stage is stage 1, the
branch harness. That is not a paradox to arbitrate, it is a stage that measured
itself out.

LIMITS
-------
2,016 blocks, not 42,400. Two projection families of seven, three depths of 28,
one model of 0.6 billion parameters, two calibration seeds. Rows are four per
cell at fixed spread positions (0, 682, 1364, 2047 of q_proj's 2,048), not a
random sample of rows. Blocks inside a row are sequentially dependent, so the
count is not a count of independent draws and no confidence interval is computed
from it.

Capture ran the original checkpoint in f32 through every layer, so the
activations are not those of a sequentially quantized model
(docs/tetra-schur-local.md). That affects the Hessian and therefore rule C. It
does not affect A against B, which read the weights, not the activations.

The refit arms of audit B are fitted on 168 blocks per cell where production
fits on 86,016, so their spread is partly estimation noise and they bound the
refit loosely. The verdict on the centroids rests on audit A, one parameter swept
exhaustively, which no amount of sample noise moves by a percent.

Nothing here is a quality claim. Retention is Shannon retention at a fixed rate,
two transpositions away from perplexity (ROADMAP.md section 2.2 bis), and the
file holds three cases where a better local proxy composed worse (ETAT.md
section 7). The 21.5 % perplexity gap between leech0c13 and Tetra bought an MMLU
difference of 1.18 pp, CI95 [-1.54; +3.98]. Any MMLU arm on this lever reports
noise until row A of ROADMAP-QUALITY.md lands.
