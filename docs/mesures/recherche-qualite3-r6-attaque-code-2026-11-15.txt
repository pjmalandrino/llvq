1. F4 AS THE NOTE USES IT: THE ERROR FOLLOWS THE ROW'S WEIGHT NORM, NOT THE SIGNAL THE ROW WRITES
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What the code says, and it agrees with F4 on structure.
- The quantized unit is 24 consecutive inputs of one output row: `let v = &row[s..s + b]` (llvq-quant/src/gptq.rs line
  312), inside `for i in 0..d_out`. Nothing is quantized across rows.
- The feedback stays in the row. The residual is formed per row (gptq.rs line 363) and propagated onto that row's
  later columns only, through the shared factor U of H (gptq.rs lines 375-389,
  `*cell -= x * factor.u(s + k, r0 + j)`).
- The row's scale is the row's RMS times sqrt(24), fixed before the loop (gptq.rs line 289; quantizer.rs line 945).
  The gain levels are one Lloyd-Max codebook per matrix on relative block gains (quantizer.rs line 896).
What follows from it, and the note skips. The block error is relative to the block gain, so a row's error energy
scales with that row's Euclidean weight energy. The row's output signal is W_i H W_i^T, which also depends on how the
row aligns with H's loud inputs. GPTQ sends every row's error to the same cheap input directions of H. A row aligned
with loud inputs has a large signal and an error priced near the floor, so its relative output error is not that of a
row aligned with quiet inputs (inference from the objective; per-row relative output error is not on disk).

In the R5 form, row k of a rotated writer is v_k^T W. Its noise then follows ||v_k^T W||^2, the writer's own weight
energy toward v_k. The stream's signal toward v_k at depth 12 is the sum of the embedding and 24 writes, with whatever
cancellations they carry. "The noise follows the signal" needs each writer's weight energy per stream direction to
track the stream's energy per direction. Nothing on disk measures either (inference).

The error is also not noise independent of the signal. It is e = dW x, linear in the writer's input. The lattice
down_proj of layers 2 to 11 shrinks its rows: coherent per-row gains p10 0.928 to 0.938, p90 0.986 to 1.000
(contrast-map matrices.tsv, sealed rows, *measured*). Rows are not treated alike to within 6 %.

Cross-row independence is an assumption. The output noise covariance is dW H dW^T. Rows coded apart, all fed back
through one H, have no guarantee of zero cross terms (inference). A noise diagonal in the rotated row basis is what R5
needs.

Row scales trained after the encode act per canonical output row (R0 note section 4, dclm-rowscales). F1's noise is
measured after them. A basis change voids them, and Q12 prices them at 3.15 points on the 4B parent (*measured*).

- Embedding. Its int4 g64 groups run along the stream axis. Stored as E V, they would hold eigen-ordered coordinates;

4. THE BASIS CHANGE IS NOT FREE: THE TIED HEAD BREAKS, AND A PER-LAYER BASIS COSTS A MATRIX A LAYER
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- RMSNorm is x / rms(x) times an elementwise gain (ops/layer_vs_output.py line 220; kernel pers_4e.cu line 311 for
  ln1, line 383 for the final norm). rms is invariant under an orthogonal V; the gain is not. The input_layernorm and
  post_attention_layernorm gains fold into the readers, as QuaRot does. That needs no new tensor (inference).
- The final norm gain does not fold. The head is the embedding itself: `Head::Dense(embed.embeddings().clone())`
  (llvq-llm/src/model.rs line 2598). With the stream in basis V the input wants E V and the head wants E diag(g_f) V.
  One tensor cannot be both. Two ways out, both *computed* from docs/fiche-4b.md (embedding 388,956,160 weights, model
  4,022,468,096):
    untie: a second int4 g64 table, 388,956,160 x 4.5 bits = 1.750e9 bits, +0.435 b/param on a 2.73 budget;
    rotate at both ends: keep E, apply V at lookup and V^T before the final norm. One 2560 x 2560 tensor, 6,553,600
    weights, +0.026 b/param in f16, and two GEMVs a token that no served kernel does today.
  R5's "needs no bit and no kernel change" holds for neither.
- One global basis is the only free one in a residual network. h_{l+1} = h_l + writes needs both terms in one basis. A
  basis per layer needs a d x d change on the skip path at each change, as SliceGPT carries: 36 x 6,553,600 =
  235,929,600 weights, +0.94 b/param in f16, +0.26 at 4.5 bits, and a stream GEMV a layer (*computed*). The depth-12
  basis applied globally is not the principal basis of depths 0 to 8 (attack 3).
- Every reader changes by diag(g) V, so all 252 matrices are re-encoded and the row scales retrained. N8 says the
  sealed branches are tied to each other: FP16's attention outputs under the sealed MLP cost 12.63 points
  (*measured*). A re-encode drops whatever compensation the sealed file holds; its net is not predictable from F1
  (inference).
- The input rotation the format applies stays as it is: V folds into W before encoding, and rot_apply rotates the
  reader's input as now (llvq_rot.cuh). The lattice kernels make no assumption on the output basis. That part is free.
