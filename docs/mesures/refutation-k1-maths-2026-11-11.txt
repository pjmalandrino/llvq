THE VERDICT
-----------
Refuted as claimed. The geometry, the basis and the closed form hold. The size of the gain does not. A lattice step
moves a block by 0.332 of its norm at the least, more than the block's own error of 0.290 (*computed*). Under the
note's own trust rule, 1 % of ‖W‖ a V phase, that lets at most 0.091 % of blocks move a phase, and 4.5 to 9.1 % over
the 50 to 100 phases planned (*computed*). Even with the exact loss known, moving the best 4.5 to 9 % of blocks cuts
the distortion by 1.0 to 8.6 % depending on how far the end-to-end target sits from the encoder's (*computed*, table
below). The project's SNR law turns that into +0.15 to +1.30 MMLU as a ceiling, with perfect information. The note's
discrete part, +0.5 to +1.5 MMLU, sits on that ceiling. The rest of its +1.8 / +4.5 centres is the corpus, which a
continuous-only arm also buys, so it is not K1's.

The seven points, one line each:
  1. Geometry                holds. 44.5 % of the 196,560 moves stay Tetra (median), the best move is a member in 46 %.
  2. Basis                   holds, with two conditions: Fisher accumulated after rotation, and Q is not only Hadamard.
  3. Cosine claim            holds (0.777 against 0.454), and is the wrong figure: step size and trust cap decide.
  4. Evidence transfer       unproven. PV-Tuning's figures are quoted right; nothing transfers to MMLU, GSM8K or 4B.
  5. Memory and time         refuted as stated. 39 GB omits the measured overhead; the V phase is not 1.7 h on a host.
  6. Project's own evidence  against, not closing. KL is not closed (0.234 nats); F6 is the direct precedent and failed.
  7. Size of the gain        refuted. Discrete part 0 to +0.5 MMLU, 0 to +1 GSM8K; "+2 to +8 GSM8K" rests on nothing.

THE SCRIPT AND WHAT IT CHECKS ITSELF AGAINST
--------------------------------------------
A numpy port of `Tetra::encode` membership, of the repository's Golay code (clmul by 0xC75, parity at bit 23) and of
the trio order of trellis.rs. Self-checks, all passing (*computed*):
  - the rank tables rebuilt by enumeration give CLASS_BOUNDS (88, [1,1,0,3,0,0,1,1]) and (96, [0,0,2,2,2,0,1,1]),
    MIXED_BOUND (72, [3,0,0,0,1,0,1,0]) and N0 = 1,240, the pinned constants of rank.rs;
  - 2,000 points drawn from the word structure (p, codeword, three rows) are all Leech points and all members;
  - the 196,560 minimal vectors, built on the repository's own octads, all have norm² 32 and lie in √8·Λ24;
  - an exact coset decoder (8,192 cosets) returns lattice points that no minimal vector improves.
Served-like blocks: 400 Gaussian blocks, encoded by the nearest lattice point over 23 scales, kept only if a Tetra
member, best cosine. Median cos² 0.9157, shells 3 to 16, mode 9 to 10. A hill-climb over member moves then polishes
each block to a local cosine optimum, cos² 0.9242. This is not the production encoder (two scales, rank-region rule,
88.89 % retention with the gain bit in how-it-works §7). The served shell histogram is not in any journal. Every
figure below is on this proxy.

POINT 1. THE GEOMETRY: HOLDS
----------------------------
Tetra is Λ24 cut per section: each of the three octads of the trio holds a rank vector, sections 1 and 3 among the
2,048 cheapest of their class (cost bound 88 or 96), section 2 among the 2,048 cheapest overall (cost bound 72).
The decoder normalises: w = c_g · rscale · y / ‖y‖ (reconstruct_shape_gain), so a move y → y + v keeps the gain bit
and changes only the direction. "Same gain" is automatic.
Membership of y + v, over all 196,560 v, 400 blocks (*computed*):
  all moves                  median 44.5 %, p5 14.2 %, p95 84.5 %, at least 12,181 members a block
  shape 1, (±2)^8            45.9 %
  shape 2, (∓3, ±1^23)       37.9 %, the shape the note feared; it does fail more, not "often"
  shape 3, (±4, ±4, 0^22)    53.5 %
  by shell                   74 % at m ≤ 6, 47 % at m = 9, 41 % at m = 10, 23 % at m = 12, 9 % at m = 14
For a random tangent gradient the unconstrained best move is a member in 45.8 % of blocks, and the best member move
has cosine 0.751 at the median against 0.777 for the best overall (*computed*).
The neighbourhood does not collapse. It thins with the shell, so the blocks of largest norm, often those of largest
error, have the fewest moves. The note's kill line of stage 0, membership under 20 %, would not fire on this proxy.

POINT 2. THE BASIS: HOLDS, WITH TWO CONDITIONS
----------------------------------------------
Codes live in the rotated input basis, W' = W Qᵀ (rotation.rs). The gradient maps exactly, G' = G Qᵀ, because Q is
orthogonal. The tangent projection at ŷ is the right Jacobian of y ↦ y/‖y‖ times c_g · rscale. A shared rotation
(LLVQ_ROT_SHARE=1) gives several matrices the same Q on their input side. Each matrix keeps its own 24-column block
grid, so the block structure survives.
Condition A. Q is (Q_odd ⊗ H_m) D, with a dense Q_odd for widths that are not powers of two (9,728 = 19 · 512 at 4B).
The note says "Hadamard and signs"; the port must carry the dense factor or it rotates into the wrong basis.
Condition B. The diagonal Fisher must be the EMA of (G Qᵀ)² taken after rotation at every step. diag(Q F Qᵀ) is not
Q diag(F) Qᵀ, so a g² accumulated in the natural basis and rotated afterwards is wrong.

POINT 3. THE COSINE AND WHAT IT IS WORTH: THE COSINE HOLDS, THE ARGUMENT DOES NOT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Reproduced with a tangent gradient: 0.777 for the best minimal vector against 0.454 for the best ±e_i (*computed*;
note 0.774 and 0.453 on full gradients). The closed form is not the bottleneck either: given the exact gradient, K1's
top-8 screen recovers 72 to 99 % of the cut the best member move gives (table, columns 4 and 5).
The step size decides, and it is fixed by the lattice:
  smallest member move a block      0.332 of the block norm (median), all member moves 0.456
  the block's own error ‖e‖/‖x‖     0.290 (median)
One step is larger than the error it is meant to repair.
The trust rule caps how many blocks can move. ‖ΔW‖/‖W‖ ≤ 0.01 allows a share of blocks f with f · 0.332² ≤ 10^-4,
so f ≤ 0.091 % a V phase, 4.5 % over 50 phases, 9.1 % over 100 (*computed*). The note's own stage 0, 0.5 % of blocks
in one phase, already reads ‖ΔW‖/‖W‖ = 2.35 %, outside its trust rule.
Which blocks can gain at all. Model: block loss (w - t)ᵀ D (w - t), w at fixed magnitude ⟨x, ŷ⟩, D diagonal
log-normal with CV 0 or 0.3 (the spread l36-capture reads), and t = x + η the end-to-end target, η random with
‖η‖ = ρ ‖e‖ standing for the coupling the encoder never saw. ρ is not measured anywhere. On the polished proxy
(*computed*, 400 blocks):

The SNR law is the repository's, acc = 54.60 + 14.60 ln SNR, so a distortion cut f is worth 14.60 · (-ln(1 - f)).
It was fitted on encoders, not on post-hoc moves; it is used here only as an order of magnitude.
Two readings. Without the trust cap the moves are large: 18 to 41 % of distortion at ρ ≥ 0.5. With it, the run moves
too few blocks to matter. Both rest on a perfect gradient and a known target; K1 has neither (point 6, and the
gradient-noise question of the closing test). On the unpolished proxy the moves also find 12 % of plain encoder
slack at ρ = 0 (*computed*): a K1 gain can be encoder repair, which a better encoder buys without training.

POINT 4. EVIDENCE TRANSFER: UNPROVEN
------------------------------------
The note's PV-Tuning figures are quoted correctly (*reported*, Table 1, Llama 2 7B): AQLM 2.01 bits, calibration
only 53.2, continuous only 56.57, STE 57.04, PV 57.81, PV + STE 58.19; GPTQ 2.14 bits, continuous 46.27, PV 52.17.
Llama 3 8B at 2.01 bits, 64.36 against 68.61 (Table 2).
What they are:
  - zero-shot averages of five tasks (ArcC, ArcE, HellaSwag, PiQA, WinoGrande) and perplexity; no MMLU, no GSM8K,
    no reasoning benchmark anywhere in the paper;
  - on learned codebooks (AQLM, VQ) and scalar GPTQ, at 1 to 2.2 bits, on Llama 2, Llama 3, Mistral and Phi-3;
  - one V step per training step, RedPajama 1 B-token sample, batches of 2^20 tokens; no per-model GPU-hours, "up to
    1.5×" the time of continuous tuning, and 2 days on 8 GPUs to pass the baselines at 70B.
The discrete step is not "worth as much as the continuous one". On AQLM the continuous step is worth +3.37 and the
discrete +1.24 to +1.62 above it, about a third. The +5.9 on GPTQ comes from a base at perplexity 3,290, broken.
The ~4B point is Phi-3-mini, AQLM 60.4 to PV-Tuning 65.70 zero-shot, which does not isolate the discrete step.
No report of a discrete-step gain on a reasoning benchmark, or at 4B within 5 GPU-hours, was found. K1's batch is
2,048 tokens a step (tetranu-rowscales, 19.8 M tokens over 9,681 steps, *measured*), 512 times smaller than
PV-Tuning's, and it runs 50 to 100 V phases where PV-Tuning runs one a step.

POINT 5. MEMORY AND TIME: REFUTED AS STATED
-------------------------------------------
Tetra weights at 4B: 118,665,216 blocks, 2.85 G weights (*measured*, artstat-4b). Not 3.6 G: v_proj and a second
type are int4 (84 of 252 records).
Memory on one L40S, 48 GB (*estimated*):
  - The row-scale mode already carries an overhead beyond the two models: 72.65 GB allocated at 8B for 32.8 GB of
    student and teacher (*measured*, dclm-8b), 119.4 GB at 14B for about 59 GB (*measured*, dclm-14b). Scaled to the
    4B's width, 25 to 30 GB over 16 GB of models.
  - Weight gradients in bf16, 5.7 GB. Two EMAs over 100 to 200 steps cannot live in bf16: an increment of 0.5 to 1 %
    sits at bf16's 0.39 % resolution and is rounded half away. In f32 they are 22.8 GB.
  - Sum: about 41 GB of today's footprint plus 5.7 plus 11.4 (bf16) to 22.8 (f32), so 58 to 70 GB. Offline teacher
    logits remove 8 GB. It does not fit the L40S; the note's 39 GB counts no measured overhead.
Time:
  - Training steps: 0.7437 s a step at the probe, 0.6479 s in the loop (*measured*). A third more for the weight
    gradient is plausible, about 0.86 to 1.0 s, 2.4 to 2.8 h for 10,000 steps (*estimated*). This part holds.
  - V phase: the note's own 10^4 to 10^5 operations a block times 1.19·10^8 blocks is 1.2·10^12 to 1.2·10^13 per
    phase (*computed*). On a host at about 10^10 simple operations a second, 2 to 20 minutes a phase, 1.7 to 17 h for
    50 phases, before the top-8 list decode and the 8 memberships a block (*estimated*). The note's "a few seconds for
    every block of a 4B" and 1.7 h hold only at the low end, or with a GPU kernel that is not in the 2 to 3 week build.
  - 4.5 h and $8 to $9 a 4B are therefore unproven, and need a larger card (h200, $4.97 an hour by jobs.csv) for memory
    alone.

POINT 6. THE PROJECT'S OWN EVIDENCE: AGAINST, NOT CLOSING
--------------------------------------------------------
- The 1.0074 figure is right: WikiText-2 perplexity 12.3268 against 12.2361 for f16, MMLU 61.11 against 70.14
  (*measured*, dclm-rowscales). The note's gloss is wrong: the generic KL is not closed. The training KL on DCLM still
  reads 0.2342 nats a token in its last quarter (*measured*, same journal). There is a signal for codes to follow.
  What the 1.0074 says is that perplexity is no gate, and that a KL fall of 3.8 % bought +3.15 MMLU: KL and MMLU are
  not on one scale, so no KL figure predicts K1's MMLU.
- F6 is the direct precedent and it failed out of sample. Re-choosing a block's direction under the conditional
  Hessian metric gained 2.393 % locally, 0.911 % chained, and lost 8.763 % on reserved activations (*measured*,
  tetra-directions-2026-09-16). K1's V step is the same act, a direction re-choice per block under a second-order
  model, with an end-to-end gradient in place of a layer Hessian. The note answers that its objective is end to end;
  the measured failure was overfitting to calibration statistics, which a 2,048-token-a-step gradient EMA risks more.
- Spherical GPTQ (+31.89 % perplexity at 0.6B, ETAT §6) changed the encoder's feedback, which K1 does not touch. It
  is not a direct precedent; it shows only that angular changes chosen by a local rule can compose badly.
- ROADMAP-QUALITY row 22 (PV-Tuning, "0 transposable") argued that the P step has nothing to move on a fixed lattice.
  The +3.15 / +3.29 / +1.67 of the row scales contradict that half of the argument. Its other half, that the
  measured gain is zero-shot perplexity on a learned codebook with no MMLU, still stands (point 4). Row 21 (Block-AP)
  is avoided: K1 does not re-encode in the loop. Row 12 (K-best beam) warns that a better local proxy has composed
  worse three times here.
- None of these rows closes K1. Together they say its stage 0 must measure out-of-sample realisation, not membership.

POINT 7. THE SIZE OF THE GAIN: REFUTED
--------------------------------------
My interval for the discrete part at 4B (*estimated*): MMLU 0 to +0.5, centre +0.2; GSM8K 0 to +1, centre +0.3.
Reasons: the trust-capped ceiling of point 3 is +0.15 to +1.30 MMLU with a perfect gradient and a known target; a
2,048-token gradient is far from perfect; F6 lost out of sample; and PV-Tuning's discrete step was a third of its
continuous step on a base that had no continuous tuning, where ours already has it.
The GSM8K centre sits under the paired floor of 1.3 to 1.9 pp the note itself gives, so stage 2 cannot see it.
"+2 to +8 GSM8K" is supported by no measurement. The two sources behind it, [S10] (GPTQ 3 bits, self-generated
calibration) and [S26] (on-policy distillation, read from an abstract), are corpus effects on other quantizers.
Neither isolates a discrete move. The note itself assigns +1 to +3 of it to the discrete part and the rest to the
corpus, which is tonight's P3 and not this invention. The stacked K1 + K2 "+4.3 to +5.5 GSM8K" multiplies two
estimated centres by a sub-additivity measured on other levers; it inherits both errors.

THE WEAKEST POINT
-----------------
The fixed step against the trust rule. One lattice step is 0.33 of a block's norm, above its 0.29 error, so a 1 %
trust ratio moves under 0.1 % of blocks a phase and under 10 % in the whole run. The note never multiplies these
two of its own numbers. Relaxing the rule to move more blocks is exactly the chained-collapse regime of F6.

THE CHEAPEST DECISIVE TEST
--------------------------
Split-half agreement on the 0.6B, $0 on the Mac, needs the operator's go for the model load. One existing Tetra
encoding of Qwen3-0.6B; gradient of the KL to f16 on two disjoint sets of 32 windows of 2,048 tokens; for every Tetra
block, the best member move chosen from half A, scored by its predicted ΔL under half B. Kill if fewer than 60 % of
the 0.1 % best moves of half A also lower half B's model, or if applying them raises held-out KL on 12 windows. It
answers the two questions this note cannot: whether a per-block gradient at this batch size is signal, and what ρ
is. Membership, which the note's stage 0 put first, is settled here at 44.5 % and need not be paid for.
