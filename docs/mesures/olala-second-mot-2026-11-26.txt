OLALA V1.0 MINI 7A1B, A SECOND TETRA WORD ON THE RESIDUAL, 2026-11-26

RESULT
  A second Tetra word encoded on the residual of the first takes the relative error of a matrix from 0.0851 to
  0.0074 at 4.00 bits a weight, and a third to 0.0007 at 6.00 (*measured*, the repository's encoder, data-free).
  int4 g128 reads 0.0100 at 4.25 bits on the same matrices, and the best fixed-rate scalar quantizer of a Gaussian
  0.0095 at 4 bits (*computed*).
  Under the noise proxy of the reconnaissance, a second word on the busiest 20 % of the routed experts halves the
  KL to bf16, from 0.125 to 0.061, and takes the top-1 agreement from 85.9 % to 90.1 % (*measured*, a proxy on
  4,088 positions, not an encode). That file would weigh 2.49 GB, 2.94 b/param (*computed*).
  No file exists. The format holds one word a block, and a second word is a format and kernel change.

WHAT WAS RUN
  Not a preregistered gate. A reading with no threshold stated before it, in the container of the reconnaissance,
  on the same weights (sha256 99a208e1...9356442), $0. Raw outputs and scripts in `olala-second-mot-2026-11-26-brut/`.
  It follows `olala-reconnaissance-2026-11-25.txt` and uses its tensors, its forward and its noise proxy.

1. WHY NOT A BETTER CODE AT 48 BITS (*computed* here; the bound is the repository's)
  Best fixed-rate scalar quantizer of a Gaussian, Lloyd's iteration on 4 M samples (`scalar.out`):
    2 bits 0.1177    3 bits 0.0346    4 bits 0.0095      Shannon: 0.0625, 0.0156, 0.0039
  Tetra's one word reads 0.0851 at 2 bits: 28 % under the scalar quantizer, 36 % over Shannon.
  `CERVEAU.md` C7 bounds what a better 24-dimension code of 48 bits could add at 0.60 dB, 13 % of the error, and Q4
  records that codebook gains under 0.4 dB did not carry to perplexity.

2. THE SECOND WORD ON REAL TENSORS (*measured*, `probe2.out`, source `olalaprobe2_main.rs`)
  Stage 1 as in the reconnaissance. Stage 2 encodes W - Q1 in the rotated basis with the same pieces: its own
  frozen row scales, its own two gain centroids, one gain bit, the block loop with retraction, H = I. Stage 3
  encodes what stage 2 leaves. 1,536 rows a tensor. Relative squared error against the original:
    tensor                         one word   two words   three words   stage 2 on its own input
    Gaussian, 768 columns           0.08513    0.00743      0.00071       0.08725
    experts.weight L17              0.08525    0.00744      0.00070       0.08730
    output_experts L17              0.08508    0.00743      0.00071       0.08731
    shared fc_1 L17                 0.08526    0.00744      0.00071       0.08732
    mixer_proj L17                  0.08535    0.00749      0.00072       0.08778

  The residual of a lattice code is as easy to encode as the weights: stage 2 leaves 0.0873 of its input where
  stage 1 leaves 0.0851. Each word multiplies the error by about 0.087. The degenerate `output_experts` of layer 1
  gains less.
  A second word costs 48 bits a block and one f16 row scale a row: 4.06 b/weight on the routed experts.

HOW TO READ IT
  The proxy is isotropic and ignores the Hessian. One noise draw an arm. The KL is summed over the bf16 model's 64
  likeliest tokens, on technical text in windows of 512; it is not the KLD the public files report on WikiText-2
  (0.0587 at 3.72 GB, 0.0218 at 5.99 GB), which is given for scale only.
  Not measured: a GPTQ loop over two stages, the decode cost of a second word in a kernel, a second word on the
  dense matrices in place of int4.
