WHAT THE SASS SAYS ABOUT 4E'S TETRA ROWS AFTER V3, AND THE ROW-LOOP MICROBENCHMARK THAT CALIBRATES THEM, 2026-11-05
==================================================================================================================

Result: the SASS of 4E v3's stream loop does not show what bounds the Tetra rows. Every limit inside the SM that can
be read off the code sits three times or more above the measured rate (*computed*, §2):
- the dependency chain of a block is about 215 cycles;
- instruction issue runs at about 24 % of the SM's capacity;
- the shared-memory pipe at about a quarter;
- each lane holds 4 blocks' words in flight.
Two models used so far are refuted by card readings already on file:
- the L1/shared pipe model behind pers-sass and the review's "Λ-transposed read": the one-launch pass with its
  tables in shared memory roughly halved that pipe's traffic and read x0.948 (pers-4e-v2 bench);
- the per-row round-trip model behind v3's estimate: v3 removed those round trips and the rows did not move
  (pers-4e-v3).
The bound is dynamic, and the next estimate needs measured rates. One card job is ready, not launched: the row loops
of 4E v2 and v3 timed alone at 4E's geometry, with per-warp clock stamps and five arms that each remove one suspect.
- Job: ops/jobs/pers-rowloop.sh.
- Prereg: proofs/preregistration-pers-rowloop-2026-11-05.md, not stamped.
- Cost: about 10 min and $0.30 (*estimated*); ceiling $0.90.

v3's MLP got slower (wall 2,934 to 3,096 us) for two reasons visible in the SASS:
- its stream loop executes about 265 instructions a block against v2's 241;
- the rows still divide unevenly over 16 warps (§3).
The microbenchmark measures both.

1. THE SASS OF THE V3 STREAM LOOP (devel ptxas 12.4, sm_89, pers_4e_tokens_f1_t512)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The card's driver ptxas (580) gave 126 registers and 0 B local to this symbol on the card (v3 journal); the devel
  ptxas gives 120 and 0 B. The v3 job dumped no cubin, so the loops below are the devel compile's. For v2 the devel
  and the card's loops agreed (241 against 241, v3-host §1).
  - Four stream loops (q/k, gate/up, two Tetra downs): 1,523 to 1,595 instructions for 4 blocks, the ring unrolled at
    compile time. A block's slot is 398 instructions. The common path is about 265 instructions: the slot without
    its row-start region (20) and its row-end region (106, warp_sum and lane 0's epilogue).
  - Against v2's 241 a block, the added instructions are control:
    - the row-boundary tests and the (row, item) counters;
    - 4 BSSY/BSYNC reconvergence regions a slot, which also wall each block's chain off from the next;
    - the refill's pointer arithmetic.
  - The decode itself is v2's: LDS.128 6, LDS 4, LDS.U8 2, LDS.U16 1, IDP 6, the 24-deep FFMA chain.
  - The ring is real. Slot d decodes the words loaded 4 slots earlier (registers R25, R24, R14, R15 of the gate/up
    loop, traced write to read).
  - gate/up's row end reloads the activation and SiLU table pointers from the argument struct (LD.E.64 at +0x118 and
    +0x120) at every row: the store through a generic pointer forbids keeping them in registers. v2's claim loop
    does the same.

2. NO LIMIT INSIDE THE SM EXPLAINS THE RATE (*computed* unless said)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  The Tetra rows in v3: about 256 GB/s on the MLP phase's mean CTA work (fixed work and int4 downs taken out as in
  v3-host §2). A warp step is 32 blocks of one row, 201 B. That is 8.97 M warp steps a second a SM.
    - issue: 265 instructions a step is 2.4 G warp-instructions a second a SM, 24 % of 4 a cycle at 2.52 GHz.
    - shared pipe: about 65 wavefronts a step, 0.58 G a second, a quarter of one a cycle. The count is 48 for the six
      LDS.128 (stride 96 B, 2-way conflicts in every quarter-warp) plus about 17 for the gathers: 3.5 each into
      the 16 KB rank table and the 2 KB branch table, 1 for the 128-byte tables and invnorm.
    - chain: 215 cycles a block (ops/sass_chain.py on v2's loop and on one v3 slot; assumed latencies: ALU 4-6,
      shared 30, L1 hit 35). A warp takes about 4,500 cycles a step on the mean CTA basis, about 3,300 per busy
      warp (§3).
    - words in flight: 16 warps x 4 x 201 B = 12.9 KB a SM; at a loaded DRAM latency of 0.5-0.7 us (*estimated*)
      that would carry 2.6-3.7 TB/s.
  Each of these four predicts 900 GB/s or more. The rate is 256.
  The int4 rows look the same from this side: 588 GB/s with 75-80 instructions a word, about one warp-instruction a
  cycle a SM, as the Tetra rows. Their rate tracks bytes per instruction (1.66 for int4, 0.76 for v3 Tetra, 0.83 for
  v2): 588 x 0.76 / 1.66 = 269 against 256 measured. A common cap near one instruction a cycle a SM fits both
  loops, and nothing in the code says what it is. That is a fit at two points, not a mechanism.

  Two card readings on file already rule out mechanisms:
    - the L1/shared pipe. tpersall_ts holds the tables in shared memory: about 65 wavefronts a step against about
      145 with the tables through L1 (pers-sass). It read x0.948, at 24 warps a SM against 32 (v2 bench, lines
      90-107). If the pipe bound, halving its traffic would have shown.
    - the words' latency. v3 keeps 4 blocks in flight a lane against v2's one, and the Tetra rows of the MLP phase
      moved from 272 to 256 GB/s (same accounting).

3. WHY V3'S MLP GOT SLOWER, AND THE WARPS' BALANCE (*computed*)
---------------------------------------------------------------
  - The loop: about 265 instructions a block against 241, +10 %. On a loop whose rate tracks executed instructions,
    that is the 5-6 % the MLP's mean work moved (2,834 to 2,975 us).
  - The rows a warp: v2 claimed rows one at a time (pers_claim), v3 assigns them statically. With equal rows both
    end on the same count, ceil(rows / 16), so this is not the regression. It is a cost of both:
      phase (4B, 142 CTAs)            rows a CTA   most a warp   balance if every row takes the same time
      gate/up pairs                   68.5         5             0.856
      Tetra down (d_in 9,728)         18.0         2             0.563
      q|k (v3's mixed split)          36.1         3             0.75
    In the bench's file every projection is Tetra. The balance from row counts alone is 0.805 at d_in 2,560 (q, k,
    v, gate, up), 0.563 at 4,096 (o) and 0.563 at 9,728 (down). The stamps check these first (prereg rule B).
  - A fix that keeps the bits exists on paper and is not built. A block's dot product is independent of the others
    and only the fold into acc is ordered. So two warps can share a long row: one computes block partials into shared
    memory, the row's owner folds them in order. Not proposed before the calibration (coordinator's order).

4. THE ROW-LOOP MICROBENCHMARK (pers_trows.cu, LLVQ_TROWS=1 in planesbench)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Its own NVRTC module, the persistent unit followed by pers_trows.cu, so 4E's module keeps its bytes. The 252
  matrices of the bare Tetra file in served order. 4E's geometry:
    - one CTA of 512 threads a SM, forced by 4E's own 57,984 shared bytes (header, tables, activation of 9,728);
    - each site's rows split over CTAs as 4E splits a phase, and over warps statically;
    - a CTA barrier between sites, no grid barrier.
  The served tv_tetra48_h gives the reference rows. With stamps, a separate launch outside the rounds writes:
    - every warp's busy cycles and the CTA's, per site;
    - %clock64 and %globaltimer at both ends of the launch.
  From those: cycles a step per class of d_in, the balance, and the SM clock in MHz (pers.rs::trows_classes,
  trows_clock_mhz).
    arm      what it is                                                         owes the bits   regs (devel)
    v2       4E v2's row (pers_tetra_row, one block a step, next words in flight)   yes          68
    v3       4E v3's stream (pers_tetra_stream, 4 in flight across rows)            yes          85
    ilp2     two blocks a step in one region, folds in the served order             yes          94
    v2u      v2 with a trip count uniform over the warp: control of the three below  yes          67
    l1w      v2u reading row 0's words for every row: no DRAM for the words          no           71
    bcast    v2u decoding lane 0's words on every lane: table reads broadcast        no           67
    xbcast   v2u reading the step's lane-0 activation block: LDS.128 broadcast        no           68
    v2_s     v2 on the sites of d_in 4,096 or less, one CTA a SM                     yes          68
    v2_o2    the same at two CTAs a SM (32 warps), launch bound (512, 2)             yes          63
  Devel SASS (ops/sass_loops.py), each arm's loop is the one intended:
    - v2's block loop is 241 instructions, as on the card's 4E v2;
    - v3's stream loop is 1,509 for 4 blocks (4E v3: 1,523 to 1,595);
    - ilp2 decodes two blocks in one 519-instruction region with a single reconvergence;
    - bcast adds 2 SHFL; xbcast keeps its 6 LDS.128, uniform at run time.
  None spills.

5. THE "Λ-TRANSPOSED READ" (review session, 2026-11-05, not committed)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
  Claim: Tetra reads 2-3 times faster from the structure y = p 1 + 2c + 4k, with identical bits, through four pieces:
    1. compute the Golay word, about 16 PRMT/IMAD, instead of 3 table reads, 2 of them dependent;
    2. gscale in registers and invnorm by a shuffle;
    3. one row a lane, 32 rows a warp on one column of blocks: the activation is broadcast, and a lane's blocks of
       one row run with no warp_sum, the 32 partial sums and the butterfly replayed in the lane for the bits;
    4. only the rank table in shared memory.
  Its figure comes from the pers-sass LSU model: about 145 accesses a warp step down to about 20, giving a 2.2-2.5 ms
  floor down to 0.3-0.45 ms.
  Against the bound:
  - Its premise does not hold in 4E. 4E already keeps every table in shared memory: about 13 shared reads and 3
    global loads a block, not 145. The arm that took tpersall from about 145 to about 65 pipe wavefronts gained
    nothing (§2). Piece 4 is what 4E already is.
  - Pieces 1 and 2 shorten the decode chain by one or two shared-memory levels and one L1 load. That helps only if
    the chain's latency binds. ilp2 measures that directly, and bcast bounds what free table reads can buy.
  - Piece 3 removes the conflicts on the activation (48 of the 65 wavefronts) and gives every lane a long row
    whose blocks can overlap. xbcast bounds the first, ilp2 the second. Its cost:
    - a load-time transpose of the words;
    - in each lane, a replay of the 32 partial sums and the butterfly, in the served order;
    - no warp_sum, which it saves.
  - So its 2-3x is not supported by any reading on file, and the microbenchmark tests each of its mechanisms in the
    same job at no extra cost. Prereg rule E reads it: the 2-3x stands only if bcast x xbcast >= 2 or ilp2 >= 2.
  - Its variant with integer activations and dp4a is a T8 matter (quality), outside this bound question.

6. HOST PROOFS ($0)
------------------
  tests/pers_matches_served.rs:
    - the_row_loop_arms_are_the_served_rows. v2, v3, ilp2 and v2u give the served rows and their f32 values before
      f2h on five sites (d_in 2,560, 4,096, 9,728, 128, and a d_out of 23), at grids 3x96, 2x64, 4x128 and 5x32,
      with and without stamps. The three diagnostics run and move rows.
    - row_loop_mutants_are_killed: 8 mutants, each caught:
      - ilp2: fold order, second activation, guard, prefetch;
      - v2u's trip count; the CTA slice; the warp stride; v3's row count.
    - Two mutants were replaced, each equivalent for the bits: zero words past the row decode to the zero vector,
      whose fold leaves acc as it is. These are the ilp2 guard removed and v2u's guard removed. v2u's guard stays: it
      keeps shared reads inside the staged activation on the card.
    - The first draft compared binary16 rows only, and the fold-order mutant survived. The test now also compares
      the f32 value before f2h, as the T tests do.
  pers.rs:
    - the_trows_unit_carries_every_arm: 4E's module text first and unchanged; 57,984 B at d_in 9,728;
    - trows_stamps_fold_by_class.
  llvq-cuda: 194 tests in 30 suites green (debug loop, nice 10). cargo clippy --all-targets: 0 warnings on the Mac;
  the Linux half of llvq-cuda: 0. llvq-llm is untouched.
