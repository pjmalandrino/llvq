1. CACHED COMPILED MODULES
--------------------------
What changes. Each module is compiled once per (module text sha256, arch, driver version) and kept as a cubin on disk.
llvq-cuda already holds the pieces: persbench.rs::driver_cubin links NVRTC's PTX with the card driver's own linker,
and dump_cubin writes the file. A load reads the file and calls cuModuleLoadData; a miss compiles as today and writes
it. The served door also stops compiling the base "4E tree" module when it launches only one arm.
Gain.
- Served load: 100.6 s, of which 4.8 s put the weights on the card and the rest installs 4E (*measured*, large-prefill).
  Warm, about 10 s [5, 30] (*estimated*): weights plus a cubin read plus 4E's own install work.
- Bench job: the r32 job spent 789 s of its 1,160 running seconds loading (*measured*). Warm load about 60 s [30, 150]:
  1,160 - 789 + 60 = 431 s, 7.2 min, $0.22 against $0.58 (*computed* from the estimate). About 25 jobs ran since
  2026-11-09; at that rate the cache returns $0.30 to $0.40 a job and 10 to 12 min of wall.
Why nothing else moves. The cubin is the same PTX through the same driver linker, so the same SASS, registers and bits.
The cache lives on disk, not on the card. No byte of the sealed file moves.
Unmeasured term: the split of a load between NVRTC's front end and the driver JIT, and 4E's install work outside
compiling. One reading bounds it: in the large-prefill job the chain process reached 4E's installed level 43 s sooner
than the eager process that ran before it (57.7 against 100.6 s, *measured*). A driver JIT cache hit on 4E's PTX
explains that gap (*estimated*): about 55 % front end, 45 % JIT. Setting CUDA_CACHE_PATH alone would take only the JIT
half. The cubin cache takes both.
Cost: 4 to 6 agent-hours. One job of about 10 to 15 min ($0.30 to $0.45): a cold process, then a warm one.
Gate: the warm cubin's sha256 equals the cold one's; registers and local bytes equal; 4E's binary16 logits (E1's logits
symbol) bit for bit between a JIT-loaded and a cubin-loaded module at 5 and 8,192 tokens; tokens 64 of 64; tok/s round
by round within the round spread; load time cold and warm. For jobs, the cubins travel through the HF bucket with a
manifest of their sha256, checked before load. A cubin is code run on the card: only files this project's jobs wrote
are loaded.
Risk: low. The driver version is in the key, so a new driver misses and recompiles.

2. THE SERVED DOOR FOR THE CHAIN PREFILL
----------------------------------------
What changes. Session::feed sends the new prompt tokens through chain_launches at 32 rows (a12ipg32), head on the last
pass only, when the decoder is 4E and the config asks for it. answer() takes the first token from that pass instead of
the eager head. The hand-over launch, a full one-row pass that only moves the last chunk's K and V (12 to 16 ms a
prompt, *measured*), becomes a copy kernel: the same bytes, written where the move writes them. A config field
(proposed "prefill": "chain32") in a new config file names it; the published configs do not change. Refused on Metal.
The row buffers (0.153 GB, *measured*) are allocated for the prefill and freed before decode.
Gain (*computed* from the bench's measured parts; the door itself is unmeasured):
- First token 0.448 / 2.681 / 7.800 s against 3.951 / 17.641 / 37.037 s: x8.8, x6.6, x4.7; with the copy hand-over the
  decode starts 12 to 16 ms sooner (large-prefill-r32, large-prefill).
- Chat turn of 256 answer tokens: 1.645 / 4.008 / 9.424 s against 5.139 / 18.953 / 38.638 s (large-prefill-r32).
- House set: prefill median 0.246 s eager against 0.032 s chain (*measured*); with 256 tokens at 219 tok/s (1.17 s),
  the turn goes from about 1.41 to about 1.20 s, x1.18 (*computed* from medians).
- Peak at 8,191: 3.560 GB against 5.946 (*measured*). The VRAM criterion gains too.
Quality. The chain is not the eager path's arithmetic. It is a12ip's bits at every length (identity R = 32 against
R = 16 against R = 1, *measured*), inside the served +-0.12 % tolerance (paired ppl +0.0007 % [-0.008, +0.009],
*measured*), 41 of 47 texts identical, the six others on near-ties. This is the tolerance the grouped attention was
served under, not the same text. The operator decides whether that counts as no trade-off.
VRAM. The load phase peaks 2.117 GB with the tree installed against 1.912 eager (*measured*): 0.153 GB of row buffers
and about 0.05 GB the file does not attribute. Kept resident, the short-context footprint would rise from 2.01 to about
2.2 GB, which breaks the rule. Hence the buffers per prefill. At 1,024 the chain's margin under eager is only 0.038 GB
(2.318 against 2.356, *measured*); below 1,024 no peak was measured. A length threshold under which the door stays
eager is the fallback.
Unmeasured terms: the door's own overhead beside the bench; the peaks at 5, 64 and 256 prompt tokens; the allocation
cost of the buffers per prefill (about 1 ms, *estimated*); GSM8K after a chain prefill.
Gates, owed before serving:
- Host twin: the door's chain path equals prefillbench's (each layer's K and V hash, first token) at 3 lengths, one
  across a 256-position split; the copy hand-over equals the one-row move bit for bit; a mutant on the copy's offset
  killed.
- Card, speed and memory (one job, about 25 min, $0.75 with item 1, $1.10 without): first token through the door at
  64, 256, 1,024, 4,096, 8,191 against the eager door, round by round; phase peak at each length at or under eager's;
  card memory after the turn within 0.02 GB of eager's; decode after the hand-over within 1 % of the eager door's;
  the 47 texts paired, every divergence on a near-tie (margin at most 0.25).
- Card, quality: GSM8K-200 through the door against the eager door, gsm8kpair, exact McNemar not separated, and the
  count of identical texts. About $0.6 to $1.2. The gsm8k binary goes through Session today, so the door wires it.
Cost: 10 to 14 agent-hours; $1.5 to $2.5 of card. Depends on item 1: without it the door loads the 4E tree module
(108 to 165 s) and a12ipg32 (about 370 s) at every start, and the load criterion falls from 100 s to about 575 s
(*computed* from the journals' compile times).
Risk: medium, in the memory gate at short prompts and in the operator's reading of quality.

3. THE CHAIN-ONLY PASS: WHAT IT SHEDS OF THE 10.84 MS, AND 64 ROWS
----------------------------------------------------------------
The question of the lost study. A 32-row pass at 8k costs 10.84 ms outside attention (*measured*, r32). A one-row 4E
token costs 4.47 ms. Could a pass built for known tokens, with no tree machinery, shed much of the gap?
The tree machinery is under 0.03 ms of the 10.84 (*computed* from pers_4et_a12g.cu):
- the move loop copies n_move x 36 slices of 4 KB, 4.7 MB a pass at 32 rows: 0.011 ms at 864 GB/s;
- the tickets and slots reset by CTA 0, and the taps (no-op at L.pad 0): microseconds;
- norm/RoPE writes K and V into the tree slots instead of the cache: the same stores;
- a head-off pass keeps 3 grid waits of the head block, about 4 us at 1.3 us a wait (spec-cout-fixe-audit);
- path and depth are read by the attention, so they sit in the 34.3 ms, not in the 10.84.
The 10.84 ms is the projection engine: projections about 8.9 ms (D1's 8.04 at 16 rows plus R32's +0.45 q/k/v and
+0.42 gate/up), folds about 0.8, prologues 0.81, norm/RoPE about 0.2 (*computed* from measured stamps). The weights'
own floor is 1.378 GB at 864 GB/s, 1.60 ms. The excess is the tree unit's loop (x1.27 of 4E's), unit starts, f32
partials and folds, the audit's ledger. None of it is tree-specific.
What a chain-only form does buy:
- No hand-over launch: the last pass writes its rows into the cache, 12 to 16 ms a prompt. Item 2's copy kernel takes
  the same gain without a new pass.
- Room past 32 rows. P4T_RMAX sizes the path rows (u8 [R][32]), the slots and the tickets; a chain needs none of them,
  so 64 rows touches only the staging and the accumulators.
Rows are the lever. From the two arms of the r32 job, a pass costs about 8.8 ms fixed plus 0.064 ms a row
((10.839 - 9.816) / 16, *computed*). Per prompt token outside attention: 0.614 ms at 16 rows, 0.339 at 32, 0.201 at 64
(*computed* on that line), 0.20 to 0.23 with a chunk penalty of 0 to 2 ms a pass (*estimated*).
Gain at 64 rows, attention a token unchanged (*estimated*): first token 1,024 from 0.448 to 0.33 s [0.30, 0.40];
4,096 from 2.681 to 2.2 s [2.1, 2.5]; 8,191 from 7.800 to 6.8 s [6.6, 7.4]. If the full tile lands, attention shrinks
and the rows' share of the first token grows, so the ratio improves.
Bits: kept if the slices stay the plan's and each tile folds in order. Gate: R = 64 equals R = 32 and R = 1 bit for bit.
How: the down at 64 rows stages 17,152 floats, over shared memory at 12 and at 8 warps (rangees study, section 3).
Staging along d_in in chunks (a2's double-buffered unit) fits and keeps the add order. Accumulators at RC = 8 add 16
registers a lane: at 384 threads that is 168 + 16 = 184 against a cap of 170; at 256 threads a10t256 holds 190 at RC = 4
(*measured*, n10), so about 206 of 255 (*estimated*).
Unmeasured terms: the row slope past 32 (one pair of points), the chunk penalty, the registers at RC = 8, L2 residency
of 64 rows of f32 partials (about 40 MB for gate/up beside the weight stream in 96 MB), and the card memory. The row
buffers scale with rows: about 0.31 GB at 64 if all of them double (*computed*). The chain's margin under eager at
1,024 is 0.038 GB, so the logits buffer must hold the last row only and the partials must be trimmed. This item fails
the VRAM rule unless that gate passes.
Cost: 16 to 24 agent-hours, one job of about 20 to 30 min, $0.60 to $0.90. Risk: medium to high.

4. 4E, CUTS F AND W, ONE JOB
----------------------------
What changes. F: q-norm, RoPE and the append done in the attention jobs' prologue; one phase and 36 waits fewer. W:
gate/up pairs claimed from a per-layer counter, on v9np's base (E1: v9np is x1.003 and x0.996 of v9, bit for bit).
Gain: signed -0.09 and -0.15 ms in the budget note; at the 41 % that 4E's versions realised, about -0.04 and -0.06 ms.
+2 % [0, +4 %] at every context (*estimated*).
Why nothing moves: same functions, same lane order; logits gated bit for bit.
Unmeasured terms: F puts 36 norm/RoPE works on the 8 attention jobs' critical path at short context (22 us mean work
today); W's gate/up rows slow under foreign traffic (E1, +178 us when 374 MB of prefetch ran under them), so the
claim's spread may cost what the jitter saved.
Cost: 10 to 14 agent-hours; one E1-shaped job, about 15 min, $0.45. Gate: E1's (logits bit for bit at 5 and 8,192,
tokens 64 of 64, ratio above 1.000 round by round at both contexts). Small but cheap in dollars; it does not pay a
day's work on today's record.

5. THE PASS'S FOLD AND UNIT STARTS
----------------------------------
What changes: the audit's E1 (a12fs, the fold step dealt over all CTAs, partial loads issued before the first add) and
E2 (a12h, the first sub-step's weight loads issued before the staging wait). Order of the adds unchanged.
Gain: at 1 row the audit priced folds 604 to 300-380 us and unit starts -130 to -260 us. At 32 rows, -0.2 ms [0, -0.5]
of the 10.84 (*estimated*): 6 ms off the 0.448 s at 1,024 (1.4 %), 50 ms off 7.8 s at 8,191 (0.7 %).
Cost: 6 to 8 agent-hours, $0.60 (one job, two arms beside a12ipg32). Easy, pays little for the prefill. It helps the
speculative profile more, which the operator set aside.

10. HOST OVERHEAD BETWEEN LAUNCHES
----------------------------------
Nothing to take. 4E makes 64 tokens a launch (served_decode.rs LAUNCH_TOKENS). A launch adds a ring upload of 256 B,
one cooperative launch, a ring readback and the counters' settle; the layer images are re-uploaded only when a cache
buffer moved. The cache grows by 256 positions, copying itself each time: at 4,096 about 1.4 ms a growth, 0.0055 ms a
token, 0.1 %; at 8,192 about 0.2 % (*computed*). The chat door decodes at 224 tok/s against 223.9 for the bench at the
same context (*measured*, n11), so no gap remains to explain.
