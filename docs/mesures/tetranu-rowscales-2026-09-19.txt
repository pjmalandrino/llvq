THE PREDICTION WAS WRONG BY TEN
--------------------------------
Signed in the prereg: +1.0 pp [-0.5, +2.5]. Revised DOWN to +0.4 [-0.8, +1.6]
in E7, before the scoring arm, on the flat training curve. Measured +4.30.

The revision made it worse, and its reasoning is the error this repository
documents five times over: a 7.5 % fall in KL on DCLM-edu was read as a small
lever. It bought 4.30 pp. KL on a calibration corpus and MMLU are not on the
same scale, and the author treated one as a proxy for the other.

Recorded because the file's rule is that a signed prediction is scored whether
it flatters or not. This one does not.

WHERE IT SITS AGAINST EVERYTHING ELSE
--------------------------------------
Under the law of mecanismes-perte-qualite (acc = 54.60 + 14.60 ln SNR), a gain
converts to a division of the noise power.

  trained row scales, measured here        +4.30 pp   noise / 1.80
  Spherical GPTQ, the paper's Table 9      +1.90      / 1.30
  the DCLM-edu corpus                      +1.58      / 1.24
  down_proj@12-23 at int4                  +1.38      / 1.21

Closing the 6.06 pp to the paper needs / 2.29. This one lever brings 1.80 of it,
and it costs no bit. It is the largest single lever this project has measured.

AND IT IS TWICE WHAT THE PAPER READS
-------------------------------------
The paper measures +2.1 pp for the same lever on the same model (Table 6, LLVQ
shape-gain 2 bit, 59.3 without fine-tuning against 60.9 with, and +2.1 is the
figure ROADMAP-QUALITY row 14 carries). We read +4.30 on Qwen3-4B.

Doubling a published effect is a claim that needs a mechanism, not a
celebration. Three readings, none settled here:

  (a) Our starting point is worse. Their 60.7 is a better object than our 54.64,
      so there is more to recover. A lever that removes a fixed fraction of the
      error returns more pp on a worse base.
  (b) Our scales were further from optimal to begin with. `gptq.rs:245` fixes
      them to the original row RMS BEFORE the loop, and the gain level is chosen
      on the block norm rather than by least squares on the retained direction.
      Two defects the paper does not have, both of which a trained scale repairs.
  (c) Their "+2.1" may not be this lever. The paper's recommended configuration
      is 0 gain bits, where the magnitude is carried by the direction code; ours
      has a gain bit. The same training may be doing different work.

Reading (b) is the one that would transfer, and it is testable: measure the
distance between our shipped row scales and the trained ones, per matrix, and
see whether the matrices that moved most are the ones whose gain rule disagrees
most (the disagreement is already measured in gain-desaccord-tetra-2026-09-14).
