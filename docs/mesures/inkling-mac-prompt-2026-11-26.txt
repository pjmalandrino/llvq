INKLING 2 MINI ON THE MAC, THE READ OF A LONG PROMPT, 2026-11-26

RESULT
  No step of this lot shortens the read on the Mac. A 9,000-token prompt is still read in 140.4 s, median of four
  runs of the unchanged path [134.15, 149.47], and a 2,000-token prompt in 23.69 s [23.27, 24.10] without one slow
  outlier at 42.83 s (*measured*, M3 Max, Metal, the long-prompt settings of the morning). The peak stays 11.42 GB.
  Two steps keep every bit, and neither moves the read beyond the spread of the runs:
    the experts' rows of a layer visit in a few launches (Metal twin of the CUDA batched read): 768 lattice launches
    for 2,000 tokens instead of 356,398, and 3,456 for 9,000 instead of 1,605,064; 131.22 s against 134.15 at 9,000
    tokens in the same window;
    the score bias built on the device (MSL twin of `attn_bias_rows`): 24.21 s against 24.10 at 2,000 tokens.
  The reason is in the profile and the kernel benches: on Metal the read is bound by the arithmetic of the 8-row
  lattice kernel (about 1 TFLOP/s) and by candle's attention products, not by the launches. The CUDA levers removed
  launches; there are almost none to remove here.
  Prereg: proofs/preregistration-inkling-mac-prompt-2026-11-26.md (commit a78a6ce, OpenTimestamps). Deviations:
  -ECARTS.md beside it. Raw files: inkling-mac-prompt-2026-11-26-brut/. Every figure is one run unless said.

WHAT THE EXPERTS COST (kernel benches on synthetic codes at the model's shapes, *measured*, kernels-*.log)
  One launch of the 8-row kernel `tv_tetra48_metal_lutdrf`, 1,152 x 2,304: 7.5 us at 1 row, 27.2 us at 8 rows,
  2.8 us more a row; 1.56 TFLOP/s at 8 rows. The decode of a block is shared by the rows; the rows themselves cost.
  One layer visit of a 512-token piece (4,096 pairs, 568 chunks), stage by stage, the device drained around each:
  gather and cast 1.34 ms, input rotation 0.60, gate and up 42.35, down rotation 0.33, down 20.52; the whole
  batched call 64.88 ms against 73.96 for the general path. The lattice products are 97 % of it.
  Two variants of the rows kernel, bit for bit too, did not pay and were removed:
    the chunk's rows staged in threadgroup memory: slower at every size, 45.09 ms for gate and up at 8 rows a
    threadgroup, 63.10 at 16, 68.92 at 32, against 38.23 unstaged in the same run (kernels-5.log);
    two output rows a simdgroup, each activation read once for both: 83.63 ms against 87.19 for gate and up in a
    window where the GPU ran slow (kernels-6.log); the end-to-end runs did not tell it from the plain kernel.
  So loads of the activation are not the bound; the multiply-adds and the decode are.
  candle's f32 product at the same shapes (plan item P3, decode once and multiply; mutants-2.log): 92.4 us for
  32 rows against 4 launches of 27.2 us for the rows kernel, 147.3 us for 256 rows (9.23 TFLOP/s) against 32
  launches. P3 pays only with many rows an expert a call. With pieces of 512 tokens an expert gets about 32 rows a
  visit, where the product alone costs what the lattice kernel costs, before the decode is paid. P3 was not built.

THE CODE (branch debit/inkling-mac-2026-11-26; none of it is on by default)
  `llvq-llm/src/inkling/attn_bias.rs`: the module covers CUDA and Metal; the MSL kernel writes
  `rel[clamp(d)] * keep + mask` with the product and the sum rounded apart (`fp contract(off)`, safe math).
  `layers::score_bias_host` is the host path, public as the reference. `inkrun --attn-bias device` on Metal.
  `llvq-llm/src/fused_metal.rs`: `moe_rows_lutdrf` (the body of `tv_tetra48_metal_lutdrf`, pointers from the slab,
  one chunk of at most 8 rows of one expert a threadgroup) and `moe_pairs_sum` (each token's rows times their
  weights, ascending expert order, from +0); `MetalRuntime::moe_rotate_pairs` runs the one-row path's `moe_rot_metal`
  over all the pairs. `ResidentExperts::forward_rows_batched` puts them together; `inkrun --moe-rows batch` on Metal.
  Tests: `tests/inkling_attn_bias.rs` (7 shapes up to 24 x 128 x 9,000, every bit, masks and signed zeros) and
  `tests/inkling_moe_rows.rs` (128 synthetic experts at the model's shapes, 2 to 1,100 tokens, skewed routings,
  small layers with tails, 1 to 12 experts a token: every f32 of the general path). Mutants, each seen red with its
  site counted (one site each): the window bound `<` made `<=` in the MSL bias (20 elements differ); `a * gs * inv`
  made `a * (gs * inv)` in `moe_rows_lutdrf`; the token sum run in descending expert order (mutants-1.log,
  mutants-2.log). On the code as committed: those two files, `inkling_wave2_moe` and `inkling_wave3_attn` pass
  (tests-final.log). `cargo clippy --all-targets` on llvq-llm and llvq-runtime with `metal`: zero warnings. The CUDA
  half (the bias module now serves both backends): `ops/check-linux-cuda.sh clippy -p llvq-llm -p llvq-runtime
  --features llvq-llm/cuda --all-targets`, zero warnings (linux-check.log). Not run on a card.

WHAT THE READING DOES NOT SAY
  That the batched experts or the device bias are useless on Metal: they are about 2 % at 9,000 tokens in the
  window where both arms ran, under the spread, and one probed pair read 117.86 s against 135.31 for arm B. A
  claim either way needs more rounds than this lot ran.
  Where the scores' 35 % at 9,000 tokens can go without changing values. They are candle's products and softmax
  over chunks of 128 rows; the bias is a small part (no gain from moving it). Shortening the key range a row sees
  changes the softmax's reduction, so it is class B (plan item P4).
  The server's first token: not re-read, the runtime did not change (E2).

WHAT IS LEFT, AND WHERE THE TIME IS
  The read is the lattice kernel's arithmetic (experts, about 45 to 55 %) and the attention products (scores and
  products, about 30 to 40 % at 9,000 tokens). On this Mac both are class B work: P3 needs more rows an expert a
  call than pieces of 512 give, so it trades against the peak; P4, one prompt-attention kernel with the bias and the
  mask inside, is what reaches the scores. P5 and P7 (read a prompt once a session) remove the wait without a kernel.
