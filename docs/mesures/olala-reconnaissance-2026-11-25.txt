OLALA V1.0 MINI 7A1B, A RECONNAISSANCE AT $0 BEFORE ANY ENCODE, 2026-11-25

RESULT
  Olala's weights are as easy for the lattice as Gaussian noise, once each row has its scale and the matrix its
  rotation: the repository's Tetra encoder, run data-free, leaves a relative error of 0.0846 to 0.0854 on 14 of the
  20 matrices tried and 0.0863 to 0.1016 on the other six, against 0.0848 and 0.0852 on Gaussian matrices of the
  same widths (*measured*).
  The routed experts hold 80.3 % of the parameters and are the least sensitive part: noise of that size on all of
  them costs +0.1045 of NLL, x1.110 of perplexity, top-1 agreement 80.9 % over 8,176 positions (*measured*, a noise
  proxy, not an encode). With the same noise added on the 0.80 B parameters of the large dense matrices, one window
  reads +0.82 where the experts alone read +0.07.
  Nothing here is a quantized file. No Hessian was collected, no GPTQ loop was run on the model, no row scale was
  trained, and no task was scored.

WHAT WAS RUN
  Not a preregistered gate. A reading with no threshold stated before it, in a cloud container: 4 vCPU, 15 GiB of
  RAM, no GPU, $0. Every script and raw output is in `olala-reconnaissance-2026-11-25-brut/`.
  Object: `OVHaiLLM/Olala-v1.0-mini-7A1B`, `model.safetensors`, 13,579,677,592 B, sha256 99a208e1...9356442
  (`model.sha256`), with the `config.json`, `modeling_olala.py` and tokenizer of the same repository on 2026-11-25.
  The repository revision was not recorded; the sha256 pins the weights.
  Forward: OVH's own `modeling_olala.py` through `transformers` 5.8, pure-PyTorch fallbacks (no `mamba_ssm`, no
  `scattermoe`), CPU. The weights load as bf16; each block is cast to float32 as the pass reaches it and dropped
  after it, so one process makes one pass (`fwd.py`). 2,048 tokens take about 60 s.
  Text: NOT WikiText and NOT dclm-edu. The text of OVH's technical report followed by `docs/how-it-works.md`,
  82,459 tokens, cut in windows of 512 from token 0. The NLL arms use 4 windows from offsets 0, 2,048, 4,096 and
  6,144. The routing census uses 24 windows from offset 2,048 and 24 from 14,336.
  Baseline NLL of the bf16 model on the four offsets: 2.1016, 1.8511, 3.0080, 3.1398.

2. WEIGHT STATISTICS (*measured*, `stats.out`, `stats2.out`; layers 0, 1, 5, 17, 19, 35 for the MoE, a few mixers)
  From layer 5 on, the expert matrices are close to Gaussian as stored: after rotation the excess kurtosis is 0.0
  to 0.4 (3.1 for `experts.weight` of layer 5) and the int4 g128 error 0.0099 to 0.0100, against 0.00996 for a
  Gaussian matrix. The median expert norm is 27 to 29 and the largest cosine between two experts of a layer 0.09
  to 0.12.
  Layers 0 and 1 are degenerate in rows, not in distribution. In `experts.weight` of layers 0 and 1, 1 % of the
  rows hold 99 to 100 % of the energy, the row norms span four decades, and two experts of a layer reach a cosine
  of 0.97 and 0.995. `mixer.in_proj` of layers 1 and 7 shows the same shape: median row norm 0.0009, largest 61 and
  28. Divided by its row norm and rotated, every one of these matrices is Gaussian again: kurtosis -0.01 to 0.01,
  int4 error 0.0099 to 0.0101, block-gain spread 0.142 to 0.146 against 0.140 at layer 17.
  Three output-side matrices keep a wide block-gain spread after rotation: `output_experts` of layers 0 and 1 (0.61
  and 0.75) and `mixer_proj` of layer 1 (1.02).

6. NOISE ARMS (*measured*, `collect.out`; the proxy is described here once)
  A matrix under noise r is replaced, row by row, by c.w + s.|w|.n with n a random unit vector orthogonal to the
  row, c = 1 - r/2: the row keeps its norm and takes a relative squared error r, the shape of the error of a
  retracted lattice code. r = 0.085 is the encoder's error of section 3; r = 0.010 is int4 g128's. The noise is
  isotropic: it ignores the Hessian, which is what GPTQ uses, so it is not the error of an encoded file.
  dNLL is the arm's loss minus the bf16 model's on the true token, mean over 2,044 positions a window, with its
  standard error over positions. Top-1 is the share of positions where both pick the same token. KL~ is
  KL(bf16 || arm) summed over the bf16 model's 64 likeliest tokens, which hold 88 to 93 % of the mass.

HOW TO READ IT
  The pooled expert arm is one noise draw a window, on 8,176 positions of technical text, in windows of 512. Its
  intervals are over positions and do not carry the draw. The dense arms are one window each.
  Public files of this model, measured by others on WikiText-2 with another protocol, for scale only: OVH's
  Q5_K_M, 5.99 GB, KLD 0.0218 and 92.2 % of top-1 against bf16; the community file of 3.72 GB, KLD 0.0587 and
  88.8 % (card of `ciselor59/Olala-v1.0-mini-7A1B-3.7GB-GGUF`). The int4-sized noise on the experts alone reads
  0.020 and 94.3 % here, on another text.
  The 53 GB of working files (weights, environment) stay in the container and are lost with it.
