1. WHY THE CAP IS 16
--------------------
The cap is a compile-time choice of accumulator width, not a register wall.
- p4t_unit_rc<RC, IP> (pers_4et_a10rc.cu) keeps each lane's accumulators as float acc[RC][4]: RC n8 tiles of the
  mma.m16n8k16 B operand, 8 RC activation rows. The MMA loop runs nt < RC under nt < NT, and p4t_store_rc stores the
  same RC tiles.
- pers_4et_a12_body and pers_4et_a12g_body refuse R > 8 RC through the abort flag (line 81 of pers_4et_a12.cu, 261 of
  pers_4et_a12g.cu). Every a12 symbol is instantiated at RC = 2: 16 rows.
- RC = 4 compiles at 384 threads. a10t384 is that build: 168 registers, 736 B local, 78,848 B shared, its 32-row guard
  bit for bit (*measured*, verify-tree-n10). a10 at 512 threads holds RC = 4 at 128 registers and 736 B local
  (*measured*, n9). The 736 to 744 B of local memory do not move with the register budget: an array kept in local
  memory, not a spill (n10's reading).
- RC = 2 was chosen for speed. RC = 4 costs 0.073 of a 4E token at R = 1 and 0.031 at R = 16 against RC = 2 in the same
  job at 512 threads (n9), and 0.088 and 0.075 at 384 threads across two jobs (n10 against n11): 0.14 to 0.39 ms a pass
  (*computed* from those *measured* c_R at 4.467 ms a token).
- The hard ceiling at 32 is P4T_RMAX = 32 (pers_4et.cu line 84). It sizes every row buffer (persist4e_cuda.rs lines
  1334 to 1361), the path rows u8 [R][32], the tickets, the slots, the move, and p4t_stream_cap, which computes the
  longest slice at 32 staged rows. chain_launches already takes 1 to 32 rows (spec_loop.rs line 318).

2. WHAT THE 9.5 MS OF A PASS IS MADE OF
--------------------------------------
Paid once a pass whatever R (the audits' ledger at R = 1, chat context):
- 4E's own work on the same words, 4.47 ms (*measured*, n11): the decode of every weight word, the int4 rows, 218 of
  the waits.
- The tree's overhead over 4E, 2.43 ms (*measured* total, spec-cout-fixe-audit): fold phases 0.60 ms over 145 grid
  waits, decode and MMA loop at x1.27 0.77 ms, unit starts and closes about 0.34 ms (*estimated*), f32 partial stores
  about 0.12 ms (*estimated*), 0.45 ms unexplained. 472 grid waits a pass against 218.
- Reading the served words in place, 2.36 ms (*measured*, spec-mem-m3: x1.27, 8.53 to 10.91 ms a pass).
- These sum to 9.26 ms against the 9.56 ms of an a12ip pass at R = 1 (*computed* against *measured*).
At 16 rows and 8,191 tokens the part outside attention reads 9.71 ms (*measured*, d1 stamps, head off): q/k/v 1.98, o
0.95, gate/up 3.55, down 1.56 (projections 8.04, 83 %), folds 0.70, prologues ln1 + ln2 + down input 0.81, norm and
RoPE 0.14, embedding 0.02.
What scales with R, read in the code:
- MMA work: NT = ceil(R / 8) n8 tiles, 3 HMMA and 6 LDS.32 of B a tile a sub-step. Tensor pipe at NT = 4, 0.64 ms a
  sub-partition a pass, against a projection wall of 8.04 ms (*computed*, the audit's 16.2 cycles an MMA).
- Staging: each unit stages R rows of its slice. 39 MB a row a pass from L2 (*computed* from the unit counts).
- f32 slice partials: 1.13 MB a row a layer written and folded, 40.5 MB a row a pass, L2-resident (19.9 MB for the
  gate/up phase at 32 rows, against a 96 MB L2).
- Folds: d_out / 4 pairs a row dealt over the grid's 54,528 threads. At 32 rows gate/up needs 77,824 pairs, two rounds;
  q/k/v, o and down stay at one.
- Prologues: row r on CTA r mod 142. Flat up to 142 rows.
- Attention: per row, a fixed amount a token across the prompt.
Measured slope: 0.091 ms a row from 1 to 16 (a12ip, head on every row, spec-mem-m3); 0.048 ms from 1 to 8 and 0.059
from 8 to 16 on a10 at RC = 4 (*computed* from n9's c_R). No knee where NT goes from 1 to 2. That is the basis for
pricing rows 17 to 32 at 0.05 to 0.12 ms each, widened upward for the second fold round and two more n8 tiles.

3. THREE DESIGNS
----------------
A. a12ipg at RC = 4, R = 32 (the one to build).
   Kernel: one more symbol, pers_4et_a12g_body<FORM, 4u, true> at 384 threads. No other line changes. The unit,
   p4t_store_rc, the folds, the prologues and p4g_attention are templated or loop over R already.
   Host: a TreeForm whose rmax() is 32 (persist4e_cuda.rs line 266 lists a12ipg at 16), its module text and install
   line, prefillbench and the chain driver given R = 32. chain_launches, tree_chain and the move take 32 today.
   Bits: equal to the 16-row chain. Row n of a projection is column n of the MMA's B and D, so no other row enters it;
   the slices S are fixed by the plan [8, 10, 8, 20, 6], never by R; each tile folds its slices in order 0 .. S - 1.
   Attention keeps each row's tiles, units, splits and fold order, all functions of the row's length. A position below
   pos0 is read from the cache and one at or above from the tree slot: the same binary16 bits, since the move copies
   them. Under a12ipg a split is shared when it ends at or below pos0, so moving pos0 from a multiple of 16 to a
   multiple of 32 changes which splits run as group jobs. D1 measured that a group job and a row job give the same
   bits (identity HOLDS at 1,024, 4,096 and 8,191).
   Registers: 168 to 170 at 384 threads (*estimated* from a10t384's 168 at RC = 4). Local memory may rise above 744 B
   if the in-place loads and the grouped attention push it past 170: the Mac compile probe below reads it.
   Shared memory: unchanged, 93,568 B. p4g_area_floats at 32 staged rows is 18,624 floats, the attention's bound. The
   down's 11 sub-steps at 32 rows stage 8,576 floats plus 5,376 of A tiles, under the area (*computed*).
   Card bytes: none added. Every row buffer is already sized for P4T_RMAX = 32 rows, 0.153 GB (*measured*, d1 loader),
   so the phase peaks stay 2.318 / 2.855 / 3.560 GB at 1,024 / 4,096 / 8,191 (*measured* for R = 16, *computed*
   unchanged), under the eager 2.356 and 5.946.
   Causal order inside the pass: the norm/RoPE phase writes every row's K and V into its tree slot, a grid wait
   follows, and attention row r reads positions pos0 .. pos0 + r from slots path[r][0 .. r]. 32 rows use slots 0 to 31,
   which exist.
B. R = 64 by raising P4T_RMAX. Stops at shared memory.
   With the slices fixed, a unit stages its whole slice for all rows before its loop. The down's 11 sub-steps at 64
   rows need 64 x 268 = 17,152 floats, plus 5,376 floats of A tiles and 128 of maxima: 90.6 KB of area, 109.7 KB with
   the 19.1 KB of header and tables, against 101,376 B (*computed*). At 8 warps it is 102.5 KB, still over. Fewer
   slices would fit but change the fold order, so the bits. Staging in chunks (a2's double-buffered unit) would fit and
   keep the bits, but that unit was replaced by the stream unit for speed, and P4T_RMAX = 64 also changes path rows,
   chain_path's type and every arm's buffers. Not built here; no estimate is signed for it.
C. Accumulators in shared memory, any R. Stops at shared memory too.
   The f32 values round-trip exactly, so loading C from shared before each MMA and storing D after keeps the bits. A
   warp's 16 output rows times 64 activation rows are 4 KB, 48 KB for 12 warps, on top of the staged slice. Registers
   were never the binding limit (section 1); the staged activation is, and C does not shrink it.
A wider launch (512 threads at 128 registers) is a10's measured geometry: at 32 rows it reads like 384 threads (c_1
1.744 against 1.755 at the same plan, n10). It is the fallback if A spills at 384, with the stream cap still at 14
sub-steps (*computed*, 16 warps).

4. ESTIMATE FOR DESIGN A (*estimated*, signed form)
---------------------------------------------------
Model: first token = passes x T_rest(R) + attention, with attention a token unchanged by R.
- T_rest(16) = 9.71 ms (*measured*, stamped).
- T_rest(32) = 9.71 + RC penalty [0.14, 0.39] + 16 rows x [0.05, 0.12] = 11.2 ms [10.7, 12.0].
- Attention over a prompt, from the measured first tokens of a12ipg less 64 / 256 / 512 x 9.71 ms: 0.110 / 1.418 /
  5.290 s at 1,024 / 4,096 / 8,191 (*computed*). Taken at x[0.95, 1.00] at R = 32: the same jobs and bytes a row, half
  the attention phases.
Pass at R = 32: short context, head off, 12.7 ms [11.7, 14.0] (attention 1 to 2 ms, from about 0.03 to 0.06 ms a row
below 256 positions). At 8,191: 44.8 ms [42.6, 45.6], attention 33.6 of it (twice d1's 16.8).
  prompt   passes   rest, s                attention, s     first token, s          today a12ipg   gain
  1,024    32       0.359 [0.341, 0.385]   0.110            0.47 [0.45, 0.50]       0.731          x1.56
  4,096    128      1.435 [1.363, 1.539]   1.418            2.85 [2.71, 2.96]       3.904          x1.37
  8,191    256      2.870 [2.726, 3.077]   5.290            8.16 [7.76, 8.37]       10.262         x1.26
At 1,024 a12ip's attention is faster (0.081 s): 0.44 s [0.42, 0.47] if the arm switches by length.
With the attention change of section 5 (groups of 6), attention x[0.85, 1.00]: 2.67 s [2.57, 2.96] at 4,096 and 7.47 s
[7.22, 8.37] at 8,191.
Measured ratios relied on: the RC penalty (n9, n10 against n11); the 1-to-16 row slope (spec-mem-m3, n9); d1's 9.71 ms
rest and the attention left by subtraction. Not measured: the cost of rows 17 to 32, and whether RC = 4 on the
in-place, grouped body keeps a10t384's registers. Earlier estimates on this pass were optimistic where they priced
work nobody had timed: D1's attention (6 to 10 ms signed, 16.8 measured) and M3's in-place speed. The terms above that
are measured carry most of the total; the 16 x [0.05, 0.12] term is the one at risk.
What makes it wrong: a spill at RC = 4 (the sub-step loop then touches local memory, possibly +1 ms or more); a row
slope above 0.15 ms past 16, which no code term predicts; L2 eviction of the partials when weights and 32-row partials
share it.

5. THE 16.8 MS ATTENTION OF A12IPG AT 8K
----------------------------------------
Most of it is per-row scalar arithmetic, not bytes.
Split under the model step = F + rows x V, with step counts from the code (*computed*):
- a12ip at pos0 8,144: 4,096 jobs a layer of 8 steps, 230.8 one-row steps a CTA a layer, 34.43 ms: 4.14 us a step.
- a12ipg: 992 group jobs and 128 row jobs a layer, 55.9 four-row and 7.2 one-row steps a CTA, 16.76 ms.
- Solving: F = 2.92 us a step, V = 1.22 us a row-step. Per pass: V terms 10.1 ms, F terms 6.6 ms (*computed*).
- F matches the bytes: 142 CTAs x 16 KB a step at 864 GB/s is 2.69 us, and 5.28 GB a pass. The two terms add rather
  than overlap.
What V is made of, per call of pers_attn_tile (8 positions, one row, two query heads a warp), read in the code:
- 128 binary16 widenings (h2f on 8 K and 8 V vectors of 8), redone for each row of the group on the same staged bytes;
- 64 FFMA for QK, 32 SHFL with 32 dependent FADD for the reductions, 64 FFMA for PV;
- 9 software ex2 (pers_ex2: FRND, F2I and a 6-FMA chain each);
- 16 LDS.128 of K and V, 4 of q, 4 of the unit state and 2 STS.128 back.
About 550 warp instructions a call, 9.44 M calls a pass: 5.2 G instructions, 3.8 ms at full issue on 142 SMs
(*computed*).
The measured V is 2.6 times that. The suspect to check first: if ptxas lowers cvt.f32.f16 to F2F (16 results a clock
an SM on sm_89) rather than HADD2.F32 on the FMA pipe, the widenings alone are about 7 ms a pass (*computed*). The SASS
of the a12ipg module answers it on the Mac at $0. Widening is exact, so a bit-exact replacement exists, but it is a
separate study.
Groups above 4: P4G_RG = 5 at two stages needs 103,936 B (the file's own count). Staging half a step (2 of 4 units, 8
KB a stage) or a one-stage ring frees 16 KB: 16,384 + 6 x 10,368 + 256 B of maxima = 78,848 B of area,
97,920 B with header and tables: RG = 6 fits, RG = 7 does not. With q held in registers instead of shared (8,320 B a
row), RG = 7 fits under half-step staging, RG = 8 does not (102,272 B). Larger groups only shrink F: at RG = 6 about
-2.2 ms of 16.8 at 8k (*estimated*, [0, -2.5]); the 10 ms of V stay.

6. COST AND GATES OF DESIGN A
-----------------------------
Build: about 6 agent-hours (*estimated*): symbol and TreeForm 1 h, install and prefillbench 1.5 h, host twin and
mutants 2 h, compile probe 0.5 h, prereg and job script 1 h.
Mac gates ($0, after the operator's go, one heavy job at a time):
- Compile probe (ops/tetra-sass-probe.sh, sm_89): the RC = 4 a12ipg symbol at most 170 registers and at most 744 B
  local. Above that, rebuild at 512 threads before any card time.
- Host twin: a chain prefill at R = 32 equals R = 16 and R = 1 bit for bit (each layer's K and V, the last row's
  logits), on a prompt whose length is not a multiple of 32 and that crosses at least two 256-position splits, so the
  group and row jobs fall differently at 16 and 32. The existing twins (R = 16 and R = 5 equal R = 1) still pass.
- Mutants, each must kill the twin: n8 tiles 2 and 3 stored at nt 0 and 1's columns; acc of tiles 2 and 3 not zeroed;
  the row cap left at 16 in the guard (the launch must abort, not run 16 rows). Count the sites each mutant changes.
Card: one l40sx1 job like d1, a12ipg at 32 against a12ipg at 16 in one process, identity at every length, first token
round by round, the 8k phase split, peak VRAM, the 47 texts. About 19 to 21 min, $0.57 to $0.63 (*estimated* from d1's
19 min and $0.57).
