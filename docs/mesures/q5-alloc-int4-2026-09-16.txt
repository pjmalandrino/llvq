================================================================================

l40sx1 alone, and the guard is for SPEED ratios, which an MMLU count is not).
68 min billed, 3.12 $ — against the 2.29 $ its 50m timeout was announced to cap.
The timeout does not cut on the minute; the run was killed on its last command

⚠️ THIS IS AN EXPLORATION, NOT A RESULT
-----------------------------------------
Six arms were compared and the intervals below are INDIVIDUAL 95 % intervals
with no correction for that multiplicity. They select a candidate. They
establish nothing, and the best of six is biased upward by construction.
The confirmation is preregistered separately
(proofs/preregistration-oproj-int4-full-2026-09-16.md).

THE SETUP
----------
Seven arms of the SAME served Q5 file: the shipped object, then one arm per
projection type taken back from the checkpoint at int4 g128. `LLVQ_RESTORE_Q4`
reads the CHECKPOINT tensor and round-trips it through the affine quantizer
(`sealed.rs:545-551`), so it measures the information cost of four bits — it
does not re-quantize the already-degraded Tetra weights, which would measure
nothing. Verified in source before the launch.

Constant file is NOT constant memory: every arm reconstructs densely, so the
engine's VRAM says nothing about the served cost. The surcharge is computed on
the 4B's shapes, in b/param of the whole model (hard rule 6).

TWO STATISTICS, AND THEY ARE NOT THE SAME
-------------------------------------------
`mmlupair` prints both and labels which one McNemar tests. At limit=40 the
sample is flat at 40 a subject while populations are not, so the two diverge:

  arm          Δ pooled (what McNemar tests)   Δ stratified (published)
  gate_proj              +2.33                        +0.99
  up_proj                +2.20                        +1.86
  o_proj                 +2.20                        +2.76
  down_proj              +4.13                        +3.79

So gate_proj's p = 0.0056 tests +2.33, not the +0.97 of the table, and there is
no contradiction with an interval that spans zero — the two describe different
quantities. A first reading of this run quoted McNemar p-values against
stratified deltas and ranked the arms by Bonferroni on that mismatch. The table
above is ranked on the stratified interval alone, which is the one that belongs
with the published number.

WHAT THE f16 TABLE DOES NOT SAY
---------------------------------
M2 (2026-09-02) measured the same types restored at f16: gate +5.18, up +4.94,
v +4.48, down +2.96, o +2.35, k +2.09, q +1.85 pp. The int4 order is different —
gate first at f16 and last here, down fourth at f16 and first here. **Sensitivity
to total quantization does not predict the gain of an intermediate precision.**

Two arms read above their f16 figure. That is not an impossibility: f16 is not a
mathematical upper bound on int4 in a partially quantized model, where errors
interact, and M2 ran against a different object with a different reference. The
two tables are not on a common scale.

WHAT IS SELECTED
-----------------
`o_proj`: +2.77 pp for +0.1954 b/param, which lands the model at 2.9599 under a
b_max of 3.00. It is the only arm that is both the largest measured gain in
budget and inside it.

`down_proj` measured more, +3.79, and is over budget as a whole type. It is NOT
dropped: restoring a subset of its layers is a lead of its own, and the gain
should not be assumed proportional to the number of layers restored.

`k_proj` has the best apparent yield per bit, 26.6 pp per b/param, and the
widest interval. That makes its estimate IMPRECISE, not lucky: nothing here
shows its yield is an artefact, only that 2,280 questions cannot resolve it.

WHAT IT ANSWERS
----------------
Yes: a lack of precision, in specific places, is part of what caps Tetra.
down_proj alone is worth almost four points. The ceiling is not reached.
