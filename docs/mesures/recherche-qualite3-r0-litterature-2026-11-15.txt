RECHERCHE QUALITE 3, STEP R0: LITERATURE ON LOST CONTRAST (N6) AND DEPTH-SPLIT LOSS (N7)
Date: 2026-11-15. Author: literature agent (Opus). No code run, no model loaded, no job launched, nothing committed.

Method. Web search plus the arXiv abstract page or HTML of each paper. "abstract only" means only the abstract page
was read. "partial HTML" means the HTML was read up to about 100,000 characters, so appendices may be missing. Every
figure is *reported* by the paper it comes from. Nothing here was re-measured. "Claims" means the authors say it and
this note did not see the evidence. "Inference" means this note's own reasoning, not a finding of the paper.

Not re-covered here, as the brief asked: GPTQ, AWQ, QuIP, QuIP#, QTIP, LLVQ, GuidedQuant, YAQA, spherical feedback,
lattice-step distillation. WaterSIC and Wu et al. come back only for points the project had not yet noted.

======================================================================================================================
A. QUIET DIRECTIONS
======================================================================================================================

A1. The theory says the layer objective is right to drop quiet directions.
- WaterSIC, Lifar et al., arXiv 2603.04956, 2026, ICML 2026 spotlight. Partial HTML.
  Measured: Llama-3.2-1B and Qwen3-8B, 1 to 4 bits, entropy-coded. Reported Qwen3-8B WikiText-2 ppl at
  2.125 / 3.125 / 4.125 bits: WaterSIC 11.37 / 10.03 / 9.79, Huffman-GPTQ 13.97 / 10.34 / 9.81, plain GPTQ 57.51 /
  12.77 / 10.10 (FP16 9.73). Llama-3.2-1B at 2 bits: WaterSIC 16.19, QTIP 18.67 at 2.02 bits.
  New point for this project: the reverse water-filling bound gives component i the rate 0.5 log max(1, s2 l_i / t).
  Any input eigen-direction with s2 l_i below the water level t gets zero rate and keeps its full variance as error.
  So under the layer MSE weighted by the input covariance, discarding quiet directions is the optimum, not a defect.
  The paper indexes GPTQ's gap by the AM/GM ratio of the squared Cholesky diagonal. It does not analyse error per
  eigen-direction, and it describes no fixed-rate variant.
- Ordentlich and Polyanskiy, "High-Rate Quantized Matrix Multiplication II", arXiv 2605.13768, 2026. Abstract only.
  Claims: for Llama-3-8B with its real activation covariance, GPTQ after a random rotation is within 0.1 bit of
  WaterSIC, depending on layer type, in the high-rate regime. Part I (arXiv 2601.17187) warns that the high-rate
  analysis can mislead below about 2 bits per entry (reported in search snippets, not opened).
  Inference: under the same objective, a rotated GPTQ loop leaves little rate to reallocate at high rate. The low-rate
  case near 2.7 bits is not covered.

A2. Measured evidence that GPTQ-family error lands in low-energy input directions: not found.
- No paper found projects the quantization error of a weight-quantized LLM onto the principal directions of the
  residual stream or of the layer input, and reports SNR per component. The closest results follow.
- Chen et al., "Why Does Post-Training Quantization Work?", arXiv 2609.11716, 2026. Partial HTML. Qwen3-4B, 8B,
  32B, 30B-A3B, OLMo3 and others, NVFP4 RTN weight-only. Reported on Qwen3-32B: the angle accounts for 88.9 to 98.7 %
  of the squared relative error of the final hidden state, which rotates about 12.5 deg while its norm moves 3.7 %.
  The new error each block adds opposes the inherited error in 82.5 % of blocks, and removing that counteraction in
  blocks 17 to 48 raises the final relative error 2.94 times. Nothing in the part read decomposes the error by
  principal direction.
- Zhou et al., "Quantization Degradation in LLMs: A Signal-Noise Perspective", arXiv 2608.08188, 2026. Partial HTML.
  Ten models including Qwen3 0.6B to 14B. RTN, GPTQ, AWQ and SpQR at W8 to W2. The source SNR splits into M (weight
  magnitude over error), S (signal energy along the weight) and N (error energy along task activations). Reported:
  GSM8K has the lowest source SNR of the three tasks probed, mainly through a larger N, which is stronger alignment of
  the error with the task's activations. Propagated error dominates source error at every depth stage. Reported
  retention at W3 runs from 30.6 % (Qwen3-0.6B) to 87.9 % (Qwen3-14B).
  Relevance: this is the only paper found that makes the alignment of the error with task-specific activations a
  measured term. It works per channel, not per principal direction of the residual stream.
- Nearby evidence from KV-cache coding: arXiv 2511.01815 ("KV Cache Transform Coding") orders PCA components by
  variance and has a DP give zero bits to many trailing components (snippet only). This is KV, not weights.
- FluxBin, arXiv 2608.15602 (snippet only), claims that with few samples the Hessian captures only the principal
  directions, and that GPTQ then overfits the calibration set. Keras and AWS documentation repeat the GPTQ calibration
  overfit as folklore. No measurement per direction was found.

A3. Interpretability: low-variance residual directions carry causal signal.
- Stolfo, Wu, Gurnee, Belinkov, Song, Sachan, Nanda, "Confidence Regulation Neurons", arXiv 2406.16254, NeurIPS 2024.
  Abstract only. Models up to 7B. Reported: final-layer "entropy neurons" have large weight norms and write into an
  effective null space of the unembedding, made of directions with tiny singular values. They change the residual
  norm,
  so the final LayerNorm rescales the logits and sets confidence. They have almost no direct effect on the logits.
  Relevance: here a direction with almost no readout controls the scale of every logit. This is a known mechanism by
  which noise in a low-readout subspace can move the contrast between options. Nobody has tested it under quantization
  (searched, not found).
- Guda, "Geometric and Behavioral Stratification in Transformer Residual Streams", arXiv 2608.12447, 2026,
  single author, not peer reviewed. Abstract only. 18 models, 7B to 120B. Claims that a low-variance,
  weakly-readout-aligned complement of the residual stream is causally and temporally load-bearing. Claims that the
  prediction direction is nearly orthogonal to the principal variance axes. The abstract gives no effect size.
- Salfati, "Variance Is Not Importance", arXiv 2604.20682, 2026, single author. Abstract only. GPT-2 and Mistral-7B.
  Reported: high-variance directions are about 96 % uncorrelated (CCA) with predictive directions. Projecting onto
  them
  keeps over 90 % of the variance and destroys perplexity.
- Ghazouani, "Variance Geometry is Not Functional Geometry", SSRN 6886378 (snippet only). Claims that a perturbation
  along the top Fisher direction costs 15 to 100 times more KL than one along the top covariance direction.
  Caution: the last three are single-author preprints, and only the Stolfo paper is peer reviewed.

A4. Objectives that reshape the Hessian weighting.
- OAC, Edalati et al., arXiv 2405.15025, AAAI 2025. Abstract only. Replaces the layer Hessian with an approximate
  output cross-entropy Hessian. Claims large gains at 2 bits and binary over SpQR and BiLLM. v2 claims it improves
  other Hessian-based PTQ methods when plugged in. No figure was read.
- HeRo-Q, Zhang et al., arXiv 2601.21626, 2026. Abstract plus search snippets. Learns a rotation-compression matrix
  that lowers the largest Hessian eigenvalue, with a diagonal smoothing by Hessian-diagonal powers alpha/2. Reported
  GSM8K 70.15 % for Llama-3-8B at W3A16. This attenuates high-curvature directions. It does not protect quiet ones.
- ARHQ, Wang, Sun, Sakaguchi, arXiv 2605.00140, 2026. Abstract only. Builds the input Hessian from activation
  quantization residuals instead of X^T X, and moves error-sensitive directions into a low-rank high-precision branch.
  Qwen3-4B-Thinking, W-A quantization, ZebraLogic. The abstract gives no numbers.
- Chen and Yeh, "Selecting GPTQ Damping by Nested-Ridge Leave-One-Out", Zenodo 23015545, v5, 2026-09-28, not peer
  reviewed. Treats damping as a ridge prior chosen by leave-one-out from the Cholesky factor. Reported: the best fixed
  multiplier runs from about 0.0064 to 0.77 depending on rows over width. Median excess cost: LOOCV 0.0048, 5 % rule
  0.0401, 1 % default 0.0843. Qwen3.5-0.8B at 4 bits: lower WikiText-2 ppl than the 1 % default in all three settings.
  Inference: damping is the one existing knob that caps GPTQ's 1/l weight error along quiet directions. This paper
  tunes it for layer MSE, not for task signal.
- Survey "Transforms for LLM Quantization: The Great Inversion", arXiv 2608.25188, 2026. Partial HTML. Claims that
  rotations cannot change the spectrum of the input second moment and that whitening is required to go further. It
  restates reverse water-filling as background. No measurement on this point in the part read.

A5. Noise shaping in weight quantization.
- SDQ-LLM, arXiv 2510.03275, 2025 (snippet only): sigma-delta with high-pass noise transfer, at 1 bit. The shaping is
  along the sequence of weights, not along activation eigen-directions.
- FBQuant, arXiv 2501.16385, 2025 (snippet only): feedback quantization. Not checked in detail.
- Noise shaping toward the input directions a downstream task does not read: not found.

======================================================================================================================
B. CONTRAST OR LOGIT SHRINK
======================================================================================================================

B1. Wu, Dhiman, Koshiyama, arXiv 2608.06564, v5 2026-10-05. HTML v2 read. Points the project had not noted:
- Reported BIC test: in damaged cells (fitted c < 0.70) the multiplicative model m' = c m + b wins 482 of 569. It wins
  all 332 damaged cells in the core families. Constant additive noise and drift-only models win none. Margin-scaled
  noise wins 87 cells, all outside the core families.
- Reported slopes on Qwen3-4B. GPTQ (tool plus general): 0.98 / 0.79 / 0.37 at w4 / w3 / w2. RTN whether-to-call:
  0.83 / 0.13 / 0.00 at 4 / 3 / 2 bits. GGUF tool: 1.00 / 0.81 / 0.59 for q4_k_m / iq3_xxs / iq2_m. Code tokens keep
  0.87 at 3 bits where whether-to-call keeps 0.13.
- Corrections: the paper notes that a global affine map of the logits cannot flip a decision, so it ran no temperature
  test. Label-free bias subtraction: median recovery 0.0 over 554 damaged cells. An oracle per-item offset recovers a
  median 0.325 of flips. One extra bit recovers a median 0.305, and nothing they tried beat it.
- Mechanism: none claimed. The residual-stream deviation grows by 1.02 to 1.07 per layer at every bit width. The
  collapse tracks the 90th-percentile input of the MLP down-projection, which is a predictor, not a cause. They name
  "decorrelation", meaning the standardized margin losing correlation with its clean value, as an open candidate.
- Reported generation-side point: at 3-bit RTN, three of five models lose more completed tool calls than correct tool
  selections (400 BFCL tasks).

B2. Shrink separated from noise.
- Wu et al. separate a multiplicative slope from additive noise statistically (B1). They do not locate either in
  depth.
- Chen et al., arXiv 2609.11716 (A2): the final hidden error is about 89 to 99 % rotation and 3.7 % norm change on
  Qwen3-32B NVFP4.
  Inference, not stated by either paper: an RMSNorm before the LM head renormalizes the hidden state, so added noise
  orthogonal to the signal lowers the signal's projection at fixed norm. A noisy hidden state then reads as a margin
  slope below one. This would turn added noise into the multiplicative shrink Wu et al. observe. It predicts the slope
  is fixed by the noise-to-signal energy at the final norm, and that no gain correction after the norm restores a
  flip.
- Zhou et al., arXiv 2608.08188 (A2), define a per-block gain G_l (propagated over accumulated NSR). They report
  sustained amplification in the middle layers of small Qwen3 models and mild attenuation in large ones. G_l values
  appear only in a figure.

B3. Temperature and confidence calibration after quantization.
- "Quantized Can Still Be Calibrated" (authors not checked), ACL 2025 (aclanthology 2025.acl-long.1473, snippet
  only). Reported: post-hoc calibration lowers calibration error for GPTQ from 0.85 to 0.74 and for BnB from 0.86 to
  0.21. This is confidence calibration on accuracy, and it cannot fix rank flips.
- A RoPE-quantization preprint, arXiv 2510.00028 (snippet only), reports an optimal temperature of 0.8 to 2.2
  depending
  on the method. Not verified.
- Logit-scale collapse linked to a specific component under quantization: not found.

======================================================================================================================
C. GENERATION AGAINST ONE-TOKEN TASKS
======================================================================================================================

C1. Generation or reasoning degrades more than MCQ or ppl: found, with mixed strength.
- LFQ, Lee, Yang, Choi, Yang, arXiv 2605.29756, 2026, reported accepted at ICML 2026. Partial HTML. Block-wise PTQ
  (FlexRound, OmniQuant, Block-AP) at W4 and W3g128 on Qwen2.5-7B and 14B Instruct, R1-distilled Llama-8B,
  L1-Qwen-7B, Qwen3-30B-A3B and Llama-3.1-8B. Reported Llama-3.1-8B FlexRound W4 against BF16: MMLU -2.15, IFEval
  -4.25,
  GSM8K -3.64. Reported L1-Qwen-7B W4: AIME'24 greedy -16.67 (30 problems, one problem is 3.3 points).
  The paper claims MSE-based block PTQ keeps ppl and MCQ and loses long generation. Its evidence is per benchmark, not
  per layer.
- Li et al., "Quantization Meets Reasoning", arXiv 2505.11574, v4 2026-01. Abstract plus snippets. Qwen and Llama,
  0.5B to 7B, AWQ, GPTQ, SmoothQuant. Snippet: GSM8K about -7.16 %, MATH about -15.2 %, AIME about -100 %, with MMLU
  unchanged. The abstract says PTQ adds method and execution errors more than conceptual errors, and that the first
  faulty step comes early in a solution and cascades to the answer. Claims 332 targeted examples and 3 to 5 GPU
  minutes of tuning bring 4-bit math back toward FP. Numbers not opened.
- Liu et al., "Quantization Hurts Reasoning?", arXiv 2504.04823, COLM 2025. Abstract only. R1-distilled Qwen and Llama
  1.5B to 70B, QwQ-32B, Qwen3-8B. Claims W4A16 and W8A8 are lossless on reasoning and lower widths are risky. Claims
  output length does not grow. Snippet: harder tasks lose up to 4 times more than GSM8K.
- Lian et al., "Quantization Inflates Reasoning", arXiv 2606.25519, 2026. Abstract only. Claims INT4 and INT3 keep
  accuracy but lengthen chains of thought, with more steps and repetition. No numbers on the page. It contradicts
  Liu et al. on length.
- Jang et al., "Flat Score, Amplified Failures", arXiv 2607.27275, 2026. Abstract only. tau2-bench, 16, 8 and 4 bit.
  Reported: the score does not move significantly, while the existing failure (tool-name hallucination) rises up to
  2.5 times. Shrinking the error budget from ten to two exposes a 17-point gap.
- Counter-evidence: IJCAI 2025 study (ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/0902, snippet only) reports that GSM8K and MMLU did
  not
  consistently show the largest drops under AWQ, with small models (2B to 7B) hit hardest on GSM8K.

C2. Depth: which blocks hurt generation.
- Zhang et al., "When Reasoning Meets Compression", arXiv 2504.02010, ICLR 2026. Partial HTML. R1-distilled Llama-8B
  and Qwen-7B, AWQ, GPTQ, GPTAQ and ANY at 3 and 4 bits. Reported: quantizing only the final-layer MLP up_proj to 3
  bits
  (0.7 % of weights) lowers average reasoning accuracy by 16.3 %. Keeping the final-layer MLP in 16 bits inside 3-bit
  AWQ (about 2 % of weights) raises the average from 46.0 to 52.57. Reported: AWQ and GPTQ compress final-layer
  modules
  the most. Gate projections in the middle layers of Llama-8B and the early layers of Qwen-7B are also
  over-compressed.
  Benchmarks: AIME 2024, FOLIO, Temporal Sequences, MuSiQue. No MMLU arm.
- Chang, Zhang, Thomason, Jia, "Why Do Some Inputs Break Low-Bit LLM Quantization?", arXiv 2506.12044, EMNLP 2025.
  Abstract only. 3 and 4 bit weight-only, 7B to 70B. Reported: errors correlate at 0.82 on average across 50 method
  pairs on FineWeb. The full-precision residual-stream norm predicts which examples will break. Patching and early
  exit
  show that the high-error examples depend on precise residual activations in the late layers. Measured on NLL, not on
  generation tasks.
- Wang et al., TASA, arXiv 2607.00908, 2026. Abstract only. Llama-3-8B and Qwen2.5-7B, 3.5 bits average. Reported:
  per-layer perplexity sensitivity and reasoning sensitivity rank with Kendall tau about 0. Reported: more than
  +20 GSM8K points over the best W3 baseline on Llama-3-8B. Which layers matter for math is not on the abstract page.
- Mixed-precision practice (mlx-optiq blog, not peer reviewed) reports last-block sensitivity up to 6.8 times the
  median layer. A per-layer 4/8-bit allocation scores 51 % GSM8K against 34 % for uniform 4-bit. Low weight.
- Measured layer-by-layer restoration that separates MCQ from generation, as N7 does: not found.

C3. Error over decoded tokens.
- Divergent Token Metrics, Deiseroth et al., arXiv 2311.01544, 2023/2024. Snippets. Uses the first token where greedy
  output departs from the base model as the compression metric. Reported: 25 % of Llama-2 attention components prune
  past 90 %. Claims it gives per-component choices that perplexity misses.
- A TMLR 2026 paper (arXiv 2609.26621, snippet only) reports a median first divergence at 34 to 50 tokens between
  precisions, with 44 % diverging in the first 30 tokens. Once trajectories differ, they stay different.
- Mix-Quant, Lu et al., arXiv 2605.20315, 2026. Abstract only. NVFP4 prefill with BF16 decode. Claims full-path
  quantization degrades agentic tasks while quantized prefill costs little. No split figure on the page.
- A statistically-lossless quantization paper, arXiv 2605.02404 (snippet only), uses teacher forcing because one
  divergent token shifts the whole continuation.
- Quantization-specific exposure-bias analyses: not found.

======================================================================================================================
D. LOCATING GENERATION LOSS FROM THE ORIGINAL MODEL ALONE
======================================================================================================================

- Zhang et al., arXiv 2504.02010 (C2): steering-vector difference-of-means plus attribution patching on the FP model,
  per linear module, for four labelled reasoning behaviours (120 instances labelled by GPT-4o). This produced the
  final-layer up_proj finding that quantization then confirmed. This is the closest existing method for D.
- Chang et al., arXiv 2506.12044 (C2): the FP residual-stream norm predicts per-example quantization error.
- Stolfo, Belinkov, Sachan, arXiv 2305.15054, EMNLP 2023. Abstract only. Causal mediation on four models, 2.8B to 7B,
  on arithmetic queries. Claims that attention moves operand information to the last token, and that a set of MLPs
  then writes the result into the residual stream (search summaries say late MLPs). No layer numbers read.
- Wiegreffe et al., "Answer, Assemble, Ace", arXiv 2407.15018, ICLR 2025 spotlight. Abstract only. Three model
  families. Reported: the MCQ answer symbol is chosen in a few middle layers by a sparse set of attention heads, and
  later layers only raise its probability.
- Lad, Lee, Gurnee, Tegmark, "Stages of Inference?", arXiv 2406.19384, v3 2025. Abstract only. Reported: models keep
  72 to 95 % of top-1 accuracy after a layer is deleted or swapped, except at the first and last layers. The last
  stage
  is "residual sharpening", where irrelevant features are suppressed to finalize the distribution.
- Logit lens on quantized models: arXiv 2505.15353 (KL scale, snippet only) treats the network up to each layer as a
  model through the logit lens, with 8-bit and 4-bit models. A 2026 survey calls logit-lens work on quantized models
  "preliminary" (snippet only).
- Caution from bit-width search: ScaleBITS, arXiv 2602.17698 (snippet only), reports that gradients taken at the
  quantized model beat gradients taken at the FP model for aggressive quantization. An FP-only predictor may
  underestimate sensitivity at about 2.7 bits.
- A logit lens or tuned lens on generated tokens, used to predict where a weight perturbation costs GSM8K: not found.

======================================================================================================================
E. IS EACH CANDIDATE IDEA PUBLISHED?
======================================================================================================================

E1. Protect quiet input directions in the encoder objective.
- Verdict: NOT FOUND as stated. Nobody found reweights the GPTQ or lattice objective to cap error on the low-variance
  subspace of the input, or of the residual stream at the answer position.
- Neighbours found: OAC (output CE Hessian, claims 2-bit gains, abstract only). GuidedQuant (known, failed here).
  HeRo-Q (attenuates the top of the spectrum, the opposite end). ARHQ (residual Hessian plus low-rank branch, no
  numbers). Damping by LOOCV (Chen and Yeh, layer-MSE target, small model).
- Theory against: reverse water-filling (WaterSIC, Ordentlich and Polyanskiy) says the layer optimum gives quiet
  directions zero rate. Protecting them only helps if the true loss weights them more than X^T X does. That is the
  claim to prove, and the interpretability papers in A3 back it only indirectly.

E2. Allocate rate along the input axis at fixed total rate.
- Verdict: FOUND in entropy-coded form, NOT FOUND in fixed-rate lattice form.
- WaterSIC (2603.04956): per-column rate by water-filling, entropy-coded. CMPQ (arXiv 2410.13056, snippet only):
  per-input-channel mixed precision at a fixed average of 2 to 4 bits, heuristic, driven by activations.
- Ordentlich and Polyanskiy (2605.13768) claim rotated GPTQ is within 0.1 bit of WaterSIC on Llama-3-8B at high rate.
  If that holds near 2.7 bits, reallocating rate under the same objective is worth at most a few hundredths of a
  b/param in MSE terms. Low-rate behaviour is unmeasured.
- Fixed-rate shape-gain or lattice codes with per-direction rate: not found.

E3. A different treatment of late blocks for generation.
- Verdict: FOUND.
- LFQ (2605.29756, ICML 2026): only the final block is fitted to the FP logits by cross-entropy, with the LM head.
  Reported Llama-3.1-8B W4 FlexRound: GSM8K 81.35 to 81.80, IFEval 70.24 to 72.09, MMLU 66.19 to 66.97, WikiText-2 ppl
  unchanged at 7.06. Reported: adding the second and third blocks from the end adds little. Gains are mostly 0.2 to
  2 points from single greedy runs.
- Zhang et al. (2504.02010): final-layer MLP kept in 16 bits, +6.57 average reasoning at 3-bit AWQ, for about 2 % of
  the weights.
- Neither restores or retunes 11 blocks. Neither reports an MMLU-against-GSM8K split per block range.

======================================================================================================================
WHAT THIS KILLS OR SUPPORTS
======================================================================================================================

N6, lost contrast.
- SUPPORTED: shrink, not additive noise, is the right model of the logit damage. Wu et al. (2608.06564) measure it
  decisively on Qwen3-4B with GPTQ, at slope 0.79 at w3.
- SUPPORTED: zero-bit global corrections cannot repair it. Wu et al. show by algebra that an affine map cannot flip a
  decision, and find label-free bias removal recovers a median of 0. This matches the project's ceiling of 6 %.
- SUPPORTED, by theory only: the layer objective discards quiet directions on purpose (reverse water-filling,
  WaterSIC).
  The quiet-direction loss in N6 is then expected behaviour of a correct encoder, not an encoder bug.
- SUPPORTED indirectly: low-variance or low-readout directions carry causal signal. Entropy neurons (2406.16254) use a
  low-singular-value subspace to set logit scale, the prediction-distal complement is reported load-bearing
  (2608.12447), and variance is a poor proxy for importance (2604.20682). None of these is in a quantization setting.
- PLAUSIBLE, not published: noise added before the final RMSNorm turns into a multiplicative shrink, since the hidden
  error is about 90 % rotation (2609.11716). If true, the slope follows the noise-to-signal energy at the final norm,
  whatever subspace the noise lives in.
- WEAKENED: "reallocate rate across input directions at fixed rate and the same objective" as a large lever.
  Ordentlich
  and Polyanskiy put rotated GPTQ within 0.1 bit of the water-filling optimum (high rate, abstract only).
- NOT CONTRADICTED by anything found: "the quiet subspace at the answer position is drowned by noise the layer
  objective considers cheap". Nobody has measured it, for it or against it.

N7, MCQ and generation lose in different blocks.
- SUPPORTED: late blocks matter for generation more than for MCQ. LFQ fixes only the final block and gains on
  generation with MMLU flat. Zhang et al. find the final-layer MLP is a reasoning bottleneck that AWQ and GPTQ
  over-compress. Chang et al. find high-error inputs depend on precise late-layer residuals.
- SUPPORTED: MCQ answers are decided mid-depth. Wiegreffe et al. place answer-symbol selection in a few middle layers,
  with later layers only sharpening it. This fits restoring the last 11 blocks giving only +0.43 on MMLU.
- SUPPORTED: early error dominates both tasks. Zhou et al. find propagated error dominates at every depth, and
  Lad et al. find the first layers among the least robust. This fits +6.12 / +7.20 for the first 25 blocks.
- SUPPORTED: ppl or MSE sensitivity does not predict reasoning sensitivity (TASA, Kendall tau about 0), so a layer
  objective tuned on ppl can miss what GSM8K needs.
- CAUTION: published late-block fixes are small. LFQ adds +0.45 GSM8K on Llama-3.1-8B W4. The project's late-11
  restoration (+4.32 GSM8K) is an FP16 ceiling, not something any quantized method has reached.
- CAUTION: Zhang et al. and LFQ work on reasoning-distilled or instruct models at W3 and W4 with scalar codes, not at
  about 2.7 b/param with lattice codes.
