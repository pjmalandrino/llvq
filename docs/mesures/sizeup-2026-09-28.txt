ONE SIZE UP AT THE SAME MEMORY: TETRA BEATS AWQ ONE SIZE BELOW ON MMLU (+1.44 AND +1.87) AND TIES IT ON GSM8K (2026-09-28)
=========================================================================================================================
Prereg:  none. This is a re-analysis of dumps already measured and committed; no model ran.
         It was asked after the 2026-09-27 review of paper 2, which found that the paper never
         compares files at equal memory. It is post hoc and is labelled so in the paper.
Code:    commit 727d96b (main), bin/mmlupair and bin/gsm8kpair, built on the Mac.
Jobs:    none. Cost 0 $. Project total unchanged: 241.88 $.
Nature:  *measured* paired gaps (the two binaries over committed dumps); memory budgets
         *computed* from measured weight bytes and the Qwen3 configs.

THE QUESTION
------------
The paper compares each sealed file with FP16 and AWQ of the same size, where it loses. Its own
motivation is to fit a larger model on the same hardware. So the question is: at about the same
memory, is our file of the next size up better than AWQ of the size below?

READING
-------
1. MMLU: one size up, our file beats AWQ at both steps, +1.44 then +1.87. Both keep p below
   10^-3, so both survive a Bonferroni correction over the two tests. It keeps 25 % of what
   the size step is worth to AWQ at 4B to 8B (1.44 / 5.65) and 43 % at 8B to 14B
   (1.87 / 4.34), *computed*.
2. GSM8K: one size up, our file ties AWQ at both steps (-0.38, p 0.74; -0.91, p 0.26). The
   reasoning loss of Tetra eats the size step. Our 14B, AWQ's 8B and FP16's 4B all score 92
   on GSM8K (92.04, 92.95, 92.12).
3. Against FP16 one size below, our file ties on MMLU at both steps (-0.56, p 0.13; +0.61,
   p 0.077). On GSM8K it loses at 4B to 8B (-3.49) and ties at 8B to 14B (-1.21, p 0.12).
4. Our 14B in 5.04 GB beats FP16's 4B in 8.04 GB by 5.52 MMLU points and ties it on GSM8K.

WHERE THE LARGER MODEL FITS (*computed*)
-----------------------------------------
KV cache, f16, from each checkpoint's config.json: 2 x layers x kv_heads x head_dim x 2 bytes.
  4B   36 x 8 x 128   147,456 B/token   1.21 GB at 8,192 tokens
  8B   36 x 8 x 128   147,456 B/token   1.21 GB at 8,192 tokens
  14B  40 x 8 x 128   163,840 B/token   1.34 GB at 8,192 tokens
The 4B and 8B have the same cache: going from 4B to 8B costs weights only.

Weights plus the cache at 8,192 tokens, without the CUDA context and the activations:
  Tetra  4B 2.59 GB   8B 3.97   14B 6.38
  AWQ    4B 3.88      8B 7.31   14B 11.32
  FP16   4B 9.25
So, by budget:
  3.97 to 6.38 GB    Tetra 8B against AWQ 4B
  6.38 to 7.31 GB    Tetra 14B against AWQ 4B (two sizes up)
  7.31 to 11.32 GB   Tetra 14B against AWQ 8B
  from 11.32 GB      both 14B, and no Tetra file above 14B is served (docs/format-noyau.md
                     section 8); AWQ 14B leads by 2.46 MMLU and 3.34 GSM8K there.

If AWQ also stored its embedding tables in 4 bits, g64 with an f16 scale and offset
(4.5 bits a weight, as ours), its weights would take:
  4B  2,666,027,672 - 777,912,320 + 218,787,840 B     = 2.11 GB
  8B  about 6.099e9 - 2,489,319,424 + 700,121,088 B  = 4.31 GB (file from 5.9566 b/param)
  14B 9,976,690,240 - 3,111,649,280 + 875,151,360 B  = 7.74 GB
With the cache: 3.32, 5.52 and 9.08 GB. The windows then narrow to 3.97 to 5.52 GB for our 8B
and 6.38 to 9.08 GB for our 14B. Between 5.52 and 6.38 GB both run the 8B, and ours scores
4.21 MMLU and 4.32 GSM8K points lower. The quality of AWQ with 4-bit tables is not measured.

CAVEATS
-------
- Post hoc. The question came after the review; no prediction was written before.
- The GSM8K pairs cross two engines: Tetra in ours, AWQ and FP16 in vLLM. The engine check at
  4B (FP16 dense - FP16 vLLM = -0.61 [-1.27, +0.06], p 0.115) found no separation; if the
  offset is real, it counts against Tetra.
- MMLU of the Tetra files is the dense reconstruction of the sealed file, as in the paper.
- One calibration draw per size (paper, Limitations).
- Speed is not compared: our 8B decodes 95.0 tok/s in our engine, AWQ 4B 200.5 in vLLM; our
  14B 57.2, AWQ 8B 123.2. Two engines; the ratio is not formed.
- Weights compare our GPU buffers with the baselines' files; the budget adds a computed cache
  and leaves out the CUDA context and activations, which were not measured.
