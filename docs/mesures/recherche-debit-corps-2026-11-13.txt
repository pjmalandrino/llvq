WHAT THE RUN SAYS
  1. A real decoder keeps part of the flat read's gain. On sealed:all the served row takes 1.803 ms, flat 1.521,
     the flat read with nothing decoded 0.993. flat recovers 0.28 ms of the 0.81 between the served row and the
     read alone.
  2. flat now reads at 0.98 of pword, the served read pattern with nothing decoded (470.5 against 482.2).
  3. Moving the row end out of the row loop is worth 5 % with the bits kept, all of it on the short rows. This
     agrees with the second run, where the side streams cost the short rows and nothing at 9,728.
  4. Staging the words in shared memory (flats) loses 13 % against reading them in place (ratio of the two medians).
  5. A thread a row is not a way: 7 to 69 threads a CTA do not hide the latency of the loads.
  6. What flat would be worth in the token is not measured. As a ceiling only (*computed*, not a forecast): if
     x1.185 carried to the 1.84 ms the token spends on its Tetra bytes, the token would go from 4.227 to 3.94 ms,
     x1.07; endpass at x1.053 would give x1.02. K10 records that gate/up already loses 10 to 16 % in the token
     against alone, so a gain alone may not carry.
