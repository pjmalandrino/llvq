THE EXPORT WAS LOST ONCE, SILENTLY, AND THAT IS THE LESSON OF THIS RUN
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
seg2 wrote the 29.5 GB `model.safetensors` through the bucket mount, ran `sync`
and read the size back. Both passed. The file was not there afterwards. The
re-export (`ops/jobs/export-14b.sh`, cpu-xl, $0.32) first proved determinism —
the same bytes give the same sha256, `07e82f7c…` — then wrote **30 parts of at
most 1 GB**, and the sum checked on the Mac at 29,536,665,800 B exactly.

A `sync` and a size read-back on the whole file are **not** a write gate on this
mount. Only the size of each write is. `ops/README.md` already carried a 4 GB
truncation; this one was 29.5 GB and passed both of the checks that were supposed
to catch it.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH
-----------------------------
- No MMLU: the census is its own job, under its own prereg.
- No kernel timing: `planesbench` refuses `d_in` 17,408 (`planesbench.rs:1917`).
- Nothing about the row scales: the training is its own prereg and its own journal.
- R against the 4B or the 8B as a size effect: the cards differ and nothing here
  separates the device term from the size term.
- `Planes14` 14B as a format verdict: another codebook, another corpus, bf16.
