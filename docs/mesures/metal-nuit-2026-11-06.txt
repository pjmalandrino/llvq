"The after arm is the Tetra twin with the highest median among the retained ones": lutd. lut+q4p and the int4 tile
are not retained, so the after arm is lutd with the served int4 kernels at 2,048 columns. M2 holds the after arm: x1.025 [1.013-1.034] lies wholly above 1.000, with lut's 256 tokens. The M1 median (x1.055) is the larger reading; M2's three-arm rounds are 40 s against M1's 80 s, so less drift falls inside a round, and its range is three times narrower.
M3 and M4 decide nothing (section 5 and M4's section 4). Their reading goes to the operator: on this Mac the grouped
form returned the expanded form's logits bit for bit on 16,380 decode steps and the same tokens at 5, 2,048 and 4,096
positions, and it decodes x1.75 faster at 2,048 and x2.38 at 4,096. The Metal door still refuses it by name.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH
----------------------------
- A served Metal configuration. No config was changed; serving lutd, or anything, on Metal is the operator's call.
- The grouped form's tolerance on the 12-window design: M3 read 4 windows. Bit identity on 16,380 steps is a strong
  reading of these 4 windows and of candle 0.9.2's Metal gemm; it is not a proof for every length or every candle.
- Any speed past a 5-token prompt. The Metal prefill is one launch a row and capped at 256 rows a call, so a long
  prompt was not timed; the grouped form's value lies at long contexts, where it was not timed either.
- A same-head ratio (rule 4). The dense arm's head copies its table every token.
- Why the prefetching loops lose on Metal. A guess: the extra registers cost occupancy. Not measured.
- The 8B and the 14B on the Mac (files not here).
