R0 LITERATURE: BATCH-1 DECODE SPEED OF QUANTIZED LLMS ON ONE GPU, STATE OF THE ART, 2026-11-12. $0.
==================================================================================================
Result: no published system found streams 2 to 3 bit weights at more than about half of datasheet bandwidth at
batch 1 on a 7B-class model. The best 4-bit and FP16 paths reach 64 to 77 %, and 2-bit paths 23 to 40 % on the same
cards (*computed* from published tok/s, section 1). Published fractions fall when the bit rate falls, when the model
shrinks and when the card's bandwidth rises (section 2). The mechanisms with measured batch-1 gains and no weight
change are fusion into persistent kernels (x1.16 to x2.5 over vLLM or SGLang), drafter-free speculation (x1.13 to
x2.22 on Spec-Bench) and split-KV attention at long context. Activation sparsity and LM-head pruning are measured
outside a +-0.12 % perplexity band or not shown exact. One paper does the dot product in the code domain on GPU for
LLM weights (CodeGEMM, A100): it requires 256-entry codebooks; 2^16 entries do not fit on chip.
Nature: literature only. No build, no run, no model load, no job. Web search and fetch, 2026-11-12. Small arithmetic
by hand, each line states it. Nothing here estimates what any mechanism gives on the project's kernel.

LABELS AND CONVENTIONS
----------------------
*measured (src)*: the figure as the source publishes it. *computed*: arithmetic done here on published figures; the
inputs are on the line. GB = 1e9 bytes. GB/s streamed = tok/s x bytes read per token. Bytes read per token = weights
read once per token: every linear layer and the LM head, without the token embedding (one row read).
Llama-2-7B parameter counts used below: 6.738 B in all, 131.1 M in the embedding and 131.1 M in the head, so 6.476 B in
the linear layers without the head (*computed* from 32,000 x 4,096 and the published total).
For orientation only, from the brief of this phase: the served 4B reads 1.38 GB a token at about 224 tok/s on the
L40S, 309 GB/s, 36 % of 864 GB/s (*computed*).

Comparability with an L40S. Same die (AD102, Ada, GDDR6): L40 (864 GB/s, the L40S memory system), RTX 6000 Ada
(960 GB/s, 142 SMs, 98,304 KB of L2 per the NVIDIA Ada professional whitepaper v1.1,
https://images.nvidia.com/aem-dam/en-zz/Solutions/technologies/NVIDIA-ADA-GPU-PROVIZ-Architecture-Whitepaper_1.1.pdf).
Same architecture, other die cut: RTX 4090 (GDDR6X, 128 SMs, 72 MB of L2). Not comparable as a ratio (rule 5): A100,
H100, RTX 3090, A6000, RTX 5090, A10, L4. Their figures are given in GB/s only.

2. WHAT BOUNDS BATCH-1 DECODE
-----------------------------
2a. The bytes argument. QuIP# and QTIP state it: small-batch decode is memory-bound, so tokens/s is at most bandwidth
over bytes a token (QuIP# poster above; Together AI QTIP blog, https://www.together.ai/blog/even-better-even-faster-quantized-llms-with-qtip).
The measured fractions in section 1 show how loose it is.

2b. Where it is shown loose.
- The fraction falls as bandwidth rises. arXiv 2605.30571: three 7-8B GQA models in bf16, batch 1, ctx 2,048 to 16,384,
  median fraction of the analytic memory floor: L4 0.694, L40S 0.516 (range 0.340 to 0.723), A100-80GB 0.275,
  H100 0.260 (*measured*). On the L40S at ctx 2,048: Qwen-2.5-7B 0.723, Llama-3.1-8B 0.715, Mistral-7B 0.523. The
  author attributes the gap to launch-side overhead. CUDA Graphs cut H100 latency by x1.259 [1.253, 1.267] and L4 by
  x1.028. Not measured on the L40S.
- The fraction falls as bits fall: QTIP 4 / 3 / 2-bit at 51 / 45 / 37 %, FP16 77 % (1e); ExLlamaV2 5 / 4 / 3 bpw at
  74 / 70 / 64 % (1c); llama.cpp Q4_0 68 % against IQ2_XXS 40 % (1b) (*computed*).
- The fraction falls as the model shrinks: QuIP# 2-bit 29.6 % at 7B against 56.8 % at 70B on one RTX 4090 (1f,
  *measured*). MPK reports its gain is largest for small models and new GPUs.
- Launch and sequencing cost. Hazy: a dummy kernel costs about 2.1 us of launch on a stream and 1.3 us in a CUDA
  graph on H100; at 5 us of stall per kernel and 7 kernels a layer, Llama-1B is capped near 770 passes a second. Their
  B200 megakernel spends about 250 of 600 us storing, fencing and reloading activations, 200 us in norm and matvec,
  30 us waiting on weights, 40 us in warp sync, 80 us in setup (*measured*, blog). NVIDIA reports CUDA graphs in
  llama.cpp worth up to x1.2, on H100 at 7B (Gray, 2024-08-07,
  https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/optimizing-llama-cpp-ai-inference-with-cuda-graphs).
- A forum thread (r/LocalLLaMA, 2024-11-02, "llama.cpp compute and memory bandwidth efficiency") observes an RTX 4090
  and an H100 at similar tg on 7B Q4_0 despite twice the bandwidth; the scoreboard of 1a gives 189 against 281 tok/s.
  Forum-grade, page not readable here (403).

2c. Achievable fraction of datasheet bandwidth for GEMV-like kernels.
- QTIP: matvec "at over 80 % of peak memory bandwidth on modern GPUs" (conclusion, kernel level, card not tied).
- QuIP#: over 50 % of peak on an RTX 4090 for its first E8P kernel (paper, as quoted by search).
- Marlin README (https://github.com/IST-DASLab/marlin): ECC on lowers achievable bandwidth by 10 to 15 %; results need
  locked clocks. The L40S ships GDDR6 "with ECC" (NVIDIA page below).
- Measured STREAM-type DRAM bandwidth of an L40S, L40, RTX 4090 or RTX 5090: not found. Chips and Cheese reports an
  RTX 4090 L2 bandwidth of about 5 TB/s with all SMs active, and warns that read combining makes its DRAM test
  over-report on that card (2022-11-02, https://chipsandcheese.com/p/microbenchmarking-nvidias-rtx-4090).
- The best published end-to-end fractions on GDDR cards in section 1 are 76 to 77 % (MLC 4-bit, FP16 in QTIP's table)
  and 88 % for FP16 on an A6000 under FLUTE's head assumption (*computed*).

2e. L2 residency. No paper found that measures CUDA access-policy windows (persisting L2) for LLM weights or KV at
batch 1. LLaMCAT (arXiv 2512.00083) reports x1.26 from last-level-cache arbitration and throttling, in what appears to
be simulation.

3. MECHANISMS THAT LEAVE THE WEIGHTS AND THE FILE UNCHANGED
-----------------------------------------------------------
For each: measured gain, batch, card, memory cost as published.

3a. Persistent megakernel, on-GPU scheduler, counter barriers, cross-op weight prefetch.
Hazy (1k): x2.5 over vLLM, x1.5 over SGLang, H100, Llama-1B bf16, batch 1; shared memory split in 13 pages of 16 KiB.
MPK (1k): x1.0 to x1.7 over SGLang and vLLM across five models and A100/H100/B200; Qwen3-8B A100 x1.16. Cohere (1k):
x1.58 over vLLM at batch 1, x1.25 to x1.41 end to end at batch 8. Memory cost: not reported by any of the three.

3b. Operator fusion in llama.cpp CUDA (Aman, 2025-12-01, https://am17an.bearblog.dev/new-post/): RMSNorm+mul+add,
GEMV+add, gate and up sharing one input read, top-k MoE softmax, Q/K/V on concurrent streams. All on against all off,
tg128: Qwen3-30B-A3B Q4_K_M RTX 4090 198.39 to 271.04 (+37 %), RTX 5090 246.96 to 352.06 (+43 %); gpt-oss-20B
+17 % and +27 % (*measured*, blog). Each PR alone under +10 %. The author puts gpt-oss-20B on the 5090 at about 80 %
of his speed-of-light estimate. Memory cost: none stated; register pressure named as the limit.

3c. CUDA Graphs: x1.2 (NVIDIA, H100, 7B), x1.259 (H100) and x1.028 (L4) in arXiv 2605.30571. Closed in this project
by its rule 3; listed only as the size of the launch overhead on those cards.

3d. Layouts built offline or at load, for tensor-core or vector loads.
Marlin (Frantar, Castro, Chen, Hoefler, Alistarh, arXiv 2408.11743, 2024): weights reshuffled offline into the
tensor-core fragment order; README claims near the 3.87x ideal of 4-bit g128 up to batch 16 to 32 on an A10. No
batch-1 tok/s in the README. arXiv 2605.30571 states Marlin is tuned for SM80 and ExLlamaV2's int4 kernels for SM89.
FLUTE: offline restructuring, a 256-entry 32-bit table (about 1 KB) duplicated in shared memory against up to 8-way
bank conflicts, Stream-K partitioning; batch 1 figures in 1h. Any-Precision LLM (Park et al., ICML 2024,
https://github.com/SNU-ARC/any-precision-llm): bitplane layout rearranged for coalescing; batch-1 numbers not found.
Memory cost of all three: zero beyond the packed weights, per their papers.

3e. Integer or tensor-core paths at batch 1. Hazy: tensor cores help marginally on B200 and CUDA cores work better on
H100 for the batch-1 matvec. llama.cpp MMVQ quantizes the activation to int8 and uses int8 dot products with float
scales (forum statement by a maintainer in the r/LocalLLaMA thread of 2b). No batch-1 measurement of a low-bit
tensor-core GEMV beating a CUDA-core GEMV was found.

3f. Decompress into on-chip cache or into a dense buffer. VQ-LLM (Liu, Luo et al., HPCA 2025, arXiv 2503.02236,
https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02236v2), RTX 4090: codebook entries placed by access frequency in registers, shared or
global memory; latency -46.13 % on average against open VQ kernels; GeMV at 0.88x the latency of AWQ/QoQ (batch not
stated). DFloat11 (lossless 11-bit BF16, arXiv 2504.11651), README: about 2x slower than BF16 at batch 1, the
decompression cost being fixed per forward pass (*measured*, README via PyPI).

3g. Attention at batch 1. Flash-Decoding (Dao, Haziza, Massa, Sizov, 2023-10-12,
https://crfm.stanford.edu/2023/10/12/flashdecoding.html): split-KV with a log-sum-exp reduction; CodeLlama-34B, batch 1,
up to x8 end to end at very long sequences, attention alone up to x50 against FlashAttention; little gain on short
prompts (*measured*, blog). No extra memory beyond the split partials.
KV quantization: KIVI 2-bit (arXiv 2402.02750) reports 2.6x lower peak memory and x2.35 to x3.47 throughput from larger
batches; no batch-1 latency. LMDeploy int8/int4 KV (https://lmdeploy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/quantization/kv_quant.html):
throughput at batch 256 only. No measured batch-1 decode gain from KV quantization on an NVIDIA card was found.

3h. LM-head shortcuts.
CSV-Decode (Liu, Yu, Lengerich, arXiv 2511.21702, 2025-11-16, https://arxiv.org/html/2511.21702v1): k-means clusters of
the vocabulary, Cauchy-Schwarz bound per cluster, clusters opened until a top-k certificate holds, full logits on
fallback (1.2 % of steps). 18 to 21 % of the vocabulary computed. Extra memory 32.8 MB for 2,000 clusters at d = 4,096.
Reported x2.75 end to end on Llama-3-8B, A100. The paper also reports 99.7 % top-1 accuracy and two certification
rates (98.2 % and 96.4 %), which do not square with an exact top-k claim; the speedups exceed what an LM head alone
can remove at 8B. Read with caution.
SpecVocab and VocabTrim (arXiv 2506.22694) and HiDRA (ICLR 2026): approximate, not certified.

3i. Early exit. No exact early-exit method found. The exact variants are self-speculative: drafting with skipped
layers of the same model and verifying with the full model. SWIFT (ICLR 2025, arXiv 2410.06916): x1.3 to x1.6,
training-free, layer set chosen on the fly; x1.56 on LLaMA-2-13B against LayerSkip and Self-SD in its appendix. CLaSp:
x1.3 to x1.7 on Spec-Bench (search summary). LayerSkip needs training. Memory: no extra weights.

3j. Activation sparsity. SiLU gating does not give exact zeros; exact sparsity needs ReLU and fine-tuning
("ReLU Strikes Back", Mirzadeh et al., ICLR 2024, arXiv 2310.04564: up to 3x fewer FLOPs after relufication).
Training-free methods threshold near-zeros and change the outputs.
TEAL (Liu et al., ICLR 2025, arXiv 2408.14690, https://arxiv.org/html/2408.14690v3): magnitude thresholding of hidden
states, batch 1, GPT-Fast with CUDA graphs. Llama-3-8B decode: A6000 x1.22 / x1.42 / x1.63 at 25 / 40 / 50 %
sparsity, A100 x1.11 / x1.25 / x1.33. Cost on Llama-3-8B WikiText perplexity: 5.87 to 5.94 (+1.2 %), 6.21 (+5.8 %),
6.67 (+13.6 %) (*computed* from the table); GSM8K 49.81 to 43.82 at 40 %. No quantized sparse kernel exists; A100 at
0 % sparsity ran at 0.91x. CATS: within 1 to 2 % of downstream accuracy (Mistral-7B, Llama-7B). R-Sparse: +43 % end
to end at 50 % sparsity, "comparable" accuracy. Prox (2026 preprint): x1.99 at 70 % FFN sparsity. None reports a
change inside +-0.12 % of perplexity.

3k. Reuse across tokens. LoRA-Drop (arXiv 2601.02569, 2026-01): reuses the previous token's hidden state in chosen
layers with a trained LoRA correction and periodic full refresh; up to x2.6, within 0.5 pp of accuracy, needs
training. FFN-SkipLLM (EMNLP 2024): skips 25 to 30 % of FFN blocks with "marginal change". No exact or certified
reuse scheme found.

3l. Speculation without a drafter model. Spec-Bench leaderboard (Xia et al., updated 2025-04-22,
https://github.com/hemingkx/Spec-Bench/blob/main/Leaderboard.md), Vicuna-7B-v1.3, FP16, batch 1, greedy, mean of 3:
| method | needs | RTX 3090 | A100 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prompt Lookup Decoding | nothing | x1.64 | x1.66 |
| Token Recycling | under 2 MB table | x1.40 | x2.22 |
| REST | a retrieval datastore | x1.28 | x1.48 |
| Lookahead | nothing | x1.13 | x1.34 |
| EAGLE-2 (for scale, trained head) | draft head | x2.19 | x2.36 |
Per-task spread is wide: PLD on summarization x2.46 to x2.66, on translation x1.06 to x1.15. Lookahead paper
(Fu, Bailis, Stoica, Zhang, ICML 2024, https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02057v1): LLaMA-2-7B-Chat on A100, MT-Bench x1.78,
CNN/DM x1.57; about 120x extra FLOPs a step at 7B; about +30 % on an RTX 3090 against over +50 % on the A100.

4. THE DOT PRODUCT IN THE CODE DOMAIN
-------------------------------------
4a. CodeGEMM (Park et al., NAVER, NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2512.17970). The one GPU kernel found that does it for LLM
weights. Inner products of each activation sub-vector with every centroid are built per input into a "Psumbook" in
shared memory; codes gather partial sums. Codebooks of b = 8 bits (256 centroids), vectors of v = 4 or 8; the paper
states 2^16-entry codebooks exceed on-chip capacity. Building the Psumbook takes 20 to 46 % of cycles on one SM.
A100 80 GB, batch 1, Llama-3-8B, summed kernel latency of a decoder block: CodeGEMM m1v4 152.7 us, LUT-GEMM 160.1,
QuIP# 162.6, CodeGEMM m2v8 172.2, QTIP 189.9, AQLM 2x8 250.1, cuBLAS FP16 332.5, AQLM 1x16 645.5 (*measured*). Loses
to cuBLAS above batch 32. Quality at these codebooks without fine-tuning: average 53.93 against 71.26 for FP16, 63.96
with PV-tuning.
4b. LUT-GEMM (Park et al., ICLR 2024, arXiv 2206.09557): tables of partial sums for binary-coding quantization on an
A100; 3-bit OPT-175B on one GPU, x2.1 over OPTQ per token (abstract). The LUT Tensor Core paper (Mo et al., ISCA 2025,
arXiv 2408.06003, https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06003v1) measured LUT-GEMM against a CUTLASS dequantization kernel on an
A100 at batch 1, 1,024 and 4,096: "the dequantization-based kernel always outperforms the LUT-based kernel" (figure,
values not in text). Table precompute costs 16.5 to 24.4 % unfused and 2.5 to 2.6 % fused (Welder). Its headline
x6.93 is simulated hardware.
4c. AQLM: the CPU kernel uses lookup of precomputed dot products with 8-bit codebooks (x2.75 to x4.07 over FP32 on an
i9, Table 5). GPU kernels: 2x8 up to x3.0, 1x16 up to x1.3 (README via HF docs,
https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/quantization/aqlm). QTIP appendix A.1.3: an AQLM 1x16 codebook at 8
dimensions would need 32 MiB of L1. Whether the GPU 2x8 kernel works in the code domain is not stated in the paper.
4d. Lookup tables on GPUs for dequantization (not ADC): QTIP HYB uses a 2^9 x 2 table, 2 KiB, duplicated 32x for bank
conflicts; a pure-lookup 32 KB codebook "only fits in GPU L1 cache with bank conflicts". QuIP# E8P: 1 KiB table,
under 4 instructions a weight. FLUTE: about 1 KB, duplicated. llama.cpp IQ2_XXS: a sign table in `__constant__`
dropped tg from 155 to 108 tok/s on CUDA (PR #4773).
4e. ADC outside LLMs. Faiss GPU (Johnson, Douze, Jegou, arXiv 1702.08734, 2017): PQ distance tables in shared memory;
the paper's 55 % of peak is for k-selection; no ADC fraction found. Bolt (Blalock, Guttag, KDD 2017, arXiv 1706.10283):
16-centroid codebooks, SIMD shuffles on CPU, up to 10x on distances and dot products, matmul faster than BLAS except
small matrices; no GPU. MADDNESS (arXiv 2106.10860), LUT-NN (arXiv 2302.03213), T-MAC: CPU, FPGA or ASIC; no GPU
measurement found. QuickerADC: not found.

5. PUBLISHED NEGATIVE RESULTS AND TRAPS
---------------------------------------
- Fewer bits, same speed or slower. AQLM 1x16 at 2 bits: x1.2 over FP16 on an RTX 3090 (1g) and slower than cuBLAS
  FP16 at batch 1 on an A100 (645.5 against 332.5 us, 4a). VQ-LLM: open VQ kernels "often" slower than FP16.
  llama.cpp on Metal: IQ2_XXS 54 tok/s against 63.5 for Q4_0. bnb-nf4 on an L4: 59.36 ms against 62.32 for bf16.
- Table placement traps: `__constant__` table 155 to 108 tok/s (llama.cpp); unmitigated shared-memory tables reach
  8-way bank conflicts (FLUTE); a 32 KB codebook does not fit L1 cleanly (QTIP).
- LUT kernels lose to dequantization on an A100 at every batch measured (LUT Tensor Core paper, 4b).
- Framework overhead hides the kernel: HIGGS 4-bit uncompiled at 41 forward passes a second against 59 for compiled
  BF16, 124 once compiled (1i). QuIP# under HF generate at 29.6 % of bandwidth (1f).
- Megakernel ideas that did not pay (Cohere, 1k): shared-memory paging for weight prefetch, dropped for its
  bookkeeping cost; dependency-affinity placement 1 to 2 % slower; topology-aware and brute-force schedules +10 % on a
  dense model that did not carry to MoE; attention-first wave order 7 to 19 % slower.
- Fusion has a ceiling per step: each llama.cpp fusion PR under +10 %; register pressure limits it (3b).
- Bandwidth fraction drops on fast cards and small models (2b). A100 and H100 rows of 1a sit at 37 to 39 % and
  below, against 63 to 70 % on GDDR cards.
- FLUTE: short of uniform-quantization kernels on an A100; padding below batch 16; K-means table updates gave no
  gain.
- TEAL: 0.91x at 0 % sparsity on an A100; does not scale with batch; Llama-3 degrades more than Llama-2 (3j).
- Lookahead: x1.13 on an RTX 3090 (Spec-Bench); slowdowns when compute-bound (3l).
- DFloat11: about 2x slower than BF16 at batch 1 (3f).
- Marlin: ECC costs 10 to 15 % of achievable bandwidth; tuned for SM80.
