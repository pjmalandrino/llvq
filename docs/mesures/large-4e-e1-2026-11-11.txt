WHAT THIS SAYS ABOUT DRAM AND INSTRUCTIONS (a reading, not a fact; no hardware counter was read)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- o rows (int4, 200.5 MB) look bound by DRAM traffic. Without their lines in L2 the mean CTA work rises from 277 to
  366 us at 5 tokens (+32 %) and from 270 to 369 at 8,192 (*measured*). 200.5 MB in 366 us is 548 GB/s (*computed*).
  When v9mv prefetches o one layer early, o is back within 9 us of v9.
- gate/up rows (Tetra, 469.8 MB) look mostly bound by instructions when they read alone. Without their prefetch the
  mean CTA work rises 6 % at 5 tokens (1,309 to 1,387 us), 15 % at 8,192 (1,219 to 1,401) (*measured*).
- But gate/up rows slow down under foreign traffic. v9mv adds 374.3 MB of q/k/v and o prefetch during them (*computed*
  by the prereg), and their mean work rises 178 us over v9np at 5 tokens, 170 at 8,192 (*measured*). At 864 GB/s
  those bytes take 433 us (*computed*): gate/up pays about 40 % of their peak-rate time. The budget note's "488 MB of
  room" during gate/up is not free in situ.
- ln1's excess over ln2 is the q/k/v prefetch in flight, as §1c of the note guessed. With it moved (v9mv), ln1's mean
  work falls from 237.5 to 158.7 us at 5 tokens, near ln2's 143.9 (*measured*). q/k/v rows lose 9 us for it.
- Attention at 5 tokens is not DRAM bound: without the prefetch it still takes 398 us for 9.3 MB of KV (*computed*
  floor 11 us). The prefetch was the work of the CTAs with no attention job: their mean work falls from 125 to 25 us.
- Attention at 8,192 with the prefetch moves 1.878 GB in 2.646 ms, 710 GB/s (*computed*); without it 1.208 GB in
  2.394 ms, 505 GB/s. The 670 MB cost it 252 us where the peak rate would ask 776: most of it rode in bandwidth the
  phase left free.
- Read together: the 670 MB are paid wherever they cross the DRAM. v9 places them where they cost least of the three
  placements tried.
