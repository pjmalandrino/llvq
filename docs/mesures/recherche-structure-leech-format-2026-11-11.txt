THE ANSWER
----------
The lattice and the code are not where the value is. Λ24 is the best lattice quantizer known up to 48 dimensions, the
Tetra words are 47.4 bits of entropy in 48, and decode at batch 1 runs at 35 % of the byte bound, so a denser or
entropy-coded stream would add work to the resource that already limits. The value sits in three places: the
allocation of rates across matrices (today a hand-picked binary choice), the input embedding of untied models, and
the cost of producing an object (a 14B chain spends about $25, of which the encoder runs 7 to 14 times its own floor).
Every one of the three applies unchanged to a larger Qwen, another family and an MoE. The MoE needs one more thing,
a calibration policy for rarely routed experts, and the published 2-bit evidence says it is not optional.

FIVE VERDICTS, WITH THEIR ARITHMETIC
------------------------------------
1. The 0.58 b/param between 2.150 and 2.73.
   The 2.150 is a kernel figure that bills the tail and row scales in f32. In whole-model accounting the Tetra
   matrices stand at 2.083 b/weight on the 4B (1.482 b/param over 2,862.6 M of 4,022.5 M parameters, *computed* from
   revue-compression §1). The excess over that is 0.649 b/param:
     4B   int4 records 770.9 M x (4.25 - 2.083) / 4,022.5 M = 0.417 (64 %)   tied embedding-head 388.96 M x 2.417 = 0.233
     8B   int4 0.270 (1,007 M weights)   embedding + untied head 0.372 (1,244.6 M)   Tetra at 2.053 b/weight
     14B  int4 0.444 (2,952 M)            embedding + untied head 0.261 (1,555.8 M)   Tetra at 2.025 b/weight
   (*computed*, same parts.) The part with the best evidence of no quality loss is the input embedding of the
   untied models: a gather, so any rate costs no decode time. At Tetra's rate it removes 0.184 b/param at 8B and
   0.127 at 14B (*computed*). The int4 records are the larger pot, 0.13 to 0.22 b/param (proposal S5), but every code
   cheaper than int4 in bits so far decodes slower per weight than int4 rows on this card, so that pot is gated on
   speed. The head stays where it is: llama.cpp keeps `output.weight` at Q5_K even for IQ2_XXS (*reported*,
   https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/src/llama-quant.cpp, read on master), and our own q8 to q4 step on
   the tied 4B table cost +1.52 % of perplexity (*measured*, ROADMAP-QUALITY row 4). Tying an untied head, or
   factorising the table, changes the model: it needs training and is out of a PTQ budget.

2. Rate. A compressed or shaped stream read by the kernel would not pay, and the reason is measured.
   - Lossless: the sum of the marginal field entropies of the sealed 4B's words is 47.4 bits of 48 (*computed*,
     `bin/tetraentropy`, HISTORIQUE 2026-11-08). The gain bit sits at 52.2 % on level 1 (*measured*, artstat-4b),
     0.9986 bit of entropy (*computed*). At most 0.025 b/weight is there.
   - Lossy, at the same distortion on a Gaussian source: Tetra keeps 88.89 % (SQNR 1.778 bits at rate 2), 0.222 b/dim
     above the rate-distortion bound; the ball 0.160; QTIP's trellis 0.069 MSE, 0.071 b/dim (*computed* from
     how-it-works §7 and from QTIP Table 1, *reported*, https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11235: E8P 0.089, 1MAD and 3INST
     0.069, bound 0.063). An entropy-coded uniform Leech code sits at its space-filling loss, 0.0839 b/dim at high rate
     (*computed* from G = 0.065771). The ceiling of any rate trick is therefore 0.14 to 0.15 b/dim, 0.10 b/param on
     the 4B's Tetra share, before any decode cost (*computed*).
   - The wall is not DRAM. 221.5 tok/s x 1.378 GB a token = 305 GB/s, 35 % of the L40S's 864 (*computed*, ETAT §2,
     recherche-debit-maths). Inside 4E, Tetra rows stream at 379 GB/s and int4 rows at 587 (*measured*, plan-300 §3,
     pers-4e-v3). Tetra rows are decode-bound; a variable-rate code adds instructions exactly there.
   - What a kernel would need: per-row byte offsets (4 B a row, 3.8 MB at 4B), a per-32-block prefix so every lane
     starts at a known bit (0.04 b/weight), and a table decode per symbol. EntQuant, which entropy-codes with nvCOMP
     ANS and inflates a block at a time, reports 1.5 to 2 times slower than BF16 at batch 4 on Llama-2 7B
     (*reported*, https://arxiv.org/html/2601.22787v2 §3.4). Verdict: closed in VRAM. Entropy coding belongs to the
     file only, where the int4 nibbles still carry slack (memoire note, row 6).

3. Distortion at the same rate. No better lattice exists in reach, and the codebook has not been the bottleneck.
   - Agrell and Allen, Table I (*reported*, https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.09605, IEEE Trans. IT 2023): Λ24 G = 0.06577,
     Λ16 (Barnes-Wall) 0.06830, E8 0.071682, best known in dimension 32 the product Λ24 x E8 at 0.06720, and in
     dimension 48 Λ24 x Λ24 at 0.06577. Nothing up to 48 dimensions beats Λ24. Moving to BW16 costs 0.027 b/dim, to E8
     0.062, to the 32-dimensional product 0.016 (*computed*, ½ log2 of the G ratios).
   - The trellis's 0.15 b/dim over Tetra on Gaussian data has not transferred to this model family: three 48-bit
     codebooks 21.5 % apart in perplexity read the same MMLU (*measured*, leech0c13), and Tetra beat the ball, which is
     0.06 b/dim better on Gaussian data, by 4.64 % of perplexity on the real 4B (*measured*, tetra-4b).
   - A learned linear transform per group before the lattice has no published win for weight-only VQ at 2 bits
     (revue-compression §6), and it adds a transform per group to a rotation path that is already a phase of its own.
   - Per-block rate inside a matrix gains little after the Hadamard rotation, which flattens column sensitivity by
     design. Per-matrix allocation keeps what the rotation cannot flatten. Q-Palette decomposes its gap to the ideal
     on Llama-3.1-8B at 3.25 bits: TCQ at widths {2, 3, 4} totals 0.0327, TCQ at every half-bit 0.0168, and the
     paper attributes that difference to bit granularity (*reported*, https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20214v2, Table 6).
     That is where the distortion is left: in the allocation (S2, S5) and in the encoder objective (YAQA, quality
     note row 4).

4. The walls.
   - The `rot_apply` wall is already lifted on the card. `rot_apply_dev` and `tv_q4_hs` return the host's bytes at
     width 25,600, the 32B's `down_proj` input, on the L40S (*measured*, lot1-4b-2026-11-04, format-noyau §8, ETAT §4).
     The line in CLAUDE.md that says the wall closes the path "whatever the format" is stale; this note does not edit
     it. What still blocks the 32B: no encode ($62, 11.4 h on rtx-pro-6000x2, *estimated*, ROADMAP §4), no wide
     kernel run inside a model, and 4E built for the three sealed shapes only.
   - MoE has no width problem. Qwen3-30B-A3B has hidden 2,048, expert width 768 = 256 x 3, 128 experts, 8 routed,
     48 layers (*reported*, https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-30B-A3B/raw/main/config.json). Its blocker is the
     calibration. 131,072 tokens x 8 / 128 = 8,192 routed tokens per expert and layer on average, against
     d_in = 2,048 for gate and up (*computed*). On gpt-oss-20b our own histogram put the least loaded expert of layer 0
     at 0.206 of the uniform load (*measured*, moe-routing-gptoss20b); at that ratio an expert of the 30B gets about
     1,690 tokens and a rank-deficient Hessian (*computed*). BitsMoE reports plain GPTQ at 2 bits on
     Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base at 34.08 average accuracy over seven tasks, against 61.91 for its own method and 52.21 for
     MoEQuant (*reported*, https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.00079.md). Naive 2-bit GPTQ collapses on this model.

5. Production cost. A 14B chain spends about $25 to make the object: encode $15.03 in two segments, re-export $0.32,
   row-scale training $9.78; its three censuses add $5.49 (*measured*, docs/data/jobs.csv rows of 2026-09-22/23).
   - The encode's quantization phase is 81.2 % of 16,194 s, about 13,150 s on 192 cores (*computed*, encode-14b).
     The Tetra encoder measures 329 µs a block on one core (*measured*, `trioencbench`, HISTORIQUE 2026-09-05). About
     542 M Tetra blocks at 329 µs over 192 threads is 929 s, or 1,860 s if the 192 are 96 physical cores (*computed*).
     The phase runs 7 to 14 times its single-pass floor. No journal names the cause.
   - Row-scale training: on the 4B the KL is acquired before step 1,210 of 9,681 (*measured*, tetranu-rowscales). The
     MMLU was read only at the end, so whether it is acquired at the same step is not known.

THE PROPOSALS, RANKED BY SOLIDITY
---------------------------------
Every effect is *estimated* unless labelled. "Criteria" are MMLU and GSM8K, whole-model b/param, VRAM, decode tok/s,
time to first token (TTFT), cost of producing the object.

S1. A cheap, repeatable chain. Engineering, known outcome.
    Change:   (a) row-scale training stops on a held-out signal instead of a fixed 9,681 steps; (b) instrument the
              quantization phase (encoder calls per block, thread occupancy, f64 update share) and fix what it names;
              (c) a GPU Tetra encoder. Within one 24-column step of GPTQ every row is independent, and the per-block
              search is a 64-state trellis pass over three sections, the shape QTIP already runs on GPUs.
    Evidence: the two facts of verdict 5.
    Effect:   cost of a 14B object $25 to $5-12, of a 32B $62 to $15-30. Every other criterion unchanged: (a) and (b)
              keep the codes; (c) must reproduce them. Cheaper encodes also make the held-out draw selection of the
              quality note (row 2, +0.8 to +1.8 pp at 4B) affordable at 14B and above.
    Wrong if: MMLU keeps moving after the KL is flat (the 4B curve was flat while MMLU moved 4.30 pp). Then the stop
              signal is MMLU on the 1,531-question validation split, which `mmlu.rs` does not load yet.
    Format:   none.
    Cost:     (a) half a day; (b) one to two days; (c) one to two weeks and a bit-identity gate on a sample of matrices.
    First:    $0. Look for the 48 checkpoints of tetranu or dclm-4b in the bucket (`hf buckets ls`, rule 9); score the
              step-1,210 checkpoint on the full split on the Mac. Gate: paired against the final scales, the interval
              contains 0 and its half-width is under 0.6 pp. If the checkpoints are gone, a 1,210-step training is about
              $0.55 on l40sx1 (0.6367 s a step plus load).
    Closed:   none touched.

S2. Allocate the rates the file already has by measured sensitivity, under a fixed byte budget. Engineering on a
    published method; the gain is a bet with a measured prior.
    Change:   estimate one coefficient a_l per matrix by noise injection shaped like each kind's real error, with KL to
              the f16 model as the loss (HIGGS linearity theorem), then solve a multiple-choice knapsack over
              {Tetra, int4 g128} per matrix at the sealed file's byte count. This replaces the hand-picked windows
              (`o_proj` everywhere, `down_proj` at 12-23 on the 4B). The same solver takes more kinds later (S3, S5).
    Evidence: HIGGS on Llama-3.1-8B, WikiText-2: dynamic 6.388 against uniform 6.643 at 3.25 bits, 5.910 against 6.015
              at 4.00 (*reported*, https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17525v1, Table 3; a_l from 15 noise levels a layer).
              Q-Palette data-free at 3 bits: 6.28 mixed against 6.81 for uniform QTIP (*reported*,
              https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20214v2). Ours: at 8B, `down_proj` in place of `o_proj` scored +0.77 [+0.26, +1.28] with fewer bytes
              (*measured*, sealed-8b-27); `v_proj` in int4 bought 70.4 MMLU points per b/param (*measured*, q5-tetra).
    Effect:   MMLU +0.3 to +1.5 at 4B and 14B at equal bytes, or -0.05 to -0.15 b/param at equal MMLU. VRAM equal or
              lower. Decode: a Tetra weight costs 0.687 ps of row time in 4E against 0.905 for an int4 weight
              (*computed*: 0.2604 B at 379 GB/s, 0.531 B at 587), so every int4 matrix the solver returns to Tetra
              speeds decode and every one it adds slows it; the gate holds tok/s inside the served range. TTFT
              unchanged. Production: one $0 map per model.
    Wrong if: the linear model fails at our rates (HIGGS reports its predictions diverging below about 3 bits for
              uniform quantization). Fallback: the data-aware mode, one measured KL per candidate swap.
    Format:   none. Both kinds exist (`kind_tag` 2 beside Tetra, format-noyau §5 bis).
    Cost:     2 to 3 days of code; the map on the Mac; a census per size $0.70 / $1.00 / $1.59 to $1.95, GSM8K
              $2.13 / $2.37 / $4.24 (*measured* prices, quality note).
    First:    $0 on the Mac (needs a go for local compute), 4B: a_l for the 252 matrices, KL on 4 windows of 512 tokens.
              Gate: the map ranks the measured 4B swaps as the censuses did: `down_proj` above `o_proj` (+3.79
              [+1.52, +6.11] against +2.77, q5-alloc-int4) and `v_proj` first per byte (70.4 points per b/param,
              q5-tetra). A map that misorders them is not used.
    Closed:   none. It generalizes ROADMAP §2.4 and revue-compression P5 with no kernel.

S3. Take the input embedding of untied models out of int4, and optionally out of VRAM. Engineering; the quality
    outcome has external evidence on a sibling family.
    Change:   the input table (a gather) stored as Tetra at about 2.08 b/weight, or as int3 g64 (3.5 b), decoded one
              row a token. The head stays int4 g64. Option: the table lives in host RAM and one row crosses PCIe a
              token (8 or 10 KB at 8B or 14B in f16).
    Evidence: CARVQ, input embedding only, WikiText-2 ppl (*reported*, https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12721, Table 1):
              Qwen2.5-7B FP16 6.85, INT3 6.86, INT2 7.44, CARVQ at 2.38 b 6.87; Llama-3.1-8B 6.24, 6.33, 8.65, 6.55.
              llama.cpp's IQ2_S puts an untied `token_embd` at IQ3_S and IQ2_XXS at Q2_K (*reported*, llama-quant.cpp).
    Effect:   b/param -0.184 at 8B and -0.127 at 14B in Tetra, -0.076 and -0.053 in int3 g64; -0.057 on a 32B, where
              the table is 2.4 % of 32.8 G parameters (*computed*). Host residency instead: VRAM -0.35 GB at 8B, -0.44 at
              14B, file unchanged (*computed*). Decode and TTFT: nothing measurable in VRAM (a gather of one row); a
              PCIe read of about 10 KB a token from host RAM. Quality: perplexity +0 to +1 % (*estimated* from the
              Qwen2.5 rows).
    Wrong if: Qwen3's input table is as fragile as Llama-3.1's (+5 % at 2.38 b). The 4B gives no test: its table is
              tied to the head.
    Format:   a Tetra or int3 record on the gather path; a reader branch, no new decoder; v1 untouched.
    Cost:     1 to 2 days; ppl on the Mac; one census per size ($1.00 / $1.59 to $1.95) and GSM8K.
    First:    $0 on the Mac (go needed): Qwen3-8B f16 with only the input table swapped through `embedq` (int4 g64,
              int3 g64, Tetra), paired perplexity over 12 windows of 4,096 in f16. Gate: Tetra or int3 within
              +0.12 % of int4 g64, the band of a one-file A/B (ETAT §4).
    Closed:   none.

S4. MoE readiness: route every calibration token through every expert, weighted by the router. Engineering for the
    Hessians; the quality is a research bet with strong published motivation.
    Change:   for expert e, H_e = Σ_t (π_te + ε) z_te z_teᵀ over all calibration tokens, π_te the router probability,
              z_te the expert's input (the shared hidden state for gate and up, the expert's own act(gate)·up for down,
              computed for every token offline), with the existing shrinkage (`LLVQ_H_SHRINK`) toward the diagonal.
              S2 then allocates per expert, with routing frequency in its coefficient. 4E gains an expert phase.
    Evidence: verdict 4 (BitsMoE: GPTQ 34.08 against 61.91 at 2 bits on this model). MoEQuant reports more than 10
              points of HumanEval on DeepSeekMoE-16B at 4 bits from expert-balanced sampling and affinity weighting
              (*reported*, https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03804). EAQuant's balancing alone gives +0.35 (W3A4) and +1.09
              (W4A4) on OLMoE (*reported*, https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13329v3).
    Effect:   the object, *computed* from the config: 30.53 G parameters; experts and attention in Tetra, embedding and
              head int4 g64, router f16: 2.14 b/param, 8.2 GB. A token reads 8 of 128 experts: 0.91 GB a token against
              the 4B's 1.378, a byte bound of 952 tok/s at 864 GB/s. Served speed unknown until 4E has an expert phase.
              Quality: unknown; the policy is what keeps it off the BitsMoE floor.
    Wrong if: router-weighted Hessians over-trust tokens the expert never sees in service. EAQuant's oversampling
              (extra data until each expert clears 2 x its uniform share) is the fallback.
    Format:   none for the codes; the file needs expert records and a router, a reader change.
    Cost:     H capture: 131,072 x 128 x 3 x 768 x 2,048 x 2 x 48 = 7.6 PFLOP (*computed*), minutes to hours on one
              card. Encode about twice a 14B by weight count, $30 today, $5 to $10 after S1. 4E expert phase 2 to 4
              weeks. Opening the lead is the operator's call (ROADMAP §4: on hold, about $1.4 to open).
    First:    $0 on the Mac, a heavy local job that needs a go: the routing histogram of Qwen3-30B-A3B on the 131,072
              DCLM calibration tokens, streamed a layer at a time with the gpt-oss tool. Gate: if more than 1 % of
              (layer, expert) pairs receive fewer routed tokens than d_in = 2,048, the all-token policy is a
              precondition of any encode.
    Closed:   MoE is on hold, not closed. gpt-oss stays ruled out.

S5. One decoder, several rates: a Tetra family. Research bet.
    Change:   keep the trio, the 64-state trellis and the Golay path (14 bits a block). The rank table is sorted by
              cost, so its first 2^r rows are the best r-bit table: section i reads a prefix of r_i bits. A word is
              14 + r1 + r2 + r3 + g bits. r = 11 is today's 48. With r_max = 13 the one table is 2 x 2^13 x 4 B = 64 KiB,
              and the members run from 36 bits (1.50 b/weight) to 55 (2.29). For 2.6 to 3.6 b/weight, a member with no
              rank table: each coordinate's rank stored directly in 2 or 3 bits, so 1 + 1 + 12 + 48 + 1 = 63 bits
              (2.63 b) or 87 bits (3.63 b), decoded by shifts, the class parity left to the encoder.
    Evidence: verdict 3 (granularity carries half of Q-Palette's gap). int4 g128 RTN has the distortion of an ideal
              code at 3.32 b on Gaussian samples and 2.92 b on Student-t5 samples (*computed*, the numpy script,
              4,194,304 samples: relative MSE 0.00997 and 0.01738). A code 0.222 b/dim above the bound, as Tetra is,
              matches it at 3.15 to 3.55 b/weight, before any credit for GPTQ feedback, which the int4 records lack.
    Effect:   b/param -0.13 to -0.21 at 4B, -0.09 to -0.14 at 8B, -0.14 to -0.22 at 14B if the int4 records move to
              such a member at int4's distortion (*computed* from the parts of verdict 1). Fractional rates for S2.
              Decode: the risk. The direct-rank member must cost no more row time per weight than an int4 weight
              (0.905 ps); a 64 KiB table takes L1 from the activation tile that gave Tetra 17.6 % (banc-252).
              TTFT moves with the same row costs. Production: encoder work per member.
    Wrong if: Gaussian retention misleads again (88.89 % predicted Tetra worse than the ball; it was better), or the
              direct-rank decode is slower than int4 rows.
    Format:   new kinds, each with its own fingerprint beside the Trio one; format v1, its `codebook_fingerprint` and
              Tetra48 untouched. A v2 header needs a rate field per matrix.
    Cost:     encoder generalization 3 to 5 days at $0; kernels 1 to 2 weeks; a card bench about $0.5.
    First:    $0 on the Mac, `llvq-bench`: Gaussian retention of each member (prefixes r = 7 to 13, direct ranks of
              2 and 3 bits) at its own rate. Gate: every prefix member within 0.25 b/dim of the bound, and the 87-bit
              member at relative MSE no worse than int4 g128 RTN's 0.00997. Then the H-weighted error on the 84 int4
              matrices of the 4B (revue-compression measure 2) before any kernel.
    Closed:   none. E3, `Golay70` and E1v decoded the ball's index; this keeps Tetra's decoder.

REJECTED, WITH THE REASON
-------------------------
- Entropy-coded or shaped Tetra in VRAM: 0.025 b/weight of lossless slack and at most 0.10 b/param of lossy gain,
  paid in decode instructions on rows that are decode-bound (verdict 2).
- Another lattice or a trellis code: Λ24 has the smallest G known up to dimension 48, and codebook changes did not
  move MMLU here (verdict 3).
- Per-block rate inside a matrix: the rotation flattens what it would exploit (verdict 3).
- Tying or factorising the embedding and the head: needs training.
- Head below int4: every practitioner keeps it at 5 to 6 bits; the speed lever on the head is plan-300's K5, at the
  same bits.

TOP THREE AND THEIR FIRST EXPERIMENT
------------------------------------
1. S1, the cheap chain. Look for the row-scale checkpoints in the bucket, then score step 1,210 against the final
   scales on the 4B, on the Mac at $0. Gate: paired interval contains 0, half-width under 0.6 pp.
2. S2, sensitivity allocation on the kinds the file has. The 4B's 252 coefficients on the Mac at $0. Gate: the map
   ranks the measured 4B swaps as the censuses did.
3. S3, the untied input embedding. Qwen3-8B f16 with only that table swapped, paired perplexity on the Mac at $0.
   Gate: within +0.12 % of int4 g64.
Each local run needs a go (rule 1, operator memory of 2026-11-09: no local compute without one, never two heavy jobs
at once). None costs card money.

WHAT THIS NOTE DID NOT CHECK
----------------------------
- The 47.4 bits are a sum of marginal entropies, an upper bound on the joint; the joint was not estimated.
- The 7-to-14 ratio assumes `trioencbench`'s 329 µs on a Mac core transfers to the card machine's cores.
- MoE expert loads for Qwen3-30B-A3B: only gpt-oss-20b's histogram exists.
- HIGGS and Q-Palette numbers are on Llama, at 3 bits and above; none is on Qwen3 at 2 bits.
- BitsMoE, MoEQuant and EAQuant were read in abstract or summary form through the fetch tool; their tables were not
  re-derived.
