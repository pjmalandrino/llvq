WHY A SECOND KERNEL AT ALL
--------------------------
tv_q4_metal stages the whole activation in threadgroup memory, d_in x 4 bytes
against the 32,768 an M-series offers, so it refuses d_in above 8,192. Every
sealed file's down_proj is above it: 9,728 at the 4B, 12,288 at the 8B, 17,408
at the 14B. M1 left the 84 int4 records materialized, which was 3.1 GB of the
5.436 the loaded model allocated.

THE GATE
--------
Bit-identical to the served kernel where the served kernel runs
(python -m llvqhf.checkq4):

  84 int4 records, 3 distinct shapes
    layers.0.self_attn.v_proj  1024 by 2560: identical at tiles 256, 2048, 8192
    layers.0.self_attn.o_proj  2560 by 4096: identical at tiles 256, 2048, 8192
    layers.12.mlp.down_proj    2560 by 9728: past the served wall, against the
                                             dense reconstruction 2.90e-07
                                             relative, passes
    6 comparisons identical, 1 shape checked against the dense reconstruction

Equality of every f32, not a tolerance. The two kernels are the same terms in the
same order, which is the claim of the prereg's section 3: a tile of a multiple of
256 columns is a multiple of 32 words, so lane l walks l, l+32, l+64 ... within
each tile, and the concatenation over tiles is the sequence the untiled kernel
walks. Nothing about that is empirical.

Then the model, the same four prompts and the same 64 greedy ids each
(python -m llvqhf.comparetokens):

  4 prompts, every id identical  -> 256 of 256

THE CONTROLS, AND ONE OF THEM MATTERED
--------------------------------------
Refusals, controls 1 and 2, each firing by name
(python -m llvqhf.checkq4guards):

  tile 128  -> "tile_cols 128 is not a multiple of 256. A tile of tile_cols/8
                words must be a multiple of 32 or the lanes interleave ..."
  tile 300  -> same refusal
  d_in 2555 -> "d_in 2555 is not a multiple of 8. Eight nibbles a word is what
                makes a row start on a word ..."
  tile 16384-> "staging 16384 columns wants 65536 B of threadgroup memory
                against 32768. That is the wall the tiled entry point exists for"

Mutants, control 4, on o_proj 2560 by 4096, caught at 3 of 3 tiles each:

  the group index shifted by one       row 0  -2.13041 vs -1.91389
  the nibble order reversed            row 0  -2.13041 vs  0.485418
  the lane stride moved off 32         row 0  -2.13041 vs -2.70310

The mutant writer nearly produced a void mutant, and the assertion is what
stopped it. tv_q4_metal_tiled comes FIRST in the shader and the served
tv_q4_metal after it, so replacing a line "in the tiled kernel" by partitioning
on its name hits BOTH functions. The reference would have moved with the subject
and all three mutants would have read as survived. The site count assertion fired
at 2 sites instead of 1, and the region is now closed at the next entry point,
with an assertion that the served kernel is not inside it. This is the fourth
time the rule of docs/METHODE.md on counting sites has paid for itself.

Control 6, nothing upstream moved: checkdecode 168 records, 118,665,216 blocks,
every point identical to the numpy decode, 38.4 s. checkdense 253 digests
identical to llvq_artifact::decode_matrix, 176.7 s. The suite, 39 passed.

Four for four, the first clean sheet on this branch, and the memory point
estimate landed exactly: 5.436 - 3.1 + 0.41 = 2.75 against 2.750 measured. The
arithmetic of the prediction was a subtraction of things already measured, which
is why it is not luck. It is also not a hard prediction to make, and the score
should be read next to the six earlier misses, not instead of them.

WHAT M2 DOES NOT ESTABLISH
--------------------------
NO SPEED, AT ANY STAGE. The per-prompt wall times in the run log are 62.2, 80.3,
149.4 and 255.0 s against M1's 141.4, 111.2, 96.9 and 72.6 s for the same four
prompts. Those swings are machine contention, not a kernel, and the two runs are
not comparable to each other. Nobody may read a regression or a gain out of
them. A throughput figure needs a quiet machine and its own prereg.

Nothing about the embedding, which is still 1.558 GB dense. That is M3.

Nothing about the served Rust path, untouched, which still refuses d_in above
8,192 in MetalRuntime::upload_int4. The Metal wall of the served engine is not
lifted by this, only the torch path's.

Nothing about the 8B and the 14B, whose down_proj are 12,288 and 17,408 and
would exercise more tiles. The argument of section 3 does not depend on the
width, but it has not been run there.

Nothing about quality. The 256 ids are four prompts of 64 greedy tokens. The
tail mutant of M1 survived 8 ids on all four prompts while the per-row error went
from 2.61e-5 to 8.79e-2, so this gate is known to be weak against a small
arithmetic drift. Here it is backed by bit-equality on the two shapes the served
kernel can take, which is much stronger, and by 2.90e-07 on the third. Whether
the per-row check becomes a gate in its own right is an open operator decision.
