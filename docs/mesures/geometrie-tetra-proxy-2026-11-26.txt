1. THE QUESTION
---------------
Tetra reads 0.375 dB above the ball of the paper's LLVQ on Gaussian blocks, MSE 0.08506 against 0.0780 a dimension
(*measured*, innovation-tetra-v2-forme section 1). That is 0.375 / 6.02 x 24 = 1.5 bits of a 48-bit block
(*computed*). About 0.30 dB of it sits in the three per-section row caps: the codebook is a product of three
8-dimension sets where the source wants one 24-dimension ball. The same note measured two repairs. One budget shared
by the three sections reads -0.30 dB for +25 to 40 % of decode instructions. A 2-bit selector among four width
profiles, its candidate k, reads -0.183 dB for +3 to 5 % and a 64 KiB table.
The operator asked whether a change of geometry could avoid the loss. Four repairs were proposed.
  1. Prefix profiles. A prefix code of variable length, read by a count of leading zeros, picks the three index
     widths. A profile of prefix length l has widths summing to 33 - l, so the word stays at 47 bits.
  2. Tetrad pairings. The 24 coordinates split into six tetrads of a sextet. The union of any two tetrads is an
     octad, so each of the 15 pairings is a trio with the same 64-state trellis. The encoder picks, per block, the
     pairing that balances the three section energies; the decoder permutes six groups of four coordinates.
  3. Pair words. One 96-bit word for two blocks, six sections under one prefix profile.
  4. Sparse corrections. A side list repairs the worst blocks and leaves the served kernel and format v1 alone.

2. THE MODEL, AND THE TWO THAT FAILED
-------------------------------------
The served code is shape-gain: the reconstruction is gain x y / |y|, so every lattice point of the codebook is a
direction. A region of radial extent rho(u) along the unit direction u holds rho(u)^24 / (24 V0) points a unit of
solid angle. The model takes the shape error of a block x as |x|^2 n(u)^(-2/23), with n(u) that density. A section of
index width w has radius 2^((w - 11) / 8). Tetra is the product of radii (1, 2^(-1/8), 1): the middle section indexes
2,048 rank vectors over both classes, the end sections 2,048 a class. Total dB uses the measured split of Tetra's
0.08506, shape 0.07652 and gain 0.00854, with the gain part held constant.
Calibration targets, both *measured* in innovation-tetra-v2-forme: the ball at -0.375 dB and k at -0.183 dB.

  model                                     ball       k         verdict
  measured                                  -0.375     -0.183
  1. hard overload outside the region       -0.218     -0.316    rejected: k beats the ball, the wrong order
  2. direction density, sum over members    -0.580     -0.380    rejected: factors 0.65 and 0.48 disagree
  3. direction density, max over members    -0.580     -0.297    kept: factors 0.647 and 0.616

Model 2 is wrong for a stated reason. The members of a union cut the same lattice, so a point two members hold is
indexed twice and adds no direction. The density of a union is the maximum over its members, which is model 3.
Model 3 overstates both targets by nearly one factor, 0.375 / 0.580 = 0.647 and 0.183 / 0.297 = 0.616 (*computed*).
Section 3 multiplies every model figure by 0.62 to 0.65. Two points fix one factor and leave no point to test it.

3. THE FOUR REPAIRS
-------------------
400,000 Gaussian blocks, seed 20261126, paired against Tetra on the same blocks (model SE 0.001 dB). The members of
a union are picked by a greedy search under the Kraft sum, on 6,000 other blocks. All figures *computed*.

  candidate (47 + 1 bits a block)                            model dB   calibrated dB      rank table
  ball, the ceiling of the family                            -0.580     -0.375 measured    none
  k, 4 profiles, 2-bit selector                              -0.297     -0.183 measured    64 KiB

  1. prefix profiles, 13 profiles, widths up to 12           -0.241     -0.15 to -0.16     32 KiB

Reading the table.
- Prefix profiles add 0.01 to 0.02 dB to k. Fifteen profiles do what four hand-picked ones did. The profile family
  is saturated near -0.2 dB, and the remaining 0.1 dB to the shared budget is not reached by power-of-two widths.
- Tetrad pairings with widths up to 11 read about what k reads, with no row added to the table. This is the one new
  fact. The 11-11 note named the 64 KiB table as k's speed risk (L1 room is worth up to 17.6 % of Tetra row speed,
  banc-252). The permutation of six groups of four coordinates in the decoder was not counted in instructions.
- Pairings alone at equal widths lose most of it: the three selector bits cost more than the balance returns.
- A pair word reads what k reads applied to each block. Sharing the budget between two blocks adds nothing here.
- The best figure, -0.21 dB, is 0.35 to 0.71 MMLU at 4B under the law of innovation-tetra-v2-forme section 3
  (1.68 to 3.36 points a dB, *computed*), against that note's bar of +3.4. CERVEAU Q4 holds that under about 0.4 dB
  a codebook change has not carried its sign to the model.

Repair 4, concentration of Tetra's excess shape error over the ball, block by block (model 3, before calibration):
  the top 1 % of blocks carry 7.0 % of the excess, the top 5 % carry 25.2 %, the top 10 % 41.8 %, the top 25 % 75.2 %.
  Tetra is finer than the ball on 25.9 % of blocks.
Half the excess needs corrections on more than 10 % of blocks. At 16 bits a correction and a position, that is over
0.1 b/weight for under 0.2 dB (*estimated*), where 0.1 b/weight of plain rate is worth 0.6 dB. Repair 4 is dead.

4. PRIOR ART
------------
- Repairs 1 and 3 are the multi-rate lattice quantizer of AMR-WB+: 8-dimension sub-vectors on the Gosset lattice
  RE8, each with a codebook of variable size, the size sent as a unary code, under a fixed bit budget a frame
  (*reported*: Xie and Adoul 1996; Ragot, Bessette and Lefebvre, ICASSP 2004; US patent 7,106,228,
  https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7106228).
- The shared budget of the 11-11 note is shell mapping: one energy budget over several sub-constellations at a fixed
  rate, 16 dimensions in the V.34 modem (*reported*: Laroia, Farvardin and Tretter 1994, US patent 5,388,124,
  https://patents.google.com/patent/US5388124).
- Repair 4 is the outlier list of SpQR and OWQ (ROADMAP-QUALITY row 18).
- Repair 2: one web search on 2026-11-26 found no earlier use of the sextet pairings to shape a sectioned Leech
  code. It returned the LLVQ paper itself (arXiv 2603.11021) and nothing closer. One search does not establish
  novelty. The parts are classical: sextets, the group M24, permutation decoding of the Golay code.
- The three-section trellis Tetra reads is Forney's coset-code construction of 1988.
