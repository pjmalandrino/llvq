Three of four. The prereg said a departure on GB read "would be a defect in one

3 % and it is not resolved here. `rtbits` reads 1.9908 b/weight of stream over

it does NOT bill tetra48's 4.1 MB row landing pad, following the convention the
slot arm's 20-byte pad and the planes arm's 4-byte pad already set. That pad is
0.4 % of the stream and does not close a 3 % gap.

THE FINDING BESIDE THE HEADLINE
--------------------------------
Read the GB/s column. Tetra sustains **232 GB/s** where Planes14 sustains 425
and AWQ 585, on the same card in the same process.

So Tetra reads 2.3 times fewer bytes than Planes14 and is only 1.25 times
faster: it is no longer traffic-bound. Against the FP16 arm's 654 GB/s it turns
**35 %** of its byte bound into time, where Planes14 turns 65 % and AWQ 89 %.

That is the decode, and it is the same mechanism `tile-sweep-2026-09-09`
measured from the other side: the Tetra word costs three 11-bit table reads, the
tables have to stay resident, and TILE_BLOCKS 128 -> 32 returned 42 % on
Blackwell by leaving them their L1. This run used the served 128 and did not
sweep. Whether 128 is also wrong on sm_89 is now the obvious question, and it is
cheap.

AGAINST QTIP, WITH ITS RESERVATION
-----------------------------------
QTIP is NOT in this run: `arms.rs` carries HAS_KERNEL[qtip] = false and refuses
the name. Its row in `echelle-formats.csv` is the 2026-08-21 measurement, a
different process, which `docs/data/README.md` forbids putting beside others.
The prereg states that before the fact.

The common baseline reproduces: Planes14 read 5.133 ms in August and 5.135 here,
0.04 % apart. On that basis QTIP's 2.246 ms is **1.83x** Tetra's 4.107.

**And the August journal's own reservation stands**: t(QTIP) 2.246 ms is BELOW
the bench's floor, t(nullk) 2.340 ms here and 2.306 then. A kernel that reads
weights cannot beat one that reads none. Until that is explained, 1.83x is not a
number to publish as a speed verdict.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH
-----------------------------
- **Nothing in tokens a second.** This times 252 projection matvecs, not a
  token. The repository has measured that 48 % of a token is outside the
  matmuls, and the end-to-end figures show it: Planes14 88.5 tok/s against
  Tetra 101.5, a ratio of 1.15 where this bench reads 1.25.
- Nothing about quality. The ball arms read the August object and tetra48 reads
  the September fine-tuned one, two different models (E2). For a kernel bench
  that is the intended setup; no quality claim is taken from it.
- Nothing about other cards. One L40S, the served tile.
- The 3 % on GB read is open.
