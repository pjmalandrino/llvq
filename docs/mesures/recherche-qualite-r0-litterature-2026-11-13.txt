R0 LITERATURE: QUALITY OF WEIGHT-QUANTIZED LLMS AT 2 TO 3 BITS, KNOWN, REFUTED AND PUBLISHED, 2026-11-13. $0.
=============================================================================================================
Result: no published study compares the task accuracy of a quantized LLM with Gaussian noise of the same amplitude on
weights or logits and finds a clear answer; the one Gaussian control found is inconclusive (section 1d). The closest
published fact to N1 is that quantization shrinks decision margins multiplicatively: a margin model m' = c m + b + e
beats three additive-noise models by BIC in all 642 damaged conditions over 16 models, with a median slope c of 0.29 at
3 bits and 0.00 at 2 bits under RTN (*reported*, [S42]). Two other papers find the weight error structured: at 2 bits
it lies in the signal subspace (alignment about 0.8 against 0.3 at 4 bits, [S28]), and on one 32B int4 tensor an error
coherent along the gradient gave 5.18 times the KL of the incoherent prediction ([S34]). One practitioner tool reports
the opposite sign on weights: isotropic weight noise overestimates the KL sensitivity of LDLQ error by up to about 6x
([S20]). Nothing published measures an interaction between attention and MLP errors (N2). Layer pairs interact with
both signs in CNNs and a ViT ([S46]); in pretrained Qwen3-32B consecutive blocks cancel about 50 % of the added error
at NVFP4 ([S4]). Per-module studies put down_proj, then up_proj, then gate_proj above attention on Qwen2.5 under FP4
([S33]), and call Qwen3-8B balanced between MLP and attention at 4 bits ([S28]). Output-aware objectives gain 1.8 to 2.8
MMLU points and 5.8 to 10.7 GSM8K points over LDLQ on Qwen3-8B at 2 bits (*computed* from [S1]). Eight sources report
cases where perplexity and task accuracy move in opposite directions (section 8). The one paper that asks for a cheap
metric that ranks near-baseline quantizations finds none: KL correlates at rho +0.00 and -0.24 in that zone ([S27]).
Nothing published analyses how gate and up weight errors combine through the SwiGLU product.
Nature: literature only. No build, no run, no model load, no job. Web search and fetch on 2026-11-13. Arithmetic by
hand, each line states its inputs. Nothing here estimates what any mechanism would gain on the project's object, and no
experiment is proposed.

LABELS AND CONVENTIONS
----------------------
*reported (Sn)*: the figure as source Sn publishes it, read on the URL listed in SOURCES. "abstract only" on a line
means the full text was not read for that claim. *computed*: arithmetic done here on published figures, inputs on the
line. pp = percentage points. "2-bit" means the source's own accounting, which usually excludes embeddings and head.
Model names are the sources' own. MMLU shot counts differ between sources and are given when the source gives them.
N1, N2 and the background facts are the anomalies and claims of `docs/CERVEAU.md` (Q4 to Q22). N1 was measured with
Gaussian noise on logits (Q7), not on weights; section 1 keeps the two apart.

1. STRUCTURED ERROR AGAINST NOISE (QUESTION A, N1)
--------------------------------------------------
1a. Margins shrink multiplicatively. Wu, Dhiman, Koshiyama, arXiv 2608.06564 v5, 2026-10-05 [S42]. 16 models of 8
families, RTN at 4, 3 and 2 bits, plus AWQ-style scaling, GPTQ and GGUF; 1,092 items including MMLU and BoolQ. The
logit margin between two first-token candidates is fitted as m' = c m + b + e, e roughly Gaussian (*reported*).
- Median surviving fraction c: 0.86 at 4 bits, 0.29 at 3 bits, 0.00 at 2 bits (*reported*).
- The multiplicative model beats constant noise, noise with drift and margin-scaled noise by BIC in all 642 conditions
  with c < 0.70; BIC gaps +28.8 to +1031.2 in the core decision kinds (*reported*).
- The offset b is shared across decisions of one kind and acts as a penalty on one token, a systematic shift
  (*reported*). Global rescaling or a bias offset cannot change a sign, and no label-free repair tested wins on most
  models (*reported*).
- A Gaussian-tail formula from fitted c, b, sigma predicts held-out flip rates to a median 1.0 pp (90th percentile 7.8)
  over 1,082 conditions (*reported*). Constants copied from another model miss by a median 26.8 pp at 3 bits.
- The paper describes the mechanism and does not explain it (*reported*, its stated limit).

1b. Error aligned with the signal at 2 bits. Zhou et al., "From Signal Degradation to Computation Collapse", arXiv
2604.19884, Findings of ACL 2026 per the abstract page [S28]. Llama-3.1-8B, with Qwen3-8B, Mistral-7B and Gemma-2-9B.
- Alignment of the error subspace with the signal subspace about 0.8 at 2 bits and 0.3 at 4 bits; activation-subspace
  similarity to FP16 above 0.8 at 4 bits and near zero at 2 bits (*reported*).
- The paper calls 4-bit error "resembling random noise" without a random-noise control (*reported*, as fetched).
- Training-free repair (layer protection, logit amplification) recovers 4-bit failure-subset accuracy; protection and
  EoRA low-rank compensation fail at 2 bits, numbers not given (*reported*).

1c. Error coherent with the gradient costs more KL. Lu, Ni, arXiv 2611.12634, 2026-11-09, preregistered [S34]. The
second-order prediction KL = 1/2 D' F D, with a diagonal Fisher, holds within 0.95 to 1.08 on Qwen3-8B (trimmed, 18
cells) at int8, fp8 and int4 (*reported*). Stochastic rounding raises KL 2.25x against 2.28x predicted. On Qwen3-32B
int4 the diagonal form misses by 5.18x (trimmed); the last layer's down projection carries most of the excess, two of
its input columns correlate at 0.94 across sequences, and a tensor-block form brings the ratio to 1.13 at a quarter
of the perturbation and 1.46 at full scale (*reported*). The localization is post hoc and the preregistered follow-up
on that tensor read "mixed" (*reported*). The paper states that the diagonal form assumes rounding errors
sign-incoherent along the gradient. It runs no iid-noise arm. Ratios re-read by the main session on 2026-11-13.

1d. Gaussian controls at equal power. Khan, L, Misri, arXiv 2611.14413, 2026-11-10, abstract only [S45]. Qwen3-0.6B and
1.7B, simulated RTN at 8, 6, 4, 3 bits, ARC, MMLU, HellaSwag, 300 items each. A Gaussian noise control at equal power
was inconclusive on whether rounding adds answer-slot skew. Flip rates stayed below an accuracy-matched baseline of
independent uniform errors (*reported*). Answer-slot skew at 4 bits rose from 0.15 to 0.53 on the 0.6B (*reported*).

1e. Weight noise in the other direction. ExLlamaV3 optimizer documentation, turboderp, GitHub, undated [S20].
Isotropic weight noise can overestimate a tensor's KL sensitivity "by up to ~6x" where input statistics are anisotropic,
v_proj worst. LDLQ error is steered away from directions the data exercises. Noise shaped by the per-layer Hessian
ranks tensors at Spearman 0.84 against real swaps, isotropic noise at 0.61 (*reported*). KL fits S_t rfn^alpha with
alpha about 2.0, steeper (about 2.35) once a tensor is mostly noise (*reported*). Practitioner documentation, not
peer reviewed.

1f. Noise models used as stand-ins. HIGGS (Malinovskii et al., arXiv 2411.17525 v1, 2024, venue not stated on the page
read) [S2] estimates per-layer coefficients with Gaussian noise scaled to each layer's norm. Its linearity
theorem, E[PPL] ~ PPL* + sum alpha_l t_l^2, assumes a near-diagonal scaled Hessian and small errors. Predictions track
measured perplexity above about 3 bits and diverge below (*reported*, Figure 1 as described). No side-by-side of real
error against equal-norm noise was found in the part read (first 100,000 characters). SLQ (Helcig, Kurtic, Alistarh,
arXiv 2605.02404, 2026-05-04) [S6] builds its KL-to-accuracy curve from uniform noise scaled to each matrix's std:
accuracy holds while KL < 0.01 and falls about linearly with KL in the near-lossless regime (*reported*). Symmetric
grids flip up to about 3x more top-1 tokens than asymmetric ones at low entropy on Llama-3.1-8B at 4 bits; the paper
attributes it to a centering inefficiency gamma that multiplies noise variance by gamma^2 (*reported*).

1g. Mean shift and bias. D2Quant (Yan et al., arXiv 2602.02546 v2, 2026, ICML keyword) [S7]: quantizing attention
gives "a clear mean shift" at the post-attention LayerNorm; MLP quantization gives much less (*reported*, figures,
no magnitudes in text). Norm Tweaking (Li et al., arXiv 2309.02784, AAAI 2024 per the AAAI link in search) [S12]:
quantized activations drift from zero mean layer by layer, attributed partly to GPTQ updates (*reported*).
Bias correction for 8-bit CNNs (Nagel et al., ICCV 2019, abstract only) [S36]: quantization error is biased and the
bias is corrected (*reported*).

1h. The output error is mostly a rotation. Chen, Beyer, Zhu, Chen, arXiv 2609.11716, 2026-09-10 [S4]. NVFP4 weights,
Qwen3 (incl. 32B and a MoE), OLMo3, Gemma3, OLMoE; RTN, GPTQ, AWQ on Qwen3-4B.
- 88.9 to 98.7 % of the squared relative error of the final hidden state is angular; mean rotation 12.47 degrees
  (*reported*).
- A block's new error opposes its inherited error: about 50.2 % of the cumulative added error cancels in pretrained
  Qwen3-32B, at most 0.16 % a layer at random init (*reported*). Removing the cancellation multiplies final relative
  error by 2.94 (*reported*).
- Random-init weights with the same reconstruction cosine accumulate 5.5x (absolute) more final hidden error
  (*reported*). This is the only "same weight error, different damage" comparison found, and it is not a noise arm.
- About 0.28 % of positions show large norm distortion that the cancellation does not contain (*reported*).

1i. Outlier weights. The super weight (Yu et al., arXiv 2411.07191 v2) [S11]: one weight in an early-layer
mlp.down_proj (layer 2 of Llama-7B, 0-indexed); pruning it takes Llama-7B zero-shot from 70.11 to 35.14 and Wiki-2
perplexity from 5.67 to 1211.11, while pruning the next 7,000 outliers gives 69.22 and 6.08 (*reported*).

1j. Signal-to-noise at the task. Zhou et al., arXiv 2608.08188 v1, 2026-08-08 [S5]. 10 models including Qwen3 0.6B to
14B, RTN, GPTQ, AWQ, SpQR at W8 to W2. Source SNR = M + S - N in dB, N the activation energy along the error direction;
GSM8K has the lowest source SNR, driven by a larger N (*reported*). The method shapes the alignment, the bit width the
magnitude (*reported*). No equal-norm noise arm. Per-module results are in a supplement not read.

What was not found for A: a published downstream-accuracy comparison between a quantizer's weight error and random
weight noise of equal norm at 2 to 3 bits; a measurement of systematic row- or column-norm loss after weight
quantization tied to accuracy; an analysis of error alignment with top singular directions measured against accuracy
(LQER and QERA came up as snippets only, section 11).

offers no mechanism (*reported*). Down's input tail ratio is 80 to 334 against 5.5 to 11 for up and gate.

2b. Interactions.
- CLADO (Deng et al., DAC 2025, arXiv 2307.05657 v2) [S46]: pairwise cross-layer terms occur with both signs; ResNet-34
  layers (12,19) real loss change 0.273 against 0.255 without cross terms (super-additive), layers (22,23) cross term
  -0.070 (sub-additive). Dropping cross terms lowers ViT accuracy at 29.84 MB from 66.60 to 54.42 % (*reported*). No LLM.
- IMPQ (Zhao et al., arXiv 2509.15455, 2025) [S8]: Shapley-based interactions for layer bit allocation on Qwen3-4B/8B,
  Llama, Gemma, 2.01 to 3.99 bits; Wiki-2 perplexity only; Qwen3-4B GPTQ 2.01 to 2.5 bits 697.28 against 1.56e3 for
  sensitivity-only (*reported*). No sign or magnitude of interactions is reported, and the unit is the whole layer.
- Counteraction across blocks [S4], 1h above: inherited and new block errors have negative cosine in 82.5 % of blocks
  (Qwen3-32B, C4, NVFP4). Not split by sublayer in the part read (first 200,000 characters).
- HIGGS [S2] assumes additivity across layers through its diagonal assumption.
- Error growth with depth: QEP (Arai, Ichikawa, NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2504.09629) [S39] states quantization error grows
  across layers and gives no per-layer ratio in the text read. A domino test in [S28] quantizes layers 0 to k at 2 bits
  on Llama-3.1-8B: failure-subset accuracy 15.03 % at k = 0, 0.38 % at k = 5 (*reported*).
What was not found for B: any published measurement of attention-by-MLP interaction (super- or sub-additive) at any bit
width in an LLM; any per-module restore study at 2 to 3 bits on Qwen3 read on MMLU.

4. PROXIES, FLIPS AND FRAGILE EXAMPLES (QUESTION D, N4)
-------------------------------------------------------
- Dutta et al., "Accuracy is not all you need", arXiv 2407.09141, NeurIPS 2024 per the page [S3]. MMLU 5-shot: GPTQ
  W4A16 on Llama2-7b chat -2.36 accuracy with 13.62 % flips (*reported*). KL and flips correlate at Spearman 0.981 on
  MMLU. Answers flip when the top margin is low; Llama2-70b correct answers carry a top margin of 0.758, incorrect ones
  0.434; with BnB 4-bit, 5.0 % of correct and 13.2 % of incorrect answers change (*reported*). Perplexity stays 4.57
  to 4.72 while KL ranges from 7.9e-5 to 0.03 on Llama2-13b (*reported*).
- Proskurina et al., NAACL 2024 Findings, arXiv 2405.00632 [S13]: 4-bit GPTQ lowers confidence in the true label, most
  on samples where the full model was already unconfident (*reported*, abstract; tables not readable in the fetch).
- Noise floor of a comparison, arXiv 2611.15671, 2026 [S44]: on Clef-flash (9B), questions with reference top
  probability under 0.7 are 16.3 % of the set and hold 100 % of Q8_0 flips, 98 % of FP8, 90 % of Q4_K_M, 86 % of NVFP4.
  NVFP4 flips 11 of 18,125 questions where the reference is at least 90 % confident (*reported*). Serving-side changes
  move 0 to 0.92 % of decisions; Q4_K_M 3.5 to 5.8 %.
- Nikolic et al., "Displacement is not direction", arXiv 2606.19558 v1, 2026-06-17 [S27]. 28 Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and 41
  Devstral-Small-2-24B quantizations, 14 metric variants. KL against composite score: rho -0.72 and -0.86 over each
  cohort, +0.00 and -0.24 (p 0.36) in the near-baseline zone, -0.80 and -0.94 in the lossy zone (*reported*). KL tracks
  the number of flips (rho +0.94 and +0.55) and not their direction (partial rho -0.35, p 0.17; -0.20, p 0.46). No
  metric ranked near-baseline quantizations; per-prompt KL as a router picks the passing model 42.3 to 49.4 % of the time
  (*reported*).
- Margin model [S42], 1a above: predicts flip rates, not accuracy, and needs the quantized model's logits.
- Signal-to-noise [S5]: typical-channel SNR orders retention by bit width at 98.3 % agreement, by method 86.8 to 89.4 %,
  by scale 87.7 to 89.2 % (*reported*). The authors frame it as a relative internal measure, not an accuracy predictor.
- SLQ [S6]: from noise injection, accuracy holds below KL 0.01 and falls about linearly with KL in the near-lossless
  regime; MMLU-Pro is called a sharper test than zero-shot suites (*reported*).
- YAQA [S1] App. A.4: KL and perplexity "measure two fundamentally different quantities" (*reported*).
- Correctness Agreement [S29], abstract only: behaviour shifts while accuracy and perplexity hold.
- Agents [S43], abstract only: quantized failure sets rank-correlate at >= 0.94 with full-precision ones; 0.18 % of
  failure events are new (*reported*). The search snippet's "0.97 correlation with closest-competitor errors" was not
  on the page fetched.
What was not found for D: a published cheap metric shown to predict MMLU or GSM8K of a quantized model at fixed bit
rate among near-baseline candidates. [S27] reports that none of 14 variants does.

AQLM's appendix states the relative drop on MMLU and GSM8K is larger than on perplexity and zero-shot suites (*reported*).
QTIP [S18]: computed codes are not fine-tuned; "fine-tuning is not a silver bullet"; at 3 and 4 bits fine-tuning has no
effect for computed codes (*reported*). NestQuant [S25], ICML 2025, abstract only: Gosset nested lattice, Llama-3-8B

Shape-gain and shrinkage. GSRQ (Kim et al., arXiv 2607.01065, 2026, KV cache) [S37]: the l2-optimal centroid's norm
is at most the members' mean norm and the gap grows with dimension; a gain-shape centroid lowers Wiki-2 perplexity from
19.50 to 12.77 at 0.375 bits per element (*reported*). WaterSIC [S38]: dequantizing off the grid with a shrink factor
matters at low rate (section 5). The LLVQ paper (Appendix I, as transcribed in the notes) reads radial drift as the
dominant failure without rotation: Euclidean GPTQ 191.90 against Spherical GPTQ 6.90 on Llama-2 7B. No paper was found
that measures a gain bias of shape-gain VQ on LLM weights and its effect on accuracy.

7. GATED-MLP MECHANICS (QUESTION G)
-----------------------------------
- Fishman, Chmiel, Banner, Soudry, arXiv 2409.12517 v2, 2025 [S10]: in SwiGLU the two weight vectors of a neuron align
  (w1 -> +-w2) under weak l2 regularisation once inputs are large, which makes the output quadratic in the input; seen
  after about 200B training tokens (*reported*). An FP8 training result, not a weight-quantization one.
- Activation spikes (Yang, Kim, Kim, arXiv 2405.14428, 2024) [S9]: the down_proj input of GLU models carries spikes from
  the gate-up product, in early and late layers and on a few tokens; quantizing only the top-4 modules by max-median
  ratio moves LLaMA-2-13B perplexity from 6.84 to 15.09 (*reported*). Activation quantization, W8A8.
- DaSS (Guo, Kamigaito, Watanabe, arXiv 2405.01943) [S32]: outliers matter less for pruning gate and up than down; at
  70 % sparsity on LLaMA2-7B, input-balanced magnitude pruning gives 10.52 against 12.42 for Wanda on gate/up, Wanda wins
  on down 6.97 against 9.03 (*reported*). SwiGLU intermediate norms are uneven across neurons.
- FP4 study [S33]: up as sensitive as down with a 30x lighter activation tail; gate below up with equal statistics.
- Depth registers (Liu, arXiv 2604.18128) [S31]: v2 is withdrawn; a 300M-parameter single-seed W4A4 study bounding the
  down_proj input by the product of its factor bounds. Not used further.
- Super weight [S11]: sits in down_proj.
What was not found for G: any published analysis of how weight errors in gate and up combine through the SwiGLU product
(multiplicative compounding, gate saturation, sparsity of the gated output) in weight quantization or pruning.

10. WHAT THE LITERATURE LEAVES OPEN
-----------------------------------
Listed in the order of the brief, not ranked.
- N1. No published paper finds which property of weight error makes it cost more task accuracy than Gaussian noise of
  the same logit amplitude. Published facts near it: multiplicative margin shrinkage with a shared offset [S42], error
  aligned with the signal at 2 bits [S28], gradient-coherent error on one tensor [S34], rotation of the final hidden
  state [S4]. Weight-side isotropic noise goes the other way in KL [S20]. The one equal-power Gaussian control is
  inconclusive [S45].
- N2. No published measurement of attention-by-MLP interaction in an LLM. Interaction signs are mixed in CNNs [S46],
  unreported in IMPQ [S8]; block-to-block cancellation [S4] is not split by sublayer.
- A. No equal-norm noise arm on downstream accuracy at 2 to 3 bits; no measurement tying row- or column-norm loss of
  quantized weights to accuracy; no study of error alignment with top singular directions against accuracy.
- B. No per-module restore study at 2 to 3 bits on Qwen3 dense models read on MMLU; [S28] covers 4 bits on a failure
  subset and 2 bits on Llama only, [S33] covers FP4 on perplexity.
- C. Output-aware objectives read on MMLU at 2 bits exist for Qwen3-8B (YAQA, D2Quant) and Llama-2 (GuidedQuant with
  LNQ); none on a 24-dimensional lattice code, none on Qwen3-4B, none reported with a frozen-code scale stage after it.
- D. No cheap metric shown to predict MMLU or GSM8K among near-baseline quantizations [S27].
- E. Two corrections are tied to a named error property and tested alone (D2Quant, Norm Tweaking); none to norm
  shrinkage of the weights on accuracy.
- F. No shape-gain gain-bias measurement on LLM weights; the 2-bit MMLU gaps for Qwen3-4B are the LLVQ paper's own.
- G. No analysis of gate and up weight errors combining through the SwiGLU product.
