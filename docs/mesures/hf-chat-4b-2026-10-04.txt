WHY NOT A SCORE
---------------
The operator asked for a model you can switch on and talk to, not an MMLU. The
reason holds: 63.37 against 70.14 says the gap exists, it does not say what it
costs you. Two answers read next to the FP16 ones say more about that, and say it
in ten minutes.

THE TWO PROMPTS, BOTH IN FRENCH
-------------------------------
1. "Explique en trois phrases ce qu'est une table de hachage, a quelqu'un qui
   debute."

   FP16: three sentences, as asked. Correct, and it names the property that
   matters, access in constant time.

   Tetra: four sentences, so the instruction was not followed. Content correct
   but vaguer, "recherches rapides" where FP16 says "temps constant", and one
   odd phrase, "les tables de mots".

2. "J'ai une liste de 10 millions d'entiers en Python et je veux les 100 plus
   grands. Quelle approche, et pourquoi pas un sort complet ?"

   FP16: leads with `heapq.nlargest(k, lst)`, which is the right answer, with
   code that runs.

   Tetra: leads with the full sort instead, the thing the question asked it to
   argue against, and its code is wrong: `liste_trier[-100]` returns one element
   where `[-100:]` returns a hundred. One spelling error, "propies" for
   "propres".

WHAT THIS LOOKS LIKE
--------------------
On these two prompts the FP16 answer is better, visibly, and the way it is better
is more useful than the 6.77 MMLU points: the losses show up as INSTRUCTION
FOLLOWING and as leading with the wrong approach, not as incoherence. The Tetra
answers are fluent, in the right language, on topic, and would pass unnoticed if
nobody held the FP16 one beside them.

Two prompts, one seed, greedy. This is not evidence of a rate and nothing here
licenses "Tetra follows instructions less well". It is a reason to look, and the
thing worth looking at next is instruction following rather than knowledge.

THE TIMINGS, AND A REAL USABILITY PROBLEM
-----------------------------------------
                          load        generation
  Tetra, f16 dense        151.6 s     35 to 37 tok/s
  FP16 checkpoint           4.5 s     35 to 38 tok/s

Generation is the same, which it should be: both are dense f16 matmuls on the
same device, and the Tetra arm has decoded its weights by then.

**The load is 34 times longer**, and that is the finding. The dense arm rebuilds
252 matrices in Python at load, in f64 then narrowed, which is two and a half
minutes before the first token. The fused arm loads in 7.7 s because it decodes
nothing, and then generates far slower because its kernel takes one activation
vector at a time. So today there is no arm that is quick to start AND quick to
talk, and a user has to pick which one hurts less.

Those two numbers are this machine under its own load. They are not divided by
anything and they are not a benchmark.
