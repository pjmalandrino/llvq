LARGE PREFILL AND CONTEXT: WHAT A CHAT USER WAITS FOR BESIDE THE DECODE RATE, AND WHAT THE TREE PASS CAN DO, SEALED 4B, L40S, 2026-11-11. $0.
=========================================================================================================================================
Result: the wait a chat user feels past a few hundred prompt tokens is the time to the first token, not the decode.
Today a turn with a 4,000-token prompt and a 256-token answer takes about 17.9 s, 14.3 answer tokens a second of wall
(*computed* from measured rates). The tree pass built this week can prefill the prompt as a chain of R rows a pass with
no new kernel: about 6.3 s for that turn at R = 16 (*estimated*). Its attention re-reads the whole KV cache once per row,
which is why its 16-row pass costs 48 ms at 8k. One read of the cache serving the R rows, plus R = 32 to 64, brings the
turn to about 2.4 to 3.6 s (*estimated*). The prefill then carries the pass's arithmetic into the K/V cache: it needs the
T8 band (paired perplexity within +-0.12 %), which nobody has run on the pass yet.

Nature: read-only study. No build, no run, no job, no model load. Sources are the code at 172e417 (this worktree) and
the journals named on each line. Labels: *measured* (a journal), *computed* (arithmetic on measured or code inputs),
*estimated* (judgement, or arithmetic with one estimated input).
Basis used throughout (all from journals):
  4E decode 227.7 tok/s at a 5-token prompt, 156.4 at 8,192 (spec-mem-m3 base-short, base-8k, *measured*);
  served-v9 sweep 226.2 / 205.8 / 194.2 / 161.5 tok/s at 5 / 2,048 / 4,096 / 8,192 (served-v9-2026-11-07, *measured*);
  a12ip pass (served words read in place, no copy) 9.56 ms at R = 1, 10.91 to 10.96 ms at R = 16 (spec-mem-m3, *measured*);
  a12ip pass at an 8,191-token prompt 48.155 ms, 92 passes, up to 16 rows (spec-mem-m3, *measured*);
  4E token 4.467 ms at the chat context (verify-tree-n11, *measured*).

1. PREFILL TODAY
----------------
How it runs (code, model.rs, fused_cuda.rs, session.rs):
- Session::feed cuts the new tokens into chunks of prefill_rows() = 4,096 and runs each through hidden_cached.
- Tetra projections go through tv_tetra48_h, 4 rows a launch (the served prefill pair 4x128).
- int4 projections go through tv_q4_hht one row a launch, and the rotations one launch per chunk of 4 rows.
- Attention among the prompt's rows is the grouped form: cuBLAS QK^T and PV, one f16 score tensor, softmax in place.
- About 486 row-granular operations a prompt token at about 8 us each (computed, debit-vague5 prefill.md section 2).
- So the claim "about one row at a time" holds for the projections; "at decode speed" holds for 4E's speed, not the
  eager decode's: 4.0 to 4.4 ms a prompt token against 4.42 ms a 4E token and 7.75 ms an eager token.
- Attention is a small share: 2.2 to 2.55 s of the 8k first token (estimated, prefill.md section 3.2).

Are the prompt's K/V bit for bit what the decode kernel would write? No, beyond layer 0.
- Layer 0's K and V come from the embedding, ln1 and the k/v projections: the rows kernel keeps the one-row kernel's
  per-row order, so they match (host test kernel_prefill_rows_are_the_kernel_rows; card evidence pic-vram-8k G3).
- Layers 1 on read the previous layers' attention, which the prefill computes with grouped cuBLAS and the decode with
  the kernel form. The two differ by 3.4e-2 to 3.9e-2 of max |dlogit| at 8,192 (gqa-ab-2026-11-01, measured).
- That mix is served under the +-0.12 % tolerance of ETAT section 4, not under identity.
- The NOT SERVED form prefill_attention kernel gives bit-for-bit decode rows (pic-vram-8k G3), at 1.14 to 1.21 times
  the first token and 1.1 GB over its band.

2. PREFILL THROUGH THE MULTI-ROW PASS
------------------------------------
The prompt's tokens are known, so a pass of R rows is a chain: depth[r] = r, path[r] = 0, 1, ..., r. Every row is
accepted, and the last row's argmax is the first answer token.

What exists already (code):
- Causal attention among the R new rows: p4t_attn_issue reads position p < pos0 from the cache and p >= pos0 from the
  tree buffer slot path[r][p - pos0] (pers_4et.cu lines 622-641). A chain is a tree.
- K/V writes for R rows: each row's K and V go to the tree buffer tk/tv, and the next launch's first phase moves the
  accepted rows into the cache, up to P4T_RMAX = 32 (pers_4et_a12.cu lines 128-139, spec_loop.rs step 5).
- A chain is already a legal draft: spec_loop.rs says "a chain of n tokens is parents 0, 1, ..., n - 1".
- The identity contract: row r depends on row r and its ancestors only, not on R nor its slot (pers_4et.cu header).
  So a chain prefill gives the same bits at any R, and equals the pass's own R = 1 decode of the prompt.
- a12 writes the five DFlash taps of every row: a chain prefill hands the drafter its prompt states for free.
- Optional logits for every row (L.logits, [R][vocab] binary16): the same pass can score perplexity windows.

What is missing:
- A host driver: prompt to chains of R, then a final move. Under the spec loop the first one-row pass moves the last
  chunk; under 4E a move-only launch (or 4E taking the move) does not exist.
- Head on the last row only. Today every row runs the head, about 0.45 to 0.49 ms a pass (n11 vt16 head phase).
- Rows above 16 on the in-place arm. a12ip is compiled at RC = 2 (8 x RC = 16 rows, refused above). The a10 family
  carries RC = 4 (32 rows) but reads the tree-tiled copies (+1.31 GB, ruled out by the VRAM rule).
- Attention that reads the cache once for the R rows (section 3).

What bounds the row count above 16:
- Registers. A unit's accumulators are RC n8 tiles of 4 floats: R / 2 floats a lane. At 384 threads a SM's 65,536
  registers allow 170 a thread, and a12ip already uses 168 with 744 B local. R = 32 needs 512 threads at 128 registers
  (a10's t512 form, 736 B local) or spills. R = 64 needs 256 threads (255 registers, 32 accumulators).
- Shared memory, area = max(prologue row, attention 59,520 B, P4T_MMA_FLOATS(nw, R)) + 2 x P4T_RMAX floats, plus
  19,072 B of header and tables, against 101,376 B on sm_89 (computed from pers_4et.cu lines 144-155):
    R     12 warps                     8 warps
    16    78,848 B (attention bound)   78,848 B
    32    78,848 B (attention bound)   78,848 B
    64    88,960 B                     78,848 B
    128   115,584 B, does not fit      101,248 B, fits by 128 B
- P4T_RMAX = 32 sizes every row buffer, the path rows (u8 [R][32]) and the move. R = 64 means raising it.

The row buffers, corrected: the 0.153 GB the loader prints is for P4T_RMAX = 32 rows, not 16 (computed from
persist4e_cuda.rs lines 1327-1354): activations and tree K/V 8.8 MB, attention split states 18.1 MB, slice partials
116.7 MB, logits 9.7 MB. The head's slice partials alone, 6 x 32 x 151,936 x 4 B, are 76 % of it. A prefill that runs
the head on its last row needs about 1.46 MB a row (0.275 activations, 0.566 split states, 0.623 gate/up partials)
plus 3.7 MB for one head row: 0.027 GB at R = 16, 0.050 at 32, 0.097 at 64 (computed).

Time to the first token through the pass (*estimated*). Model: ceil(N / R) passes x T(R), plus attention.
  T(16) = 10.93 ms (measured, a12ip); T(32) = 12.4 to 13.6 ms and T(64) = 15.2 to 19.0 ms (estimated: 0.09 to
  0.15 ms an added row, the measured a12ip slope of 0.091 ms, widened for 512 or 256 threads). Cross-check: the batch
  GEMM, the same arithmetic family, ran 128 rows at 4,467 rows/s, 0.224 ms a row with 128 attentions (batch-l40s-v4).
  Attention today: each row costs 2.33 ms at 8,192 positions ((48.155 - 10.93) / 16, computed), taken linear in the
  context. Over a prompt of N tokens: 2.33 ms x N^2 / (2 x 8,192), so 0.15 s at 1,024, 2.39 s at 4,096, 9.54 s at 8,192.
  D1 and D2 are the attention fixes of section 3.

VRAM it adds or removes (*computed*, base 2.014 GB measured):
- No score tensor and no eager chunk activations: the 8k peak falls from 5.95 to 6.01 GB (measured) to about 3.3 to
  3.4 GB (2.014 + KV 8,448 positions 1.246 + pass buffers 0.03 to 0.15).
- At 4,096 about 2.7 to 2.8 GB against about 4.0 today; at 1,024 about 2.23 to 2.35 against about 2.3.
- Below about 1,000 tokens the pass buffers are a net cost: +0.03 GB at R = 16 with prefill-sized buffers (+0.06 b/param
  of the whole model at peak), +0.15 GB with today's full set (+0.30 b/param). With the spec loop served, it is
  already paid.
- Risk: kprefill missed its computed peak by 1.1 GB (pic-vram-8k, not traced). The pass allocates nothing per prompt,
  so that trap should not apply; the card must show it.
- b/param of the file (2.73) does not move: no format change.

Quality status:
- The K/V and the first token come from the pass's arithmetic: f16 weights with scales folded, f16 activations, f32
  MMA accumulation. That is a T8 event for the served prefill.
- Evidence so far: on GSM8K-200 the pass's greedy text equals 4E's on 177 of 200 prompts and grades 167 against 166,

  the gate to run, now on the prefill: 16 windows of 2,048 scored through chain passes with all-row logits, paired
  against the served eager prefill; then GSM8K-200 with 4E decoding after a chain prefill, McNemar.
- D1 keeps the pass's bits. D2 changes the attention arithmetic and needs its own run of the same gate.

3. THE 48 MS PASS AT 8K
-----------------------
Why it costs about 8 tokens of 4E (48.155 ms against 6.15 ms a 4E token on the same text, measured), from the code:
- p4t_attention deals jobs over the grid: one job is (row r, split s of 256 positions, KV head h), rows one after the
  other (pers_4et.cu lines 661-669).
- Each job stages its own split's K and V for its own row and its 4 query heads (p4t_attn_issue, qs = that row's q).
- No job reads for another row. Row r reads the whole cache below pos0, so 16 rows read it 16 times.
- 4E reads it once a token (pers_attention, llvq_pers.cuh, one row).

Bytes (computed): 8 KV heads x 128 x 2 B x 2 (K and V) = 4,096 B a position a layer.
  per row, one layer at 8,192 positions        33.55 MB
  per row, 36 layers                           1.208 GB
  per 16-row pass                              19.33 GB
  per 4E token                                 1.208 GB
Jobs a layer: 16 x 8 x 33 = 4,224 for the pass, 264 for 4E, over 142 CTAs: about 30 against 2 a CTA (computed).
Rates (computed from measured walls): the pass spends 48.155 - 10.93 = 37.2 ms above its short-context wall, 19.3 GB
in 37.2 ms is 520 GB/s. 4E spends 1.77 to 2.00 ms above its short wall at 8,192 for 1.208 GB, 604 to 683 GB/s. Both
sit near DRAM rate (864 GB/s), so the per-layer working set of 33.5 MB is not being reused through the 96 MB L2.
Reading: the pass's 8k cost is 16 cache reads at about 4E's rate. Not established: no phase split was printed at 8k
(spec-mem-m3, NOT ESTABLISHED). The stamped launch exists (L.stamps); the first experiment prints it.

The fix on paper, one read of the cache serving the R queries, as the projections already decode a weight once for R:
- D1, same bits. A job becomes (split s, KV head h, group of Rg rows). The step stages the split's K/V tile once; each
  warp task is (unit, head pair, row) and runs today's pers_attn_tile on its own state. Positions p >= pos0 differ per
  row (tree slots), so the tree tail (at most 16 positions) and a row's last, partial split stay per row, as today.
  Each row keeps its tiles, units, splits and fold order, so its bits do not move (the ring depth already changed
  nothing in form 6). The bound is shared memory: a row's unit states and q weigh 10,368 B. Rg = 4 with a 2-stage
  ring needs 74.2 KB of area (today 59.8 KB); with the 19.1 KB of header and tables it fits under 101,376 B at one CTA
  a SM. Rg = 16 does not fit; it needs
  the states in registers.
  Expected at 8k, R = 16, Rg = 4: 4.8 GB read at 0.61 to 0.86 TB/s, 5.6 to 7.9 ms; scalar tiles for 16 rows 2.9 to 5.9 ms
  (264 M warp-instructions a row at 8k, IPC 2 to 4 on 142 SMs at 2.52 GHz), overlapped. Attention 6 to 10 ms against
  37.2; pass 17 to 21 ms, 2.7 to 3.4 tokens of 4E (estimated).
- D2, MMA attention. The 16 rows x 4 query heads of a KV head form a 64 x 128 query tile; QK^T and PV run on the tensor
  cores against the staged split, flash-decoding style, the online softmax in registers. One read: 1.2 GB, 1.4 to
  2.0 ms; MMA work small. Pass about 13 to 14 ms, 2.1 to 2.3 tokens of 4E (estimated). New arithmetic: the T8 gate again.
- For prefill the gain is larger: 8,192 rows over a growing cache read 4.9 TB today (computed), 1.24 TB under D1 at
  Rg = 4, about 0.08 TB under D2 at R = 64.

Does 4E's own attention at 8k have the same slack? No. 4E reads each cache byte once a token; there is no repeated read
to remove. Its slack is bandwidth efficiency only: 1.208 GB at 604 to 683 GB/s against 864 GB/s peak. At peak it would
save 0.37 to 0.60 ms a token, 161.5 to at most 172-179 tok/s at 8,192 (computed ceiling). Only fewer bytes (an int8 or
int4 KV cache) moves it further, and that touches quality (ETAT section 5).

5. THE FIRST EXPERIMENT
-----------------------
Chain prefill through the existing a12ip pass at R = 16. No kernel change: a host driver in specbench that feeds the
prompt as chains of 16 (depth 0..15, path the chain), moves each chunk at the next launch, and ends with a one-row
pass on the first answer token, then 4E decodes 256 tokens.
Before the card ($0, on the Mac after the operator's go): the host twin (tests/tree_verify_host.rs) checks a chain
prefill at R = 16 and R = 5 against the R = 1 pass decode of the same prompt, bit for bit; a mutant (path off by one)
must die.
On the card, one process, prompts of 1,024, 4,096 and the M3 8,191-token prompt (da4edbd6), 1 discarded + 3 rounds a
form: served eager prefill against chain prefill, one process a form so each peak is its own, no drafter loaded, the
VRAM sampler beside each, and one stamped chain launch at the 8k prompt's last pass.
Gates:
- G1 identity: the chain's last-row logits at 1,024 equal the pass's R = 1 decode bit for bit; chain R = 8 equals R = 16
  at 4,096.
- G2 speed: first token at 4,096 at most 6.5 s (signed 5.2 [4.0, 6.5]); at 1,024 0.85 [0.7, 1.1]; at 8,192 15.1 [11, 18].
- G3 memory: 8k peak at most 4.0 GB with today's full pass buffers (signed 3.45 [3.2, 4.0]).
- G4: the phase split of the 8k pass printed, attention against the rest.
- Not gated here: quality. The paired-perplexity gate of section 2 is the next job, and the decision to serve waits
  for it.
Cost: two loads and installs of about 4 min each (M3 loaded in 240.8 s), 4 min of eager rounds (4 x 57 s), 1.5 min of
chain rounds, 0.2 min of R = 1 identity decode, about 3 min of margin: 16 to 19 min of L40S, $0.48 to $0.57 at $1.80 an
hour (estimated); cap $1. The image needs a rebuild for specbench.
Riskiest assumption: that the 8k pass's extra 37 ms is the attention, linear in the context. The 1,024 figure barely
depends on it; the 4,096 and 8,192 figures do. G4 settles it in the same job.

OTHER MULTI-ROW KERNEL, FOR COMPARISON
--------------------------------------
tv_batch_gemm (tv_bg_n8 to n128) is in the same image and runs 128 rows at 0.224 ms a row (batch-l40s-v4, measured).
Routing a prompt's rows through it is ROADMAP section 4's "prefill by GEMM" (plan-300 K10, T8). It keeps the grouped
cuBLAS attention and its 2.15 GB score tensor at 8k, so it helps the first token (about 1.8 s of projections at 8k,
estimated) but not the memory. The tree pass helps both.
