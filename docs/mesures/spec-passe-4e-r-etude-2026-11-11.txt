SPEC PASS GROWN FROM 4E'S ROW LOOP (4E-R): A DESIGN STUDY, SEALED 4B, L40S SHAPES, 2026-11-11. $0.
================================================================================================
Result: a multi-row pass grown from 4E's own row loop is bit-identical to 4E row by row, but each added row costs
about 0.22 of a 4E token [0.19, 0.26] (*estimated*), because the measured cost of a second f32 activation in 4E's
Tetra loop is 0.378 of the loop (*measured*, shaker-4e and spec-micro). So it wins only at R = 2 to 4: c_2 1.22, c_3
1.44, c_4 1.66 (*estimated*), against a12's 1.556 at R = 2 (*measured*). For the weightless drafter at R = 3 that
projects 265 tok/s [239, 278] on GSM8K against 244 for a12 at 16 rows and 218.8 for greedy (*estimated*). For DFlash it
loses: a12's tree stays at about 502. The register-fed MMA variant (B) has the better slope but is not bit-identical and
its fixed cost is unknown. Build first: (C), K = 2 and 3 on 4E's natural staging. Riskiest assumption: the micro's
f = 0.378 holds in situ. Smallest test: a zero-layout K = 2 probe of the 4E module, about $0.30 on one L40S.
Nature : read-only, *computed* from the code and the journals cited, *estimated* where marked. No build, no run.
Inputs : llvq-cuda/kernels/pers_4e.cu, llvq_pers.cuh (sections 2 to 10), llvq_f1rank_v3.cuh, llvq_f1rank.cuh,
         llvq_tetra48.cuh, pers_trows.cu, pers_vrows.cu; journals verify-tree-s1, n1 to n11, spec-axe-memoire,

1. THE 4E v9 ROW LOOP AS WRITTEN
--------------------------------
Served form: PERS_4E_ROWS = 7, 512 threads a CTA, one CTA a SM, 142 CTAs, 128 registers, 0 B local, 85,952 B shared
(step3-4e). Tetra rows run pers_tetra_rows_static, which calls pers_tetra_row<24, false, true> row by row.
Row assignment. A CTA owns a static slice of rows; warp w takes rows lo + w, lo + w + 16, and so on. One warp is one row
at a time. Lane l takes blocks j = l, l + 32, l + 64 of that row.
Word decode, a lane a block (f1r_load, f1r_v3_quads):
- two u32 loads at word (3j) >> 1, and lo / hi16 selected by the parity of j: 6 bytes a block, a warp reads 192
  contiguous bytes a step; block j + 32's words are loaded while block j decodes (PF);
- fields p, r, s8, b1, i1, b2, i2, b3, i3 by shifts and masks;
- six shared-table reads: prefix, branch, suffix (the chain s8 to br to s16 to c3), three rank rows of 4,096 u32;
- three sections of two quads each, by two prmt and a byte mask (IMAD, IMAD, LOP) and one LOP3;
- six quads of biased bytes, value + 128, |value| <= 10, in TETRA48_ORDER;
- 24 floats by prmt into 0x4b0000xx minus the bias, two instructions a coordinate.
Dot and fold (pers_block): 24 __fmaf_rn from 0 against xb[TETRA48_ORDER[4i + j]], then pers_fold: the shell m from
dp4a of the XORed quads (tetra48_n2), t = a x gscale[g] (FMUL), acc = fma(t, invnorm[m], acc).
Activation. f32 in shared memory, staged by every CTA in its own copy, natural layout b x 24 + c, the f16 tail after
the last block. For q and k it is ln1(x) rotated (pers_ln_1rt then pers_rot_finish, every CTA on the whole vector).
Reduction. Each lane sums its blocks in increasing j; warp_sum (XOR butterfly); lane 0 adds the f16 tail in order,
fma(acc, rscale, tail), f2h. No shared-memory reduction and no CTA barrier inside a row phase.
int4 rows (v, o, the 12 int4 downs, and the head in g64): pers_q4_row_tile on words relaid at install. One uint4 load
gives lane l words 128t + l + 32s, s = 0..3. Each word: scale and bias of its group of 128 from global, eight
deq = fadd(fmul(h2f(s), q), h2f(b)) and eight fma against xs[c + k] in increasing k, then warp_sum. The inputs of the
int4 rows are f16-exact: v reads ln1(x) widened, o reads ctx widened, the int4 down reads the f16 product.
Phases. One grid barrier a phase boundary, 2 + 6 x layers a token (pers.rs::n_bar_4e), 218 at 4B; form 5 stages the
next layer's constants during the waits. Attention has no barrier inside: a per-KV-head ticket folds its 256-position
splits.
Shared layout (*computed* from the code and the module sizes): header 256 B, decoder tables 18,816 B, one region of
59,520 B that is both the row phases' f32 vector (max(inter, 2 x hidden) = 38,912 B) and attention's cp.async ring,
the form 5 area 7,360 B. Headroom to the sm_89 limit of 101,376 B: 15,424 B.
In-situ split of the 4B token (ops/debit_maths.py, *computed* from v6 stamps, v8 and v9 kept the loop): Tetra rows
1.81 ms, int4 rows 0.551 ms, head 0.36 ms, of a token of 4.467 ms in one-token launches (n11, *measured*).

2. THE COST OF AN ADDED ROW IN THE SCALAR LOOP: ALREADY MEASURED
----------------------------------------------------------------
The K2 arm of pers_trows.cu (trows_row_k2) is design A at R = 2: one decode, two f32 activations, each sum in the
served order. Its rows equal the one-activation rows bit for bit on both activations (*measured*, shaker-4e).
The second row costs f = 0.378 [0.365-0.398] of the loop (shaker-4e) and 0.381 [0.377-0.384] (spec-micro), at 4E's
geometry, standalone (*measured*).
By instruction count the added row is 24 FFMA, about 15 LDS after merging, and a FMUL and FFMA of the fold: about 41
of the served 237 a block, a ratio of 0.17 (*computed* from the source, not from SASS). The measured 0.378 is twice
that, in line with "237 to 145 gave only x1.143".
A likely cause, not measured: the natural stride of 24 words puts lanes l and l + 4 on the same bank, so each scalar
LDS of the activation is 8-way conflicted (*computed* from the address formula). The trio staging of llvq_pers.cuh
section 3 (pers_block_trio, the same 24 fma on the same values in the same order, so the same bits) reads six LDS.128
at a stride of 28 words, which no quarter-warp conflicts on (*computed*). It has never been timed with K > 1.
Tetra part of an added row in situ: 0.378 x 1.81 ms = 0.684 ms = 0.153 of a 4E token (*computed* from a measured f
and a computed in-situ time; that the micro's f holds in situ is *estimated*).

3. DESIGN A: 4E-R, SCALAR, GENERAL R
------------------------------------
Shape. 4E's phase list, grid, barrier count, row assignment and word stream are kept. Every row function takes K
activations: a block is decoded once and dotted K times, each dot its own fma chain in the served order, each row its
own warp_sum and epilogue. The words are read once for K rows, so no DRAM byte is added.
Activation for R rows: in the 59,520 B region, R vectors side by side. The int4 inputs are f16-exact, so they can be
staged as f16 and widened at the read with the same values (bit-identical). Per added row (*computed*):
  q/k/v    xr f32 10,240 B + xn f16 5,120 B   R <= 3 in 59,520 B; R = 4 if the form 5 area moves above it (74,944 B)
  o        ctx f16 8,192 B                     R <= 7
  gate/up  f32 10,240 B (trio 11,936 B)        R <= 5 (trio 4)
  int4 dn  f16 19,456 B                        R <= 3
  Tetra dn f32 38,912 B (trio 45,392 B)        R = 1 only: R >= 2 needs d_in slices
Down slices. Slice boundaries at multiples of 32 blocks keep every lane's blocks in increasing j, with the lane's
accumulator live across the slices, so the bits are kept. The cost is one CTA barrier and one restage a slice: 2 slices
at R = 2, 3 at R = 4 (natural staging), 24 Tetra-down layers. No cross-CTA fold: each CTA still owns whole rows. That
is what separates it from the tree pass, whose cross-CTA partial fold cost 0.22 of a token at R = 1 and 1.00 at R = 16
in n3, and about 600 us of fold phases at vt1 in n11.
Registers. Each added row adds about 2 to 4 live registers in the Tetra loop (*estimated*). The v9 symbol sits at the
128-register cap of 512 threads; v7 was refused for 24 B of local memory at 128. So R = 2 is already a register risk,
and R >= 8 means 384 threads, which is a10t384's geometry, not 4E's.
What R the CTA holds: R <= 3 without moving anything but the down's slices, R = 4 with the form 5 area moved, R >= 5
only with slices in every phase and a new thread count.
Cost per added row (*estimated*, at GSM8K contexts):
  Tetra rows      0.153 natural staging (from the measured f); 0.08 to 0.12 with trio staging (f 0.19 to 0.30,
                  between i16's measured 0.187 and f32's 0.378; trio at K > 1 never timed)
  int4 rows       0.019 to 0.043 (f 0.15 to 0.35 of 0.551 ms; never measured)
  head            0.012 to 0.028 (same f on 0.36 ms)
  other phases    0.005 to 0.02: the prologues on 4E's path, per-row norm, RoPE and append, per-row attention
                  arithmetic. Bounded by a10t384r16's whole extra row, 0.0123 at chat and 0.0236 at rag (*measured*, n11)
  down slices     0.003 to 0.01
  total           natural 0.22 [0.19, 0.26]; trio 0.16 [0.12, 0.26]
c_R (c_1 by dispatch to the served symbol; others *estimated*):
                  c_1    c_2    c_4                 c_8                 c_16
  natural         1.00   1.22   1.66 [1.57, 1.78]   2.54 [2.33, 2.82]   4.30 [3.85, 4.90]
  trio            1.00   1.16   1.48 [1.36, 1.78]   2.12 [1.84, 2.82]   3.40 [2.80, 4.90]
  c_8 and c_16 need slices everywhere and a thread count 4E does not run: they are not buildable as 4E-R.

4. DESIGN B: A SMALL MMA FED FROM REGISTERS
-------------------------------------------
Premise check against the v9 code (the note of 2026-11-09, about 212 instructions a block for 8 rows against 237):
- Holds: the weights fit an int8 fragment. A quad XOR 0x80808080 is four signed int8 values; tetra48_n2 already
  computes that XOR for dp4a. Six quads plus two zero quads make K = 32 of mma.m16n8k32.s8. The quads carry
  coordinates in TETRA48_ORDER, so the activation must be staged in that order, as trio staging does.
- Fails: the fragment does not come from 4E's row loop. An MMA's A rows share one B, so its M = 16 rows must be 16
  weight rows at the same block position. 4E's warp holds one row and 32 blocks. Thread (g, t) needs quads t and t + 4
  of rows g and g + 8: about 12 SHFL and 8 selects a thread an MMA, about 24 SHFL a lane a block (*computed*). The row
  assignment changes to 16 rows a warp, a warp's word load touches 16 rows' sectors instead of one 192 B run (the
  concern M1 wrote for a12ip), and a CTA's slice of 18 down rows fills 16-row tiles at 56 %.
- Fails: bit identity. The int8 MMA needs an integer activation. Two int8 planes of an int16 activation measured 91.6 %
  rows identical, 0 out of tolerance, worst |dy| 6.4e-3 of the rms (spec-micro, *measured*). An f16 MMA rounds the
  activation and accumulates in another order. No MMA reproduces 4E's sequential fma chain.
- My own count (*computed* from the source): decode about 148, gather 40, B fragments and D fold about 40, about 228
  a decode for up to 8 rows against 237. The 212 is plausible in kind; counts are a weak predictor here.
So B is a12 without the shared tile and without cross-CTA folds, not a variant of 4E's loop.
Cost (*estimated*): c_1 1.00 by dispatch to 4E. Fixed cost at R >= 2 0.05 to 0.30: 4E's rows (2.72 ms) times the
measured x1.27 of decode+MMA loops at worst, plus the scattered word stream. Per added row 0.012 to 0.020 (a10's
0.0123 and S1's 0.0196, *measured*). c_4 1.11 to 1.36, c_8 1.15 to 1.44, c_16 1.25 to 1.60, central 1.20 / 1.26 /
1.39. Texts would differ from 4E's on near-ties, as the tree pass's 23 of 200 GSM8K texts do.

5. DESIGN C: R = 2 TO 4, 4E'S STRUCTURE UNTOUCHED
-------------------------------------------------
The cheapest thing that verifies a short chain. K is a template parameter of the row functions, K in {2, 3, 4}. For
K = 1 the served module runs, so c_1 = 1.00 by construction.
- Tetra rows: trows_row_k2's body generalized to K, on 4E's natural staging (the measured arm, bit for bit).
- int4 rows: pers_q4_row_tile with the dequantized value reused for K fma chains.
- Staging: R = 2 fits as today's f32 layout (q/k/v 40,960 B); R = 3 stores the f16-exact inputs as f16. The only
  structural change is the Tetra down's 2 or 3 slices.
- Phase list, barrier count (218), grid, 512 threads, row assignment, word stream: unchanged.
- Chains only. A tree's siblings share a position, and 4E's attention reads the cache by position; a chain keeps every
  row's attention equal to 4E's at its own position.
c_2 1.22 [1.19, 1.26], c_3 1.44 [1.38, 1.52], c_4 1.66 [1.57, 1.78] (*estimated*, section 3's natural row cost).
Not offered: c_8, c_16.

6. THE REST OF A MULTI-ROW PASS THAT 4E DOES NOT HAVE
-----------------------------------------------------
- Norm, RoPE, append: R rows at positions pos .. pos + R - 1, one CTA a head as 4E does: 40 jobs a row, 120 at R = 3
  on 142 CTAs, one wave. The appended K/V of rejected rows are overwritten by the next pass; nothing to undo.
- Attention: a job is a KV head over a 256-position split. With R rows a job loads its K/V once and runs R x groups
  query rows, row i masked past pos + i. Row i must use the split count 4E would use at pos + i, ceil((pos + i + 1) /
  256), and the same fold order, so jobs and tickets go per (KV head, row). The added row pays only the score,
  softmax and PV arithmetic.
- Head: K-row int4 g64 over 151,936 rows, one argmax slot a row with 4E's packed keys. Recycling's top-k a row is an
  extra not in 4E; its cost is inside the drafter's 0.75 ms below.
- Taps (DFlash only): the R rows' residual at the tap layers, 5,120 B a row a layer. Negligible (*computed*).
- Launches: one launch a pass, as the measured c's already assume (n11 times 4E in one-token launches).
Bit identity: every row of A and C runs 4E's arithmetic on its own activation. That holds for the Tetra blocks
(trows_row_k2, *measured*), int4 words, warp_sum, epilogue, norms, RoPE, attention splits and argmax keys, so each row's
token is 4E's greedy token at that position (by construction, to be held by the host twin). The text is the served
greedy text, not "no measured difference". B is not bit-identical.

8. WHAT I WOULD BUILD FIRST
---------------------------
C at K = 2 and 3, on 4E's natural staging. It is the measured arm (trows_row_k2) placed in the served module, the only
design that keeps 4E's bits and the served text, and the only one whose dominant term has a measured basis. A trio
staging step follows if C holds; B stays a separate route under the tolerance rule, not under identity.
Riskiest assumption: an added f32 activation costs about 0.38 of the Tetra loop in situ, as in the standalone micro.
The in-situ Tetra rows (1.81 ms, *computed*) sit at the micro's word-stream time (pword 1.81 ms against v2 2.51 ms),
so the added row could be partly hidden (c_2 near 1.10) or fully exposed and worse (c_2 above 1.30, where C no longer
pays at R = 3). A second risk: the K = 2 symbol leaves the 128-register cap with local memory.
Smallest experiment, in two steps:
1. $0, Mac: compile a 4E v9 module whose Tetra and int4 row functions take K = 2, with the Mac's devel ptxas for sm_89
   (ops/tetra-sass-probe.sh). Gate: 128 registers, 0 B local. The driver's ptxas can still differ.
2. One l40sx1 job, about 10 min, about $0.30 (*estimated*, from step3-4e's $0.28 for 9 min): a zero-layout probe. The
   second activation is the first buffer plus a kernel-argument offset set to 0, so the compiler cannot merge the two
   dots. The shared layout is unchanged and the down needs no slices. Time it against the served module in one-token
   launches, rounds interleaved, with the oracle and identity gates of n11. Read c_2(probe). Under 1.15, C goes on to
   real activations. Above 1.25, C stops, since its R = 3 projection then falls under 250 tok/s.
Both steps need the operator's go, and the card step a stamped prereg.
