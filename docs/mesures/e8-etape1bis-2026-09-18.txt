WHAT THIS REPAIRS
------------------
The run of the same day (e8-etape1-2026-09-18.txt) is void by its own deviation
E3: arm A was a sequential GPTQ witness and arm B a plain per-block encode, so
its ratio measured the compensation and not the codebook.

`E8Cubed` now implements `llvq_quant::quantizer::BlockQuantizer`, so
`quantize_layer` runs both arms with the same `GptqFactor`, the same
`GptqConfig`, the same gain centroids and the same row scale. The only
difference left is which codebook the loop calls. The trait's own documentation
invites this: the loop is meant to be exercised against codebooks that have
nothing to do with the Leech lattice.

THE GATE, WHICH THE PREREG MADE EXPLICIT
-----------------------------------------
The prereg refuses to read its own primary unless the two arms put their error
in the same place, because otherwise something other than compensation
separates them.

  |B/isotropic - A/isotropic| = 0.0008 at the norm-10 cap, 0.0041 at norm 8.
  The bar was 0.05. Both pass.

Arm B's ratio to isotropic went from **0.996** as a plain encode to **0.135**
inside the loop, against arm A's 0.134. The 6.8 of the void run was the
compensation, entirely, exactly as that file predicted.

Four families, four widths, four ratios inside 1.035 to 1.056. The consistency

AT EQUAL BITS
--------------
Neither arm sits at Tetra's 1.9907. Linear interpolation between the two
measured points, 0.592 of the way from 1.9167 to 2.0417 (*computed*):

  J_B/J_A at equal rate       **1.146**
  unweighted at equal rate      1.126

The arbitration dossier predicted **1.09** from the ratio of normalized second
moments, G(E8)/G(Leech) = 1.0899. The prereg registered 1.09 with an interval of
[0.95, 1.30]. Both measured arms are inside it, so the verdict does not depend
on which one is read, and the interpolated figure sits 0.056 above the point
prediction.

So on the model's own rotated blocks, inside the production loop, the lattice is
worth what the asymptotic second moment says it is worth, and no more. That the
bound holds at 2 bits per dimension with shape-gain, a regime it was not derived
for, was not obvious before this run.

WHAT IT DECIDES
----------------
The prereg's rule: a ratio inside [0.95, 1.30] makes E8 a **simplification**
candidate and moves the question to the kernel.

The asymmetry that matters is not in the numbers above, it is in what produces
them. The norm-10 E8 codebook is 56,880 points of 8 coordinates, **455 kB as
i8**, and its exact nearest neighbour is a table scan. The Leech machinery is
7,138 lines (*measured*, `llvq-core/src` 572, `llvq-search/src` 4,373,
`llvq-search/src/tetra` 2,193) and its encoder is a trellis over three sections
and two parities.

Paying 4.7 % of weighted error to replace that with a table is a kernel and
maintenance question, and this file does not answer it. What it establishes is
that the quality cost of asking is small and now measured.

AND THE LARGER NUMBER BESIDE IT
--------------------------------
The compensation moves the weighted error by a factor of **7.5**: a residual
placed by the loop costs 0.134 of what a residual of the same energy in a random
direction would cost. The codebook moves it by 1.05 to 1.29 depending on the bit
budget.

Whatever is left between this format and the paper's LLVQ, the lattice is not
where to look for it.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH
-----------------------------
- No quality claim. `J_local` is a local activation metric over one projection,
  not the model's loss, not perplexity, not MMLU.
- Nothing about a kernel. No E8 decoder has run on a card, and arm B's b/weight
  assumes an integer index per sub-block and ignores what a real packing pays
  in addressing.
- The equal-rate 1.146 is interpolated between two points, not measured. A cap
  landing exactly on 1.9907 needs a partial shell and an ordering rule inside
  it (deviation E2).
- `Tetra` is a trellis-searched subset of the Leech lattice, not its Voronoi
  quantizer. The bound is confirmed for the served format, not for the lattice
  in general.
- Three layers of 36, four rows of thousands, one model, one calibration seed.
  `down_proj` carries 1.7 calibration samples per dimension even here.
