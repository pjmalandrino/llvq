INVENTION STUDY: THE LEECH LATTICE OUTSIDE THE WEIGHT MATRICES IT ALREADY CODES, SEALED 4B, 2026-11-11. $0.
=========================================================================================================
Result: no idea of this mechanism clears the bar on the strength of the lattice. The lattice buys 0.8 dB of
signal-to-noise over a uniform scalar quantizer at equal rate in the KV regime (*computed*, section 2), which is
0.14 b/dim. On the 8k KV cache that is about 11 MB. One idea keeps a large effect, the KV cache cut to 4.7 to 5.6 b/dim
after a Hadamard rotation, and its lever is the rotation and the bits, not the lattice. Stacked with the chain prefill
and the freed 8k residue it reaches 2.47 to 2.54 GB at 8k, -29 to -31 % against the chain's 3.56 and -57 to -58 %
against the served 5.95 (*estimated*). It sits on the bar, not over it. The other seeds fall under it: the int4
projections moved to a lattice member save 0.06 to 0.12 b/param at 4B, the tied table cannot go below int4 without
a quality loss, the rate ladder moves no criterion, and the 0.60 GB overhead is not a lattice question.

Nature: desk study. No job, no build, no model load. One numpy script on the Mac (7.5 s and 3 min of one core, nice
10): an exact nearest-point search in a Leech box code over all 8,192 Golay cosets, against scalar quantizers, on
synthetic sources. Web sources fetched tonight, each with its URL. Read: format-noyau.md §1-4, how-it-works.md §6-9,
ETAT.md §4-6, llvq-paper-notes.md, llvq-llm/src/kvq.rs, mesures/kvq8-4b-2026-08-15.txt, and tonight's
gains-criteres-memoire, recherche-structure-leech-format, recherche-runtime-inference and
recherche-methode-quantification. Labels: *measured* (a journal), *computed* (arithmetic on measured or code inputs,
or the numpy script), *estimated* (judgement, or one estimated input), *reported* (a third party, URL given).

1. THE STARTING POINT
---------------------
- KV bytes a position at 4B: 36 x 2 x 8 x 128 x 2 B = 147,456 B, 73,728 values (*computed*). 1.208 GB at 8,192.
- 8k peak served 5.95 GB: base 2.01, KV 1.208, one score tensor 2.147, activations 0.25, residue 0.33. Chain prefill
  3.56 GB: 2.117 + 1.208 + residue 0.235 (*measured* and *computed*, gains-criteres-memoire §1).
- Decode at 8,192: 161.5 tok/s against 226.2 at 5 tokens (*measured*, served-v9). 6.19 ms against 4.42 ms. The 1.77 ms
  gap is mostly the KV read, 1.208 GB at about 733 GB/s = 1.65 ms (*estimated*, large-4e-budget).
- The int8 KV that exists (kvq.rs) is group-affine, 64 values along head_dim, f16 scale and bias, 8.5 b/value, no
  rotation. Its paired perplexity read +0.0494 % [-0.0713, +0.1703] on 12 windows of 4,096 (*measured*, kvq8-4b).
  "Short context only" in CLAUDE.md means its throughput series at 1,024 new tokens was abandoned, not that it failed
  at long context.

2. WHAT THE LATTICE BUYS ON A KV-LIKE SOURCE (numpy, tonight)
------------------------------------------------------------
Code. The Leech box code LB_b keeps the served construction x = p + 2c + 4k (Golay codeword c, parity p, Σk ≡ p mod 2)
and stores each coordinate's rank directly in b bits, from the value lists of how-it-works §7. A block of 24 costs
13 + 24b bits: 1.542 / 2.542 / 3.542 b/dim at b = 1 / 2 / 3. The search is exact: per coset, the best value of each
parity per coordinate, then the cheapest parity repair. One global scale is tuned per rate; each 128-dim vector
carries an f16 gain (+0.125 b/dim). Source: 128-dim Gaussian vectors normalised to unit rms, 600 vectors, the first
120 dims coded as 5 blocks of 24.

  code                                         b/dim     SQNR dB   label
  LB_1 + gain                                  1.667     7.64      computed
  LB_2 + gain                                  2.667    12.78      computed
  LB_3 + gain                                  3.667    17.84      computed
  uniform scalar 2 / 3 / 4 b + gain            2.125 / 3.125 / 4.125     9.26 / 14.26 / 19.34   computed
  uniform scalar interpolated at 2.667         2.667    11.97      computed
  uniform scalar interpolated at 3.667         3.667    17.01      computed
  int4 g128 min-max (the int4 records' form)   4.250    19.95      computed
  int8 g64 min-max (the shipped q8)            8.500    45.51      computed

The lattice gains 0.81 and 0.83 dB over the uniform scalar quantizer at equal rate, 0.14 b/dim (*computed*). Theory
caps it near 1.03 dB of granular gain plus at most 1.53 dB of shaping that a box code does not collect (Conway and
Sloane, Agrell and Allen, cited in recherche-structure-leech-format). A better shaped Leech code (a Voronoi code
Λ/2^rΛ, as NestQuant does with E8) reaches at most about 2.5 dB, 0.42 b/dim, and its decoder needs a nearest-point
search at every read (section 4).

Rotation dominates. A synthetic key source with 2 of 128 channels at 15 times the rms of the rest:
  int4 g64 min-max, no rotation (4.5 b)                16.42 dB
  Hadamard per head, then int4 g64 min-max (4.5 b)     22.54 dB   (+6.1 dB)
  Hadamard, uniform 4 b + gain (4.125 b)                20.66 dB
  Hadamard, LB_3 + gain (3.667 b)                       19.06 dB
  int8 g64 min-max, no rotation (8.5 b)                 41.04 dB
(*computed*, synthetic; the real outlier ratio of Qwen3 keys is not measured here.) The rotation is worth six times
what the lattice is worth. A rotated scalar quantizer gets almost everything a rotated lattice gets.

3. THE QUALITY GATE FIXES THE RATE, AND IT IS HIGH
--------------------------------------------------
The int8 interval failed the ±0.12 % band by its width. At 41 to 45 dB of SQNR its true effect is far below the
noise of 12 windows. A 4-bit KV is 100 to 300 times more noise. External figures at that rate:
- NestQuant, E8 nested lattice, Hadamard on queries and keys, Llama-3-8B, WikiText-2 at 2,048: weights alone 6.308,
  weights + KV 6.379 at q = 14 (3.81 b + scales), 6.475 at q = 12, 6.640 at q = 10, 6.968 at q = 8 (*reported*,
  https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09720, Table 3). The KV adds +1.13 % at about 3.8 b and +10.5 % at about 3.0 b.
- CommVQ, learned additive VQ commuting with RoPE, Llama-3.1-8B: LongBench 47.98 against 48.05 at 2 b, yet
  in-domain perplexity 11.54 against 10.17, +13.5 % (*reported*, https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18879v1). Task averages
  hide what our band measures.
- TurboQuant: "quality neutral" at 3.5 b on LongBench, a task average (*reported*, recherche-runtime-inference §1).
From NestQuant's ladder, the KV excess grows about as the square of the MSE (+10.5 / +1.13 = 9.3 for an MSE ratio of
(14/8)² = 3.06, *computed*). To bring +1.13 % under +0.12 % needs 9.4 times less excess: 1.75 to 9.4 times less MSE,
+0.8 to +1.6 b/dim. The band therefore asks 4.6 to 5.4 b/dim plus the gain, 4.7 to 5.6 b/dim (*estimated*: one model
family, quantized weights, a different corpus window). At that rate the lattice's 0.14 b/dim is 3 % of the rate.

4. THE ENCODE COST AT DECODE TIME
---------------------------------
Blocks a token: 36 layers x 8 KV heads x 2 x 5 = 2,880 Λ24 blocks, plus 576 E8 blocks for the 8 dims left of each
head (128 = 5 x 24 + 8) (*computed*).
- The weight encoder: 290 to 329 µs a block on one core (*measured*, trioencbench, HISTORIQUE 2026-09-05).
  2,880 x 290 µs = 0.835 s of one core a token, 190 times the 4.42 ms token. Not affordable, on any CPU count.
- Exact coset enumeration on the card: 8,192 cosets x 24 coordinates x about 1 op = 2.0e5 ops a block, 5.7e8 a token.
  At 30 to 50 % of the L40S's 4.5e13 lane-ops a second: 25 to 42 µs a token, 0.6 to 1 % of a token (*computed*,
  *estimated* efficiency). A prompt of 8,192 positions: 0.21 to 0.35 s, 3 to 4 % of the chain's 7.8 s first token.
- A bounded-distance Leech decoder (Vardy and Be'ery's maximum-likelihood decoder is known at a few thousand real
  operations, not re-checked tonight) cuts that 50 times. Not needed at this budget.
- The decode in attention: LB_3 needs the 12-bit Golay word (two 6-bit edge tables, as Tetra), then about 4 ops a
  coordinate, about 110 ops a block, plus 96 FMAs for the 4 query heads of a KV head. At 8,192 positions 23.6 M blocks
  a token, 5e9 ops, 0.11 ms at the card's peak, 0.3 to 0.5 ms realistic (*estimated*). A rotated int5 decode costs about
  2 ops a value, three times less. A Voronoi decode adds a Leech nearest-point search per block read: about 2 ms a token
  at 8k, which closes that form.

5. THE KEPT IDEA
----------------
K1. A rotated KV cache at the rate the band allows, with the lattice as an optional 0.8 dB.
Design. After RoPE, each key head vector is rotated by a 128-point Hadamard with fixed signs, normalised to unit rms,
the f16 gain kept, and coded: arm S, uniform scalar at b bits; arm L, LB_3 on 5 blocks and E8 or scalar on the 8-dim
tail. The query is rotated the same way, so q·k is unchanged. Values are coded the same way; the attention output is
rotated back once per head, 32 FWHTs of 128 a layer and token. The grouping stays inside one token, so prefill and
decode write the same bytes (the kvq.rs invariant). Positions are never re-quantized: the error does not accumulate.
Why the lattice: it does not need it. Arm L is +0.8 dB at equal bytes, 0.14 b/dim, and costs about three times the
decode instructions of arm S. Arm L is kept only as the measurement that settles it on real keys.
Bytes, at 4.7 to 5.6 b/dim with the gain: 34,700 to 51,600 B a position against 147,456 (*computed*).
  KV at 8,192: 1.208 GB to 0.355 to 0.423 GB, -0.785 to -0.853 GB.
  8k peak, served eager prefill: 5.95 to 5.10 to 5.17 GB, -13 to -14 %. Under the bar.
  8k peak, chain prefill: 3.56 to 2.71 to 2.78 GB, -22 to -24 %. Under the bar.
  Chain prefill plus the 0.235 GB residue freed (gains-criteres-memoire #3): 2.47 to 2.54 GB, -29 to -31 % against the
  chain, -57 to -58 % against today's door. On the bar.
  Short context: KV 0.038 GB at 256 positions. Nothing.
  b/param: nothing; the cache is not a parameter.
  At 32,768 positions, outside the bar: KV 4.83 GB to 1.42 to 1.69 GB.
Time. Decode at 8,192: 1.1 ms less read, 0.1 to 0.5 ms more decode, 6.19 ms to 5.4 to 5.7 ms, +9 to +15 % (arm L) or
+14 to +20 % (arm S) (*estimated*). Short-context decode: the FWHTs cost under 1 % (*estimated*). First token: arm S
nothing measurable, arm L +3 to +4 % at 8k by enumeration.
Gate. Paired WikiText-2 perplexity within ±0.12 % on 24 windows of 4,096 in f16 against the f16 cache, then the
47 near-tie texts and GSM8K-200 not separated. Prefill-decode byte identity (LLVQ_VERIFY_CACHE=1), a mutant that
drops the rotation of the query killed.
What makes it wrong. Qwen3's KV excess grows faster than NestQuant's ladder, so the band asks 6 to 8 b: then the
saving is 0.60 to 0.75 GB and the stack lands at -24 to -27 % against the chain. The rotation does not spread Qwen3's
key outliers because some sit in the RoPE pairs' low frequencies (KVQuant's pre-RoPE finding); then keys need a
pre-RoPE path, which 4E's cache layout does not have.
Cost to build. Eager quality arm on the Mac: about half a day (a KvMode beside Q8, a round trip like
quantize_dequantize). 4E and the chain prefill: 1 to 2 weeks for a cache read in a new dtype in two kernels, then a
card gate of about $1 to $2.
Cheapest kill test, Mac, $0, needs a go for local compute. KvMode round trips at S5, S6, S8 and L3 on the sealed 4B,
paired against f16 over 24 windows of 4,096 (about 11 min an arm, from kvq8's 5 min 43 s for 12 windows: about 1 h).
Kill if S6's interval is not inside ±0.12 %. Drop arm L if it does not beat S at equal bytes by more than the paired
noise. A first pass on Qwen3-0.6B, 12 windows, takes minutes. Verdict: on the bar only with the chain prefill and the
residue, and not a lattice idea. It belongs in the operator's KV decision of ETAT §5 ("touches quality").

6. THE SEEDS THAT FALL, WITH THE ARITHMETIC
-------------------------------------------
(b) Embedding and head on the lattice. The tied 4B table is 388.96 M weights at 4.5 b, 0.219 GB, 0.233 b/param over
the Tetra rate (*computed*, recherche-structure-leech-format). Tetra on it would give 2.73 to 2.50 b/param. Its q8 to
q4 step already cost +1.52 % of perplexity (*measured*, ROADMAP-QUALITY row 4), and practitioners keep the head at
5 to 6 bits. A 2-bit head fails "same quality" by an order of magnitude. A two-pass head (coarse lattice pass, exact
refine) moves bytes read, not bytes held, and it is speed. The untied input embedding of the 8B and 14B is the
structure note's S3, -0.18 and -0.13 b/param; nothing to add.
(c) The int4 projections back onto the lattice. At 3.54 b/dim LB_3 sits 2.1 dB below int4 g128 min-max at 4.25 b
(17.84 against 19.95 dB, section 2). Matching int4 needs about 3.9 b with this code, 3.6 to 3.7 b with an ideal Leech
Voronoi code whose decode costs a search per block. Saving 0.35 to 0.6 b on 770.9 M weights: 0.067 to 0.115 b/param
at 4B, 2.73 to 2.62 at best (*computed*). With S3 stacked: 8B 1,007 M x 0.35 to 0.6 / 8,193 M = 0.043 to 0.074, plus
0.186, 2.70 to 2.44 to 2.47; 14B 2,952 M x 0.35 to 0.6 / 14,793 M = 0.070 to 0.120, plus 0.130, 2.73 to 2.48 to 2.53.
None reaches 2.4. GPTQ feedback, which the RTN int4 records lack, is the only credit left; it applies as well to an
int3 GPTQ record with no lattice. The structure note's S5 estimate of 3.15 to 3.55 b assumed a code 0.222 b/dim from
the bound. The box code measured tonight is 0.58 b/dim from it at 3.54 b (3.5 dB).
(d) Nested shells as a rate ladder. A prefix of the file serving a smaller model moves none of the criteria: the
served object keeps its bytes and its quality. Scalar quantizers are also embedded (drop the low bits), so the ladder
is not specific to the lattice either.
(e) The 0.60 GB fixed overhead. It is the CUDA context, cuBLAS, the local-memory reservation and 4E's install
(gains-criteres-memoire §1). No code changes it. The ledger that splits it rides the next card job (same note,
precondition and rows 3 and 4).
(f) Searched and not found. Integer lattice keys allow attention scores in integer arithmetic (dp4a on coordinates of
|x| ≤ 15 against an int8 query). That is order-free and batch-invariant, but it changes the query's values, it is a
speed and determinism lever, and it does not move memory.

7. WHAT THIS NOTE DID NOT CHECK
-------------------------------
- Real Qwen3 keys and values. Every SQNR here is on synthetic sources; the outlier ratio of Qwen3 keys is unmeasured.
- The KV rate the band allows is extrapolated from one Llama-3-8B ladder.
- The box code's scale is one global value on a coarse grid, with no per-block gain; a better encoder could add a few
  tenths of a dB, which leaves the verdict unchanged.
- Vardy and Be'ery's operation count, and the Leech Voronoi shaping gain, were not re-derived.
- NestQuant and CommVQ figures are theirs, on their models, read through the fetch tool.

g(x) = x^11 + x^10 + x^6 + x^5 + x^4 + x^2 + 1 and checked at 759 octads. Kept in the session scratchpad, not
