Research phase on decode speed (CERVEAU O11), R1 and R2 again: the framing after the first card run.
Main session, 2026-11-12, on the operator's word ("recadre"). No measurement was run for this note. Every figure is
copied from recherche-debit-banc-2026-11-12 (measured, L40S, 15 rounds) unless labelled otherwise. This note
replaces sections R1 to R3 of recherche-debit-r1-cadrage-2026-11-12, which stays as written.

WHAT FELL
The first framing said the Tetra rows are bound by their decode, 243 instructions a block, and set the search on
decoders with fewer instructions. The card run read the sealed file's Tetra words with nothing decoded, 24
instructions a block against 226: 493 GB/s against 407 for the served row, x1.22. A decoder of zero instructions,
on this read pattern, is worth x1.22 at most. The search space of the first framing has its ceiling before it was
searched.

R1. THE PROBLEM
The number: the rate at which a kernel at 4E's geometry reads the sealed file's Tetra words, in GB/s, on the L40S.
  the card, bare read, best of 16 configurations                 751 [748-753]
  the card, one u32 a load                                       653 [651-655]
  int4 bytes of the sealed file, read and not decoded (q4read)   675 [670-684]
  Tetra words of the sealed file, read and not decoded (pword)   493 [487-501]
  Tetra rows, served decoder                                     407 [398-411]
The reference is now measured: 751 GB/s. The gap: the Tetra words stream at 0.66 of the card with no decode at all,
where the int4 bytes stream at 0.90. Nothing in the repository explains it. It is an anomaly, not a gap to a
bound we know how to close.

What the problem is worth at most (computed, a ceiling and not a forecast): the token of the 4B reads 750.0 MB of
Tetra words. At 407 GB/s they take 1.84 ms; at 751 they would take 1.00 ms. The token would go from 4.227 to
3.38 ms, x1.25, if every Tetra byte were read at the card's rate and the decode cost nothing. The share the read
pattern alone holds, 493 to 751 with the decode kept as it is, is 0.52 ms of the 1.84 (computed from the two rates
on the sealed file's 0.7487 GB: 1.519 ms against 0.997).

R2. THE ROUTE
Explanation first, then search. MODES.md gives the explanation route to an anomaly with no verifier for its cause;
here the killing experiments are diagnostic arms of the bench that already exists, a job of about $0.10 each.

The anomaly: a loop that only loads the Tetra words and xors them reads 493 GB/s where the card reads 751.

Rival hypotheses, each with the arm that would kill it. An arm differs from pword by one thing.
  H1 the side streams. pword still runs the row's binary16 tail against the activation, reads the row scale and
     writes y. Arm: pword with no tail, no scale, no store. Dead if the rate moves by less than 3 %.
  H2 the row. A row of d_in 2,560 is 640 bytes of words: four loop turns, then a warp reduction and a store. Arm:
     the site's words read as one flat stream by the same warps, no row boundary. Dead if under 3 %.
     Against it already: d_in 9,728 rows, four times longer, read slower (433), not faster.
  H3 the load. A block is 48 bits read as two unaligned loads a lane. Arm: the same bytes read by aligned 128-bit
     loads, one a lane. Dead if under 3 %. Against it already: the probe at one u32 a load still reads 653.
  H4 the site. 168 sites a pass, each staging its activation and tables in shared memory behind barriers, with
     no weight read meanwhile. Arm: pword with the staging skipped. Dead if under 3 %.
  H5 the geometry of the claim. Rows are dealt to warps statically inside a CTA and sites to CTAs by a split; the
     slowest CTA sets the wall. Arm: the probe's own schedule (every thread a contiguous share of the site) on the
     Tetra words. Dead if under 3 %.
  H0 the dull one: nothing of the above, the launch and its synchronisation. The bench times on the host around
     168 sites in one launch. Arm: an empty launch at the same geometry, subtracted. Dead if under 20 us.
The hypotheses are not exclusive. The run reads how much of 493 to 751 each arm recovers, and what is left.

The search opens after it, on what the explanation names: read schedules and load-time layouts of the same words
at the same VRAM, each scored with the real decoder in it. The registry and the Mac gate built for the first
framing serve as they are.

THE FIDELITY RULE
The first rule failed on one control of twelve, by 0.032 for a tolerance of 0.03, while the journal's own
round-by-round range for that control is 0.043 wide. The next prereg will hold each control to its journal's
range widened by 0.02 on each side, and keep the window on the served row as it is. This rule is written after
seeing the first run; it is tested on the next run's data, not on the first's, and the first run stays a
description.

Cost so far in the phase: $0.10 of $5. Under the cap: $19.32 of $25.
