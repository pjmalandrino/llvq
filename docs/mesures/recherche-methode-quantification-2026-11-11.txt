THE QUANTIZATION METHOD ITSELF: WHAT THE FIELD DOES AT 2 TO 3 BITS, AND EIGHT CHANGES FOR LLVQ (2026-11-11)
===========================================================================================================
Nature: desk review with web access, $0. No job, no model load, no build, no test.
Read:   docs/ETAT.md, docs/ROADMAP-QUALITY.md, docs/how-it-works.md, docs/STYLE.md,
        docs/archive/revue-structurelle-2026-10-20.md, docs/mesures/gains-criteres-qualite-2026-11-11.txt,
        docs/data/jobs.csv, ops/llvqtune/README.md, llvq-bench/src/bin/rtbits.rs (header).
Ask:    method changes that last for every future model (larger Qwen, other families, MoE), on the
        fundamental criteria: quality, whole-model b/param, VRAM, decode speed, time to first token,
        cost of producing the object. Improving one criterion must not trade off another.
Labels: *measured* (this repository), *computed* (arithmetic here or in a cited note), *estimated*
        (judgement), *reported* (a primary source, URL in the source list at the end, [S1] to [S24]).

THE ANSWER
-----------
Three changes carry most of the expected value, and none changes the served kernel. First, the
encoder objective: YAQA's output-aware Kronecker Hessian reports +2.1 MMLU and +7.5 GSM8K on Qwen3-8B
at 2 bits for 2 GPU-hours [S1]. Second, calibration designed for the served use: GPTQ is the method most
sensitive to the calibration domain, and self-generated reasoning text moved a 3-bit GSM8K from 57.5 to
75.7 [S10]. Third, a sensitivity-driven rate allocation over a ladder of lattice rates: HIGGS reports
+4.06 MMLU at 3.25 bits from allocation alone [S8]. One change is certain and small: coding the f32 tail
columns inside the lattice takes the 4B from 2.164 to 2.016 kernel b/weight (*computed* below). End-to-end
training with codes that move (PV-Tuning, QAT) is where the 2-bit leaders get their last points. It is
feasible for us only in a narrow form, and full QAT is out of a $100 budget at 4B and above. Entropy coding
of the indices is the one daring lead I advise against: it buys nothing on VRAM at our decode speed.

WHAT THE FIELD REPORTS, BY LEVER
---------------------------------
Numbers are *reported* by the source named. Protocols differ from ours (zero-shot averages, MMLU-Redux,
perplexity at other contexts), so no row is comparable to ETAT §3 without a rerun.

1. Encoder objective with output curvature (YAQA, ICML 2026) [S1]
   - Qwen3-8B, QTIP 2-bit, no fine-tuning. LDLQ (4K sequences, 1.5 GPU-h): MMLU 63.94, GSM8K 56.31,
     KL 0.285. YAQA-B 2K sequences (1 GPU-h): 65.74, 62.14, 0.227. 4K (2 GPU-h): 66.04, 63.82, 0.221.
     64K (30 GPU-h): 66.75, 66.07, 0.205. FP16: 72.99, 84.31.
   - Qwen3-8B at 3 bits: MMLU 70.40 to 71.36, GSM8K 80.81 to 82.55, KL 0.0681 to 0.0479.
   - With QuIP#-style recovery fine-tuning (Llama 3.1 8B, 2 bits): KL 0.185 to 0.147 (YAQA-B), zero-shot
     average 65.44 to 67.54 (YAQA-A). Fine-tuning narrows the gap; YAQA keeps 10 to 20 % lower KL.
   - The quantizer, the codebook and inference are unchanged. Hessian storage roughly doubles.
   - Third party: on Qwen3-8B 2-bit, YAQA reads 72.14 zero-shot average against 72.04 for QTIP and 74.12
     for FP16 [S20].
   - GPTAQ (asymmetric target, ICML 2025) and GuidedQuant (end-loss gradient, ICML 2025) are the cheaper
     cousins [S6] [S7]. Neither abstract gives a 2-bit MMLU.

2. Fine-tuning after rounding [S2] [S3] [S4] [S5]
   - QuIP# 2-bit, WikiText-2 at 4K: Llama 2 7B 8.22 to 6.19 with fine-tuning, 13B 6.06 to 5.35, 70B 4.16
     to 3.91 [S4]. The blockwise stage fine-tunes the not-yet-quantized layers of a block to compensate for
     the quantized ones, then trains the remaining continuous parameters end to end. About 50 GPU-hours
     at 70B.
   - PV-Tuning, AQLM 2.01 bits, Llama 2 7B: calibration only 7.38 ppl and 53.2 average accuracy;
     continuous parameters only 6.69 and 56.57; straight-through 6.41 and 57.04; PV with STE 5.90 and
     58.19 [S2]. At 2 bits the gain over AQLM is +4.88 / +4.33 / +1.97 average accuracy at 7B / 13B /
     70B. The V step re-encodes only the weights with the largest update, under a trust ratio of 0.01.
     Longest run: 2 days on 8 GPUs to beat the baselines.
   - EfficientQAT: Llama-2-70B at 2 bits in 41 hours on one A100-80GB, 69.48 against 72.41 [S5].
   - QTIP: computed codes are not tunable and are not fine-tuned; only its hybrid code is [S3].
   - mlx-lm ships the frozen-code form as DWQ: scales and biases distilled from the unquantized teacher,
     best at 2 to 4 bits, no figure given [S17].

3. Calibration data [S10] [S11] [S12] [S13] [S18]
   - DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, 3-bit weights: GPTQ on WikiText-2 reads GSM8K 57.5, average 28.3; on
     self-generated reasoning traces 75.7 and 38.1 (+9.8). AWQ moves +1.8 only [S10].
   - Calibrating on the target task alone can hurt; adding general data stabilises it, and perplexity
     sensitivity is uncorrelated with reasoning sensitivity (Kendall tau near 0) [S11].
   - Ternary PTQ of Qwen3: the full-precision model's own reasoning traces as calibration context give
     +8.97 % at 4B, with 4 M calibration tokens [S12].
   - Self-calibration (model-generated text) often beats real data (NAACL 2025) [S13].
   - EXL3 calibrates its final conversion on a self-generated trace, 250 rows of 2,048 tokens [S18].
   - MoE: expert-balanced self-sampling gains more than 10 HumanEval points at 4 bits on DeepSeekMoE-16B
     [S14].

4. Rate allocation across tensors [S8] [S9] [S18] [S11]
   - HIGGS linearity theorem: perplexity increase is close to the sum over layers of alpha_l times the
     relative MSE of layer l [S8]. Llama 3.1 8B at 3.25 bits: uniform HIGGS 7.110 ppl and 57.56 MMLU,
     dynamic data-free 6.388 and 61.62 (+4.06), uniform GPTQ 58.37.
   - EXL3 measures each tensor's KL sensitivity with noise shaped like the quantizer's error, fits
     KL = S_t * rfn_t(K)^alpha with alpha near 2, and solves the byte budget greedily. Shaped noise ranks
     tensors at Spearman 0.84 against real swaps, isotropic noise 0.61. About two hours for a 27B [S18].
   - Q-Palette: fractional-bit trellis quantizers plus a knapsack allocator, NeurIPS 2025 [S9].
   - Residual VQ is how QuIP# reaches 3 and 4 bits: the 2-bit E8P codebook applied twice gives 4 bits.
     Llama 2 7B at 4K context: 6.19 / 5.41 / 5.19 ppl at 2 / 3 / 4 bits [S4].

5. Codes trained (QAT) [S15] [S16] [S19] [S20] [S21]
   - LC-QAT (ICML 2026): codewords c = Az + B over z in {0..3}^4, trained with a gradient estimator.
     Qwen3-8B 2-bit: 10.23 ppl and 72.18 average against 74.12 for FP16. Llama-3-8B: 73.42 with 4 B tokens
     against ParetoQ's 72.07 with 30 B tokens. Wall clock 61 h on 8 A800 for the 8B, PTQ included [S20].
   - ParetoQ (NeurIPS 2025): a learning transition between 2 and 3 bits; below it, representations
     change drastically, so 2-bit QAT is closer to retraining than to calibration [S15].
   - BitNet b1.58 2B4T: ternary, trained from scratch on 4 trillion tokens [S16].
   - PrismML Bonsai 8B (Qwen3-8B architecture): 1.125 b/weight embeddings included, 1.15 GB. MMLU-Redux
     65.7 against 83 for Qwen3-8B, GSM8K 88 against 93, their harness [S19]. The recipe is not published.
   - UniSVQ: affine maps of integer lattices plus blockwise fine-tuning, ICML 2026 [S21].

6. Entropy coding [S22] [S23]
   - EntQuant (2026): FP8 or int8 weights with rate-regularised scales, ANS-coded with nvCOMP. Llama-2-70B
     at 2.1 effective bits: 4.52 ppl and 68.6 average against 3.32 and 72.8 for BF16; QuIP# 2-bit reads
     4.16 and 70.9 in the same table. Inference 1.5 to 2x slower than BF16, decoding block by block [S22].
   - Lossless coders (DFloat11, ECF8, EntroLLM) target 8 to 16-bit weights or edge CPUs [S23].

7. Lattices and transforms [S24] [S4]
   - NestQuant (ICML 2025): nested Gosset lattices for weights, activations and KV at 4 bits, Llama-3-8B
     6.6 ppl against 6.14 FP16 and 7.3 for SpinQuant [S24].
   - QuIP# trains its Hadamard sign vectors as real vectors during fine-tuning [S4].

WHERE LLVQ STANDS ON THESE AXES
--------------------------------
- Objective: GPTQ minimises tr(E H_in E') row by row; no output information enters (review 2026-10-20 §1.2).
- Fine-tuning: one continuous lever, row scales, trained against the f16 teacher with codes frozen:
  +3.15 / +3.29 / +1.67 MMLU at 4B / 8B / 14B (*measured*, ETAT §4). The free_params mode exists and never
  ran. Codes never move after encoding.
- Calibration: DCLM-edu, 64 x 2,048 tokens per encode; the seeded draw samples a buffer of about 127
  documents with 27 % duplicates (audit ML 2026-11-02). Draw sigma 1.7 to 2.92 MMLU pp (*measured*).
- Allocation: two tiers, Tetra at 2.150 kernel b/weight and int4 g128 at 4.25 (*computed*, ROADMAP-QUALITY:
  +2.10 per weight moved). The int4 records are min/max RTN from the checkpoint, outside GPTQ and outside
  the row-scale training. The choice is by projection type and layer window, set by hand.
- Codebook: on Gaussian blocks Tetra keeps 88.89 % of the signal, the 47-bit ball 92.00 % (how-it-works
  §7). The Gaussian rate-distortion bound at 2 bits is 1 - 2^-4 = 93.75 %. Read as 1 - relative MSE, Tetra
  sits 2.5 dB, about 0.4 b/weight, from the bound, the ball 1.1 dB (*computed*). The repository measured
  that the codebook choice does not move MMLU beyond noise (leech0c13, ROADMAP-QUALITY row 3).
- Tail: d_in mod 24 columns per row, stored f32 on disk and counted f32 by rtbits, f16 on the card.

The gaps to FP16 (6.77 MMLU and 9.63 GSM8K at 4B, ETAT §3) are largest on reasoning, and the field's
largest reported levers on reasoning are the objective (YAQA, GSM8K +7.5) and the calibration domain.

THE EIGHT PROPOSALS, RANKED BY SOLIDITY
----------------------------------------
Noise floors used: MMLU paired CI95 about 0.6 pp between two files on the full split; draw sigma 1.7 to
2.92 pp as soon as an arm re-encodes; GSM8K paired half-width 1.3 to 1.9 pp; perplexity 0.12 % at
constant file; KL to f16 on 12 windows, noise under 2 % (review §1.2). Prices *measured* in jobs.csv:
4B row scales $4.00, 14B row scales $9.78 (h200), MMLU $0.70 / $1.00 / $1.60-1.95, GSM8K $2.13 / $2.37 /
$4.24, 14B encode on card $15.35, 14B chain about $32. An l40sx1 hour is $1.80.

P1. YAQA: an output-aware Kronecker Hessian in the encoder objective
   What:     replace the per-row objective tr(E H_in E') by tr(H_O E H_in E'), H_O dense over output rows,
             sketched from one torch backward over the calibration set (YAQA sketch B). The block-of-24
             sweep keeps its H_in feedback within a row and adds feedback across rows through H_O.
             Codebook, format, kernel and file width unchanged. Row scales train on top as today.
   Evidence: Qwen3-8B 2-bit, the closest published object to ours: MMLU +2.10 and GSM8K +7.51 at 4K
             sequences, KL -22 % [S1]. After recovery fine-tuning the KL gain is still 10 to 20 %. The
             method is quantizer-agnostic by construction; it chooses a point within the quantized space.
             Transfer to a 24-dim lattice: the sweep is new code (both axes), the search is unchanged.
   Effect:   MMLU +0.5 to +1.7 pp at 4B, +0.3 to +0.9 at 14B; GSM8K +1 to +5 pp at 4B (*estimated*: the
             review's +0.9 to +1.7 transfer, widened for our row-scale stage absorbing part of the gain,
             and the measured sub-additivity 0.62 to 0.79). Wrong if row scales already recover what the
             output metric adds; the KL gate below detects that before any MMLU spend.
   Others:   b/param, VRAM, decode, first token: unchanged, same file format and byte count. Production
             cost rises: the sketch is 1 to 2 L40S hours at 4B ($2 to $4) and 4 to 8 at 14B ($7 to $15),
             encoding time x1.2 to x2, H_O storage about 31 GB at 4B and 97 GB at 14B in f32 (*estimated*,
             d_out squared per matrix). That is a small move on the cost axis, inside a $100 budget.
   Build:    5 to 10 agent-days (review §3). Recurs per model at the sketch price only.
   Settle:   Qwen3-1.7B, three calibration draws x {GPTQ, YAQA}, Mac encodes at $0 under a go, KL to f16
             on 12 windows. Gate: KL lower by 10 % or more in all three pairs. Then the 4B chain (encode,
             row scales $4, MMLU $0.70, GSM8K $2.13): gate MMLU or GSM8K paired gain above 0 at CI95, the
             other not below -0.6.
   Closed:   none. L10 (ROADMAP-QUALITY) scored it 0 on a misreading (review §1.2). The closed Spherical
             GPTQ feedback is a different objective.
   Small team, $25-100: feasible at 4B and 8B; 14B fits at about $40 with the chain.

P2. The tail coded inside the lattice
   What:     pad each row's d_in mod 24 columns into one more Tetra block, the activation padded with zeros.
             The padded coordinates multiply zero, so the encoder searches with H zero on them: 48 bits
             carry 16 real weights (2560-wide inputs) or 8 (5120 and 17408). The f32 tail disappears from
             the file and the f16 tail from the card.
   Evidence: arithmetic, no literature needed. On the 4B Tetra set (q, k, gate, up in 36 layers, down in 24)
             the tail holds 14.65 M weights; kernel b/weight goes from 2.164 to 2.016 (*computed*, rtbits
             accounting with f32 tail and row scales; the published 2.150 counts a slightly different set).
   Effect:   whole-model b/param -0.105 at 4B, -0.061 at 8B, -0.026 at 14B in rtbits accounting; card
             bytes -23.6 / -27.8 / -18.4 MB (*computed*, f16 tail on the card). Quality 0 to -0.3 MMLU pp
             (*estimated*: 0.5 % of the weights at 3 to 6 b/weight in a rotated basis where no column is
             salient, ROADMAP-QUALITY row 13). Wrong if the tail carries the rotation's Kronecker remainder
             with structure; the free arm below measures it.
   Others:   decode: fewer bytes and no tail epilogue, so neutral or faster (*estimated*). First token:
             the prefill path reads the same block type. Production cost unchanged.
   Build:    1 to 2 days encoder side, 3 to 5 days format v2 and the CUDA, Metal and CPU decoders, oracle
             on every backend. Breaks format v1: the full suite runs before the commit.
   Settle:   $0 then $0.70: code the 4B tails on the Mac into a RESTORE-style dense arm, paired perplexity
             (bar 0.12 %), then a full-split MMLU. Gate: MMLU paired within +-0.6 and perplexity within
             +0.3 %. Only then the format.
   Closed:   none. ROADMAP-QUALITY row 7 (f32 to f16 tail) is accounting only; row 13 permutes columns.
   Small team: yes, cheapest of the eight. It is a one-time format change that serves every model whose
             widths are not multiples of 24, and the gain shrinks with width.

P3. Calibration as a designed object: self-generated, mixed, deduplicated
   What:     build the calibration set from the model's own chat-template generations (thinking on and
             off) on a broad prompt pool with math and code, mixed with DCLM-edu, deduplicated, with a
             fixed buffer and Hessian shrinkage rho in [0.7, 0.9]. Use it twice: for H_in (and H_O under
             P1) in the encoder, and as the corpus of the row-scale distillation. For MoE, balance the
             tokens across experts (EBSS [S14]). No benchmark split enters; a contamination gate like
             ops/mmlu_aux_overlap.py runs first.
   Evidence: GPTQ 3-bit GSM8K 57.5 to 75.7 on a 1.5B reasoning model with self-generated traces [S10];
             target-only data can hurt, the mix helps [S11]; EXL3 and AYOT calibrate on self-generated
             traces [S18] [S12]. All of it is on scalar or ternary quantizers and on reasoning-distilled
             models; our encoder is GPTQ-like, the most domain-sensitive family in [S10].
   Effect:   GSM8K +1 to +5 pp at 4B, MMLU -0.5 to +1.0 (*estimated*). Shrinkage alone: -31 % median
             perplexity at 0.6B (*measured*, m1-hessienne-shrink), MMLU never measured. Wrong if DCLM-edu
             already spans what the chat traces add; the constant-code arm below isolates the distillation
             half.
   Others:   format, kernel, VRAM, decode and first token unchanged. Production cost: generating 20 M
             tokens with vLLM on an L40S, $1 to $3 at 4B (*estimated*).
   Build:    2 to 4 days (generation script, mix, dedup, seed buffer fix). Recurs per model at a few dollars.
   Settle:   constant codes first: retrain the 4B row scales from the pre-row-scale DCLM codes on the mix
             instead of DCLM ($4 + MMLU $0.70 + GSM8K $2.13 + generation $2, about $9). Gate: GSM8K paired
             gain above 0, MMLU paired not below -0.6, perplexity not above +0.5 %. The Hessian half comes
             after, read in KL on a held-out chat set and on WikiText-2, three draws.
   Closed:   none in ETAT §6. The operator declined the mmlu-aux corpus on 2026-09-23: that was benchmark-
             format text; this is the model's own generic output. ROADMAP-QUALITY row 6 (volume) is the
             same family and stays open.
   Small team: yes.

P4. Rate allocation by measured sensitivity, over a ladder of lattice rates
   What:     two steps. (a) An allocator: measure each tensor's KL sensitivity on the f16 model (EXL3's
             shaped noise, or a swap per tensor), fit KL = S_t * rfn(K)^alpha, and solve the byte budget
             per tensor, not per type window. Every tier goes through the same Hessian encoder and the same
             distillation, so the int4 records stop being RTN. (b) A rate ladder inside the lattice:
             residual Tetra, the same 48-bit word applied twice at 4 b/weight as QuIP# does with E8P [S4],
             with the allocator choosing among Tetra-2, Tetra-4 and int4 for compatibility.
   Evidence: HIGGS +4.06 MMLU at 3.25 bits from allocation, Llama 3.1 8B [S8]; EXL3 per-tensor optimizer
             in production [S18]; Q-Palette knapsack [S9]; perplexity sensitivity misses reasoning
             sensitivity [S11]. Ours: v_proj to int4 gained +3.47 MMLU for +0.0493 b/param, and at 8B
             moving int4 from o_proj to down_proj gained +0.77 for fewer bytes (*measured*,
             ROADMAP-QUALITY row 1, sealed-8b-27). Allocation is already our largest lever after row
             scales, chosen by hand.
   Effect:   at constant 2.73 b/param, MMLU +0.5 to +2.5 pp at 4B, GSM8K +1 to +4 (*estimated*); or the
             same quality at 2.55 to 2.65 b/param. Wrong if the hand allocation is already near the
             knapsack optimum; step (a) prices that for about $2 before any format work.
   Others:   b/param and VRAM equal or lower by construction of the budget. Decode: step (a) keeps today's
             two kernels; step (b) adds a word type whose decode costs about twice Tetra's per block,
             +-5 % tok/s (*estimated*), gated. First token: the GEMM prefill needs the new word type too.
   Build:    (a) 3 to 5 days, Python plus int4swap. (b) 3 to 5 weeks: encoder residual stage, format v2
             record kind, CUDA, Metal and CPU decoders, 4E rows. Sensitivity recurs per model, $1 to $4.
   Settle:   (a) on the 4B: per-tensor sensitivity on 12 windows (one L40S hour, $1.80), knapsack at the
             served byte count with tiers {Tetra, int4}, then the file by int4swap and MMLU + GSM8K ($3).
             Gate: predicted KL down by 10 % or more against the served allocation, then MMLU paired gain
             above 0 at CI95 and GSM8K not below 0.
   Closed:   E3 and Golay70 are single-rate layouts; group_scales and design C are not allocation. None
             applies. The operator's "no int4 tweaks" concerns hand moves; this is the rule that makes them
             automatic for every model.
   Small team: (a) yes. (b) yes in time, weeks of kernel work, run cost small.

P5. Blockwise compensation fine-tuning during encoding
   What:     inside each transformer block, after q/k/v are coded, train the block's still-f16 matrices
             (o, gate, up, down) for a few hundred steps to restore the f16 block output on calibration
             activations, then code them; at the block's end, train its row scales and norms to the block
             output. Codes never move inside the loop, so no re-encoding sits in a gradient step.
   Evidence: QuIP# fine-tuning, 2-bit Llama 2 7B 8.22 to 6.19 ppl (blockwise plus end-to-end, not
             separated) [S4]; EfficientQAT Block-AP and UniSVQ use a blockwise stage [S5] [S21]. Ours: the
             intra-block calibration defect of ROADMAP-QUALITY row C (o_proj and down_proj calibrated on
             unquantized inputs, 35 % of the file).
   Effect:   MMLU +0.5 to +2 pp at 4B on top of today's row scales, GSM8K +1 to +4 (*estimated*). Wrong if
             the end-to-end row-scale stage already recovers the blockwise gain; QuIP# does not separate
             the two halves.
   Others:   format and kernel unchanged. Production: the training is seconds per block on a card
             (6 x 100 M parameters x 131 k tokens is about 8e16 FLOP an epoch, *computed*); the cost is moving
             the Rust encoder and a torch trainer into one card job, about the 14B encode's $15 at 14B and
             $3 to $6 at 4B (*estimated*).
   Build:    1 to 2 weeks (Rust-torch hand-off per matrix, resume, journal). Recurs per model.
   Settle:   0.6B, 28 blocks, three draws, KL to f16 on 12 windows, then row scales on both arms. Gate: KL
             lower by 10 % after row scales on all three pairs.
   Closed:   ROADMAP-QUALITY row 21 discarded Block-AP because it re-encodes inside the loop; this variant
             does not. Row C is its first step.
   Small team: yes.

P6. Codes that move: continuous distillation plus a PV-style discrete step
   What:     phase 1, the existing free_params mode (row scales, f16 tails or P2's tail codes, centroids,
             norms) on a mixed corpus. Phase 2, the V step: from the end-to-end gradient on the decoded
             weights, select the 1 % of blocks with the largest update and re-encode each toward
             W - grad / L with the Tetra search, then return to phase 1.
   Evidence: on AQLM 2 bits, continuous parameters alone take 53.2 to 56.57 average accuracy, PV with STE
             to 58.19 [S2]: the discrete step is worth about as much again as the continuous one. On
             Qwen3-8B, PV-Tuning reads 70.99 against 72.14 for YAQA without fine-tuning [S20], so P1 comes
             first.
   Effect:   MMLU +0.5 to +2 pp at 4B above P1 and P5, GSM8K +1 to +4 (*estimated*, wide). Wrong if the
             fixed lattice leaves the V step only moves that GPTQ already found.
   Others:   format and kernel unchanged. Production: the V step at 1 % of 119 M Tetra blocks costs about
             357 core-seconds at 300 us a block (*computed*), a few seconds on a 64-core host; 500 steps
             plus training is $15 to $40 at 4B and $60 to $150 at 14B (*estimated*).
   Build:    3 to 5 weeks. Recurs per model.
   Settle:   phase 1 alone on the sealed 4B: $3.10 training + MMLU $0.70 + GSM8K $2.13, as in the quality
             note's row 5. The V step on 0.6B in KL, three draws.
   Closed:   ROADMAP-QUALITY row 22 discarded PV-Tuning because the P step has little to move on a fixed
             lattice. That holds for P; the V step changes codes and was not assessed. Not an ETAT §6 lead.
   Small team: 4B within $100, 14B at the edge.

P7. Quantization-aware training proper (latent weights, codes every step)
   What:     train latent f16 weights with the lattice quantizer in the forward pass (STE, or an LC-QAT-like
             relaxation over the integer coordinates x = p + 2c + 4k), on 0.5 to 4 B tokens.
   Evidence: LC-QAT Qwen3-8B 2-bit 72.18 against 74.12 FP16, 61 h on 8 A800 [S20]; ParetoQ 30 B tokens
             [S15]; BitNet 4 T [S16]; Bonsai, recipe unpublished [S19]. QAT is where the 1 to 2-bit models
             get their retention.
   Effect:   possibly half the remaining gap (*estimated*, uncertain). Production cost: about 488 A800-hours
             for an 8B in LC-QAT's setting, $500 to $1,500 at our prices (*estimated*); a GPU Tetra encoder
             does not exist and STE needs one at every step.
   Others:   the object is unchanged; only the cost axis moves, by a factor of 10 to 50.
   Settle:   a Qwen3-0.6B pilot under a cap of $30, KL and GSM8K against P1 to P6 on the same model. It
             prices the ceiling; it does not serve.
   Closed:   none, out of budget (Bonsai memory: QAT at 4B about $1,000 to $2,000, *estimated*).
   Small team, $25-100: no, at 4B and above.

P8. Entropy-coded or entropy-constrained indices to go under 2.15 b/weight
   What:     code the 48-bit words with ANS, or encode with a Lagrangian rate term so that frequent classes
             get short codes.
   Evidence: the marginal entropy of the sealed 4B word is 47.5 of 48 bits (agent measure, not journaled,
             review §2), so plain coding saves about 1 %. EntQuant reaches 2.1 effective bits but decodes
             1.5 to 2x slower than BF16, block by block into a buffer [S22]. A variable-length word breaks
             the random access of the fused kernel and 4E's row loop.
   Effect:   b/param -0.01 to -0.15 (*estimated*) against decode speed down 30 to 50 % if decoded on the
             card, or zero VRAM gain if decoded at load. That trades one criterion for another.
   Verdict:  not proposed for VRAM. Disk and download size only, where it costs nothing at run time and is
             not a fundamental criterion here. P2 reaches 2.016 kernel b/weight at 4B without it.
   Small team: feasible, not worth it.

NOT PROPOSED, AND WHY
----------------------
- A learned transform before the lattice: QuIP#'s real-valued sign vectors are the input-side diagonal our
  RMSNorm training already covers, +0.02 MMLU (*measured*, sealed-rownorms); output rotation is about 0
  across the paper's Table 9 (ROADMAP-QUALITY, Discarded); the 2026 transform survey shows the best
  transform depends on the format and gives no lattice case [S24b].
- Another codebook (trellis or a larger shaping region): Tetra sits about 0.4 b/weight from the Gaussian
  bound (*computed* above), but leech0c13 measured that a 21.5 % perplexity spread between codebooks leaves
  MMLU in the noise, and the review closed the trellis switch.
- Best-of-N draw selection on a held-out split: it is a measurement design, already row 2 of the quality
  note of the same day.
