THE PORT IS FREE, AND THE CONTROL IS EXACT
-------------------------------------------
Arm A's NVRTC source hashes **b848b0c2ca1f97e3...**, which is the sha256 the
reference journal records. Byte for byte the same kernel source, the same
file, the same flags, the same tile. The only thing that changed between the
two is the dispatch: 40 `cfg` sites and two concrete CUDA types became one
`Arc<dyn LatticeProj>`.

99.3 against 98.3, ranges [96.6-99.8] against [97.5-98.6], overlapping over
most of their width. There is no cost to find. The dynamic dispatch was
*computed* at 0.010 % to 0.025 % of a decode step and is, as far as this bench
can see, not there.

**32 tokens identical to the dense arm on both arms**, which is the gate that
mattered. A dispatch refactor that moved one token would be reverted whatever
it measured.

BOTH SIGNED PREDICTIONS WERE WRONG, IN OPPOSITE DIRECTIONS
-----------------------------------------------------------
  arm A    predicted [97.0, 98.8]    measured **99.3**    OUTSIDE, above
  arm B    predicted [110, 120]      measured **105.8**   OUTSIDE, below
  VRAM     predicted 1.39 GB         measured 1.39 GB     correct

Arm A. The interval was built from the reference's median and allowed only
downward movement plus its own noise. It ignored that these are two separate
jobs, possibly on two physical cards: arm A's own range is [96.6-99.8], 3.2 %
wide, against the reference's 1.1 %. Between-job spread is three times
within-job spread, and the interval was narrower than the wrong one of the
two.

Arm B is the interesting error, and it is a units error of a family this
repository has caught before.

`tuile-l40s-2026-09-20` measures the tile at **+16.1 %** on the projection
bench: 4.078 ms to 3.423 ms. I carried the PERCENTAGE across to a different
total and damped it by hand. The percentage does not carry. What carries is
the **absolute** saving, 0.655 ms a token, because the tile changes the matvec
and nothing else:

  arm A step   1 / 99.3   = 10.07 ms
  minus                     0.655 ms   (the tile's measured saving)
  arm B step              =  9.42 ms  ->  106.2 tok/s
  measured                                **105.8**

0.4 % out. The arithmetic I should have done was available before the run, in
a journal written the day before. Taking a ratio measured on one total and
re-applying it to another is the same shape as reading b/param where a
document means b/weight, which cost this repository two arms in September.

WHAT THIS SETTLES
------------------
- The device port is free at the throughput the bench can see, and it is now
  the served dispatch on both backends.
- The served number for the fine-tuned object moves from 98.3 to **105.8
  tok/s in 1.39 GB**, x2.44 against its own dense arm in the same process.
  That gain is the TILE, measured on 2026-09-20, not the port.
