WHAT THE LATTICE ALLOWS IN THE ENCODER AND IN TRAINING: ONE MECHANISM KEPT, ONE STACK PART, TWO REJECTED (2026-11-11)
=====================================================================================================================
Nature: invention study, $0. No HF job, no model load, no cargo build. One numpy script on the Mac, under one second
        (the 196,560 minimal vectors of the scaled Leech lattice, their membership, their alignment with random
        gradients, and a closed-form search over them checked against brute force). Web access for prior art.
Read:   docs/llvq-paper-notes.md (not the PDF), docs/ETAT.md (with §6), docs/ROADMAP-QUALITY.md, docs/how-it-works.md
        §3-8, llvq-quant/src/gptq.rs, llvq-search/src/tetra/encoder.rs (header), ops/llvqtune/README.md,
        docs/data/jobs.csv (row-scale rows), and the journals tetra-directions-2026-09-16, l36-capture-2026-09-13,
        artstat-4b-2026-11-04, dclm-rowscales-2026-09-20, tetranu-rowscales-2026-09-19; tonight's notes
        recherche-methode-quantification, gains-criteres-qualite, recherche-structure-leech-format.
Bar:    close half the FP16 gap at 4B, so +3.4 MMLU or +4.8 GSM8K, at the same file format and byte count, with an
        encoding cost under about $100 for a 4B, by a method that carries to larger models.
Labels: *measured* (a journal of this repository), *computed* (arithmetic, or the numpy script here), *estimated*
        (judgement), *reported* (a paper, URL in the source list).

THE VERDICT
-----------
No idea clears the bar alone at its central estimate. One comes close on GSM8K and is new: training with codes that
move, where the move is a step to an adjacent Voronoi cell of Λ24 chosen exactly among the 196,560 minimal vectors.
That search is a closed form plus a soft-decision Golay decode, so it costs a few seconds for every block of a 4B. No
other codebook family has a local move set like it, and the repository has never moved a code after encoding.
Alone, with a distillation corpus designed for reasoning, it is *estimated* at +1 to +3 MMLU and +2 to +8 GSM8K at 4B,
centres +1.8 and +4.5. Stacked with K2 below, the GSM8K centre is about +5, at the bar with roughly even odds. The
MMLU centre stays near +2 and does not clear +3.4. Seed idea (b), a Hessian-metric block search, is rejected on the
repository's own measurement. Seed idea (c), joint coding across blocks or rows, needs a format change or reduces to
the row scales already trained.

The gap that is left is mostly invisible to generic-text KL. After row scales the 4B reads 1.0074 times the FP16
perplexity and still sits nine MMLU points under it on that base (*measured*, dclm-rowscales: ppl 16.2509 to 12.3268).
A KL fall of 3.8 % on DCLM bought +3.15 MMLU (*measured*, same journal). Any training lever is therefore only as good as
the text it is trained on, and every estimate below assumes a corpus built for chat and reasoning.

FACTS THE STUDY RESTS ON
------------------------
F1. The minimal vectors. In the integer scaling √8·Λ24 they have norm² 32 and three shapes: (±2)^8 on an octad with an
    even number of minus signs, 97,152; (∓3, ±1^23) with the signs of a Golay codeword, 98,304; (±4, ±4, 0^22), 1,104.
    The script builds all 196,560 and every one passes the three conditions of `Leech::contains` (*computed*).
F2. Alignment with a gradient. For 2,000 random gradient directions in 24 dimensions, the best minimal vector has
    cosine 0.774 at the median, 0.747 at p5, 0.731 at worst. The best single-coordinate step ±e_i, which is what an
    adjacent-cell move is for a scalar grid, reads 0.453, 0.369 and 0.319. The 1,104 vectors of the third shape alone
    read 0.595 (*computed*). One lattice step captures 0.774² = 60 % of the first-order descent of a block, a scalar
    step 20 %.
F3. Exact search in closed form. The best of the 196,560 for a linear score ⟨g, v⟩ splits by shape. Shape 1: for each
    of 759 octads, the signs of g, and if the minus count is odd, flip the smallest |g_i|. Shape 3: the two largest
    |g_i|. Shape 2: for each of the 24 positions, a soft-decision maximum-likelihood decode of the Golay code with
    input g where g_i is multiplied by -3. Tetra already ships that code as a 64-state trellis over the trio. The
    script checks the closed form against brute force over all 196,560 on 200 gradients: largest difference 3.6e-15
    (*computed*). The cost is about 10^4 to 10^5 operations a block instead of 9.4·10^6 for the brute product.
F4. Step size. A minimal vector has norm √32. A Tetra point of shell m has norm √(16 m). A step orthogonal to y turns
    the block's direction by atan(√(2/m)): 26.6° at m = 8, 22.2° at m = 12, 19.5° at m = 16 (*computed*). Tetra's
    quantization error on Gaussian blocks has angle acos(√0.8889) = 19.5° (*computed* from how-it-works §7). One
    lattice step is about one cell. The shell histogram of the sealed 4B is not in any journal, so m is a range here.
F5. The repository's codes never move after encoding. llvqtune holds the invariant "the gradient never reaches the
    decoded directions" (ops/llvqtune/README.md). Row scales are the one trained lever: +3.15 / +3.29 / +1.67 MMLU at
    4B / 8B / 14B (*measured*, ETAT §4).
F6. A local metric search was measured here and collapses when chained. Re-choosing a block's direction under the
    conditional Hessian metric gained 2.393 % of conditional cost at a fixed state, 0.911 % when chained, and
    -8.763 % on reserved activations, on 48 rows of the 4B (*measured*, tetra-directions-2026-09-16).

K1. LATTICE-STEP DISTILLATION: CODES THAT MOVE BY ONE VORONOI NEIGHBOUR
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Algorithm.
  Start from the sealed file: codes, two centroids a matrix, row scales, tails, norms. The student forward uses the
  decoded f16 weights, as llvqtune does today. The teacher is the FP16 checkpoint, or its top-64 logits stored offline.
  Loss: token KL to the teacher on a mixed corpus. One third DCLM-edu, two thirds the teacher's own chat completions
  with thinking on and off over a broad prompt pool with math and code. A last phase regenerates that part on policy:
  the student writes, the teacher scores.
  P phase (continuous, every step): Adam on row scales, RMSNorm weights, centroids and tails, the free_params mode that
  exists and never ran. Beside it, accumulate for every Tetra weight an EMA of the gradient g and of g², the diagonal
  Fisher F. The gradient is taken on the natural-basis export and rotated into the coded basis by the matrix's own
  Hadamard and signs.
  V phase (every 100 to 200 steps):
    1. For every Tetra block, project g on the tangent of the unit sphere at the decoded direction ŷ, since the decoder
       rebuilds c_g · rscale · y/‖y‖. Score the 196,560 moves y → y + v by the closed form of F3, and the gain-bit flip.
       Keep the best 8 per block (list Viterbi on the shape-2 decode).
    2. Evaluate each kept move exactly under the second-order model ΔL = ⟨g, Δw⟩ + ½ Σ F Δw², with Δw the true change of
       the 24 decoded weights after normalisation. Test membership of y + v in Tetra with `Tetra::encode`, which returns
       the word or None.
    3. Accept the moves of most negative predicted ΔL, under PV-Tuning's trust rule: ‖ΔW‖/‖W‖ ≤ 1 % a matrix a V phase.
    4. A second move type, for the 0.1 to 1 % of blocks with the largest predicted gain: re-encode the Newton target
       x - g/F with the production encoder (329 µs a block, *measured*, HISTORIQUE 2026-09-05). This is PV-Tuning's own
       V step; the lattice step is the cheap exhaustive one beside it.
    5. Re-decode the changed rows, rotate back, write them into the student, reset their EMAs.
  Write-back: a `codeswap` binary in the manner of `rowscale` replaces words in the sealed file. Same widths.
Why it is specific to the lattice.
  The move set is the same for every block of every matrix, it is the set of Voronoi-relevant vectors of smallest norm,
  and its best element is found exactly by the Golay trellis Tetra already holds (F1, F3). AQLM's V step needs a beam
  search per block over learned codebooks, the expensive half of PV-Tuning [S2]. QTIP's computed codes are not tuned
  at all [S3]: one bit of a trellis word changes many weights. A scalar grid moves one coordinate at a time with 20 %
  of the descent captured, against 60 % here (F2).
Evidence.
  - PV-Tuning, Llama 2 7B, AQLM 2.01 bits, average zero-shot: calibration only 53.2, continuous only 56.57, STE 57.04,
    PV 57.81, PV plus STE 58.19 [S2]. The discrete step is worth +1.2 to +1.6 above the continuous one on a strong base,
    and +5.9 on a GPTQ base (46.27 to 52.17). Llama 3 8B at 2.01 bits: 64.36 against 68.61 FP16 [S2]. No MMLU there.
  - Ours: the continuous half alone gave +3.15 MMLU at 4B for zero bits (F5).
  - Corpus: GPTQ at 3 bits, GSM8K 57.5 to 75.7 by calibrating on self-generated reasoning [S10]. Teacher-forced
    distillation leaves math impaired; on-policy distillation, Qwen3-1.7B at W2.79, GSM8K 54.28 against 44.28 for
    continued QAD [S26]. The last phase of K1 is that on-policy form.
  - Prior art: no published method moving LLM weight codes along lattice minimal vectors was found (searches of
    2026-11-11). GLQ and QuIP# use E8 codes with frozen indices after encoding [S4] [S27].
Expected gain at 4B (*estimated*).
  MMLU +1 to +3, centre +1.8. GSM8K +2 to +8, centre +4.5. Of that, the discrete part is +0.5 to +1.5 MMLU and +1 to +3
  GSM8K; the rest is the corpus, which a continuous-only arm on the same corpus also buys (tonight's P3). The GSM8K
  range is wider because its gap is wider (9.63 against 6.77) and because the corpus is aimed at it.
  Wrong if: (1) fewer than a fifth of the best moves are Tetra members, so the cheap step rarely applies; (2) the
  second-order model predicts gains the forward pass does not realise, as F6's chained collapse did for a local proxy;
  (3) held-out KL falls and MMLU does not move, the seventh dissociation; (4) GSM8K stays put because of exposure bias
  despite the on-policy phase.
Why the served object does not move.
  A move changes a 48-bit word's value, never its width. Centroid count, row-scale count and tails keep their widths.
  The byte count is identical, so b/param and VRAM are identical. Decode cost does not depend on the word's value, so
  tok/s and first token are unchanged. One guard: the centroids may train, and the served S grid needs
  c1/c0 in [1/3.14, 3.14] (artstat-4b reads 1.2610 to 2.0106 today); the trainer clamps the ratio. The int4 records
  are left alone by K1. They can take ±1 nibble moves by the same loop, a scalar rider that is not part of this claim.
Cost.
  - Step time: the weight-gradient GEMMs add about a third to today's student plus teacher step, 0.74 s on l40sx1
    (*measured*, tetranu-rowscales), so about 1.0 s (*estimated*). 10,000 steps: 2.8 h.
  - V phase at 4B, 118,665,216 Tetra blocks (*measured*, artstat-4b): the brute product is 1.12·10^15 operations
    (*computed*), about 12 s of tensor cores, and its top-k streams about 47 TB if the scores are materialised, about
    55 s at 864 GB/s (*computed*). The closed form of F3 avoids both. Exact evaluation and membership on 8 candidates a
    block on the host: minutes. 50 V phases: about 1.7 h (*estimated*).
  - Memory on l40sx1: student 8 GB, teacher 8 GB or offline logits, gradient and two EMAs on the 2.86 G Tetra weights
    in bf16, 3 × 5.7 GB, activations with checkpointing about 6 GB: about 39 GB of 48 (*estimated*).
  - Per 4B object: corpus generation $2 to $3, training about 4.5 h, $8 to $9, MMLU $0.70, GSM8K $2.13: about $15.
    The 4B program with its control arm and both evaluations: about $25 (*estimated* on the prices of jobs.csv).
  - 14B on one h200 ($9.78 for 118 min, *measured*): teacher logits offline, about 5 h of training, corpus $5,
    MMLU $1.95, GSM8K $4.24: about $36 (*estimated*). 32B: the gradient buffers exceed one h200; two cards or
    gradient EMAs offloaded a layer at a time.
  - Build: 2 to 3 weeks. Torch side (gradient EMAs, tangent projection, V-phase driver), Rust side (closed-form
    screen, membership and word packing by `Tetra::encode`, Newton re-encode, `codeswap`), and the decoder identity of
    every written word checked against the sealed file's untouched words.
Why it is not a closed lead.
  - ROADMAP-QUALITY row 22 discarded PV-Tuning: its P step has little to move on a fixed lattice, and it priced 1 to 2
    months and $270 to $1,250. K1's P step is the row-scale training that already exists and was measured. The V step
    is new and cheap because of F3, with no latent weights and no Adam state on the weights.
  - Row 21 discarded Block-AP because it re-encodes inside the loop. K1 re-encodes at most 1 % of blocks in a V phase.
  - Row 15 noted that the gradient never crosses the decoder. The V step is the crossing.
  - Spherical GPTQ feedback (ETAT §6) changed the encoder's feedback. K1 does not touch the encoder.
  - Row 12 and F6 are local proxies. K1's objective is the end-to-end KL, read on held-out text.
Cheapest test that can kill it.
  Stage 0, $0 on the Mac under a go, about one hour. Qwen3-0.6B, one existing Tetra encoding, gradient and Fisher of
  the KL over 64 windows of 2,048 tokens of the mixed corpus, one V phase at τ = 0.5 % of blocks. Read: the Tetra
  membership rate of the best move; predicted against realised KL change on 12 held-out windows, over 20 batches of
  moves. Kill if membership is under 20 %, or the Spearman correlation is under 0.3, or held-out KL rises.
  Stage 1, under $3 on l40sx1. Qwen3-0.6B at three calibration draws. Arm A: continuous training on the mixed corpus,
  2,000 steps. Arm B: A plus a V phase every 100 steps. Same codes at the start, same data order, same seed, so the
  draw noise cancels inside each pair. Gate: held-out KL of B at least 10 % under A on chat text and on WikiText-2, in
  3 pairs of 3. The three draws say whether the effect survives a change of encoding, which one encode cannot.
  Stage 2, about $25. The sealed 4B, arms A and B as above at full length, MMLU on 14,042 questions and GSM8K on 1,319,
  both paired against the sealed file and against each other. Both arms start from the sealed codes, so the
  re-encoding sd of 1.7 to 2.9 pp does not enter A against B or B against sealed. The floor is the paired interval,
  about 0.6 pp of MMLU and 1.3 to 1.9 of GSM8K, plus the training-seed spread, which is not measured; a second seed of
  arm A ($5) prices it when B - A lies under 1 pp. Bar: B - sealed at least +4.8 GSM8K or +3.4 MMLU with the lower
  bound above 0. Credit to the lattice step: B - A above 0 at CI95 on either axis.
Verdict: under the bar alone on its centres, inside it on the top of the GSM8K range. The only seed with a path there.

K2. KRONECKER-METRIC DESCENT FROM THE SEALED CODES: YAQA'S OBJECTIVE, SOLVED BY LATTICE STEPS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Algorithm.
  The layer objective with output curvature is L = tr(H_O E H_in Eᵀ), E = Ŵ - W, H_in captured as today (hcapture),
  H_O sketched from one backward over the calibration set (YAQA sketch B [S1]). Start from the sealed codes, no
  re-encode. Each round: G = 2 H_O E H_in, two products a matrix; per block, the same screen as K1 on the tangent part
  of G, exact ΔL from the Kronecker diagonal terms, membership, and acceptance of a small non-interacting set (one block
  per row and per column block a round). 20 to 50 rounds, then row scales retrained as today.
Why it is specific to the lattice. It reuses F3. YAQA as published needs its rounding sweep rewritten on two axes,
  5 to 10 agent-days for us (tonight's P1). Here the solver is the same closed-form neighbour search for every metric.
Evidence. YAQA, Qwen3-8B at 2 bits on QTIP, no fine-tuning: MMLU 63.94 to 66.04 and GSM8K 56.31 to 63.82, KL -22 %
  [S1]. Its gain over LDLQ survives recovery fine-tuning at 10 to 20 % lower KL [S1].
Expected gain at 4B (*estimated*): MMLU +0.5 to +1.7, GSM8K +1 to +5, tonight's P1 range. A descent from a GPTQ point
  may stop in a local minimum short of YAQA's sweep; that is the main way it falls short.
The served object: same as K1, words change and nothing else.
Cost: H_O sketch $2 to $4 on l40sx1 (tonight's P1). The rounds on the Mac: G for the 168 Tetra matrices is about
  5·10^13 flop a round, 10 to 20 s; 50 rounds well under an hour (*estimated*). Storage of H_O about 31 GB in f32 at 4B.
  Then row scales $4, MMLU $0.70, GSM8K $2.13: about $11 at 4B, about $25 at 14B.
Its advantage in measurement: it starts from the sealed codes. A YAQA re-encode drags in the draw sd of 1.7 to 2.9 pp;
  this post-pass is paired against the sealed file at constant initial codes.
Kill test: Qwen3-0.6B on the Mac under a go, three existing encodings, sketch in torch, KL to f16 on 12 held-out
  windows before and after the post-pass. Gate: KL at least 10 % lower in 3 pairs of 3, against YAQA's 22 %. Kill if
  any pair rises, the F6 pattern.
Verdict: under the bar alone. A stack part, and the cheaper of the two to settle.

THE STACK
---------
K2 first, then K1 trained from K2's codes on the designed corpus. Centres K1 +1.8 / +4.5, K2 +1.0 / +2.5 (MMLU /
GSM8K). With the sub-additivity this repository measured, 0.62 to 0.79, the stack reads MMLU +1.7 to +2.2 and GSM8K
+4.3 to +5.5 (*computed* from the *estimated* centres). GSM8K reaches the +4.8 bar at its centre, MMLU does not.
Tonight's P4 (a), sensitivity allocation of the int4 tier, is independent of both and adds its own +0.3 to +1.5 MMLU
estimate before discount; with it the MMLU centre rises to about +2.5, still short of +3.4.
Total at 4B: about $40 with both kill stages, both control arms and all evaluations. At 14B: about $65.

REJECTED SEEDS
--------------
(b) Babai or sphere decoding under the block Hessian metric.
  The repository measured it in the form that matters. A metric-aware choice among the encoder's candidates gained
  2.393 % of conditional cost locally, 0.911 % chained and -8.763 % on reserved activations (F6). The metric's spread
  bounds what any exact search can win. The rotated diagonal per block reads max/min 1.93 to 3.98, CV 0.17 to 0.40, on a
  256-token wiring dump that is not a measurement (l36-capture). If it held, the shaping gain of a diagonal metric is
  the ratio of arithmetic to geometric mean, exp(σ²/2) for a log-normal spread: 1.4 % to 7.7 % of the layer proxy,
  0.06 to 0.32 dB (*computed*). Outside, GPTQ is Babai's nearest plane [S28], and OJBKQ's randomized K-best Babai
  moves Qwen3-4B at 3 bits from 61.76 to 62.03 zero-shot average [S29]. Under the bar by a factor of ten. GPTQ leaves
  little on the table per block because the error feedback already spends the off-diagonal coupling. If ever wanted,
  F3's screen solves it at no extra cost.
(c) Joint coding across blocks or rows.
  Cosets, gluing two blocks into 48 dimensions, or a shared shift per column block need a new word type or side data:
  a format change, outside the brief. Λ24 x Λ24 is also the best known quantizer in 48 dimensions, so gluing buys no
  density (Agrell and Allen, cited in recherche-structure-leech-format). A shared gain per row from shell statistics
  is the row scale, already trained (F5); its closed-form constant moved +0.13 pp unweighted (L01). Coupling across
  rows through H_O is K2.
(d) Other leads considered.
  Training the rotation signs re-encodes everything, which is K1 at full width and full cost. Per-matrix crossover of
  several calibration draws, chosen on a held-out split, is real but not lattice-specific; it belongs with tonight's
  draw-selection row.

WHAT THIS NOTE DID NOT CHECK
----------------------------
- Membership of y + v in Tetra. Tetra names a 2^47 subset of Λ24 with ranks at most 4 and the best 2,048 rank vectors a
  class. Shape-3 and shape-1 moves change a few ranks; shape-2 moves change the parity of every coordinate and may
  fail often. Stage 0 measures it.
- The sealed 4B's shell histogram, which fixes F4's step angle.
- The training-seed spread of llvqtune at 4B.
- MMLU or GSM8K evidence for a discrete step on any Qwen3 model: PV-Tuning reports zero-shot averages and perplexity
  only, on Llama.
- The on-policy result [S26] was read from its abstract page; its tables and its quantizer were not.
