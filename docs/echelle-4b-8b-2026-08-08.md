# The scaling point: 4B, 8B, 14B, measured with a single variable (2026-08-08, extended 2026-08-10)

> The project's central question from the start: **does the 2-bit deficit shrink as the model
> grows?** It had a partial answer in perplexity and none in capabilities. It now has one on
> both axes.
>
> Protocol: same codebook (`leech1c12`), same calibration corpus (C4, 131k tokens), same
> rotation, same harness, same card (L40S), same token fingerprints (**ppl `3f1baca9033bf251` ·
> MMLU `65dcd53655e8bfa5`**) across the six measurements of each model. The only variable is
> size. Sources: [`mesures/a4-campagne-2026-08-06.txt`](mesures/a4-campagne-2026-08-06.txt),

| metric | step 4B→8B | step 8B→14B | the slowdown |
|---|---|---|---|
| **perplexity** *(paired, 12 windows, same text at all three sizes)* | ×0.881211 [0.856, 0.907], t = −9.62, 12/12 | ×0.974855 [0.959, 0.991], t = −3.38, 10/12 | **RESOLVED**. step1 − step2 = **−0.100992** [−0.137670, −0.064313], t = −6.06, 11/12 |
| **MMLU gap to 4-bit** *(not paired across sizes, SEs in quadrature)* | −6.96 pp, p = 0.0001 | −1.40 pp, p = 0.40 | **NOT RESOLVED** on the second step |

**This is not a contradiction, it is information.** Perplexity is paired **across sizes**, same
window, same text, fingerprint `3f1baca9033bf251` at all three campaigns, and weighs 49,140
scored tokens. MMLU combines two independent campaigns of 2,280 questions, with no pairing
possible across sizes. So the two do not have the same power, and they do not measure the same
thing. §3ter of `CLAUDE.md` has established since 2026-08-02 that 2 bits damages **reasoning**
far more than **recall**, and recall is mostly what a perplexity corpus measures. **The correct
form, everywhere: "the slowdown is resolved in perplexity and is not resolved on the MMLU gap
to 4-bit".**
([`mesures/ppl-appariee-4b-2026-08-17.txt`](mesures/ppl-appariee-4b-2026-08-17.txt),
[`data/ppl-genou.csv`](data/ppl-genou.csv).)

**The MMLU side in detail.** The three gaps are of the same species, so the drop from one step to
the next can be **tested** (SEs combined in quadrature, *computed*; distinct campaigns, so no
cross-model pairing, which would make no sense):

| step | drop in the gap | SE | z | p | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4B → 8B | 6.96 pp | 1.82 | 3.82 | 0.0001 | **RESOLVED** |
| **8B → 14B** | **1.40 pp** | **1.68** | **0.83** | **0.40** | **NOT RESOLVED** |
| 4B → 14B | 8.36 pp | 1.91 | 4.38 | ≈ 1e-5 | **RESOLVED** |

**On MMLU, the first closing is real, the second is in the noise.** The slowdown there is a
property of the estimated points that the bars do not separate. Caveat: **p = 0.40 does not
prove equality either.** On that step the data are **silent**: "it slows down" and "it
continues" both remain compatible with these three points. **The perplexity verdict does not
lift that non-verdict**: a second metric that answers does not make the first one talk.

**This paragraph said "on the perplexity axis the knee is not even testable, and for a retention
reason", REFUTED on the evening of 2026-08-17.** The reason given was correct for the state of
things then: the step 8B→14B had its bar (excess over f16 shrinking by **−13.9%, 95% CI
[−22.8, −4.9]**, real but **badly bounded**, a factor 4.6 between the endpoints) while the step
4B→8B, which carries the "−43%" and therefore the whole force of the argument, was held to admit
no bar, so that "we compare a step with bars to a bare step". **The 4B NLLs were recovered from
the job logs**, the step is **−42.8%, 95% CI [−51.8, −33.5]**, and the perplexity knee is
**RESOLVED** (table above). Caveat: what its bar does not cover is that it carries corpus
variability alone. The knee compares **three artifacts each produced once**, and the
**calibration** draw is absent at all three scales. A t = −6.06 on corpus variability says
nothing about what three other seeds would have given.
Against the **AWQ** reference, the one that carries the product argument, the shrinkage is
**−1.58%, 95% CI [−3.14, −0.004]**, t = 2.2063 against a threshold of 2.200985: it excludes zero
**by 0.005**. **Never write "the gap closes significantly".** This caveat **stands as written**
after 2026-08-17: against the AWQ reference the *knee* is resolved as well
(−0.057562 [−0.101127, −0.013997], t = −2.91, 10/12) but **half as clearly** as against f16
(t = −6.06), and the *step* 8B→14B stays at the limit of detection there. **The resolved knee
and the borderline step are two different statements about the same column of numbers**; do not
carry the confidence of one over to the other.
Caveat: **do not read −13.9 against −1.58 as a ratio of 9, they are TWO PARAMETERIZATIONS.** The
−13.9% is the shrinkage of the **excess**; the −1.58% is the shrinkage of the perplexity
**ratio**, the only form the journal publishes against the AWQ reference. At equal
parameterization, ratio against ratio, the two references give **−2.51% [−4.12, −0.88]** (f16)
and **−1.58% [−3.14, −0.004]** (AWQ).
([`mesures/ppl-appariee-8b-14b-2026-08-17.txt`](mesures/ppl-appariee-8b-14b-2026-08-17.txt).)

**What stays withdrawn, and already was**: "the gap to 4 bits shrinks twice as fast as the
deficit itself", true from 4B to 8B (14.45 → 7.49), false from 8B to 14B (7.49 → 6.09).

**What all this reinforces**: we do not publish a scaling law on three points, and
**the 32B point remains what would settle it**.

## 1bis. What pairing takes away, and why that is progress

The two-point version concluded that "4 bits is starting to pay": indistinguishable from f16 at
4B (−0.28 pp), it lost 3.07 pp at 8B. The 14B gives −0.76 pp, which invites writing that the 8B
was an exception.

**`bin/mmlupair` forbids both readings.** On the 14B, where the per-question dumps finally
exist, **all THREE pairs since 2026-08-17**:

| comparison | stratified micro Δ | 95% CI | McNemar | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| f16 − AWQ | +0.76 pp | **[−0.65, +2.17]** | p = 0.089 | **not resolved** |
| f16 − LLVQ | +6.85 pp | [+4.52, +9.12] | p = 8.7·10⁻¹⁶ | resolved |
| **AWQ − LLVQ** | **+6.09 pp** | **[+3.62, +8.52]** | **p = 1.143·10⁻¹¹** | **resolved** |

**This table had only two rows, and the sentence introducing it added "the third one does not
exist, its dumps are lost".** False: they were sleeping in the **mounted bucket** (SE 1.25 pp;
A right/B wrong 230, A wrong/B right 106, 14.7% discordant),
[`mesures/mmlupair-14b-2026-08-17.txt`](mesures/mmlupair-14b-2026-08-17.txt).

So one can say **neither** that 4 bits pays at 14B **nor** that it does not. That is not a
disappointing result: it is the first time one of the small gaps in this record is *tested*
instead of being read off two independent sampling bars, which the `mmlupair` header calls "the
wrong test, and the conservative one".

**Consequence for the paper**: the sentence at `paper/sections/intro.tex:73`, "AWQ itself
starts paying (−0.3 points at 4B, −3.1 at 8B)", does not survive a third point. It rests on two
gaps of which **none has been tested in pairing**, and the only one that has returns an interval
containing zero. The replay of the 4B and 8B dumps, which §2 already demanded, is now the
condition for keeping that sentence in any form at all.

## 3. What this is worth, and what it is not worth

**What is established**: **three** points, measured in a single harness, with a single variable,
with identical token fingerprints. Across these three points the direction is clear and
consistent between perplexity and capabilities. That was not a given: the project learned at its
own expense that the two metrics do not measure the same thing.

**What this does not prove**: a scaling law. Three points still do not make a curve, and
extrapolating to 70B would be exactly the kind of shortcut this record refuses.

**And the third point makes the back-of-the-envelope extrapolation less tempting, not more.** The
two-point version wrote: "*if* the trend continues, the gap to 4 bits closes around 16-32B". The
14B **is** inside that window, and the gap there is still **6.09 pp** [+3.62, +8.52]: the window
is crossed without the gap closing. That is exactly why we do not publish a scaling law on two
points, nor on three.

**This sentence said "it closed by 19% where the previous step had halved it", and it is
withdrawn as of 2026-08-17.** It stated the slowdown of the **MMLU gap** as a fact two lines
before the paragraph that retracts it. The two drops are **−6.96 pp** (4B→8B, SE 1.82,
p = 0.0001, resolved) and **−1.40 pp** (8B→14B, SE 1.68, p = 0.40, **not resolved**): comparing
"19%" to "halved" compares two estimated points that the bars do not separate. Caveat: the
opposite cannot be said either, p = 0.40 leaves the data **silent** on that step, not equal.

**"The measurement says the decrease is slowing" was WITHDRAWN on 2026-08-17 (morning), and the
perplexity half of it was GIVEN BACK that evening. The sentence is publishable only if it NAMES
ITS METRIC.** On the **MMLU gap to 4 bits** the measurement does not say that: **4B→8B −6.96 pp,
SE 1.82, z 3.82, p = 0.0001 → resolved**; **8B→14B −1.40 pp, SE 1.68, z 0.83, p = 0.40 → NOT
resolved**; 4B→14B −8.36 pp, p ≈ 1e-5 → resolved. **The slowdown there is not separated by the
bars**, it is a property of the estimated points, and p = 0.40 does not say either that the
closing continues. On **perplexity**, the measurement says exactly that: step1 − step2 =
**−0.100992** [−0.137670, −0.064313], t = −6.06, 11 windows out of 12 in the direction of the
knee.

The 32B point would cost ~$60 and one night. It is still the measurement that would settle this,
and it stays more interesting than before: it will say **whether** the **capability** curve
flattens, a question that three points leave open on that metric.
**This sentence said "it will say whether the 14B knee is a plateau or an accident", which
presupposed the knee**, something the MMLU test does not give. Caveat: it is not repaired by
invoking perplexity either. The knee is resolved there, but what the product buys is judged on
capabilities, and **no perplexity verdict carries over to MMLU**. That is the lesson of §3ter,
"never again present perplexity alone as evidence of quality".

**The product reading**: at 4B, 4 bits dominates without discussion (−0.28 pp for half as many
bits). At 8B the choice becomes a real trade-off: **7.5 points of MMLU against 10.6% less
memory**. `Planes14` + q8 embedding weighs **5.322 b/param whole model** (`rtbits`, *measured on
the file*, and reproduced to the thousandth by the engine's VRAM report) against **5.956** for
the real AWQ in the same accounting
([`mesures/rtbits-planes-8b-2026-08-09.txt`](mesures/rtbits-planes-8b-2026-08-09.txt),
which prints that "10.6%" itself). The direction of the trade-off depends on what is scarce: the
card or the precision.

## 4. The external reference point

The LLVQ paper gives Qwen3-8B without fine-tuning at **×1.13** in perplexity; we are at
**×1.220** with ~100× fewer calibration tokens and input rotation alone. At 4B we were at ×1.385
against their ×1.374, which is parity. The gap that appears at 8B is consistent with a
calibration deficit that weighs more as the model grows: a lead, not a conclusion.

Caveat: **the 14B has no external reference point**. Table 6 of the paper covers only the 4B and
the 8B. Our ×1.189 therefore compares to nothing published, and it must not be presented as if it
did. What it documents is **internal**, the shape of our own scaling curve.
