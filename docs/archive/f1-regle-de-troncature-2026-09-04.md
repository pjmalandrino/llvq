> Question posée : existe-t-il une règle de troncature par section qui soit à la fois
> une **bijection exacte**, **décodable en décalages et masques** dans le budget du noyau
> servi, et proche des **0,7292 dB** de la boule de dimension 8 ?

> chacune attaquée par un relecteur qui recompte tout lui-même, puis une synthèse.
>
> **Réponse : `a_rule_exists_with_a_cost`.**

## 1. Le résultat qui décide

A per-section rule meeting all three constraints exists — the lowest-norm ±pair table plus a per-state even-weight sign mask — at ~250-270 ALU ops per block (parity with Planes14's ~250) and 0.6895 dB, 0.026 dB under its own 8-ball ceiling; its cost is L1 footprint, because the region is per-coset data and the only symmetry the shipped kernel can apply for free has 67 orbits on the end-section cosets and 9 on the middle, i.e. 1112 KiB of tables at (12,15,12) against F1a's 16 KiB gate, or 80 KiB if the codebook is restricted to the odd Leech coset. That settles constraint (b) as an L1 question rather than an ALU one, and it does not save F1b: I re-derived by exact enumeration that the best any per-section region can reach is 0.7150 dB at 39 bits (88.98%) and 0.7292 dB at 40 (89.10%), against a kill that needs 0.8742 dB, so F1b dies on the word structure whatever the truncation rule.

## 3. Ce que ça change pour la spec F1b

Seven changes, if this answer is taken.

1. **Replace the truncation rule.** Out: "the 2^w lowest-norm points of the coset, ties broken lexicographically" with an unrank decode. In: "the 2^(w−1) lowest-norm ± PAIRS of the base coset, one table row per pair, plus one sign bit per section." Same region, same dB; the bijection becomes a finite-table property checkable offline (rows pairwise distinct as unordered pairs; no row with x = −x) instead of an argument, and the kernel never re-derives it. The word stays exactly 8 + 3w + 1 bits with no fractional rate anywhere — the 2026-08-04 failure mode.

2. **Pair-granular, not point-granular.** The spec must say the selection is made on pairs. The literal lowest-norm set is *not* negation-closed: measured on 1⁸+4E8 at w=13, 6812 of 8192 points have their negative inside and the boundary shell is partial (3016 of the 4396 points at ‖x‖² = 104). Both counts are even, so a pair-respecting selection with the same norm profile exists — but it has to be specified, not assumed.

3. **Add the per-state sign mask to the decode.** An 8-bit even-weight mask from a 256-entry prologue table, one XOR per slot. Without it the rule prices a region it does not produce: a shared table is a translate, and the delivered region measures 0.3008 dB instead of 0.6895 dB, with max|coord| going 9 → 11 so the "3 magnitude bits + 1 sign = one nibble, 8 nibbles = one 32-bit row" premise fails. The load-bearing lemma is that every Golay codeword meets an octad evenly, so all admissible mod-4 patterns on a section have even weight, and even-weight diagonal sign matrices lie in Aut(E₈) (h′ − h has even weight, hence lies in D₈).

4. **Drop "one base table per section".** F1a's green at 8.0 KiB assumed it. The measured orbit counts are 67 for the end lattice 4E₈ under the 128 even-weight sign flips (128 odd cosets → 2 orbits, 128 even cosets → 65) and 9 for the middle lattice 2A under all 256 sign flips (all sign flips preserve A, since they do not change the mod-2 pattern). At (12,15,12) with 2^(w+1)-byte tables that is 67 × 8 KiB + 9 × 64 KiB = 1112 KiB. Restricted to the odd Leech coset it is 2 × 8 + 1 × 64 = 80 KiB. Neither is 16 KiB, and F1a's table gate should be re-marked accordingly.

5. **Change the split.** (13,13,13) is the worst legal allocation, not the neutral one: section volumes are 2^(w+16)/2^(w+12)/2^(w+16), so isotropy wants w₂ = w₁ + 4 and (13,13,13) is 16:1 anisotropic. Ceilings: (13,13,13) 0.5175 dB / 87.34%; (12,15,12), (12,16,11) and (11,16,12) all 0.7150 dB / 88.98%; (12,16,12) at 40 bits 0.7292 dB / 89.10%, which spends the gain bit — and `llvq-cuda/src/planes14_host.rs:113` refuses any gain field but 1 bit, so that is a format change, not a knob. The roadmap's `[state 8][s₁ ~13][s₂ ~13][s₃ ~13][gain 1]` costs 1.6 pp of retention against (12,15,12) for nothing.

6. **State what F1b can now report.** With the repaired rule at (12,15,12): 0.6895 dB, 88.77%. Ceiling for a perfect per-section region at 39 bits: 0.7150 dB, 88.98%. Kill: 0.8742 dB, 90.3%. F1b as specified will return a number in the 88.7–89.1% band. It is a benchmark of the coupling, not of the truncation rule, and the answer to ETAT §5bis's open question is now bounded: for any rule whose section point depends only on (state, label_i), the region is a product and the 8 state bits buy exactly zero shaping — they move the granular lattice, never the region. Only a genuinely 24-dimensional region escapes, and 4·(√8Λ₂₄) ⊂ √8Λ₂₄ at index 4²⁴ = 2⁴⁸ *does* fit a 48-bit lattice field at 1.0292 dB / 91.6%; what forbids it is a 24-dimensional nearest-point decode at ~3600 ops (Vardy–Be'ery), i.e. the same constraint (b), and the path `format-noyau.md` §1 already declares closed.

7. **Strike the covolume caveat.** ETAT §5bis's "the bijection is argued from covolume rather than proved" is discharged. For a fixed-body region: Λ′ ⊂ Λ of index 2^w makes t + Λ a disjoint union of exactly 2^w cosets of Λ′, each meeting a fundamental domain of Λ′ once, for *every* offset t — no radius, no offset dependence. For the table region: a finite table of distinct pairs plus a sign bit. Two proofs, both survived their adversary.

## 4. Recommandation de la synthèse

1. Record the structural result, which is the durable output of the four leads and does not depend on any gate: there are exactly two ways to define a per-section region, and each fails one constraint by construction. A *fixed-body* region (Voronoi of an index-2^w sublattice) is offset-independent, needs no table, and settles the bijection group-theoretically — but "which point of this coset lies in the body" is a nearest-point decode, ~1000 ops/block against ~250. A *per-coset* region (lowest-norm table) decodes in one table row plus a sign, at parity with Planes14 — but the region is data, so you need one table per coset shape class. The only isometry group the shipped kernel can apply for free is coordinate sign flips (a permutation would need `xb[perm[j]]`, the dynamically-indexed shared read `llvq_planes.cuh:32-37` exists to forbid), and I measured its orbits: 67 on the 256 end-section cosets of 4E8, 9 on the 16 middle cosets of 2A, with the 128 odd cosets collapsing to 2 orbits and the 128 even ones to 65. That measurement, not an opinion, is what prices the family.

2. Two corrections belong in the record regardless of what happens to F1. First, ETAT §5bis's caveat that "the bijection is argued from covolume rather than proved" can be struck: two independent constructions now prove it offset-independently, and both were confirmed by their adversaries. Second, the 89.10% product figure was right. Two submissions attacked it with finite-N granularity corrections (−0.089 dB and −0.28 dB); I enumerated the actual regions and the correction is −0.02 to −0.06 dB per section (worst case −0.189 dB on the single origin-containing coset), and the same estimator returns −0.0202 dB on the served side from the Λ₂₄ theta series, whose cumulative reproduces `N_SHELL_13_CUMULATIVE` exactly. The corrections largely cancel.

3. Do not run F1d as written, whoever decodes. QTIP is 2.246 ms in its own `<<<128,1024,64 KiB>>>` grid; our own no-weights floor `nullk` is 2.306 ms in ours. Adopt is 1.15 × 2.246 = 2.583 ms, below our floor. A zero-cost decoder at the gate's own 2.20 b/weight ceiling reads ~1.00 GB, 1.19 ms at the 836 GB/s net rate FP16 demonstrates, landing at ~3.50 ms against a kill of 3.369 ms. So F1d would fire on any decoder, for a reason that has nothing to do with F1, and `format-noyau.md` §6 already forbids the cross-grid subtraction it implies ("Subtracting nullk from an arm on another grid is illegitimate"). Two leads found this independently and both adversaries confirmed the arithmetic. Re-anchoring it on our own floor is a $0 edit, and it must happen before the prereg is timestamped.

4. What is reserved to you, and I am not deciding it: whether to raise the 16 KiB table gate (an F1a adoption number, not a measurement — the repo has measured *one* 16 KiB read per block as free, never three tables of 16–64 KiB alongside a 12 KiB activation tile, though QTIP's own arm ships 64 KiB of dynamic shared memory in a different grid); whether the odd-coset-only variant is admissible at all, since it changes the fine lattice; whether to spend anything on F1b given that its best possible outcome is 88.8–89.1% against a 90.3% kill; and whether F1 retains value for MODEL CLASS alone. On that last point, the arithmetic only: a 2.20 b/weight kernel layout would be the first in the repo under the product triplet's b_max of 3.00, which Planes14 exceeds by 60%, and it would move the largest admissible class well past 43.3 billion parameters — but the repaired rule's own predicted speed is nowhere near the served 2.15×, so that would be buying model class with throughput. That trade is yours.

5. If anything is spent next, spend $0 first: compile the repaired decoder and read its register count and `local_size_bytes()` out of `preflight`/`cuhcheck`. Planes14 holds 40 registers and 0 local bytes; E1v held 79 and measured 0.25× FP16. Every op count in all eight documents, mine included, is source-level, and `rankdec.rs`'s own comment says no such count has survived contact with a card here.

## 5. Ce qui reste inconnu

- No decoder in any of the four families has been compiled. Every op count here — mine, the four authors', the four attackers' — is source-level, and `llvq-search/src/rankdec.rs`'s own comment says no such count has ever survived contact with a card in this repository. Register pressure is not in any of the models: Planes14 holds 40 registers and 0 local bytes, E1v held 79 and measured 0.25× FP16 at 25 GB/s. A `preflight`/`cuhcheck` register readout is a $0 gate that has not been run.
- My 67 and 9 orbit counts rest on my model of which section cosets occur: v = p·1⁸ + 2·(c restricted to the octad) with that restriction of even weight, times the running-Σk parity shift (4,0,…,0), with 4E₈ absorbing the all-ones octad because 2·1⁸ = 4h and h ∈ E₈. That model reproduces the brief's covolumes (2^16, 2^12, 2^16) and one independent spot check by another lens, but it has not been read out of `llvq-search`'s own trellis construction. If the occurring coset set is different, the table budget moves.
- That the middle section's 32 outgoing branch cosets glue to a single coset of covolume 2^12 is asserted in the framing and verified by nobody. If it fails, the branch bits must be read out of the label and the per-state offset probe becomes a second dependency level — which would break the repaired rule's 'one dependency level, never a chain' property and put constraint (b) back in doubt on its own terms.
- The 16 KiB table gate is an F1a adoption number, not a measurement. What the repository has measured to be free is ONE 16 KiB table read per block (the `cwtab` exoneration). Three probes into 16–64 KiB of tables, by 32 lanes at 32 addresses, sharing a 128 KiB unified L1 with a 12 KiB activation tile and a streaming ~1.0 GB, has never been measured. Note also that such tables must not go in `__constant__` memory, which is broadcast-optimised and would serialise 32 ways on divergent addresses.
- The alphabet width holds on the odd half only. I measured max|coord| = 9 for the odd base coset at w = 13 (magnitudes {1,3,5,7,9}, 3 bits + sign = one nibble, 8 nibbles = one 32-bit row) but 10 for some even cosets at the same w — six magnitudes, which breaks the single-32-bit-row premise on exactly the half of blocks the repo measures at 50.0%.
- The odd-coset-only variant, which is the only way the table budget approaches the gate, has not been assessed for admissibility. It changes the fine lattice from Λ₂₄ to a coset of its even sublattice: +0.2507 dB of granular loss, rate 1.9583 b/dim, my computed retention 88.53%. Whether that is a legal `.llvq` codebook at all, and what it does to `codebook_fingerprint`, is untouched.
- Every dB in this synthesis is a continuous-region second moment applied to sections holding ~3 points per dimension. I bounded the error by direct enumeration (−0.02 to −0.06 dB per section, worst case −0.189 dB, and −0.0202 dB on the served side) rather than assuming it away, and my estimator passes the cube test that the repo's earlier accounting failed. But no discrete quantizer MSE has been simulated on real or Gaussian data; F1b's 20,000-block benchmark is what settles it, and the encoder's overload behaviour under a fixed gain bit is unmeasured in every family.
- I did not enumerate every 8-dimensional lattice for the fixed-body family, nor every 24-dimensional one. The searches were partial: subcodes of RM(1,3) plus D8 controls for the off-grid widths, and Λ₂₄ / Λ₁₆+E₈ / K₁₂+K₁₂ in dimension 24. A lattice with a cheap nearest-point map and a normalized second moment between 0.0682 and 0.0658 would reopen the fixed-body family; none is known to me, and none was proved not to exist.
