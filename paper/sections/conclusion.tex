\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The missing piece of Leech-lattice LLM quantization was never the math ---
it was the decoder. We showed that the full multi-shell codebook can be
decoded inside a matvec faster than the FP16 matvec it replaces; that the
in-VRAM rate is a design axis of its own, independent of the disk rate
(5.51~$\to$~4.80~$\to$~4.34~b/weight at bit-exact decoded content, with the
knee located by a measured negative result at 3.59); that the remaining
2.04~ms between our kernel and its own DRAM floor is latency and decode
rather than bandwidth, of which launch fusion has already recovered 39\%;
and that the integrated system serves a 2-bit 4B model from 2.60~GB at
${\sim}1.4\times$ a corrected reference engine, $2.03\times$ the engine as
it ships.

What we did not show is that 2 bits is the right operating point for a 4B
model; it is not, and our own measurements say so. The open questions that
matter are whether the 8B scale signal ($\times1.267$ at fewer bits) holds
to 70B, and whether the reasoning-concentrated MMLU deficit closes with
paper-scale calibration. Both are measurable with the methodology used
here --- interleaved arms, matching token fingerprints, per-job cost
accounting --- and the entire evidence base of this paper regenerates from
committed CSVs for \$5.04 of GPU time.
