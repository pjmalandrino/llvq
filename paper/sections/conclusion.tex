\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The missing piece of Leech-lattice LLM quantization was the decoder, not the
math. The full multi-shell codebook can be decoded inside a matvec at
memory-bound speed; the in-VRAM rate is a design axis of its own (5.51 to
4.80 to 4.34~b/weight at bit-exact decoded content, with the floor mapped by
a negative result at 3.59); and the integrated system serves a 2-bit 4B model
from 2.60~GB. But a deployed 4-bit kernel converts 88\% of its byte advantage
into time where our best layout converts 65\%, and that gap --- not the
factor against FP16 --- is what a successor should attack.

What we did not show is that 2 bits is the right operating point for a 4B
model; it is not. The open questions are
whether the 4B-to-8B trend holds to the 70B class, and which untested quality
lever (calibration composition, learned column scales, low-rank compensation)
closes a reasoning-concentrated MMLU deficit that scaling calibration alone
will not. Both are measurable with the methodology used here: interleaved
arms, matching token fingerprints, pre-registered gates, per-job cost
accounting.
