\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The missing piece of Leech-lattice LLM quantization was the decoder, not
the math. We showed that the full multi-shell codebook can be
decoded inside a matvec at memory-bound speed, that the in-VRAM rate is a
design axis of its own (5.51 to 4.80 to 4.34~b/weight at bit-exact decoded
content, with the floor
mapped by a measured negative result at 3.59), and that the integrated
system serves a 2-bit 4B model from 2.60~GB at twice the reference
engine's throughput, with the caveat, stated wherever the number appears,
that much of that factor is a fix to the reference engine's own output
head. At 8B the same stack serves 5.45~GB against the official 4-bit
checkpoint's 6.10, from a file half the size, and the quality gap to
4-bit halves as the model doubles.

What we did not show is that 2 bits is the right operating point for a 4B
model; it is not, and our own measurements say so. The open questions that
matter are whether the 4B-to-8B trend (quality gap halving while the
memory verdict flips) holds to the 70B class, and which of the untested
quality levers (calibration composition, learned column scales, low-rank
compensation) closes the reasoning-concentrated MMLU deficit that scaling
calibration alone is unlikely to close. Both are measurable with the
methodology used here: interleaved arms, matching token fingerprints,
pre-registered gates, per-job cost accounting. The entire evidence base of
this paper regenerates from committed CSVs and billed \$19.82 of GPU time.
