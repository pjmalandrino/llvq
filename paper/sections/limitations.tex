\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

We list these in the order a skeptical reader should weigh them.

\textbf{The MMLU deficit is real, reproduced, and unresolved.} $-14.6$
points against FP16 on the 4B served arm, reproduced three times across two
machines ($-14.73$ paired on the sealed artifact, the arm every paired
statistic uses; 10.57 at 8B and 6.85 at 14B, all three resolved). The
per-subject profile shows the mechanism: abstract algebra and accounting
fall to 25\%, exactly chance, while history, law and psychology hold above
80\%. Two-bit quantization damages \emph{reasoning} far more than
\emph{recall}, and a perplexity corpus mostly measures recall; perplexity
alone should never be presented as evidence of quality at this rate. The
obvious suspects are measured and bounded:
output-side rotation (near-zero marginal effect in the original paper's own
ablation); a free-magnitude variant with a closed-form scale solve
($\times1.99$ perplexity at full depth on the 0.6B proxy, killed by a
pre-registered gate); and calibration volume: we calibrate on
${\sim}131$k tokens, roughly $100\times$ less than the original paper, but
a deliberate-contamination oracle (calibrating on the evaluation text
itself, the upper bound on anything volume or corpus can buy) recovers only
$1.6\%$ of perplexity on a proxy, and a $\times13$ volume curve $1.2\%$
--- a bound weaker than it looks (perplexity, 3 blocks, a 0.6B proxy; it
does not bound MMLU), but enough that we did not commission a $\times100$
run. What remains untested: reasoning-weighted calibration
composition, learned per-column scales (worth about two MMLU points under
the original paper's fine-tuning protocol), and low-rank compensation
adapters.

\textbf{We are slower than the 2-bit kernel we set out to beat, and the
margin is large.} QTIP's kernel runs $2.27\times$ our production layout on
matching shapes in the same process (\S\ref{sec:qtip}); the motivation we
inherited from the original paper is confirmed, against us. We measured it
as shipped, in its own launch geometry, and tuned nothing in either
direction --- a tuning pass could move that number, and we did not attempt
one. The mechanism is diagnosed and is not a defect of our
implementation: the two formats store the same rate on disk, and the gap is
entirely the load-time unfolding a combinatorial lattice index requires and
a sliding-window trellis does not. What follows from that is a research
direction rather than a patch, and it is not obviously open: the granular
gain $\Lambda_{24}$ contributes over a deep trellis is at most a decibel,
which a long-memory trellis on incoherence-processed (hence approximately
i.i.d.\ Gaussian) weights already captures. We state this as the reading
that our own measurements support and not as a proof.
The same gap remains for Marlin's batched 4-bit GEMM and for the other 2-bit
vector-quantized systems of \S\ref{sec:related}.

\textbf{Three models end to end, one serving GPU, greedy decoding.}
Everything end-to-end is Qwen3-4B, 8B and 14B on a single L40S at batch
size 1; only the layout bench runs on a second architecture, and what it
finds there bounds the claim rather than extending it (\S\ref{sec:a100}).
Batched serving changes the arithmetic-intensity regime that makes the
kernel memory-bound; larger models change the embedding share and the
tail-column overhead.

\textbf{The scale argument is three points, and not a law.} On the 4B,
4-bit AWQ dominates on every axis except disk and served footprint
(Table~\ref{tab:campaign}), a ${\sim}3\%$ b/param margin that does not,
on its own, justify a 14.6-point MMLU loss. The 8B moves every relevant
number in the 2-bit direction (Table~\ref{tab:campaign8b}); the 14B keeps
the direction and \emph{loses the rate} (Table~\ref{tab:scale}). Whether
the trend continues to the 70B class (the regime that matters for the
sovereignty argument) is measurable with this methodology and is not
measured here, and the third point is exactly the reason not to extrapolate
from the first two. The qualifications on that curve (one verdict per metric on the
slowdown, every interval paired and resting on committed raw output, a
memory margin present at all three sizes but not monotone) are carried
once, in \S\ref{sec:evaluation} and Appendix~\ref{app:scale}, and are
not restated here.

\textbf{Our speedups are over FP16, not over the state of the practice at
either rate.} At the \emph{kernel} level both comparisons exist and both go
against us: the AWQ GEMV converts 88\% of its byte bound against our 65\%,
and the QTIP kernel is $2.27\times$ faster outright (\S\ref{sec:layouts},
\S\ref{sec:qtip}). At the \emph{engine} level the comparison only exists
within each stack ($2.413\times$ for AWQ--Marlin in vLLM against
$1.11\times$ for us in \texttt{candle}; \S\ref{sec:integration}), and it
goes against us too --- with the further caveat that the two stacks are far
apart independently of any weight format: vLLM's own FP16 control reaches
83.1~tok/s on this model where ours reaches 43.6, so roughly a factor 1.9
of the end-to-end distance is engine, not decoder. That factor is a real
cost to a user and a confound to a reader, which is why we report the two
ratios side by side and never divide them. Tables~\ref{tab:campaign}
and~\ref{tab:campaign8b} carry no 4-bit throughput column because a vLLM
tok/s and a \texttt{candle} tok/s are not the same measurement.

\textbf{Speedups carry ranges, and the two protocols differ.} Inter-process
dispersion on identical binaries spans several percent, so every bench
ratio (\S\ref{sec:layouts}, Table~\ref{tab:layouts},
Figure~\ref{fig:layouts}) is a median of per-round ratios over interleaved
arms, with its min--max range. The end-to-end figures of
\S\ref{sec:integration} are medians of five timed generations per arm, but
their ratios are quotients of two medians with a conservative envelope,
because the two arms load their models exclusively and their rounds never
coexist. Effects smaller than a range are treated as unresolved, and second
decimals read as dispersion, not measurement.

\textbf{The rotated basis is a real serving constraint.} Weights are stored
in the incoherence-rotated basis; any consumer of the artifact must apply
the activation rotation. Our rotation kernel is verified bit-exact on CUDA
but exists nowhere else (no Metal port yet), so the served path is currently
NVIDIA-only, which sits awkwardly with the sovereignty motivation and is
first in line for future work.

\textbf{That same rotation caps the servable width one model class below
the one the argument wants.} The rotation is a Walsh--Hadamard transform: a
ladder of barriers, logarithmic in the transform length, and a barrier
synchronizes one block, so the whole activation must be resident in that
block's shared memory, one f32 per coordinate whatever the input dtype. The
binding width is \texttt{intermediate\_size}, the input of
\texttt{down\_proj}, and the ceiling follows from arithmetic alone:

\midrule
Qwen3-4B  &  9\,728 &  38\,912 & fits & fits \\

Qwen3-14B & 17\,408 &  69\,632 & does not fit & fits \\
Qwen3-32B & 25\,600 & 102\,400 & does not fit & \textbf{short by 1\,024 bytes} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
This hardware exposes \emph{two} bounds and our host code checked one: a guard
comparing against the 49\,152-byte per-block default refused to launch the
14B --- correctly, since an overrun would corrupt silently --- which cost
one job (\$0.24, no tokens produced) and is how the wall was found. The
larger bound is opt-in, requested once per function, and is
\emph{measured} on the card that served (101\,376 bytes); the kernel did
not change a line, only the host arithmetic did. The 8B sat exactly on the default
bound, to the byte, and nothing ever flagged it because nothing ever
failed. The 32B misses the opt-in bound by 1\,024 bytes; the shortfall is the
driver's reserve, which no host-side trimming can reclaim. This is a bound on what the served
path can serve, independent of any format: every layout of \S\ref{sec:layouts}
decodes weights in the rotated basis and calls the same rotation, so no
layout verdict moves it. One escape is named and deliberately \emph{not}
designed: carrying the shared activation in half precision would halve the
footprint below the opt-in bound, but at $n = 25\,600$ it would place an
accumulation of depth equivalent to ${\sim}14.6$ Hadamard stages in half
precision: a genuine numerical trade, and we have neither made nor
priced it.
