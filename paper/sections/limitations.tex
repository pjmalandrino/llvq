\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

We list these in the order a skeptical reader should weigh them.

\textbf{The MMLU deficit is real, reproduced, and unresolved.} $-14.6$
points against FP16 on the 4B, reproduced three times across two machines
($-10.6$ on the 8B). The per-subject profile shows the mechanism: abstract
algebra and accounting fall to 25\%, exactly chance, while history, law and
psychology hold above 80\%. Two-bit quantization damages \emph{reasoning}
far more than \emph{recall}, and a perplexity corpus mostly measures
recall; perplexity alone should never be presented as evidence of quality
at this rate. We measured and eliminated the obvious suspects rather than
leaving them open. Output-side rotation: the original paper's own ablation
puts its marginal effect near zero once input rotation is present. A
free-magnitude variant with a closed-form scale solve (the configuration
the original paper's appendix recommends, as we read it): $\times1.99$
perplexity at full depth, killed by a pre-registered gate. Calibration
volume and corpus: a deliberate-contamination oracle (calibrating on the
evaluation text itself) recovers at most $1.6\%$ of perplexity on a
proxy, so scaling calibration cannot close the gap by itself. What remains
untested: reasoning-weighted calibration composition, learned per-column
scales (the original paper's fine-tuning, which reports $-9.5$ points
under its protocol), and low-rank compensation adapters.

\textbf{Two models, one GPU, greedy decoding.} Everything end-to-end is
Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B on a single L40S with batch size 1 and greedy
decode. Batched serving changes the arithmetic-intensity regime that makes
the kernel memory-bound; larger models change the embedding share and the
tail-column overhead. Two scale points are not a scaling law.

\textbf{The scale argument is two points, not a law.} On the 4B, the
honest summary of Table~\ref{tab:campaign} is that 4-bit AWQ dominates on
every axis except disk. The 8B moves every relevant number in the 2-bit
direction (Table~\ref{tab:campaign8b}): the quality gap halves and the
memory verdict flips. Whether the trend continues to the 70B class, the
regime the sovereignty argument actually cares about, is measurable with
this methodology and is not measured here.

\textbf{AWQ speed is not comparable.} The AWQ arm was never executed in our
engine (its quality was, under matching fingerprints). Cross-engine
throughput comparisons are meaningless and we make none.

\textbf{Speedups carry ranges, not third decimals.} Inter-process
dispersion on identical binaries spans several percent; all ratios are
medians of per-round ratios with min--max ranges, and effects smaller than
the range are treated as unresolved.

\textbf{The rotated basis is a real serving constraint.} Weights are stored
in the incoherence-rotated basis; any consumer of the artifact must apply
the activation rotation. Our rotation kernel is verified bit-exact on CUDA
but exists nowhere else (no Metal port yet), so the served path is currently
NVIDIA-only, which sits awkwardly with the sovereignty motivation and is
first in line for future work.
