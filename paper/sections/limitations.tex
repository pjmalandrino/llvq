\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

We list these in the order a skeptical reader should weigh them.

\textbf{The MMLU deficit is real, reproduced, and unresolved.} $-14.6$
points against FP16, reproduced three times across two machines. The
per-subject profile shows the mechanism: abstract algebra and accounting
fall to 25\% --- exactly chance --- while history, law and psychology hold
above 80\%. Two-bit quantization damages \emph{reasoning} far more than
\emph{recall}, and a perplexity corpus mostly measures recall; perplexity
alone should never be presented as evidence of quality at this rate. The
leading surviving suspect is calibration volume (${\sim}131$k tokens versus
the original paper's 6\,100 sequences, roughly $100\times$ less); the
original paper reports a $-9.5$-point drop under its protocol, so part of
our deficit may be recoverable, but we have not closed the gap and do not
claim we will.

\textbf{One model, one GPU, greedy decoding.} Everything end-to-end is
Qwen3-4B on a single L40S with batch size 1 and greedy decode. Batched
serving changes the arithmetic-intensity regime that makes the kernel
memory-bound; larger models change the embedding share and the tail-column
overhead. We measured one scale point (8B, quality only), not a scaling
law.

\textbf{The scale argument is a bet, not a result.} On the 4B itself, the
honest summary of Table~\ref{tab:campaign} is that 4-bit AWQ dominates on
every axis except disk. The case for the 2-bit point is the model class
that does not otherwise fit --- and that case rests on the single 8B
degradation point and on extrapolation.

\textbf{AWQ speed is not comparable.} The AWQ arm was never executed in our
engine (its quality was, under matching fingerprints). Cross-engine
throughput comparisons are meaningless and we make none.

\textbf{Speedups carry ranges, not third decimals.} Inter-process
dispersion on identical binaries spans several percent; all ratios are
medians of per-round ratios with min--max ranges, and effects smaller than
the range are treated as unresolved.

\textbf{The rotated basis is a real serving constraint.} Weights are stored
in the incoherence-rotated basis; any consumer of the artifact must apply
the activation rotation. Our rotation kernel is verified bit-exact on CUDA
but exists nowhere else (no Metal port yet), so the served path is currently
NVIDIA-only, which sits awkwardly with the sovereignty motivation and is
first in line for future work.
