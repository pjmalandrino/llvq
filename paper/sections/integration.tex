\section{End-to-End Integration, and a Finding About the Reference Engine}
\label{sec:integration}

A kernel benchmark is not a serving number. This section takes the
\textsc{Planes14} kernel into a full autoregressive decode loop (greedy,
batch size 1, single L40S; Qwen3-4B unless another size is named) and
attributes where the time actually goes.

\textbf{Setup.} The serving stack is \texttt{candle}~0.9.2
\citep{candle2023}, a widely used Rust inference engine, with our fused
kernel replacing the 252 linear projections; activations pass through a
dedicated rotation kernel (verified bit-exact on device) before each
projection, since the weights live in the incoherence-rotated basis. The
embedding table (9.7\% of the model, untouched by the 2-bit format) is
stored int8 with per-group scales, a change measured to be free on both
evaluation metrics. Correctness gate: identical greedy tokens across the
two engines until a tie-break-level divergence (token 89), on top of the
row-level f64 verification of \S\ref{sec:decoder}.

\textbf{The finding.} Table~\ref{tab:phases} shows our dense reference arm
spending 26.7~ms per token in the output head, twice the cost of all 36
transformer blocks. The cause is a tensor primitive: the head applies
$h W^{\top}$ through \texttt{Tensor::broadcast\_matmul}, whose rank-2-rhs
path materializes a 778~MB transposed copy of the vocabulary matrix
\emph{on every token} (\texttt{candle}~0.9.2 carries its own
\texttt{TODO:\ Avoid concretising} at the site, \texttt{tensor.rs:1550}).
Replacing the head with an int8 kernel that reads its 413~MB in place
collapses the phase from 25.9 to 0.6~ms. The copy scales with the
vocabulary projection: on the 8B (hidden size 4096, untied head) it is
1.24~GB per token, and the same replacement doubles end-to-end throughput
there (34.1 to 68.2~tok/s).

\textbf{Whose defect this is.} It is a trap in the primitive, not a defect
of \texttt{candle}'s models: \texttt{candle\_nn::\allowbreak Linear::forward} folds the
leading dimensions of its input into the row dimension precisely to avoid
the broadcast path, so models built on \texttt{Linear}, including
\texttt{candle\_transformers}' own, never pay this copy. Our head calls the
primitive directly, so our dense baseline pays it where a stock deployment
would not --- hence the headline below is measured with the \emph{same}
head on both arms. Reported upstream with a reproducer and a
patch.\footnote{\ifanon{\candleissueurl}{Upstream issue URL withheld for double-anonymous review; it names its reporter.}}

\textbf{The double formulation.} End-to-end, the fused engine reaches
87.0~tok/s against the dense engine's 43.5, a factor of $2.00\times$. Most
of that factor is the head fix, which the dense arm could adopt too; we do
not derive the split from the fenced phases (synchronization serializes work
the real decode overlaps, so phase sums attribute time but do not compose
into a throughput). Instead we report the measured lower bound: with the
defective f16 head path held identical in both arms, the fused engine gains
$1.11\times$ on the 4B (48.3 versus 43.5~tok/s), $1.29\times$ on the 8B
(34.1 versus 26.4) and $1.41\times$ on the 14B (23.9 versus 17.0).
\emph{That same-head series is the paper's cleanest statement about the
kernel, and it is monotone in model size} --- the gross series ($2.00$,
$2.57$, $2.55$) is not, and never was a statement about the kernel, since
its denominator carries a defect that grows and then shrinks with the
model's head. Each figure is a median of five timed generations with its
min--max range (the protocol of \S\ref{sec:layouts}, brought to the
end-to-end runner on 2026-08-19); the ratios are quotients of two medians
rather than medians of per-round ratios, because each arm loads its model
exclusively and their rounds never coexist. Read their second decimal as
dispersion.

\textbf{The third size.} Serving Qwen3-14B first required raising the
rotation kernel's shared-memory ceiling by opt-in (\S\ref{sec:limitations},
where that ceiling is a result in its own right). It serves at 43.3~tok/s in
9.39~GB of card memory against 17.0~tok/s in 29.54~GB dense, all 128 greedy
tokens identical across the two arms --- the 4B arms diverge at a tie-break
at token 89, the 8B and the 14B do not diverge at all. The memory ratio is
direct, $3.14\times$ less card memory: two readings of the same card by the
same instrument, with no defective arm in the denominator. It is also a
third route to that model's whole-model b/param, landing $0.38\%$ from the
byte-level \texttt{rtbits} figure of \S\ref{sec:evaluation} against a
$\pm0.5\%$ band fixed before the run. The raw throughput ratio
($2.55\times$) is again not a kernel statement: the 14B unties its heads,
so the dense arm copies a 1.56~GB vocabulary table every token. The
same-head arm is the $1.41\times$ above --- and it is a number the fenced
profile could not have supplied: the two directions of substituting the head
phase into that profile reconstruct $1.78\times$ and $1.24\times$, and the
measurement falls between them, which settles which reconstruction to
believe: neither. Data:
\texttt{docs/\allowbreak mesures/\allowbreak fusedrun-14b-2026-08-17.txt}
and \texttt{docs/\allowbreak mesures/\allowbreak
b2-fusedrun-plages-2026-08-18.txt}.

\textbf{What quantization buys inside its own stack.} We also timed the
official 4-bit checkpoint in the engine it is deployed in --- vLLM 0.26.0,
image pinned by tag and digest, same L40S, the same five prompt token ids,
128 tokens with \texttt{ignore\_eos}, two discarded generations then five
timed --- alongside the same dense model in that engine as its own control.
Quantization buys $2.413\times$ there, $[2.412, 2.414]$, against
$1.11\times$ here. Those are two \emph{within-stack} ratios set side by
side, which is the only licit form: they do not divide, and neither do the
two f16 controls behind them (83.09~tok/s in vLLM against our 43.6), a gap
that mixes engine quality with our own \texttt{broadcast\_matmul} defect
and separates neither. The remaining qualifications do not all point one
way: our denominator carries the defective head on \emph{both} arms, which
pulls $1.11\times$ toward 1 and understates our own kernel, while $M = 1$
is not where a Marlin GEMM is at its best (its smallest M tile is 8), so
$2.413\times$ does not upper-bound 4-bit either. What ran is settled by the
engine's own log: on Ada, vLLM repacks the AWQ checkpoint into Marlin, and a
second arm that explicitly requested the \texttt{awq} kernel resolved to
the same path, character-identical output, $0.10\%$ apart --- so those two
arms bound this bench's own resolution, not the convergence of two kernels.
Data: \texttt{docs/mesures/awq-vllm-4b-2026-08-17.txt}.
