\section{End-to-End Integration, and a Finding About the Reference Engine}
\label{sec:integration}

A kernel benchmark is not a serving number. This section takes the
\textsc{Planes14} kernel into a full autoregressive decode loop (greedy,
Qwen3-4B, single L40S) and attributes where the time actually goes.

\textbf{Setup.} The serving stack is \texttt{candle}~0.9.2
\citep{candle2023}, a widely used Rust inference engine, with our fused
kernel replacing the 252 linear projections. Weights are stored in the
incoherence-rotated basis required by the quantizer, so activations pass
through a dedicated rotation kernel (verified bit-exact on device) before
each projection. The embedding table (9.7\% of the model, untouched by
the 2-bit format) is stored int8 with per-group scales and offsets
(groups of 64), a change measured to be free on both evaluation metrics. Correctness gate: the
fused and dense engines emit identical greedy tokens until a
tie-break-level divergence (token 89), on top of the row-level f64
verification of \S\ref{sec:decoder}.

\textbf{The finding.} Table~\ref{tab:phases} shows our dense reference arm
spending 26.7~ms per token in the output head, twice the cost of all 36
transformer blocks. The cause is a tensor primitive: the head applies
$h W^{\top}$ through \texttt{Tensor::broadcast\_matmul}, whose rank-2-rhs
path materializes a 778~MB transposed copy of the vocabulary matrix
\emph{on every token} (\texttt{candle}~0.9.2 carries its own
\texttt{TODO:\ Avoid concretising} at the site, \texttt{tensor.rs:1550}).
Replacing the head with an int8 kernel that reads its 413~MB in place
collapses the phase from 25.9 to 0.6~ms. The copy scales with the
vocabulary projection: on the 8B (hidden size 4096, untied head) it is
1.24~GB per token, and the same replacement doubles end-to-end throughput
there (34.4 to 69.3~tok/s).

\textbf{Whose defect this is.} It is a trap in the primitive, not a defect
of \texttt{candle}'s models, and the distinction matters because the
defective arm is ours. \texttt{candle\_nn::Linear::forward} deliberately
folds the leading dimensions of its input into the row dimension to avoid
the broadcast path, so models built on \texttt{Linear}, including
\texttt{candle\_transformers}' own, never pay this copy. Our head calls the
primitive directly, so our dense baseline pays it where a stock
\texttt{candle} deployment would not --- hence the number we put forward
below, measured with the \emph{same} head on both arms. We
reported the primitive upstream with a reproducer and a patch that moves the
same fold one level
down.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/huggingface/candle/issues/3871}}

\textbf{The double formulation.} End-to-end, the fused engine reaches
88.4~tok/s against the dense engine's 43.5, a factor of $2.03\times$.
That factor has to be split: most of it is the head fix, which the dense arm
could adopt too and which a stock \texttt{candle} deployment never needed. We do not derive the split from the
fenced phases: synchronization serializes work the real decode overlaps,
so phase sums attribute time but do not compose into a throughput. Instead
we report the measured lower bound: with the defective f16 head path held
identical in both arms, the fused engine gains $1.12\times$ on the 4B
(48.7 versus 43.5~tok/s) and $1.29\times$ on the 8B (34.4 versus 26.6).
These are single-run quotients of end-to-end throughput, not the
round-by-round medians of \S\ref{sec:layouts}, and one of them crosses
invocations: the 8B's 34.4 comes from the f16-embedding job and the 26.6
from the int8 job. Formed inside the single job that timed both arms it is
$34.4/26.5 = 1.30\times$; the 4B ratio is insensitive to the same choice
($48.7/43.6 = 1.12$ within its own job, $48.7/43.5 = 1.12$ against the
tabulated dense arm). We keep 1.29 because 26.6 is the dense figure
Table~\ref{tab:campaign8b} carries, and flag that the second decimal is
inter-run dispersion.
Both numbers understate the kernel, whose own phase runs $1.27\times$
faster (13.3 to 10.4~ms across the transformer blocks) and whose projection
benchmark runs $2.15\times$ over FP16; the giant head phase dilutes them.
Only the $1.12\times$ is a claim about the kernel.\footnote{88.4~tok/s is the final-campaign run;
the integration run of Table~\ref{tab:progression} reports 88.5 on the same
configuration. The difference is inter-run dispersion, not a change of
object.}

\textbf{What quantization buys inside its own stack.} On 2026-08-17 we
timed the official 4-bit checkpoint in the engine it is deployed in --- vLLM
0.26.0, image pinned by tag and digest, same L40S, the same five prompt
token ids passed as ids so no template can enter, 128 tokens with
\texttt{ignore\_eos}, prefix caching read back as disabled, two discarded
generations then five timed --- alongside the same dense model in that same
engine as its own control. Quantization buys $2.413\times$ there,
$[2.412, 2.414]$, against $1.12\times$ here. Those are two
\emph{within-stack} ratios set side by side, which is the only licit form:
they do not divide, and neither do the two f16 controls behind them
(83.09~tok/s in vLLM against our 43.6), a gap that mixes engine quality with
our own \texttt{broadcast\_matmul} defect and separates neither. Three
qualifications, and they do not all point the same way. The two ratios are
not the same kind of number: theirs is a median of five ratios formed round
by round with a min--max range, ours a single-run quotient of two points
with no range at all (\S\ref{sec:limitations}). Our denominator carries the
defective head on \emph{both} arms, so the defect pulls our ratio toward 1
and $1.12\times$ understates our own kernel --- that bias runs against us.
And $M = 1$ is not where a Marlin GEMM is at its best --- its smallest M
tile is 8 --- so $2.413\times$ does not upper-bound 4-bit either, which
runs against them. Neither correction is applied, and neither closes a
comparison that does not exist. What ran is settled by the
engine's own log, \texttt{Using MarlinLinearKernel for
AutoAWQMarlinLinearMethod}: on Ada, vLLM repacks the AWQ checkpoint into
Marlin. A second arm that explicitly requested the \texttt{awq} kernel
resolved to that same path --- same resolved quantization string,
character-identical output, $0.10\%$ apart --- so those two arms bound this
bench's own resolution and not the convergence of two kernels; the
pre-registered clause that at $M = 1$ all 4-bit kernels meet the same
bandwidth bound is untested by this run, in either direction. Data:
\texttt{docs/mesures/awq-vllm-4b-2026-08-17.txt}.
