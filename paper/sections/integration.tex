\section{End-to-End Integration, and a Finding About the Reference Engine}
\label{sec:integration}

\textbf{Setup.} Decode loop (greedy, batch size 1, single L40S; Qwen3-4B
unless another size is named): our fused \textsc{Planes14} kernel replaces
the 252 linear projections of \texttt{candle}~0.9.2 \citep{candle2023}, a widely
used Rust engine, and a rotation kernel (verified
bit-exact on device) precedes each projection, the weights being
incoherence-rotated. The embedding table (9.7\% of the model,
untouched by the 2-bit format) is int8 with per-group scales, measured free
on both evaluation metrics. Correctness gate: identical greedy tokens
across the two engines until a tie-break
divergence (token 89), atop
the row-level f64 verification of \S\ref{sec:decoder}.

\textbf{The finding.} Our dense arm spends 26.7~ms per token in the output
head, twice the cost of all 36 transformer blocks (Table~\ref{tab:phases}).
The cause is a tensor primitive: the head applies $h W^{\top}$ through
\texttt{Tensor::broadcast\_matmul}, whose rank-2-rhs path materializes a
778~MB transposed copy of the vocabulary \emph{on every token}
(\texttt{candle}~0.9.2 carries its own
\texttt{TODO} there\ifanon{, \texttt{tensor.rs:1550}}{}).
An int8 head kernel reading its 413~MB in place collapses the phase from
25.9 to 0.6~ms. The copy scales with the vocabulary projection: 1.24~GB per token
on the 8B (hidden size 4096, untied head), where the same replacement
doubles throughput (34.1 to 68.2~tok/s).

\textbf{Whose defect.} The trap belongs to the primitive:
\texttt{candle\_nn::\allowbreak Linear::forward} folds its input's leading
dimensions into the row dimension to avoid the broadcast path, so models
built on \texttt{Linear}, \texttt{candle\_transformers}' own included,
never pay it. Ours calls it directly, so our dense baseline pays where
a stock deployment would not; hence the headline below uses the \emph{same}
head on both arms. Reported upstream with a reproducer and a
patch.\footnote{\ifanon{\candleissueurl}{Upstream issue URL withheld for double-anonymous review.}}

\textbf{The double formulation.} End-to-end the fused engine reaches
87.0~tok/s against the dense engine's 43.5, $2.00\times$. Most of that is
the head fix, which the dense arm could adopt too; we do not split it out
from the fenced phases (synchronization serializes work the real decode
overlaps). Instead, a measured lower bound: with the defective f16 head
identical in both arms, the fused engine gains $1.11\times$ on the 4B (48.3
versus 43.5~tok/s), $1.29\times$ on the 8B (34.1 versus 26.4) and
$1.41\times$ on the 14B (23.9 versus 17.0). That series is our cleanest kernel
statement, monotone in size; the gross series ($2.00$, $2.57$,
$2.55$) is neither, its denominator carrying a defect that grows then
shrinks with the model's head. Each figure is a median of five timed
generations with its min--max range (protocol of \S\ref{sec:layouts},
brought to the end-to-end runner on 2026-08-19); the ratios are quotients
of two medians: each arm loads its model exclusively, their rounds never
coexist, and no per-round ratio exists. Read their second decimal as
dispersion.

\textbf{The third size.} Qwen3-14B first required raising the rotation
kernel's shared-memory ceiling by opt-in (\S\ref{sec:limitations}). It
serves 43.3~tok/s in 9.39~GB of card memory against 17.0~tok/s in 29.54~GB
dense, all 128 greedy tokens identical; unlike the 4B, the 8B and 14B never
diverge. The memory ratio is direct, $3.14\times$ less: two readings of one
card by one instrument, no defective arm in the denominator. It
is also a third route to its whole-model b/param, $0.38\%$ from the
byte-level \texttt{rtbits} figure of \S\ref{sec:evaluation}, within a
$\pm0.5\%$ band fixed before the run. The raw ratio is again no kernel
statement: the 14B unties its heads, so the dense arm copies a 1.56~GB
vocabulary table per token. The same-head arm above is one the fenced
profile could not supply: the $1.41\times$ above, which its two
reconstructions bracket at $1.78\times$ and $1.24\times$. Data:
\texttt{docs/\allowbreak mesures/\allowbreak fusedrun-14b-2026-08-17.txt}
and \texttt{docs/\allowbreak mesures/\allowbreak
b2-fusedrun-plages-2026-08-18.txt}.

\textbf{What quantization buys in its own stack.} We also timed the
official 4-bit checkpoint in its deployment engine (vLLM 0.26.0, image
pinned by tag and digest, same L40S, same five prompt token ids, 128 tokens
with \texttt{ignore\_eos}, two discarded generations then five timed), with
its dense model as control. Quantization buys $2.413\times$
there, $[2.412, 2.414]$, against $1.11\times$ here: two \emph{within-stack}
ratios side by side, the only valid form of the comparison. They do not
divide, nor do the f16 controls behind them (83.09~tok/s in vLLM against
our 43.6), a gap mixing engine quality with our \texttt{broadcast\_matmul}
defect and separating neither. The qualifications do not all point one way:
our denominator carries the defective head on \emph{both} arms, pulling
$1.11\times$ toward 1 and understating our kernel, while $M = 1$ is an
unfavorable regime for a Marlin GEMM (smallest M tile 8), so $2.413\times$
does not upper-bound 4-bit either. The engine's log settles what ran: on
Ada vLLM repacks the AWQ checkpoint into Marlin, and a second arm
explicitly requesting the \texttt{awq} kernel resolved to the same path,
character-identical output $0.10\%$ apart, so those arms bound this
bench's resolution, not two converging kernels. Data:
\texttt{docs/mesures/awq-vllm-4b-2026-08-17.txt}.
