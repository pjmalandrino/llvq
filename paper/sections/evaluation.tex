\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\textbf{Protocol.} All quality numbers come from one harness on one machine
(NVIDIA L40S), f16 on both sides, token-level
fingerprints required to match across arms:
\texttt{3f1baca9033bf251} for perplexity (WikiText-2
test~\citep{wikitext2016}, context 4096, 12 windows) and
\texttt{65dcd53655e8bfa5} for MMLU (2\,280 questions, 40 per
subject from a fixed seed~\citep{mmlu2021}, micro-averaged as in the original
paper: macro-averaging inflates the quantized arm's
deficit). Its FP16 arm validates the harness: MMLU $70.32 \pm 1.28$
on Qwen3-4B~\citep{qwen3_2025} against the paper's
70.2~\citep{llvq2026}. The quantized weights are the sealed artifact
(47-bit indices $+$ 1 gain bit, from Spherical GPTQ with input
rotation and 131k tokens of C4~\citep{c4corpus} calibration); evaluation scores
files, not reconstructions.

Columns 3 and 4 of Table~\ref{tab:campaign}
serve the same quantized linears: the transcoding is proved a
bijection of decoded content on all 150{,}681{,}600 blocks, every row of
every arm is verified against f64 before any timing (\S\ref{sec:decoder}),
and both engines emit identical greedy tokens to a tie-break. The
table isolates the serving path's one lossy change, the int8
embedding: perplexity and MMLU move within sampling noise. The
$2.00\times$ throughput and $3.09\times$ VRAM reduction thus rest on content
proved bit-identical plus an embedding change measured free.

\textbf{Memory accounting: two denominators, always labelled.} Model-level
comparisons use bits per parameter over the \emph{whole model, embedding
included}: rates over the quantized linears alone overstate compression
badly on small models (the 4B's embedding is 9.7\% of its parameters) and
mix denominators against other methods' figures. The served
footprint is 5.162~b/param
(\texttt{docs/mesures/rtbits-planes-8b-2026-08-09.txt}), below the official
AWQ 4-bit checkpoint's 5.302 in its own engine. \S\ref{sec:layouts}
necessarily uses the other denominator, what the kernel reads per
quantized projection weight, where the same competitor reads \emph{less}
than we do (4.18 against 4.80). The two are never subtracted.

\textbf{What 2 bits costs.} Against FP16 the price is $\times1.384$
perplexity and $-14.6$ MMLU points on the \emph{served} int8 arm,
$-14.73$ on the \emph{sealed} artifact, which carries every paired
statistic here as the only arm whose per-question dumps exist; the two
differ by less than the sampling error of either.
Paired intervals are oriented FP16 \emph{minus} the
quantized arm (positive = loss): $+0.27$, $[-1.63, +2.13]$, containing zero
for the 4-bit arm, against $+14.73$, $[+11.98, +17.47]$ for the 2-bit arm,
which does not --- subject-stratified paired bootstraps over the same
2\,280 questions, with the same run fingerprint on both arms
(\texttt{docs/\allowbreak mesures/\allowbreak
mmlupair-4b-8b-2026-08-13.txt}). On a
4B, 4-bit dominates on every axis but disk and served footprint (a
${\sim}3\%$ margin that does not offset the
quality loss); \S\ref{sec:limitations} discusses where 2 bits can
still win.

\textbf{The scale point: three models.}
Table~\ref{tab:campaign8b} is the second model; a third, Qwen3-14B, ran
under the same 4B configuration. Table~\ref{tab:scale} sets the three side
by side. The direction holds on both axes --- perplexity falls
$\times1.3845 \to \times1.2201 \to \times1.1894$, and the MMLU loss
paired against FP16 is 14.73, then 10.57, then 6.85 points, all
resolved (sealed-artifact arms throughout; the served
int8 arms lose 14.6 and 10.5 at 4B and 8B, and the 14B has no served arm).
The \emph{rate} does not. Read as excess over FP16, the fall is
42.8\% from 4B to 8B and 13.9\% from 8B to 14B, each with a paired 95\%
interval (Table~\ref{tab:scale}). Whether that slowdown is itself
\emph{resolved} has one answer per metric: the bars decline to separate it on
the MMLU gap and separate it decisively on perplexity (below, and
Appendix~\ref{app:scale}). Neither supports an extrapolation; we report the
direction and refuse it.

\textbf{The MMLU gap to 4 bits.} All three gaps in Table~\ref{tab:scale} are
subject-stratified paired bootstraps with 95\% intervals and McNemar
tests, computed question by question over the same 2\,280 questions. The
per-question dumps behind all nine arms are committed, so any can be
recomputed --- or contradicted --- without rerunning a campaign; the 14B dumps passed three authenticity checks first
(stratified micro-averages, run fingerprint, and the published
FP16-minus-2-bit pair replayed to the digit;
\texttt{docs/\allowbreak mesures/\allowbreak mmlupair-14b-2026-08-17.txt}).

\textbf{Does the closing itself slow? Two metrics, two answers.} On
perplexity the steps can be differenced window by window and the slowdown
resolves (Table~\ref{tab:scale}). On the MMLU gap no cross-size pairing exists and
standard errors compose in quadrature: the first step separates from zero
($p = 0.0001$) and the second does not ($p = 0.40$), that step being
uninformative, likeliest for want of power (49\,140 paired tokens against
2\,280 unpaired questions). Under both readings, no scaling law from three
models. Appendix~\ref{app:scale} carries the tests, intervals and
authentication controls, and the reservation on all nine intervals: they
cover evaluation-corpus sampling, not the calibration draw.

\textbf{What one calibration draw is worth, measured.} Three
requantizations of the 4B differing only in the calibration draw (same
corpus, configuration and evaluation fingerprint) give perplexities
of 16.74, 15.88 and 15.10: a range of 10.3\% of the median, every
pairwise difference resolved window by window ($t = 4.5$, $10.9$, $7.7$).
An absolute degradation figure at this
budget (${\sim}131$k tokens, two orders of magnitude below the
source paper's) is the value of one draw. It does \emph{not} touch
comparisons at fixed artifact, nor the three
sizes of Table~\ref{tab:scale}, which share one deterministic window
selection over the same text. It does touch any comparison that
recalibrates, including two of our own calibration-volume bounds, whose
effects ($-1.6\%$ and $-1.2\%$) sit inside this range and so bound
the mean without ever separating from the noise. A plausible
mechanism, untested here: a small calibration set moves the mean little and
carries variance (\texttt{docs/mesures/f5-graines-4b-2026-08-19.txt}).

\textbf{Does the 4-bit baseline start paying?} In the FP16-minus-arm
orientation the 4-bit loss is
$+3.07$ points at 8B, paired interval $[+1.61, +4.69]$, excluding zero; at
4B it is unresolved (above) and at 14B unresolved again
($+0.76$, $[-0.65, +2.17]$). The verdict is specific to the 8B, with no
monotone tendency implied. Two reservations: the 4B verdict depends on the
accounting --- the unweighted control gives $+1.97$, $[+0.92, +3.02]$,
excluding zero, the disagreement carried by \texttt{professional law}
alone (10.9\% of the stratified weight) --- and none of \emph{these}
intervals tests a difference \emph{between}
scales, the bootstrap pairing arms, not sizes, the cross-size test being the
weaker quadrature construction above.

\textbf{Memory, at all three sizes.} Whole-model b/param, embedding
included, is below the official AWQ checkpoint at every size
(Table~\ref{tab:scale}), by 2.6\%, 10.6\% and 5.5\%.
That margin is not monotone and no trend should be read into it: it
tracks the embedding's share (9.7\% at 4B, whose
heads are tied, 15.2\% at 8B, 10.5\% at 14B), a table AWQ leaves in FP16
and we quantize to int8. At 14B the served path reaches the same quantity
independently (\S\ref{sec:integration}): 5.0866~b/param, $0.38\%$ from the
5.106 of Table~\ref{tab:scale}, inside a band fixed before that run --- a
cross-check only, since it divides a rounded byte total. At 8B the choice between 4 and
2 bits is no longer one-sided: 7.5 MMLU points against 11\% of VRAM
and half the disk. Data: the CSVs of Table~\ref{tab:scale}, journals beside
them in \texttt{docs/\allowbreak mesures/}.

\textbf{Against the published 2-bit methods.} At exactly matched code
rate (48 bits per 24-weight block on every 2-bit row), our 4B arm sits
level with the original paper's nearest configurations ($\times1.38$
against $\times1.37$ for QTIP and its 0-gain-bit variant) and behind
its 2-gain-bit line; at 8B we trail both LLVQ variants and beat QTIP alone,
and the 4B MMLU shortfall (55.59 against 60.7, on a baseline our
harness reproduces to 0.12 points) is wider than perplexity suggests.
Table~\ref{tab:lit} in Appendix~\ref{app:scale} carries the comparison and
its qualifications; none of the differences carries error bars, the original
paper publishing none.

Every cell above traces to a dated GPU job
with its billed cost (\texttt{docs/\allowbreak data/\allowbreak jobs.csv}): \$84.68 across 58
jobs, dominated by the model builds (\$27.67 the 14B quantization, \$21.45
the three-seed calibration study, \$12.61 the 8B) against \$2.33 for the
whole kernel campaign of \S\ref{sec:layouts}. Producing the objects costs
an order of magnitude more than measuring them; adding an arm to an
interleaved run costs cents --- hence more measurements than models, and
more negative results than the budget suggests. The figure is a floor: five jobs from the CUDA
porting day are journaled without an amount.
