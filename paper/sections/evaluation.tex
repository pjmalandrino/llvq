\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\textbf{Protocol.} All quality numbers come from one harness on one machine
(NVIDIA L40S), f16 on both sides of every comparison, with token-level
fingerprints printed on each result line and required to match across arms:
perplexity fingerprint \texttt{3f1baca9033bf251} (WikiText-2
test~\citep{wikitext2016}, context 4096, 12 windows), MMLU
fingerprint \texttt{65dcd53655e8bfa5} (2\,280 questions sampled 40 per
subject from a fixed seed~\citep{mmlu2021}, micro-averaged, the original
paper's convention; macro-averaging inflates the quantized arm's deficit
and is not reported here). The harness is validated by its FP16 arm: MMLU
$70.32 \pm 1.28$ on Qwen3-4B~\citep{qwen3_2025} against the 70.2 that the
original paper's Table~6 reports for the same
model~\citep{llvq2026}. The quantized weights are the sealed artifact
(47-bit indices $+$ 1 gain bit, produced by Spherical GPTQ with input
rotation and 131k tokens of C4~\citep{c4corpus} calibration), and the evaluation scores
files, not in-memory reconstructions.

\textbf{What the serving stack does and does not change.} Columns 3 and 4
serve the same quantized linears. The layout transcoding is proved a
bijection of the decoded content on all 150{,}681{,}600 blocks of the
artifact, every row of every arm is verified against an f64 reference
before any timing (\S\ref{sec:decoder}), and the fused and dense engines
emit identical greedy tokens up to a tie-break-level divergence. The
quality scores themselves run through the dense evaluation path; what the
table shows directly is the cost of the int8 embedding, the one lossy
change on the serving path: 16.9358 versus 16.9422 perplexity and $55.70$
versus $55.59$ MMLU, both within sampling noise. The $2.00\times$
throughput and $3.09\times$ VRAM reduction ride on content proved
bit-identical plus an embedding change measured to be free.

\textbf{Memory accounting: two denominators, always labelled.} Model-level
comparisons --- this section, the abstract's serving figures --- are in bits
per parameter over the \emph{whole model, embedding included}: per-weight
rates over the quantized linears alone overstate compression badly on small
models (the 4B's embedding is 9.7\% of its parameters) and silently mix
denominators against other methods' figures. On this accounting the served
footprint is 5.162~b/param
(\texttt{docs/mesures/rtbits-planes-8b-2026-08-09.txt}), below the official
AWQ 4-bit checkpoint's 5.302 in its own engine. \S\ref{sec:layouts}
necessarily uses the other denominator --- what the kernel reads per
quantized projection weight, the quantity a bandwidth argument is about ---
and there the same competitor reads \emph{less} than we do (4.18 against
4.80). Both are true of different questions: what fits on the card, and what
the matvec streams. They are never subtracted from one another.

\textbf{What 2 bits costs.} Against FP16 the price is $\times1.384$
perplexity and $-14.6$ points of MMLU on the \emph{served} int8 arm; on the
\emph{sealed} artifact it is $-14.73$, and the sealed arm is the one every
paired statistic in this paper uses, since it is the one whose per-question
dumps exist. The two differ by less than the sampling error of either. The
AWQ arm makes the comparison sharp: at 4 bits on this model, quality is
essentially free --- $\times1.105$ perplexity and 0.27 MMLU points below
FP16. Every paired interval in this paper is oriented FP16 \emph{minus} the
quantized arm, so a positive number is a loss for that arm: $+0.27$ with a
95\% interval $[-1.63, +2.13]$, which contains zero, against $+14.73$ with
$[+11.98, +17.47]$ for the 2-bit arm, which does not. Both intervals are
subject-stratified paired bootstraps over the same 2\,280 questions
(\texttt{docs/mesures/mmlupair-4b-8b-2026-08-13.txt}). On a 4B, 4-bit
dominates 2-bit on every axis except disk and served footprint (5.162
against 5.302 b/param above --- a ${\sim}3\%$ margin that does not offset the
quality loss); \S\ref{sec:limitations} discusses where the 2-bit point can
still win.

\textbf{The scale point: three models, and what the bars separate.}
Table~\ref{tab:campaign8b} is the paper's second model, requantized under the
exact 4B configuration; a third, Qwen3-14B, was measured under the same one.
Table~\ref{tab:scale} puts the three side by side.

The direction holds on both axes. Perplexity: $\times1.3845 \to
\times1.2201 \to \times1.1894$. MMLU, as a paired loss against FP16: 14.73,
then 10.57, then 6.85 points --- all three paired and all three resolved
(sealed-artifact arms at every size; the served int8 arms lose 14.6 and 10.5
at 4B and 8B, and the 14B has no served arm). On the point estimates the
\emph{rate} does not hold. Read as excess over FP16 --- the quantity that must
go to zero for the 2-bit thesis to close --- the fall is 42.8\% from 4B to 8B
and 13.9\% from 8B to 14B, each with a paired 95\% interval,
$[-51.8, -33.5]$ and $[-22.8, -4.9]$. Whether that slowdown is itself
\emph{resolved} has two answers, one per metric --- the bars decline to
separate it on the MMLU gap and separate it decisively on perplexity (below,
and Appendix~\ref{app:scale}) --- and neither buys an extrapolation. We
report the direction and refuse it.

\textbf{The MMLU gap to 4 bits.} The sequence is 14.45, 7.49, 6.09 points,
and all three are subject-stratified paired bootstrap estimates with 95\%
intervals and McNemar tests, computed question by question over the same
2\,280 questions with the same run fingerprint on both arms: $[11.60,
17.27]$, $[5.28, 9.70]$ and $[3.62, 8.52]$. The per-question dumps behind
all nine arms are committed in \texttt{docs/data/mmlu-dumps/}, so any of
these can be recomputed --- or contradicted --- without rerunning a
campaign; the 14B dumps passed three authenticity checks before use
(stratified micro-averages, run fingerprint, and the already-published
FP16-minus-2-bit pair replayed to the digit;
\texttt{docs/\allowbreak mesures/\allowbreak mmlupair-14b-2026-08-17.txt}).

\textbf{Does the closing itself slow? Two metrics, two answers.} On
perplexity, where the 12 evaluation windows are the same text at every size
and the steps can be differenced window by window, the slowdown resolves:
the two steps differ by $-0.1010$ in log perplexity ratio,
$[-0.1377, -0.0643]$, $t = -6.06$. On the MMLU gap, where no cross-size
pairing exists and standard errors compose in quadrature, the first step
separates from zero ($p = 0.0001$) and the second does not ($p = 0.40$) ---
silence, not evidence of constancy, the likeliest reason being power
(49\,140 paired tokens against 2\,280 unpaired questions). \emph{A claim
about this curvature that does not name its metric is half wrong either
way}; under both readings, no scaling law from three models.
Appendix~\ref{app:scale} carries the tests, the intervals, the controls
that authenticate the raw output behind them, and the reservation bounding
all nine intervals: they cover evaluation-corpus sampling, not the
calibration draw.

\textbf{What one calibration draw is worth, measured.} That second component
is no longer a caveat without a number. Three requantizations of the 4B
differing only in the calibration draw --- same corpus, same configuration,
same evaluation fingerprint --- give perplexities of 16.74, 15.88 and 15.10:
a range of 10.3\% of the median, with every pairwise difference resolved
window by window ($t = 4.5$, $10.9$, $7.7$). \emph{The choice of calibration
windows is worth more than most of the effects this literature reports}, and
an absolute degradation figure at this calibration budget (${\sim}131$k
tokens, two orders of magnitude below the source paper's) is the value of one
draw. What it does \emph{not} touch: comparisons at fixed artifact, which
recalibrate nothing, and the three sizes of Table~\ref{tab:scale}, which
share one deterministic window selection over the same text. What it does
touch is any comparison that recalibrates --- including two of our own bounds
on calibration volume, whose effects ($-1.6\%$ and $-1.2\%$) sit inside this
range, so that they bound the mean without ever having separated from the
noise. A plausible mechanism, not tested here: a small calibration set moves
the mean little and carries variance
(\texttt{docs/mesures/f5-graines-4b-2026-08-19.txt}).

\textbf{Does the 4-bit baseline start paying?} At 8B, yes, and it is the
only size where the answer is clean. In the FP16-minus-arm orientation used
throughout, the 4-bit loss is $+3.07$ points at 8B with paired interval
$[+1.61, +4.69]$, which excludes zero. At 4B the same comparison is
unresolved ($+0.27$, $[-1.63, +2.13]$), and at 14B it is unresolved again
($+0.76$, $[-0.65, +2.17]$). So the sentence describes the 8B, not a
monotone tendency: the sequence resolved/unresolved is not ordered by scale.
Two further reservations. First, the 4B verdict depends on the accounting:
the unweighted control over the same questions gives $+1.97$,
$[+0.92, +3.02]$, which \emph{does} exclude zero, and the disagreement is
carried by \texttt{professional law}, one subject with 10.9\% of the
stratified weight. Second, none of \emph{these} intervals tests a difference \emph{between}
scales: the bootstrap pairs arms, not sizes, and the cross-size test is the
weaker quadrature construction above, which resolves one step and not the
other.

\textbf{Memory, at all three sizes.} Whole-model b/param, embedding
included, is 5.162, 5.322 and 5.106 against the official AWQ checkpoints'
5.302, 5.956 and 5.404: below at every size, by 2.6\%, 10.6\% and 5.5\%.
\emph{That margin is not monotone and no trend should be read into it}: it
tracks the share of the model the embedding occupies (9.7\% at 4B, whose
heads are tied, 15.2\% at 8B, 10.5\% at 14B), a table AWQ leaves in FP16
and we quantize to int8, so the 8B's peak embedding share makes it the peak
margin. Three points, one mechanism, no law. At 14B the served path gives an
independent route to the same quantity (\S\ref{sec:integration}), the card
figure's 5.0866~b/param landing $0.38\%$ from the 5.106 above, inside a
band fixed before that run --- a cross-check, not a replacement, since it
divides a rounded byte total. At 8B the choice between 4 and 2 bits is no
longer one-sided; it is 7.5 MMLU points against 11\% of VRAM and half the
disk. Data: \texttt{docs/data/echelle-4b-8b.csv},
\texttt{docs/\allowbreak data/\allowbreak mmlu-appariee.csv}; journals
\texttt{docs/\allowbreak mesures/\allowbreak mmlupair-4b-8b-2026-08-13.txt},
\texttt{mmlupair-14b-2026-08-17.txt} and
\texttt{rtbits-14b-2026-08-17.txt}.

\textbf{Against the published 2-bit methods.} At exactly matched code
rate --- 48 bits per 24-weight block on every 2-bit row --- our 4B arm sits
level with the original paper's nearest configurations ($\times1.38$
against $\times1.37$ for QTIP and for its 0-gain-bit variant) and behind
its 2-gain-bit line; at 8B we are behind both LLVQ variants and improve on
QTIP alone, and the 4B MMLU shortfall (55.59 against 60.7, on a baseline our
harness reproduces to 0.12 points) is wider than perplexity suggests.
Table~\ref{tab:lit} in Appendix~\ref{app:scale} carries the comparison and
its qualifications; none of the differences carries error bars, the original
paper publishing none.

\textbf{Cost of evidence.} Every cell above traces to a dated GPU job
with its billed cost (\texttt{docs/data/jobs.csv}): \$84.68 across 58
jobs, dominated by the model builds (\$27.67 the 14B quantization, \$21.45
the three-seed calibration study, \$12.61 the 8B) against \$2.33 for the
whole kernel campaign of \S\ref{sec:layouts}. \emph{Producing the objects
costs an order of magnitude more than measuring them}, which is why this
paper has more measurements than models --- and more negative results than a
rented-GPU budget would suggest, since adding an arm to an existing
interleaved run costs cents. The figure is a floor: five jobs from the CUDA
porting day are journaled without an amount.
