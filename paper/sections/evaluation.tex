\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\textbf{Protocol.} All quality numbers come from one harness on one machine
(NVIDIA L40S), f16 on both sides of every comparison, with token-level
fingerprints printed on each result line and required to match across arms:
perplexity fingerprint \texttt{3f1baca9033bf251} (WikiText-2 test, context
4096, 12 windows), MMLU fingerprint \texttt{65dcd53655e8bfa5} (2\,280
questions, micro-averaged --- the paper convention; macro-averaging inflates
the quantized arm's deficit and is reported nowhere here). The harness is
validated by its FP16 arm: MMLU $70.32 \pm 1.28$ against the original
paper's 70.2 for the same model. The quantized artifact is the sealed
1.77~GB file --- 47-bit indices $+$ 1 gain bit, produced by Spherical GPTQ
with input rotation and 131k tokens of C4 calibration --- and the evaluation
scores the file's bytes, not an in-memory reconstruction.

\textbf{The kernel and format are quality-free.} Columns 3 and 4 of
Table~\ref{tab:campaign} serve the \emph{same file}: perplexity 16.9358 vs
16.9422 and MMLU $55.70$ vs $55.59$ (same questions, same windows, same
fingerprints) are within noise. The $2.03\times$ throughput and
$3.09\times$ VRAM reduction cost nothing in quality --- they are pure
systems work.

\textbf{Memory accounting, stated once and used everywhere.} Comparisons
are in bits per parameter over the \emph{whole model, embedding included}.
Per-weight rates over the quantized linears alone overstate compression
badly on small models (the 4B's embedding is 9.7\% of its parameters), and
they silently mix denominators when set against other methods' figures. On
this accounting the served footprint is 5.15~b/param --- below the official
AWQ 4-bit checkpoint's 5.30 in its own engine.

\textbf{What 2 bits costs.} Against FP16 the price is $\times1.384$
perplexity and $-14.6$ points of MMLU. The AWQ arm makes the comparison
sharp: at 4 bits, quality is essentially free ($\times1.105$,
$-0.3$~points). On a 4B model, 4-bit dominates 2-bit on every axis except
disk; we say so plainly, and \S\ref{sec:limitations} discusses where the
2-bit point can still win.

\textbf{One scale data point.} The same protocol on Qwen3-8B yields
$\times1.267$ perplexity degradation at 2.0436 b/weight --- less degradation
than the 4B at fewer bits. One point is not a law, but it moves in the
direction the 2-bit thesis needs: the target regime is the 70B-class model
that 4 bits cannot fit into a given memory class at all.

\textbf{Cost of evidence.} Every cell above traces to a dated GPU job with
its billed cost (\texttt{docs/data/jobs.csv}); the entire measurement
campaign --- integration, ablations, negative results included --- billed
\$5.04.
