\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\textbf{Protocol.} All quality numbers come from one harness on one machine
(NVIDIA L40S), f16 on both sides of every comparison, with token-level
fingerprints printed on each result line and required to match across arms:
perplexity fingerprint \texttt{3f1baca9033bf251} (WikiText-2
test~\citep{wikitext2016}, context 4096, 12 windows), MMLU
fingerprint \texttt{65dcd53655e8bfa5} (2\,280 questions sampled 40 per
subject from a fixed seed~\citep{mmlu2021}, micro-averaged, the original
paper's convention; macro-averaging inflates the quantized arm's deficit
and is not reported here). The harness is validated by its FP16 arm: MMLU
$70.32 \pm 1.28$ on Qwen3-4B~\citep{qwen3_2025} against the 70.2 that the
original paper's Table~6 reports for the same
model~\citep{llvq2026}. The quantized weights are the sealed artifact
(47-bit indices $+$ 1 gain bit, produced by Spherical GPTQ with input
rotation and 131k tokens of C4~\citep{c4corpus} calibration), and the evaluation scores
files, not in-memory reconstructions.

\textbf{What the serving stack does and does not change.} Columns 3 and 4
serve the same quantized linears. The layout transcoding is proved a
bijection of the decoded content on all 150{,}681{,}600 blocks of the
artifact, every row of every arm is verified against an f64 reference
before any timing (\S\ref{sec:decoder}), and the fused and dense engines
emit identical greedy tokens up to a tie-break-level divergence. The
quality scores themselves run through the dense evaluation path; what the
table shows directly is the cost of the int8 embedding, the one lossy
change on the serving path: 16.9358 versus 16.9422 perplexity and $55.70$
versus $55.59$ MMLU, both within sampling noise. The $2.03\times$
throughput and $3.09\times$ VRAM reduction ride on content proved
bit-identical plus an embedding change measured to be free.

\textbf{Memory accounting: two denominators, always labelled.} Model-level
comparisons --- this section, the abstract's serving figures, the limitations
--- are in bits per parameter over the \emph{whole model, embedding
included}. Per-weight rates over the quantized linears alone overstate
compression badly on small models (the 4B's embedding is 9.7\% of its
parameters) and silently mix denominators against other methods' figures. On
this accounting the served footprint is 5.162~b/param, recomputed from the
exact bytes and the exact parameter count
(\texttt{docs/mesures/rtbits-planes-8b-2026-08-09.txt}), and that is what the
tables carry. The 5.15 this paper published earlier is the same object read
off the card's rounded display (2.60~GB shown for an exact 2.595~GB) and is
kept here only so the correction is visible. Either way it is below the official AWQ
4-bit checkpoint's 5.30 (5.302) in its own engine. \S\ref{sec:layouts} necessarily
uses the other denominator --- what the kernel reads per quantized projection
weight, the quantity a bandwidth argument is about --- and there the same
competitor reads \emph{less} than we do (4.18 against 4.80). Both are true of
different questions: what fits on the card, and what the matvec streams. They
are never subtracted from one another.

\textbf{What 2 bits costs.} Against FP16 the price is $\times1.384$
perplexity and $-14.6$ points of MMLU on the \emph{served} int8 arm; on the
\emph{sealed} artifact it is $-14.73$, and the sealed arm is the one every
paired statistic in this paper uses, since it is the one whose per-question
dumps exist. The two differ by less than the sampling error of either. The
AWQ arm makes the comparison sharp: at 4 bits on this model, quality is
essentially free --- $\times1.105$ perplexity and 0.27 MMLU points below
FP16. Every paired interval in this paper is oriented FP16 \emph{minus} the
quantized arm, so a positive number is a loss for that arm: $+0.27$ with a
95\% interval $[-1.63, +2.13]$, which contains zero, against $+14.73$ with
$[+11.98, +17.47]$ for the 2-bit arm, which does not. Both intervals are
subject-stratified paired bootstraps over the same 2\,280 questions
(\texttt{docs/mesures/mmlupair-4b-8b-2026-08-13.txt}). On a 4B, 4-bit
dominates 2-bit on every axis except disk and served footprint (5.162
against 5.302 b/param above --- a ${\sim}3\%$ margin that does not offset the
quality loss); \S\ref{sec:limitations} discusses where the 2-bit point can
still win.

\textbf{The scale point: three models, and what the bars separate.}
Table~\ref{tab:campaign8b} is the paper's second model, requantized under the
exact 4B configuration; a third, Qwen3-14B, was measured under the same one.
Table~\ref{tab:scale} puts the three side by side.

The direction holds on both axes. Perplexity: $\times1.3845 \to
\times1.2201 \to \times1.1894$. MMLU, as a paired loss against FP16: 14.73,
then 10.57, then 6.85 points --- all three paired and all three resolved
(sealed-artifact arms at every size; the served int8 arms lose 14.6 and 10.5
at 4B and 8B, and the 14B has no served arm). On the point estimates the
\emph{rate} does not hold. Read as excess over FP16 --- the quantity that must
go to zero for the 2-bit thesis to close --- the fall is 42.8\% from 4B to 8B
and 13.9\% from 8B to 14B, each now with a paired 95\% interval,
$[-51.8, -33.5]$ and $[-22.8, -4.9]$. A draft of this paper, with two points
in hand, wrote that the gap \emph{halves}; the third point's estimate says it
does not. Whether that slowdown is itself \emph{resolved} is a further
question, and it has two answers, one per metric: on the MMLU gap to 4 bits
the bars decline to separate it, on perplexity they separate it decisively.
The paragraphs after next give both, in that order, and neither buys an
extrapolation. We report the direction and refuse it.

\textbf{The MMLU gap to 4 bits.} The sequence is 14.45, 7.49, 6.09 points,
and all three are subject-stratified paired bootstrap estimates with 95\%
intervals and McNemar tests, computed question by question over the same
2\,280 questions with the same run fingerprint on both arms: $[11.60,
17.27]$, $[5.28, 9.70]$ and $[3.62, 8.52]$. The per-question dumps behind
all nine arms are committed in \texttt{docs/data/mmlu-dumps/}, so any of
these can be recomputed --- or contradicted --- without rerunning a
campaign. Three authenticity checks ran before the 14B dumps were used for
anything: the three stratified micro-averages replay 78.97 / 78.21 / 72.12,
the run fingerprint matches the 4B and 8B campaigns, and the
already-published FP16-minus-2-bit pair replays its point estimate, interval,
standard error and McNemar $p$ to the digit
(\texttt{docs/\allowbreak mesures/\allowbreak mmlupair-14b-2026-08-17.txt}).

\textbf{Does the closing itself slow? Two metrics, two answers, both
reported.} With the three MMLU gaps and the nine perplexity intervals all
paired, the question the word \emph{knee} asks can be tested --- and the two
metrics do not answer alike. On perplexity, where the evaluation windows are
the same text at every size and the steps can be differenced window by
window, the slowdown resolves: the two steps differ by $-0.1010$ in log
perplexity ratio, $[-0.1377, -0.0643]$, $t = -6.06$. On the MMLU gap, where
no pairing across sizes exists and standard errors compose in quadrature,
only the first step separates from zero ($-6.96$ points, $p = 0.0001$)
against $-1.40$ and $p = 0.40$ for the second. The likeliest reason is power
--- perplexity averages 49\,140 paired tokens where MMLU rates 2\,280
unpaired questions --- and $p = 0.40$ is silence about that step rather than
evidence of constancy. \emph{A claim about this curvature that does not name
its metric is half wrong either way.} What follows operationally is the same
under both readings: no scaling law from three models. Appendix~\ref{app:scale}
carries the tests, the intervals, the controls that authenticate the raw
output behind them, and the reservation that bounds all nine intervals --- they cover
the sampling of the evaluation corpus and not the calibration draw, which is
one seed per artifact.

\textbf{Does the 4-bit baseline start paying?} At 8B, yes, and it is the
only size where the answer is clean. In the FP16-minus-arm orientation used
throughout, the 4-bit loss is $+3.07$ points at 8B with paired interval
$[+1.61, +4.69]$, which excludes zero. At 4B the same comparison is
unresolved ($+0.27$, $[-1.63, +2.13]$), and at 14B it is unresolved again
($+0.76$, $[-0.65, +2.17]$). So the sentence describes the 8B, not a
monotone tendency: the sequence resolved/unresolved is not ordered by scale.
Two further reservations. First, the 4B verdict depends on the accounting:
the unweighted control over the same questions gives $+1.97$,
$[+0.92, +3.02]$, which \emph{does} exclude zero, and the disagreement is
carried by \texttt{professional law}, one subject with 10.9\% of the
stratified weight. Second, none of \emph{these} intervals tests a difference
\emph{between} scales. The paired bootstrap pairs two arms on the same
questions; it does not pair two model sizes, and two intervals failing to
overlap is not a test of the difference of differences. Such a test exists
now, but it is a separate and weaker construction --- the quadrature
composition two paragraphs above, which assumes only that the campaigns are
independent --- and it resolves one of the two steps and not the other.

\textbf{Memory, now at all three sizes.} The 8B verdict stands --- 5.45~GB
served against 6.10, a file $1.9\times$ smaller --- and the reservation that
accompanied it here is lifted. This section said the memory half of the
argument was a two-point reading, because no \texttt{rtbits} pass had been run
on a sealed 14B; that pass ran on 2026-08-17, on the sealed artifact recovered
from the same mounted bucket as the dumps, for no GPU time. Whole-model
b/param, embedding included, is 5.162, 5.322 and 5.106 against the official
AWQ checkpoints' 5.302, 5.956 and 5.404: below at every size, by 2.6\%,
10.6\% and 5.5\%. \emph{That margin is not monotone and no trend should be
read into it.} It peaks at the 8B, and the mechanism has nothing to do with
the method --- it is the share of the model the embedding occupies (9.7\% at
4B, whose heads are tied, 15.2\% at 8B, 10.5\% at 14B), a table AWQ leaves in
FP16 and we quantize to int8, so our advantage grows with the share carried.
The 8B is a local maximum of embedding share and therefore a local maximum of
margin. Three points, one mechanism, no law. At 14B the served path gives an
independent route to the same quantity (\S\ref{sec:integration}): the card
figure gives 5.0866~b/param against the
5.106 above, $0.38\%$ apart and inside a band fixed before that run --- a
third and independent route to the same quantity, not a replacement for it,
since it divides a card display rounded to the hundredth of a gigabyte, which
is the same route that once printed the 4B's 5.162 as 5.15.
At 8B the choice between 4 and 2 bits is no longer
one-sided; it is 7.5 MMLU points against 11\% of VRAM and half the disk.

\textbf{Against the published 2-bit methods.} Tables~\ref{tab:campaign}
and~\ref{tab:campaign8b} compare against FP16 and against a deployed 4-bit
checkpoint. Table~\ref{tab:lit} adds the comparison a reader of the original
paper will make anyway. Rate is matched exactly rather than approximately:
every 2-bit row spends 48 bits per 24-weight block, and the configurations
differ only in how those bits split between direction index and gain
($48+0$, $47+1$ and $46+2$ in the original paper's Table~8). Ours is that
table's $47+1$ line, the one its own distortion study ranks first, while its
LLM results use the $48+0$ and $46+2$ variants. What our sealed file adds on
top of that payload --- full-precision tail columns and per-row scales,
about 8\% on the 4B and less on the 8B, whose \texttt{down\_proj} is exactly
block-aligned and carries no tail --- sits outside this comparison: the
original paper never states its own tail policy, so the two artifacts cannot
be set against each other at that level. The code rate can be, and it is
equal.

Absolute perplexities do not cross harnesses, so the ratio to each harness's
own FP16 baseline is the comparable quantity. The two baselines differ by
1.4\% on the 4B (12.41 against our 12.2369) and agree on the 8B to the two
decimals the original paper prints. The verdict is split. At 4B we sit level with the configurations nearest
ours: $\times1.38$ against $\times1.37$ for the 0-gain-bit variant and for
QTIP, behind the 2-gain-bit variant at $\times1.25$. At 8B we are behind both
LLVQ variants ($\times1.22$ against $\times1.13$, 0.78 perplexity points on a
shared baseline) and improve on QTIP alone. On MMLU, where only the 4B has
published points, the shortfall is wider: 55.59 against 60.7, on a baseline
our harness reproduces to 0.12 points.

We claim no statistical significance for these differences: the original
paper publishes no error bars, our $\pm$ is single-arm sampling error rather
than a paired test, and across papers the question samples differ. What the
matched payload does establish is that the 8B shortfall is not bought back by
a rate advantage, and that the excess our file carries on disk is smallest
exactly where the shortfall is largest. We do not explain it. The natural
suspect, roughly $100\times$ less calibration data, is bounded in
\S\ref{sec:limitations} at an effect too small to be the whole story --- and
that bound is measured on perplexity, which does not constrain MMLU.
