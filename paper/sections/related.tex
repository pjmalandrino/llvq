\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Extreme low-bit weight quantization.} GPTQ~\citep{gptq2023}
established the second-order, error-propagating framework most post-training
methods refine. At 2 bits, vector and trellis codes replaced scalar grids:
QuIP~\citep{quip2023} introduced incoherence processing,
QuIP\#~\citep{quipsharp2024} added E8-lattice codebooks, and
QTIP~\citep{qtip2024} reached the previous state of the art with trellis
quantization and hardware-friendly decoders. LLVQ~\citep{llvq2026} improved on
QTIP's quality with a spherical GPTQ variant on the Leech lattice; we
implement its quantizer independently in Rust and supply the systems half it
leaves open: its kernel decodes one shell, is reported slower than QTIP's, and
no multi-shell decoder existed before. Rather than inherit that report we test
it, porting QTIP's kernel into our harness (\S\ref{sec:qtip}); it holds,
at $2.27\times$. The lattice, its Golay-code construction and its optimality
are classical~\citep{conway1999sphere,cohn2017}; Leech-based nearest-neighbor
search dates to \citet{adoul1988}.

A second family keeps a scalar grid, spending its bits on what it cannot
hold: SpQR~\citep{spqr2023} and SqueezeLLM~\citep{squeezellm2024} isolate
outlier weights into a sparse side-channel --- our sparse overlay's move
(\S\ref{sec:layouts}), paid in the same coin: a second irregular memory
stream. AQLM~\citep{aqlm2024} (additive quantization) and
VPTQ~\citep{vptq2024} run at our rate with released kernels: the baselines a
systems paper at 2 bits should eventually run. GPTVQ~\citep{gptvq2024}, from
the group behind LLVQ, is the ancestor of that line. We measure none of them (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\textbf{Rotations, and why they bound our serving path.} Incoherence
processing is a rotation; the line making it practical at 4 bits
(QuaRot~\citep{quarot2024}, then SpinQuant~\citep{spinquant2024} with
learned rotations) meets the constraint ending \S\ref{sec:limitations}: a
Hadamard transform is a barrier ladder, a barrier synchronizes one block, so
the whole activation must fit in one block's shared memory. Ours meets that
wall at a measured 101\,376 bytes, one model class below the width the
sovereignty argument wants. Those systems place the transform differently and
would meet it elsewhere; none removes it.

\textbf{Kernels for quantized inference.} Below 8 bits the \emph{decode}, not the
multiplication, is the problem --- the premise of \S\ref{sec:layouts}. LUT-GEMM~\citep{lutgemm2024} and FLUTE~\citep{flute2024}
answer with lookup tables sized for fast memory, as our class table does; but
a Leech class is a permutation pattern where theirs hold values, so ours is
read once per block, not per weight. AWQ~\citep{awq2024} is the deployed
4-bit reference we port unchanged into our harness;
Marlin~\citep{marlin2024} shows what a mature mixed-precision kernel achieves
at 4 bits, including in the batched regimes we do not address.
ExLlamaV2~\citep{exllamav2} ships comparable formats without a publication; we cite it as software. We ask instead how far below 4 bits the
\emph{in-VRAM} rate can go before decode cost overtakes bandwidth savings,
and find the knee near 4.3~b/weight for lattice codes on this hardware.

\textbf{Performance models.} The quantity organizing \S\ref{sec:layouts} ---
the fraction of its byte bound each layout converts into time --- is a
roofline argument~\citep{roofline2009} with two adaptations. First, the
ceiling is measured by a kernel that reads no weights, not taken from a
datasheet. Second, the same layouts sit on opposite sides of the ridge point
on two cards: on an L40S every decoding arm is bandwidth-bound and the byte
savings convert; on an A100, whose HBM raises the memory ceiling but not
per-SM decode throughput, they turn compute-bound and fall below the FP16
baseline. One machine's roofline does not describe the other.

\textbf{Serving engines.} Our integration builds on
\texttt{candle}~\citep{candle2023}; the 4-bit throughput reference of
\S\ref{sec:integration} was measured in vLLM~\citep{vllm2023}, a different engine,
so we use it only within its own stack. The output-head
materialization of \S\ref{sec:integration} is a property of the
\texttt{broadcast\_matmul} primitive, which we found unreported and have
since reported
upstream;\footnote{\ifanon{\candleissueurl}{Upstream issue URL withheld for double-anonymous review.}}
not of \texttt{candle}'s served models, which reach their heads through
\texttt{Linear} and avoid the path.
