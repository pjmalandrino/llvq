\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Extreme low-bit weight quantization.} GPTQ~\citep{gptq2023}
established the second-order, error-propagating framework that most
post-training methods refine. At 2 bits, vector and trellis codes replaced
scalar grids: QuIP~\citep{quip2023} introduced incoherence processing,
QuIP\#~\citep{quipsharp2024} added E8-lattice codebooks, and
QTIP~\citep{qtip2024} reached the previous state of the art with trellis
quantization and hardware-friendly decoders. LLVQ~\citep{llvq2026} improved
on QTIP's quality by quantizing on the Leech lattice with a spherical
variant of GPTQ; our work implements its quantizer independently in Rust
and supplies the systems half the paper leaves open --- its kernel decodes
one shell, is slower than QTIP's, and no multi-shell decoder existed prior
to this work. The lattice itself, its Golay-code construction and its
optimality properties are classical~\citep{conway1999sphere,cohn2017}; the
nearest-neighbor literature for Leech-based spherical codes goes back to
\citet{adoul1988}.

\textbf{4-bit systems.} AWQ~\citep{awq2024} is the deployed 4-bit
reference we compare against; Marlin~\citep{marlin2024} demonstrates what a
mature mixed-precision kernel achieves at 4 bits, including in batched
regimes we do not address. Our layout study is complementary: it asks how
far below 4 bits the \emph{in-VRAM} rate can be pushed before decode cost
overtakes bandwidth savings, and finds the knee near 4.3~b/weight for
lattice codes.

\textbf{Serving engines.} Our integration is built on
\texttt{candle}~\citep{candle2023}. The output-head materialization of
\S\ref{sec:integration} is, to our knowledge, unreported; similar
transpose-materialization taxes have been folklore in other engines, which
is precisely why per-phase attribution with synchronization barriers ---
rather than end-to-end numbers alone --- belongs in systems evaluations.
