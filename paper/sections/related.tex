\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Extreme low-bit weight quantization.} GPTQ~\citep{gptq2023}
established the second-order, error-propagating framework that most
post-training methods refine. At 2 bits, vector and trellis codes replaced
scalar grids: QuIP~\citep{quip2023} introduced incoherence processing,
QuIP\#~\citep{quipsharp2024} added E8-lattice codebooks, and
QTIP~\citep{qtip2024} reached the previous state of the art with trellis
quantization and hardware-friendly decoders. LLVQ~\citep{llvq2026} improved
on QTIP's quality by quantizing on the Leech lattice with a spherical variant
of GPTQ; our work implements its quantizer independently in Rust and supplies
the systems half the paper leaves open: its kernel decodes one shell, is
reported slower than QTIP's, and no multi-shell decoder existed prior to this
work. The lattice itself, its Golay-code construction and its optimality
properties are classical~\citep{conway1999sphere,cohn2017}; the
nearest-neighbor literature for Leech-based spherical codes goes back to
\citet{adoul1988}.

A second family keeps a scalar grid and spends its bits on what the grid
cannot hold. SpQR~\citep{spqr2023} and SqueezeLLM~\citep{squeezellm2024}
isolate outlier weights into a sparse side-channel, the same
structural move as the sparse overlay of \S\ref{sec:layouts}, paid for in
the same coin: a second, irregular memory stream. Two further
vector-quantized systems operate at our rate with released kernels and are
the baselines a systems paper at 2 bits should eventually run:
AQLM~\citep{aqlm2024}, which uses additive quantization, and
VPTQ~\citep{vptq2024}. GPTVQ~\citep{gptvq2024}, from the group that later
produced LLVQ, is the direct ancestor of its vector-quantization line. We
measure none of them (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\textbf{Rotations, and why they bound our serving path.} Incoherence
processing is a rotation, and the line that made it practical at 4 bits
(QuaRot~\citep{quarot2024}, then SpinQuant~\citep{spinquant2024} with
learned rotations) shares the constraint that ends \S\ref{sec:limitations}: a
Hadamard transform is a barrier ladder, a barrier synchronizes one block, so
the whole activation must fit in one block's shared memory. Our rotation
kernel meets that wall at a measured 101\,376 bytes, one model class below
the width the sovereignty argument wants. Those systems place the transform
differently and would meet it at a different width; none of them removes it.

\textbf{Kernels for quantized inference.} Below 8 bits the multiplication
stops being the problem and the \emph{decode} becomes it, which is the
premise of the layout study in \S\ref{sec:layouts}. LUT-GEMM~\citep{lutgemm2024}
and FLUTE~\citep{flute2024} answer with lookup tables sized to fit in fast
memory: the same instinct as our class table, though a Leech class is a
permutation pattern where theirs hold values, so ours is read once per
block instead of once per weight. AWQ~\citep{awq2024} is the deployed 4-bit reference we
port unchanged into our own harness, and Marlin~\citep{marlin2024}
demonstrates what a mature mixed-precision kernel achieves at 4 bits,
including in the batched regimes we do not address. On the community side,
ExLlamaV2~\citep{exllamav2} ships comparable formats without an accompanying
publication; we cite it as software, since that is what it is. Our layout
study is complementary to all of these: it asks how far below 4 bits the
\emph{in-VRAM} rate can be pushed before decode cost overtakes bandwidth
savings, and finds the knee near 4.3~b/weight for lattice codes.

\textbf{Performance models.} The quantity that organizes
\S\ref{sec:layouts}, the fraction of its own byte bound each layout
converts into time, is a roofline argument~\citep{roofline2009} with two
adaptations forced by the setting. First, the ceiling is measured rather than taken from a datasheet, by a
kernel that reads no weights at all.
Second, the same layouts sit on opposite sides of the ridge point on two
cards. On an L40S every decoding arm is bandwidth-bound and the byte savings
convert; on an A100, whose HBM raises the memory ceiling without raising the
per-SM decode throughput, the same arms cross into the compute-bound region
and fall below the FP16 baseline. A roofline drawn for one of those machines
does not describe the other, which is the sharpest way to say what
\S\ref{sec:layouts} found.

\textbf{Serving engines.} Our integration is built on
\texttt{candle}~\citep{candle2023}, and the 4-bit throughput reference of
\S\ref{sec:integration} was measured in vLLM~\citep{vllm2023}, a different
engine, so that number is only used within its own stack. The output-head materialization of
\S\ref{sec:integration} is a property of the \texttt{broadcast\_matmul}
primitive, which we found unreported and have since reported
upstream;\footnote{\ifanon{\candleissueurl}{Upstream issue URL withheld for double-anonymous review.}}
it is not a property of \texttt{candle}'s served models, which reach their
heads through \texttt{Linear} and avoid the path.
