\begin{abstract}
Leech-lattice vector quantization (LLVQ) is the state of the art for 2-bit
LLM weight compression, but its published kernel decodes a single lattice
shell, is slower than the trellis-based competition, and leaves the
multi-shell decoder that the 2-bit operating point actually requires
unwritten. We present that decoder: a fused dequantize-plus-matvec CUDA
kernel for the full multi-shell codebook (301 coordinate-permutation classes
spanning eleven lattice shells in the shipped configuration), together with a
family of VRAM layouts that decouple the on-disk rate from the in-memory
rate. Our best layout, \textsc{Planes14}, replaces the 5.51-bit-per-weight
one-hot coordinate masks with binary bit planes at a uniform 14-byte stride
and reaches $2.15\times$ the throughput of an FP16 matvec while reading
4.80 bits per weight; a
sparse-overlay variant reaches 4.34 bits per weight with bit-exact
reconstruction. We also run the comparison such papers usually omit: a
deployed 4-bit kernel, ported unchanged into the same process and the same
rounds, is faster than our best layout and reads fewer bytes per projection
weight ($3.37\times$ at 4.18 against 4.80). That is \emph{kernel}
accounting over the quantized projections; on the whole-model bits per
parameter reported below the verdict runs the other way, and the two
denominators are never mixed. Read instead as fractions of each kernel's own
byte bound, the result is sharper and does not depend on which denominator
one picks --- the 4-bit kernel converts 88\% of its byte advantage into time
where ours converts 65\% --- and it relocates the remaining work from the
format to the decode.
Integrated end-to-end, a 2-bit Qwen3-4B runs at 88.4~tok/s
in 2.60~GB of VRAM: $2.03\times$ the throughput of the FP16 reference
engine in $3.09\times$ less memory, with the serving stack adding no
measurable quality cost on top of the quantized artifact. Much of that
speedup comes from replacing a per-token 778~MB transposed copy in our own
dense arm's output head, a tensor-primitive trap that stock
\texttt{candle} models avoid; with that head path held identical in both
arms, the measured kernel-plus-format gain is $1.12\times$ on the 4B and
$1.29\times$ on the 8B, and that is the number we claim. On Qwen3-8B the served model takes 5.45~GB versus
6.10~GB for the official AWQ 4-bit checkpoint, at $2.61\times$ the
reference engine's throughput, and its pre-baked int8 file is 3.16~GB,
roughly half of AWQ's. The quality gap to 4-bit shrinks from 14.5 to 7.5
MMLU points as the model doubles. We also report what 2 bits
costs ($\times 1.384$ perplexity and $-14.6$ points of MMLU on the 4B,
a deficit concentrated in reasoning-heavy subjects whose leading suspects we
have eliminated rather than explained) and a negative result we attacked
twice: a Golay-payload layout at 3.59 bits per weight whose per-slot coset
decode leaves the kernel compute-bound at 30\% of its byte bound, and which
hoisting that decode to a per-block prologue --- at zero change to the
stored bytes --- lifts only to 40\%, short of a replacement acceptance
criterion we published and timestamped before measuring it. Every figure in
this paper regenerates from CSV files committed alongside the code and the
layout table is checked against its CSV by the build; the full measurement
campaign billed \$22.83 of recorded GPU time.
\end{abstract}
