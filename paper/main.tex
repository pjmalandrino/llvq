\begin{abstract}
Leech-lattice vector quantization gives the best published quality at two
bits per weight, but its kernel decodes a single lattice shell, and the
multi-shell decoder the rate actually requires was never written. We write
it: a fused dequantize-plus-matvec CUDA kernel for the full 301-class
codebook, and a family of VRAM layouts that decouple the on-disk rate from
the rate the kernel reads. Replacing one-hot coordinate masks with binary bit
planes makes the format simultaneously smaller and faster --- 4.80 against
5.51 bits per weight, $2.16\times$ an FP16 matvec --- and a sparse overlay
reaches 4.34 at bit-exact reconstruction. Below that the curve turns: a
Golay-payload layout reaches 3.59 bits per weight but leaves the kernel
compute-bound, missing an acceptance criterion fixed before measuring.

Three measurements bound what any format can buy. A kernel that reads no
weights at all costs 45\% of the served arm, capping format work at
$4.77\times$ FP16. A deployed 4-bit GEMV, ported unchanged into the same
process and rounds, converts 88\% of its byte advantage into time where our
best layout converts 65\% --- what is left to win lies in the decode, not in
the format. And the advantage belongs to the memory hierarchy as much as to
the format: on an A100, where FP16 converts HBM bandwidth an L40S cannot,
every decoding arm falls below the FP16 baseline. Integrated end to end, a
2-bit Qwen3-4B serves at 87.0~tok/s in 2.56~GB of VRAM; with the output head
held identical in both arms, the kernel-plus-format gain grows with model
size --- $1.11\times$, $1.29\times$, $1.41\times$ at 4B, 8B and 14B.
\end{abstract}
