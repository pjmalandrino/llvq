\begin{abstract}
Leech-lattice vector quantization (LLVQ) is the state of the art for 2-bit
LLM weight compression, but its published kernel decodes a single lattice
shell, is slower than the trellis-based competition, and leaves the
multi-shell decoder --- the one the 2-bit operating point actually requires ---
unwritten. We present that decoder: a fused dequantize-plus-matvec CUDA kernel
for the full multi-shell codebook --- 301 coordinate-permutation classes
spanning 12 lattice shells in the shipped configuration --- together with a family of VRAM
layouts that decouple the on-disk rate from the in-memory rate. Our best
layout, \textsc{Planes14}, replaces one-hot coordinate masks with binary bit
planes at a uniform 14-byte stride and reaches $2.14\times$ the throughput of
an FP16 matvec while reading 4.80 bits per weight; a sparse-overlay variant
reaches 4.34 bits per weight with bit-exact reconstruction. Integrated
end-to-end, a 2-bit Qwen3-4B runs at 88.4~tok/s in 2.60~GB of VRAM ---
$2.03\times$ the throughput and $3.09\times$ less memory than the FP16
reference engine, at quality identical to the published artifact. We report
the comparison honestly: half of the end-to-end speedup comes from fixing a
per-token 778~MB transposed copy in the reference engine's output head, and
against the corrected engine the kernel is worth ${\sim}1.4\times$. We also
report what 2 bits costs --- $\times 1.384$ perplexity and $-14.6$ points of
MMLU on a 4B model, a deficit concentrated in reasoning-heavy subjects ---
and a measured negative result: a Golay-payload layout at 3.59 bits per
weight whose double-coset decode makes the kernel compute-bound. Every number
in this paper regenerates from CSV files committed alongside the code; the
entire measurement campaign cost \$5.04 of GPU time.
\end{abstract}
