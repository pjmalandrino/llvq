\begin{abstract}
Leech-lattice vector quantization gives the best published quality at 2 bits,
but its kernel decodes a single lattice shell, and the multi-shell decoder
the rate actually requires was never written. We write it: a fused
dequantize-plus-matvec CUDA kernel for the full 301-class codebook, and a
family of VRAM layouts that decouple the on-disk rate from the rate the
kernel reads. Replacing one-hot coordinate masks with binary bit planes makes
the format simultaneously smaller and faster --- 4.80 against 5.51 bits per
weight, $2.15\times$ an FP16 matvec --- and a sparse overlay reaches 4.34 at
bit-exact reconstruction. Below that the curve turns: a Golay-payload layout
reaches 3.59 bits per weight, but its per-slot coset decode leaves the kernel
compute-bound, and hoisting that decode to block level, at zero change to the
stored bytes, recovers only part of the loss. Both attempts missed acceptance
criteria fixed before measuring.

We also run the comparison this literature usually omits. A deployed 4-bit
GEMV, ported unchanged into the same process and the same rounds, converts
88\% of its byte advantage into time where our best layout converts 65\%.
That gap, not the speedup against FP16, is the result: what is left to win
lies in the decode, not in the format. Integrated end to end, a 2-bit
Qwen3-4B serves at 88.4~tok/s in 2.60~GB of VRAM; with the output head held
identical in both arms (our dense arm materialized 778~MB per token;
\S\ref{sec:integration} has the fix), the kernel-plus-format gain is
$1.12\times$ on the 4B and $1.29\times$ on the 8B. Two bits costs $\times1.384$ perplexity
and 14.6 MMLU points against FP16 at 4B; at 8B the perplexity cost falls to
$\times1.220$, the gap to a 4-bit baseline halves from 14.5 to 7.5 points,
and the served model takes 5.45~GB against that baseline's 6.10 from a file
half the size. The campaign billed \$22.83 of recorded GPU time.
\end{abstract}
