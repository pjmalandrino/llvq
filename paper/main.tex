\begin{abstract}
Leech-lattice vector quantization (LLVQ) is the state of the art for 2-bit
LLM weight compression, but its published kernel decodes a single lattice
shell, is slower than the trellis-based competition, and leaves the
multi-shell decoder that the 2-bit operating point actually requires
unwritten. We present that decoder: a fused dequantize-plus-matvec CUDA
kernel for the full multi-shell codebook (301 coordinate-permutation classes
spanning eleven lattice shells in the shipped configuration), together with a
family of VRAM layouts that decouple the on-disk rate from the in-memory
rate. Our best layout, \textsc{Planes14}, replaces the 5.51-bit-per-weight
one-hot coordinate masks with binary bit planes at a uniform 14-byte stride
and reaches $2.14\times$ the throughput of an FP16 matvec while reading
4.80 bits per weight; a
sparse-overlay variant reaches 4.34 bits per weight with bit-exact
reconstruction. Integrated end-to-end, a 2-bit Qwen3-4B runs at 88.4~tok/s
in 2.60~GB of VRAM: $2.03\times$ the throughput of the FP16 reference
engine in $3.09\times$ less memory, with the serving stack adding no
measurable quality cost on top of the quantized artifact. Much of that
speedup comes from replacing a per-token 778~MB transposed copy in the
reference engine's output head; with that head path held identical in both
arms, the measured kernel-plus-format gain is $1.12\times$ on the 4B and
$1.29\times$ on the 8B. On Qwen3-8B the served model takes 5.45~GB versus
6.10~GB for the official AWQ 4-bit checkpoint, at $2.61\times$ the
reference engine's throughput, and its pre-baked int8 file is 3.16~GB,
roughly half of AWQ's. The quality gap to 4-bit shrinks from 14.5 to 7.5
MMLU points as the model doubles. We also report what 2 bits
costs ($\times 1.384$ perplexity and $-14.6$ points of MMLU on the 4B,
a deficit concentrated in reasoning-heavy subjects whose leading suspects we
have eliminated rather than explained) and a measured negative
result: a Golay-payload layout at 3.59 bits per weight whose double-coset
decode makes the kernel compute-bound. Every number in this paper
regenerates from CSV files committed alongside the code; the full
measurement campaign billed \$19.82 of GPU time.
\end{abstract}
