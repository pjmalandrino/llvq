\title{Unfolding the Leech Lattice: Fused Multi-Shell Decoding and VRAM Layouts for 2-Bit LLM Weights}
\subtitle{What a combinatorial index costs to serve, measured against deployed 4-bit and 2-bit GEMV kernels}


\author{Pier-Jean Malandrino}
\affiliation{%
  \institution{Scub}
  \city{Dijon}
  \country{France}}
\email{pierjean.malandrino@scub.net}

\renewcommand{\shortauthors}{Malandrino}

\begin{abstract}
Leech-lattice vector quantization reports the strongest published 2-bit
quality under its evaluation protocol. Its kernel decodes one shell; we found
no implementation of the multi-shell decoder the rate requires. This paper
supplies one and measures its serving cost for decode-phase GEMV at batch~1. First, a fused
dequantize-plus-matvec CUDA kernel decodes the full 301-class codebook
without warp divergence, verified against f64. Second, the
in-VRAM rate is a design axis distinct from the on-disk rate. Four
bit-exact layouts timed in one process show binary bit planes beating
one-hot masks on size and speed at constant bandwidth (4.80 bits per
weight, $2.15\times$ FP16). Below that rate a second, irregular stream
enters, and at 3.6 bits the decode stops being shifts and masks. Third,
deployed four-bit (AWQ) and two-bit (QTIP) GEMV kernels run in the same
process. The trellis kernel reads $2.40\times$ fewer bytes than our served
layout and runs $2.27\times$ faster. The byte ratio is the gap: a codebook
too large for a lookup table unfolds at load time. Fourth, we measure the validity envelope: the trellis kernel outruns our
no-weights control, so its launch geometry, not the card, sets that floor;
an attribution places the largest term in launch geometry; and on a second
memory hierarchy every lattice arm falls below FP16. End to end, a 2-bit Qwen3-4B serves at
87.0~tok/s in 2.60~GB; with the output head held identical across arms,
the kernel-and-format gain grows with model size: $1.11\times$,
$1.29\times$, $1.41\times$.
\end{abstract}
