\begin{abstract}
Leech-lattice vector quantization (LLVQ) is the state of the art for 2-bit
LLM weight compression, but its published kernel decodes a single lattice
shell, is slower than the trellis-based competition, and leaves the
multi-shell decoder --- the one the 2-bit operating point actually requires ---
unwritten. We present that decoder: a fused dequantize-plus-matvec CUDA kernel
for the full multi-shell codebook (301 coordinate-permutation classes in the
shipped configuration), and we use it to make a second point: \emph{the rate a
weight format costs in VRAM is a design axis independent of the rate it costs
on disk}, with a measurable knee. Three layouts map that axis at fixed decoded
content and bit-exact reconstruction --- one-hot masks at 5.51 bits per
weight, binary bit planes at 4.80, a sparse overlay at 4.34 --- and a fourth
locates the floor as a measured negative result: a Golay-payload format real
at 3.59 bits per weight whose double-coset decode drops achieved bandwidth
from 425 to 195~GB/s. Against an FP16 matvec the bit-plane layout is worth
$2.14\times$; we also report where the remaining factor goes, attributing the
2.04~ms between our kernel and its own DRAM floor to unmasked latency (39\%),
payload streaming (33\%) and residual decode (19\%) with an instrumented job,
rather than asserting that the kernel is memory-bound. Integrated end-to-end,
a 2-bit Qwen3-4B serves from 2.60~GB of VRAM at ${\sim}1.4\times$ the
throughput of a corrected reference engine, and $2.03\times$ that of the
engine as it ships today --- the difference being a per-token 778~MB
transposed copy in its output head, which we characterize and which taxes
unquantized models equally. Quality is unchanged from our own sealed 2-bit
artifact, and we state what 2 bits costs against full precision:
$\times 1.384$ perplexity and $-14.6$ points of MMLU on a 4B model, a deficit
concentrated in reasoning-heavy subjects whose leading suspects we have
eliminated rather than explained. Every number regenerates from CSV files
committed alongside the code; the entire measurement campaign cost \$5.04 of
GPU time.
\end{abstract}
